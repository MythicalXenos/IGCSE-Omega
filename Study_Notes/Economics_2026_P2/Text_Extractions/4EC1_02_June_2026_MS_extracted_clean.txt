Question 
Number 
With reference to the data above and your knowledge of economics, 
assess the advantages of using pollution permits to decrease the 
amount of pollution in India.                                                                                                                                     
 
Indicative content                                                                                              
2(g) 
AO2 (3 marks)/AO3 (3 marks)/AO4 (3 marks) 
 
AO2 and AO3 
 
• Pollution permits are a market-based scheme aimed at reducing 
pollution 
• This is because firms have a financial incentive to cut emissions as 
they can sell excess permits if they pollute less 
• The GPCB found that firms in the scheme emitted 20-30% less than 
those that did not partake in the scheme 
• The trading system allows firms to choose between reducing 
pollution or purchasing permits, leading to cost-effective 
reductions in emissions 
• Firms aiming to cut costs may invest in greener production methods 
to reduce the need to buy permits 
• The initial allocation of permits can provide government funds for 
environmental projects 
• Firms that find it costly to reduce pollution can purchase permits 
instead, allowing them to operate while maintaining economic 
efficiency 
• Since the total number of permits is limited, the government can 
ensure emissions do not exceed a certain level 
 
AO4 
 
• The system relies on accurate emissions reporting and firms may 
attempt to under-report or evade regulations, especially in a large 
country like India 
• Running and regulating the permit system requires resources which 
may be costly for India 
• If too many permits are issued, pollution reduction may be 
ineffective, as firms can continue polluting 
• Large firms with more financial resources can afford to buy permits, 
while smaller businesses may struggle to compete 
• If permits are purchased mainly by firms in one area, local pollution 
levels may remain high, leading to environmental and health issues 
• Deciding how to distribute permits fairly can be challenging and 
firms may lobby for favourable treatment, leading to inefficiencies 
• The effectiveness of pollution permits depends on how well the 
system is implemented and enforced

Question 
Number 
With reference to the data above and your knowledge of economics, 
evaluate the effectiveness of monetary policy to reduce unemployment in 
Hungary.               
                                                                                                         
Indicative content                                                                                                    
4(c) 
AO2 (4 marks)/AO3 (4 marks)/AO4 (4 marks) 
 
AO2 and AO3 
 
• The National Bank of Hungary's decision to hold interest rates at 6.5% 
provides stability which can boost business confidence, encouraging 
hiring and investment 
• When interest rates go down from a high of 10.75% to 6.5%, it 
becomes cheaper to borrow, so households are more willing to buy 
more goods and services 
• Firms are also in a better position to invest by borrowing money to 
purchase items for expansion such as property and equipment 
• This increased spending by households and firms creates greater 
demand for the goods and services that firms produce, which spurs 
them to hire more workers, potentially reducing demand-deficient 
unemployment 
• The data shows a decline of 33,000 employed people. Monetary 
policy could help stabilise employment by stimulating sectors reliant 
on domestic demand 
• Lowering rates could weaken the currency making Hungarian exports 
more competitive internationally, which may create jobs in export-
oriented industries 
 
AO4 
 
• With unemployment already rising and the economy contracting, the 
time delay for monetary policy to impact the labour market may mean 
its benefits are not immediate 
• The 33,000-employment decline may reflect structural issues, such as 
skills mismatches or declining demand in specific industries, which 
monetary policy cannot resolve 
• As the data highlights slowing domestic activity, firms and consumers 
may remain cautious, limiting the effectiveness of interest rate cuts in 
stimulating employment 
• It could lead to inflationary pressures, particularly if the economy 
recovers too quickly or supply remains constrained 
• Hungary's economic challenges may be influenced by external factors 
such as European market conditions or trade disruptions, which 
monetary policy alone cannot counteract
