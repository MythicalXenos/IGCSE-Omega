## Question 3 — 16/20

| Part | Tariff | Awarded | Why |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 3(a) fringe benefits | 1 | **1** | Borderline — see note |
| 3(b) customer loyalty | 2 | **2** | Method + context + consequence |
| 3(c) current ratio | 2 | **2** | 26 000 ÷ 14 852 = **1.75** ✓ |
| 3(d) break-even limits | 6 | **4** | Level 2 (3–4): two real limitations, not the MS's |
| 3(e) on/off-the-job | 9 | **7** | Level 3 (7-9): both options, drawbacks, judgement |

**3(a) is a gift.** *"Benefits given to employees"* defines the word with itself. The MS wants
*"additional benefits **over and above the normal wage or salary**"*. A strict marker gives 0 for
circularity. **Say "extra" or "in addition to pay" and it is safe every time.**

**3(d) — two limitations, and both are the right kind.** The script argues a break-even chart
*"does not show the effect on the business's brand and reputation"* or *"customer loyalty"*, and
that it *"only project[s] whether a product may break even"*. That is genuine analysis of the
model's blindness, and it is applied to a 35-year-old brand where reputation is the asset. It
misses the MS's two named limitations — **that the chart assumes every unit is sold**, and that
**costs are drawn as a straight line** — which are the more technical, and more examinable, points.

**3(e) — why 7.** It sets the scene with the business's actual need (*"expanding its delivery
services… needs more workforce"*), chains on-the-job training to *"they can immediately join
production"*, chains off-the-job to *"more expensive"* and *"isn't working"*, and then decides
**on-the-job because the expansion needs a worker now**. The MS's AO4 for Option 1 — *"only has 3
employees so there may not be someone who can dedicate the time to train"* — is nearly the
candidate's own point about errors and wasted materials, reached from a different direction.
That is a clear, contextual judgement: Level 3.

## Question 4 — 12/20

| Part | Tariff | Awarded | Why |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 4(a) operating profit margin | 2 | **2** | 67 590 ÷ 330 606 × 100 = **20.44%** ✓ — matches the MS exactly |
| 4(b) importance of place | 6 | **4** | Level 2 (3-4): location and accessibility, no online place |
| 4(c) financial motivation | 12 | **6** | Level 2 (5-8): judgement present, the *financial* methods undeveloped |

**4(a) was nearly marked wrong, and this is why the MS is checked first.** 67 590 ÷ **330 000** is
20.48, which is not what the script wrote. The MS uses **330 606**, and 67 590 ÷ 330 606 = 20.44 —
exactly the candidate's figure. Had the marker trusted mental arithmetic over the mark scheme, a
correct 2/2 would have been recorded as 1/2. **The mark scheme outranks the marker.**

**4(b) — the missing half is *place* as more than a shop.** The answer is entirely about the
physical location: familiarity, accessibility, "customers must enjoy going". It never mentions the
**local delivery service** or the **website**, which are the MS's second AO2 and second AO3 points,
and which the case study provides. That is the difference between 4 and 5–6.

**4(c) — why 6, not 9.** The judgement is real and it is conditional: *"whilst financial methods
of motivation may be effective, it may only be partly effective in Irsi Chocolatier's case as
there are many other factors."* That is AO4, and it is the move most candidates never make.

But the question is the **impact of financial methods**, and the answer spends two thirds of its
length on everything *except* them. Bonuses, insurance and discounts are **listed, not applied**:
there is no chain from a bonus to a target to output to revenue, which is the MS's AO3. The
evaluation is then carried by *other* factors — monotony, environment, conflict, feeling undervalued
— which is relevant but answers a different question.

**Level 2 is the right band**: "sound application… interconnected points with chains of reasoning,
although there may be some logical inconsistencies", with a judgement "based on sound evaluation".
It is not Level 3 because application is "sound", not "detailed… throughout".

**What would reach 9–10 in the same words:** keep the judgement exactly as it is; add one chain
before it — *"a monthly bonus tied to boxes sold would push the two chocolatiers to raise output,
which raises revenue without hiring"*; and add the MS's own AO4 — *"if the same employee wins it
every month, the other two stop trying."*

## The four findings that matter, in order of value

### 1. Six marks were lost to answers that simply stop
- **1(f)** — *"If a business uses test marketing it can"* (0/3)
- **2(e)** — *"workers and managers"* (0/3)

Both are cut off mid-sentence, exactly where the marks were about to be earned. This is the same
pattern as the English Q3 the week before: **the answer dies at the point of development.** On this
paper alone, finishing those two sentences properly was worth more than every vocabulary
improvement combined.

### 2. Four answers defined the term instead of using it
1(b) and 1(c) are *define* questions, so definitions are correct there. But **1(g)** opens with
the business's history as background rather than as branding, and **3(e)**/**4(c)** explain what
job enrichment and financial methods *are* before applying them. In a level-based question, a
definition earns nothing: *"there are no AO1 marks for this question"* (P1 report, Q1(g));
*"there are no knowledge marks available for this type of question"* (P2 report, Q3(e)).

### 3. The judgement is there; the *condition* is what's missing
3(e), 2(f) and 4(c) all decide. That is rarer than it sounds and it is why the paper reached 55.
What none of them does is name **what the decision depends on** — *"…unless the expansion is
overseas"*, *"…if the bonus is team-based"*. That is the last mark in the AO4 band.

### 4. Calculations were flawless; one MCQ was not read
**6/6 on the three calculations** (1(e), 3(c), 4(a) — every one exact). The lost marks
were 1(a)(iii), where **C** was chosen over **D – Secondary**, and 1(a)(vi), left blank. That is
2 marks sitting in multiple-choice questions, which are the cheapest marks on the paper.
