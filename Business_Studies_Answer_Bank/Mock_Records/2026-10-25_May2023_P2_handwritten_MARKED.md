## Q1(f) — 3 marks — awarded **1**

*"Explain one reason why businesses want to use resources effectively in manufacturing."*

**What the mark scheme requires** (verbatim):
> "Award 1 mark for a reason why businesses want to use resources effectively plus 2 further
> marks for explaining the reason, for a maximum of 3 marks."
> "Answers that list two reasons with no explanation will gain 1 mark only."

**The script, as written:**
> "Businesses want to use resources efficiently so that they are more profitable and more
> profitable."

**Why 1.**
A reason is present — *"so that they are more profitable"* — and nothing explains it. The
repetition ("and more profitable") adds no second link. That is exactly the mark-scheme case:
a reason with no explanation = **1**.

**The report's own 2-mark anchor**, for comparison:
> "the candidate clearly understood the benefits of using left over material that can be
> reused. However there was **limited development** of the response and therefore only gained
> two marks."

So 2 requires a named benefit *plus* one consequence. 1 is the correct ceiling here.

**What would have scored 3:** name the waste, then the consequence, then the money —
*"using fewer raw materials (reason) means less is thrown away (1) so the business spends less
on stock (1), which lowers unit cost and raises profit (1)."*

## Q1(g) — 6 marks — awarded **4**

*"Analyse the benefits to IKEA of all its stores being franchises."*

**What the mark scheme requires:** AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3. Indicative content is *only* that
franchisees pay a percentage of profit to IKEA, and that IKEA gains revenue from every
franchised store, creating wealth.

**The examiner report's rules, verbatim:**
> "Candidates must be reminded that **there are no marks available for knowledge**, and that
> the response must be in context of IKEA."
> "Candidate should avoid describing what a franchise is."

And the printed 0-mark anchor:
> "there is a very detailed knowledge of what a franchise is but **without any application to
> the benefits** of this type of business, hence no marks were awarded."

And the printed 3-mark anchor:
> "Comments relating to the fact that **he does not have full responsibility for all the
> shops. He gains some of the profit that each of the stores make.** However there was **no
> detailed analysis** of the response which put the response at the bottom of Level 2."

**The script, as written:**
> "A franchise is the means IKEA gives the right to open up an IKEA at a location and have
> management rights over it for a share of the revenue. The franchisee gets to use IKEA's
> business model and brand at the cost of a share of their money.
> This is useful because IKEA doesn't have to manage the franchise. They share costs and
> revenue and let the franchisee use their proven successful business model. This allows them
> to expand very easily. They can open in many locations and countries globally without the
> hassle of fully running [?] on the business model and brand, and getting a share of their
> revenue."

**Why 4.**
- The first two sentences **describe a franchise**. Under the report's rule that earns nothing,
  and it is the same start that scored 0 in the printed exemplar. It does not sink the answer,
  but it earns no mark.
- After that the script does what the 3-mark exemplar does — *"doesn't have to manage"* maps to
  *"he does not have full responsibility"*; *"a share of their revenue"* maps to *"he gains some
  of the profit"* — **and then goes further**: franchisees carry the cost, so IKEA can open in
  many countries quickly. That is a third link, and it is applied to IKEA's global expansion.
- It stops short of Level 3 (5–6) because the expansion point is asserted three times
  (*"very easily"*, *"in many locations and countries"*, *"without the hassle"*) rather than
  developed once with a consequence — no mention of the capital saved, the risk transferred,
  or faster market entry than opening company-owned stores.

**What would have scored 5–6:** one chain, completed —
*"a franchisee pays for the store and the fit-out, so IKEA grows without spending its own
capital (AO2); that means IKEA can open in countries it could not otherwise afford to enter,
and still takes a share of every sale (AO3)."*

## Q3(e) — 9 marks — awarded **6**

*"To motivate employees working in its factories, IKEA is considering two motivational methods.
Option 1: bonus. Option 2: job enrichment. Justify which one of these two options IKEA should use."*

**What the mark scheme requires:** AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3. Level 2 = 4–6, Level 3 = 7–9.
The examiner report's rule:
> "It may help some candidates to gain the higher level if they give an **evaluative comment as
> to the chosen option and why it would be more suitable**."

**The report's printed 5/9 anchor (Level 2):**
> "The candidate has given reasons for both options and how it would benefit the employees.
> However the response **is not developed** and there is **limited evaluative comments**."

**The script, as written:**
> "IKEA is a huge firm with many franchisees globally. They have large factories and employee
> retention can be an issue because of motivation and the nature of factory jobs.
> IKEA could use bonuses to motivate employees. This is a financial method of motivation. By
> giving bonuses tied to performance, workers can be more motivated to work. IKEA could give
> bonuses for fulfilling quotas which would increase production or productivity. However, as
> this is a financial method of motivation, it is limited. Workers may have issues with the
> workplace and environment. They may feel like they aren't valuable or get tired or bored of
> the monotonous work.
> IKEA could use job enrichment to motivate employees. Job enrichment means to give workers
> more authority over their own work, more freedom. Giving workers discretion in decision
> making and allowing them to use different and more challenging skills [?] making them feel
> valued, making their work feel valuable. And also giving them feedback. This makes the
> workers feel more valued, more like they are making an impact and like the firm cares for
> them. It lessens the monotonousness. As a result, they are more motivated. However, workers
> may care more about money and financial benefits. Even still, because IKEA is huge, job
> enrichment would work better because of its massive scale."

**Why 6, not 5 and not 7.**
- **More developed than the 5/9 anchor**: both options are explained *and* chained
  (bonus → quota → output; enrichment → discretion → felt value → less monotony). The 5/9
  exemplar was explicitly "not developed".
- **It has a decision**, which the 5/9 exemplar arguably lacked: *"job enrichment would work
  better"*. That is the AO4 the report asks for, so it clears Level 2 comfortably.
- **It misses Level 3 (7–9)** on the quality of that decision. The stated reason — *"because of
  its massive scale"* — is the one link that does not hold: scale makes enriching every
  factory job *harder*, not easier, and the script does not say why scale helps. A Level 3
  judgement names the condition — *"job enrichment is better here because the work is
  repetitive; at IKEA's scale, a bonus scheme would have to be paid to thousands of staff,
  whereas enrichment costs training time rather than money."*
- One more point the report's indicative content offers and the script misses: **retention**
  (a bonus "will add to their salary", so employees stay). The script raises retention in the
  opening but never returns to it, so the best point it sets up is left unused.

**Also correct to note:** the definition (*"Job enrichment means to give workers more authority…"*)
earns nothing. "There are no knowledge marks available for this type of question" (June 2022
4BS1 02R report, and the same rule applies to 3(e)). It costs about 30 seconds and roughly
25 words of a 9-mark answer.

## Q4(c) — 12 marks — awarded **4**

*"Evaluate the importance to IKEA of using demographic segmentation when targeting its
customers."*

**What the mark scheme requires:** AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3. Level 1 = 1–4,
Level 2 = 5–8, Level 3 = 9–12.

**The examiner report, verbatim:**
> "A number of candidates **only completed a few lines** and this will therefore limit them on
> the marks they are able to gain."
> "Some candidates have used bullet points or numbered items, this will **not allow them to
> gain the higher levels**."
> "**Well over a third** of the cohort were able to gain a Level 2 mark."

The printed 10/12 anchor:
> "A well developed response… sound knowledge, application… analysis with **limited
> judgements**."

**The script, as written:**
> "IKEA is huge and has locations in many countries and regions. Different regions have
> different demographics. They have different needs and wants. Different populations,
> population ages and elderly.
> Therefore, IKEA cannot use the same methods and information when to target customers. They
> have to separate methods for separate demographics in different locations as the same
> methods won't work everywhere.
> If they don't specifically target different demographics in different locations using
> specific methods, it won't work effectively. They would lose out on potential profit and
> revenue. They would be less profitable.
> Therefore, it is essential that Ikea uses demographic segmentation."

**Why 4.**
About 150 words where the paper allows 22 minutes. Four things are missing, and each is worth
marks:

1. **No example of a demographic variable.** The mark scheme's indicative content is age,
   income, family life-cycle: *"families with young children"*, *"the income of its customers,
   as this varies from country to country"*, *"the older generation may need reliable and
   comfortable chairs"*. The script says "different demographics" four times without naming one.
2. **No product.** It never says what IKEA would stock differently — the entire AO2/AO3 content
   of the mark scheme is furniture (flat-pack tables, chairs, ornaments).
3. **No counterargument.** AO4 is worth 3 of 12. The scheme expects *"not all families fit into
   a category"* and *"no guarantee that IKEA's customers will buy what is expected of them"*.
   The script contains no "however" at all.
4. **The judgement is asserted, not argued.** *"Therefore, it is essential"* restates the
   question. The 10/12 anchor still only had "limited judgements", so the ceiling here is not
   the issue — the floor is.

That places it at the **top of Level 1**: application to IKEA is present but generic (it could
be any multinational), chains exist but are short, and AO4 is a slogan.

**What would have scored 7–8:** 250 words — one demographic variable applied to one IKEA product
*("in a country with a low average income, IKEA stocks its cheapest flat-pack ranges, because
families there are price-sensitive")*, one counterargument *("however, a household's income
does not tell IKEA whether they want a wardrobe in white or oak")*, and a conditional judgement
*("segmentation matters most where income varies sharply between IKEA's markets").*

## What the four answers show together

| Behaviour | Evidence | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Defining the term instead of answering | Q1(g) opens with a definition; Q3(e) defines job enrichment | 0 marks both times; a printed 0/6 exemplar does exactly this |
| Repeating a point rather than developing it | "very easily / many locations / without the hassle"; "different demographics" ×4 | holds Q1(g) at 4 and Q4(c) at 4 |
| A judgement without a condition | "job enrichment would work better because of its massive scale"; "it is essential" | the gap between 6/9 and 7/9, and between 4/12 and 7/12 |
| No counterargument on the 12-marker | Q4(c) has no "however" | AO4 = 0 of 3 on the paper's biggest question |

**The single most valuable fix before the exam:** on every level-based question, write
**"However, …"** before the judgement, and make the judgement name a condition
(*"…depends on…"*, *"…is most important when…"*). Those two moves are worth roughly 5 marks
across these four questions alone.
