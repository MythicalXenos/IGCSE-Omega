## What could not be read

- *"~~continue with providing~~ ^shutdown"* — the candidate struck **continue with providing** and wrote
  **shutdown** above the line. The strike is the interesting part: the first draft was **neutral**
  ("choose whether or not to continue"), the correction makes it a **decision**.
- *"high ~~growth in a~~ market share"* — a deletion squiggle sits over *growth in a*. What was meant is
  unambiguous from the surrounding words (*"high market share in a growing market"*), but the printed
  words cannot be recovered exactly.

## What makes it 6/6, in one line

**Two quadrants, and each one ends in a business decision rather than a definition** — *"choose whether
or not to shutdown… or re-charging and re-advertising"*, then *"prioritise supporting the correct
holiday services"*. **The Boston Matrix is never explained.** No grid, no axes, no *"a dog is…"* — which
is exactly what the cohort commentary says the weaker scripts did: *"Some candidates gave a very
detailed description of the Boston Matrix but did not apply it to TUI."*

**And note where the AO3 actually sits:** in the last clause of each paragraph
(*"improve the efficiency of the business"*, *"greatly increasing their revenue"*), not in the middle.
