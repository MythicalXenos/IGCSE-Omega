## L6 — what the candidate was saying, and what the question was asking

*What the candidate argues:* satisfying customers makes them loyal, loyal customers recommend CCT, the
customer base grows and income rises; if satisfaction is missed, customers switch to a rival and sales
and reputation fall. Aiming at profit is a sound financial objective: a well-reviewed tour can carry a
higher price, so the owner gets a good return; but tourists may see the price as exploitative and
refuse, and they may never come back to Shanghai anyway, so charging a high price once is the rational
move.

*What the question asked:* **which of the two should the owner use to measure success** — that is, as a
**yardstick**. Is customer satisfaction a better *indicator* that CCT is doing well, or is profit?

> ### 🚨 THE FAILURE, IN ONE SENTENCE
> **Every paragraph answers "should CCT try to achieve customer satisfaction / profit?" instead of
> "should CCT judge itself by customer satisfaction / profit?"** The candidate has answered a
> *Justify which option to choose* question — the wording of the previous question on the paper — and
> the stem says **measure the success of the business**.
>
> **The tell is grammatical.** A measure is a **noun you look at**: *"satisfaction is a good measure
> because a rise in it shows…"*. A course of action is a **verb you do**: *"By satisfying customers,
> CCT will increase…"*. **This answer is all verbs.** The candidate's own first three words — *"By
> satisfying customer's"* — decide the mark.
