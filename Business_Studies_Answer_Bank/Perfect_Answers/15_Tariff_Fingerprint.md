# The tariff fingerprint — what each mark value is *made of*

**Why this file exists.** Every other page tells you what to write. This one tells you **what the
marks are for**, derived from all 38 mark schemes rather than described — so that a candidate can look
at the number in brackets and know, before reading a word of the scheme, **which assessment objectives
are in the question and which are absent.**

Checked by `../verify_tariff_fingerprint.py`. Every number below is printed by it.

## 1. The fingerprint

| Tariff | Items in 38 papers | AO composition — **every paper** | Per paper | Command word in the stem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 456 | **AO1 274 · AO2 181** *(the split VARIES — see §4)* | ~7 AO1 + ~5 AO2 | *Which one of the following* (202) · *Define* (114) · *State* (114) · *What* (18) · other (8) |
| **2** | 152 | **AO2 304** (2 × 152) — AO2 only, every item | 8 | *Calculate* (104) · *Outline* (38) · *Using…* (10) |
| **3** | 152 | **AO1 456** (3 × 152) — AO1 only, every item | 12 | **Explain** (152 of 152) |
| **6** | 114 | **AO2 342 · AO3 342** (3 each × 114) → per paper **9 + 9** — **no AO1, no AO4** | 18 | **Analyse** (114 of 114) |
| **9** | 76 | **AO2 228 · AO3 228 · AO4 228** (3 each × 76) — **no AO1** | 18 | **Justify** (76 of 76) |

**The command word is a property of the tariff, not of the topic.** In 38 papers there is **not one**
6-mark Analyse that is really asking for something else, not one 9-mark that is not a Justify, not one
12-mark that is not an Evaluate, and not one 3-mark that is not an Explain. **The verb in the question
is the tariff's name.**

## 2. What each fingerprint means for the answer

### 1 mark — *a fact, or a fact applied*
**AO1 274 : AO2 181.** A *Define* is AO1 and wants the term's elements. A *State* is usually AO2 and
wants **one applied fact** — *"Just one piece of context will suffice."* **One line.** Do not explain.

### 2 marks — *application, twice over*
**AO2 only, 152 items of 152, worth 304 marks.** There is no knowledge mark and no analysis mark. *Calculate* → **method (1) +
answer (1)**; *Outline* → **point (1) + link to this business (1)**. **The second mark is a context
mark, not a second idea.**

### 3 marks — *knowledge, as a mechanism*
**AO1 only, 152 items of 152, worth 456 marks.** This is the tariff where the bank's biggest warning lives: **there is no AO2
in a 3-mark Explain, so case-study application is not required** — Pearson says so in as many words
(*"often we see candidates applying context unnecessarily"*). **And "NB No marks are awarded for a
definition"** — the three marks are a **chain**: cause → effect → **business outcome**, not a glossary.

### 6 marks — *application and analysis, and NOTHING else*
**AO2 9 + AO3 9 per paper. No AO1. No AO4.** Two consequences, both measured:

- **A conclusion earns nothing on a 6-marker**, because there are no AO4 marks to award. The strongest
  6/6 in this bank (June 2025 P1R Q3(d)) is two short paragraphs with **no conclusion**, and the other
  6/6 (June 2022 P2R 1(g)) is **three sentences, 43 words**.
- **Definitions earn nothing either** (no AO1 at 6). *Analyse* wants **chains** that end on the
  business.

### 9 marks — *analysis plus judgement, and NO knowledge*
**AO2 6 + AO3 6 + AO4 6 per paper. No AO1.** Pearson, twice: *"there are no knowledge marks available
for this question"* and *"avoid giving detailed knowledge of the two options."* **Three of the nine
marks are AO4** — a judgement — which is why a script with good chains and no decision caps in the
middle band.

### 12 marks — *the only question that pays for knowledge*
**AO1 3 + AO2 3 + AO3 3 + AO4 3 in every paper.** The 12-marker is **equally weighted** across all
four objectives, which is the only place in the qualification where knowledge is worth as much as
judgement. **This is why "define the term first" is right on a 12-marker and wrong on a 9-mark
Justify** — the schemes are not being inconsistent; the tariffs are different questions.

## 3. The shortest route to the right shape

Before writing anything, read the number in brackets and answer the **one** question it forces:

| Bracket | The question you must be able to answer before writing | If you cannot |
|---|---|---|
| **(1)** | What is the fact, and which of *this* business's nouns does it touch? | write the noun, not the business's name |
| **(2)** | How does it work, and what does it change for *this* business? | you have a point; add the link |
| **(3)** | What is the chain, and what is the business outcome at the end? | you have a definition; convert it |
| **(6)** | What are **two** chains, each ending on an outcome? | you have one; add the second |
| **(9)** | Which option, and what would make the other one right? | you have not decided |
| **(12)** | What are both sides, and what does the verdict turn on? | you have not weighed |

## 4. Devil's advocate — where the fingerprint does **not** hold

The map above is a **fingerprint, not a law**, and the honest exceptions are worth knowing:

1. **A 1-mark item can be a calculation.** May–June 2019 **P1R 1(vi)**: *"Calculate the total cost of
   selling 1,650 cakes in a week."* **(1)** — one mark, no method mark. **The tariff wins over the
   verb:** one mark means one line, whatever the command word.
2. **One-mark MCQs are phrased as calculations and still pay one mark** — *"How much did Backyard
   Shoez have to pay in Kenyan Shillings…"*, *"Using Figure 1, which one of the following is the
   discount received…"*. **A calculator question is not automatically a 2-mark question.**
3. **The 1-mark AO split is the only one that varies.** Per paper it is **7 AO1 + 5 AO2** in 29 papers,
   **8 + 4** in four, **9 + 3** in three, **6 + 6** in one and **6 + 5** in one. Every other tariff is
   exactly constant. **So do not learn a fixed count of "context marks" on the short questions — learn
   the principle: if the stem names a business and asks what it *could do*, that mark is AO2.**
4. **The 6 + 5 paper is a known defect in Pearson's own scheme, not a variation in the paper.**
   May–June 2019 **P2R 1(a)(iii)** is a **withdrawn item that still carries a mark but no AO label**
   (`KNOWN_DEFECTS.md`, and `README.md`'s note on the 79-mark file). **Twelve marks, eleven AO labels.**
5. **A 2-mark "Outline" can open with "Using Figure 1…"** — ten items do — **but it is still two
   marks, not a calculation.** Read the second half of the stem.

> ### 🎯 THE RULE THAT SURVIVES ALL FIVE EXCEPTIONS
> **The bracket tells you how many marks exist. The stem tells you what they are for.** The fingerprint
> tells you what is *usually* true — and it is true of **every** 3-, 6-, 9- and 12-mark question in 38
> papers, without exception. **On the short questions, read the stem; from 3 marks up, trust the
> bracket.**
