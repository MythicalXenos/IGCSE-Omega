# 1. June 2026 — Paper 1, Question 4(c)

**Question:** *Evaluate the extent to which quality will give Violet Cakes a competitive advantage.* (12)

**Context:** Violet Cakes is a cafe and cake business; competitors include Gail's Bakery and Flour Cafe.

> ## `[CONSTRUCTED GRADE 9 TARGET]`
>
> Quality means a product is free from defects and meets or exceeds what the customer expects. High quality at Violet Cakes is achieved through using high-quality materials and through quality control or total quality management. Quality is important if Violet Cakes is to gain a competitive advantage over rivals such as Gail's Bakery.
>
> If the food served in the cafe is of a high quality, this should attract consumers to visit, and if the cookies and cakes are of a high quality this should lead to word-of-mouth recommendations. This may improve the reputation of Violet Cakes and enable it to stay competitive against businesses such as Gail's Bakery. It may also lead to more high-profile orders, such as the cakes supplied for the wedding of Harry and Meghan, resulting in increased publicity and a larger market share. A stronger reputation is difficult for a rival to copy quickly, which is what makes quality a genuine source of competitive advantage rather than a short-term gain.
>
> However, other factors such as the location of the cafe and the price of the cakes may be more important to potential customers of Violet Cakes. A customer choosing where to buy lunch weighs convenience and cost heavily, and may never taste the difference in quality before deciding. In addition, the cost of high-quality ingredients such as chocolate from France may increase the cost of the cakes, making them uncompetitive against other businesses such as Flour Cafe. If Violet Cakes has to raise its prices to protect its margin, it risks losing exactly the price-sensitive customers that quality was supposed to win.
>
> Overall, quality is likely to give Violet Cakes a competitive advantage, but only to a limited extent. This depends on whether customers in its area value quality over price, and on whether the high-profile orders continue. In the short term the higher ingredient costs may reduce profit, but in the long term a strong reputation is more likely to make Violet Cakes the bakery of choice.

**Mark scheme content used:** AO1 (quality meets/exceeds expectations; QC/TQM) · AO2 (attracts consumers; word-of-mouth) · AO3 (reputation vs Gail's Bakery; Harry and Meghan order → publicity/market share) · AO4 (location and price may matter more; French chocolate cost → uncompetitive vs Flour Cafe)

# 2. June 2026 — Paper 2, Question 4(c)

**Question:** *Evaluate the extent to which market orientation will guarantee the future success of Nando's.* (12)

**Context:** Nando's is a restaurant chain.

> ## `[CONSTRUCTED GRADE 9 TARGET]`
>
> Market orientation means considering the opinions and preferences of the target market, and basing a product on customers' needs and wants rather than on what the business itself thinks is right for the customer. For a restaurant chain such as Nando's this shapes both the menu and the marketing.
>
> Market orientation will allow Nando's to understand what foods and tastes consumers prefer. Researching new dishes will help Nando's to assess whether new meals on its menu may be successful before it commits to a national launch. This may allow Nando's to sell meals that suit the tastes of its customers and encourage repeat purchases, which is important in a competitive restaurant market where customers can easily switch.
>
> However, researching trends and preferences takes time, and other restaurants may respond more quickly to changes in food trends. If a rival launches a similar dish first, Nando's has paid the research cost without gaining the advantage. Nando's may also prefer to use a product-orientated approach and focus on developing and improving dishes based on what it believes is superior quality and innovation, rather than primarily on customer preferences or market trends. This matters because Nando's built its reputation on a distinctive flavour that customers came to it for, and chasing every customer preference could weaken that identity.
>
> Overall, market orientation will help Nando's remain competitive to a large extent. This depends on how quickly trends change in its market and on whether Nando's can act on its research faster than its rivals. In the short term market research adds cost, but in the long term it should keep the menu aligned with what customers are willing to pay for.

**Mark scheme content used:** AO1 (market orientation definition) · AO2 (understand tastes; assess new dish success) · AO3 (meals suiting tastes → repeat purchases; changes to menu) · AO4 (research takes time, rivals respond faster; product-orientated alternative based on superior quality/innovation)

# 3. May/June 2025 — Paper 1, Question 4(c)

**Question:** *Evaluate how Four Fitness may respond to the changes in the market.* (12)

**Context:** Four Fitness is a small gym owned by Gianni; more people are using online fitness videos.

> ## `[CONSTRUCTED GRADE 9 TARGET]`
>
> Changes in customer needs and changes in consumer spending patterns directly affect how a small gym such as Four Fitness must operate. Two of the most significant changes are that customers now expect up-to-date equipment and knowledgeable instructors, and that many people are willing to exercise at home instead of paying for a gym.
>
> Gianni needs to make sure the gym equipment and the knowledge of the instructors are up to date so that the new needs of customers are met. As more people use the internet to view fitness videos there is now more choice between paying to go to the gym and working out from home at a lower cost. With a growing health and fitness industry, keeping up to date could draw health-conscious individuals to Four Fitness over the other gyms in the area, and this is something Gianni can control directly.
>
> However, this could also be a threat to Four Fitness that it would have to address in order to keep its customers. One response would be to introduce its own online videos, charging a small amount to encourage customers to watch them and offer an alternative to free platforms. This protects revenue from customers who would otherwise stop attending altogether. There are a lot of fast-paced changes in customer needs, though, and as Four Fitness is only a small business it may not have the funds to keep up with all of them. Bigger gyms offering more services at a more reasonable price may outcompete Four Fitness as they may offer more choice, and Gianni cannot match them on either scale or price.
>
> Overall, Four Fitness must respond, and the most effective response is a combination of updating its equipment and instructors and offering its own low-cost online content. This depends on how much Gianni can invest and on how many members prefer home workouts. In the short term the investment in new equipment will reduce profit, but in the long term it is the only way for a small gym to stay credible against both larger gyms and free online alternatives.

**Mark scheme content used:** AO1 (changes in customer needs; changes in spending patterns) · AO2 (up-to-date equipment/instructor knowledge; online videos as lower-cost alternative) · AO3 (growing industry draws health-conscious individuals; threat requiring response; online videos at a small charge) · AO4 (fast-paced change, small business lacks funds; bigger gyms outcompete on price and choice)

# 4. May/June 2024 — Paper 1R, Question 4(c)

**Question:** *Evaluate the importance of using methods of market research for Café Pinch to meet customer needs.* (12)

**Context:** Café Pinch serves breakfast, lunch and drinks.

> ## `[CONSTRUCTED GRADE 9 TARGET]`
>
> Market research is the collection of information about customers, competitors and the market. Café Pinch can use primary market research such as questionnaires, and secondary market research such as the internet. The two methods serve different purposes and are most valuable when used together.
>
> Primary research will allow Café Pinch to speak directly to customers who come for breakfast, lunch and drinks, which means the information is specific to its own clientele rather than a general market. Secondary research will allow Café Pinch to search for specific menu information that other cafes in the area are offering. Customers completing questionnaires will give Café Pinch feedback on the menu, giving it ideas on how it can improve and further meet the needs of its customers. The internet has a vast amount of information on it, so Café Pinch could even find information about suppliers and source one that is more local and cheaper, reducing its costs as well as improving its offer.
>
> However, without conducting secondary research as well as primary, Café Pinch will not have wider research such as knowledge of the local competitors and their business activity. A questionnaire only tells Café Pinch about the customers it already has, not about the people who chose a rival cafe instead, and those are precisely the customers it needs to attract. There might also be too much information available and this could make decision making harder and possibly less accurate, because Café Pinch may find contradictory findings and lack the expertise to interpret them.
>
> Overall, market research is very important to Café Pinch. This depends on how much time and money the business can devote to it and on whether it acts on the findings. In the short term research costs money and gives no immediate return, but in the long term it reduces the risk of launching menu items or prices that customers do not want.

**Mark scheme content used:** AO1 (primary — questionnaires; secondary — internet) · AO2 (speak directly to its customers; search competitors' menus) · AO3 (feedback on menu → improvements meeting needs; internet → local, cheaper suppliers) · AO4 (without secondary it lacks competitor knowledge; too much information makes decisions harder and less accurate)

# 5. November 2024 — Paper 1, Question 4(c)

**Question:** *Evaluate the effects on Saha of introducing Total Quality Management (TQM).* (12)

**Context:** Saha manufactures robots used in hospitals, restaurants and hotels.

> ## `[CONSTRUCTED GRADE 9 TARGET]`
>
> Total Quality Management is an approach in which quality is built into every stage of production and every employee is responsible for it. TQM should ensure that Saha's robots are manufactured without any faults, and it involves training every employee in quality assurance to reduce waste and costs. For a manufacturer supplying hospitals this is significant, because a faulty robot could harm a patient.
>
> If the robots are subject to quality checks throughout the production process, they should be delivered to the restaurants and hotels with no defects. Successful training of each employee should lead to a reduction in waste in the production of the new robots built for the home. This may improve the reputation of Saha and attract more customers to buy the robots, increasing the revenue for its business. In a market where a product failure could be dangerous as well as expensive, that reputation is worth more than the cost of the training.
>
> However, TQM involves disruption to the manufacturing facility as new processes are introduced, which could cause delays and late deliveries of robots to the hotel and hospital customers. A hospital that is let down may not order again, so the disruption itself carries a commercial risk. The cost of training all employees in TQM takes time and is costly, which could reduce profitability in the short term and increase the price of the robots, making Saha uncompetitive in the robot market. Saha is therefore trading an immediate fall in profit for a longer-term gain in quality and reputation.
>
> Overall, introducing TQM would have a positive effect on Saha in the long term, despite the short-term cost and disruption. This depends on how quickly the new processes are embedded and on whether Saha's customers value reliability over price. In the short term profitability and delivery may suffer, but in the long term defect-free robots should secure both reputation and repeat orders.

**Mark scheme content used:** AO1 (TQM ensures fault-free manufacture; trains all employees to reduce waste and cost) · AO2 (quality checks throughout → no defects on delivery; training → less waste in home robots) · AO3 (improved reputation → more customers → increased revenue) · AO4 (disruption → delays/late deliveries to hotels and hospitals; training cost and time → lower short-term profitability, higher prices, less competitive)

# 6. November 2025 — Paper 1, Question 4(c)

**Question:** *Evaluate the benefits to Ocean Vibes of becoming a franchisor.* (12)

**Context:** Ocean Vibes runs boat tours in Portugal; the owners are Alfred and Pedro.

> ## `[CONSTRUCTED GRADE 9 TARGET]`
>
> A franchise is a business that gives the right to another person or business to sell its products or services. As a franchisor, Ocean Vibes would receive set-up fees and royalty fees from each franchisee. This offers a route to growth that does not require Alfred and Pedro to fund every new location themselves.
>
> The franchisees can assist in the growth of Ocean Vibes by offering boat tours in other tourist resorts, so the business expands without Ocean Vibes having to raise the capital or manage each site directly. Ocean Vibes will receive revenue from the franchisees operating in other tourist resorts in Portugal. This will allow the brand name of Ocean Vibes to be extended to other areas of Portugal and encourage customer loyalty. The fees received by Ocean Vibes can be used to invest in the research of wildlife to fulfil the goal of Alfred and Pedro, which means franchising funds their original purpose rather than distracting from it.
>
> However, Ocean Vibes risk the chance of their reputation being damaged if the franchisees do not operate the boat tours in a responsible manner. Because wildlife tours depend on the environment, a careless operator elsewhere could harm the very thing the brand is built on, and the damage would fall on Ocean Vibes rather than on the franchisee. There are also many competitors in the boat tour market, and Alfred and Pedro may fail to find franchisees who are willing to pay the franchise fees to Ocean Vibes. Without franchisees there is no fee income, only the cost of preparing the franchise offer.
>
> Overall, becoming a franchisor would benefit Ocean Vibes to a large extent. This depends on how carefully franchisees are selected and how much demand exists among potential investors. In the short term Ocean Vibes must invest in setting up the franchise, but in the long term the fee income and wider brand recognition should grow the business far faster than opening new sites alone.

**Mark scheme content used:** AO1 (franchise definition; set-up/royalty fees) · AO2 (franchisees grow the business in other resorts; revenue from franchisees in Portugal) · AO3 (brand extended, loyalty encouraged; fees fund wildlife research fulfilling Alfred and Pedro's goal) · AO4 (reputation damaged if franchisees operate irresponsibly; many competitors and possible failure to find franchisees willing to pay fees)
