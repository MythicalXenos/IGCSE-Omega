# 16 — the five verified 12/12s, side by side

**Why this file exists.** The bank now holds **five** full-mark Evaluate scripts. They vary in paragraphing, length and conclusion placement; these examples document what those candidates did, not a required answer shape.

**Provenance.** Every script below is Pearson's own printed candidate response with Pearson's own
score. The sources and transcriptions are cited in the table and below. **None of them is a model written by this bank.**

| # | Series | Paper | Case | Question | Words (approx.) | Has a definition paragraph? | AO4 route |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Jun 2024** | P1 Q4(c) | PPS | importance of marketing to keep market share | ~340 | **yes** — *"Marketing is the use of advertising…"* | explicit *"This depends on…"* **+** short vs long term |
| 2 | **May/Jun 2023** | P1 Q4(c) | Irsi Chocolatier | impact of financial methods of motivation | **~330, 2½ paragraphs** | **no** | **named condition** — *"could be limited because it depends on factors such as nature of employee"* |
| 3 | **Jun 2022** | P1R Q4(c) | Can Culleretes | venture capital to fund Can Café | ~400, 4 paragraphs | **yes** — *"A venture capitalist is when…"* | **condition + comparison** — *"it depends on how much profit they are making"*, then a different source |
| 4 | **Jun 2022** | P1 Q4(c) | TBTS | importance of good internal and external communication | **~320, 4 short paragraphs** | **no** | **timeframe** — *"In the short term… However, in the long term…"*, no "it depends" anywhere |
| 5 | **Jun 2024** | P1R Q4(c) | Café Pinch | importance of market-research methods | ~420, 5 paragraphs | **yes** — primary and secondary defined in two sentences | **two named conditions + short run** — *"this may depend on the market and customers… in the short-run needs could be met if…"* |

> **These examples show why fixed-form advice is unsafe:** three scripts include a definition and two do not; TBTS reaches 12/12 without the phrase *"it depends"*. Their varied lengths also do not establish a target word count.

## What these five scripts illustrate

Their common evidence is not a required sentence or paragraph pattern. Each script gives the examiner enough relevant reasoning to understand the candidate's evaluation and judgement, but they express that judgement differently: a named condition, a comparison, a time horizon or an integrated final evaluation. Several develop benefits and drawbacks; these scripts do not establish that every answer must use a separate “two sides” structure or end with a condition.

| | Script 1 (PPS) | Script 2 (Irsi) | Script 3 (Can Culleretes) | Script 4 (TBTS) | Script 5 (Café Pinch) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **evaluative emphasis** | cost and research quality; short vs long term | employee type and possible effect on quality | profit sharing and comparison with alternatives | short- and long-term effects | market/customer conditions and research limits |
| **judgement placement** | explicit final paragraph | integrated in final paragraph; no separate conclusion | explicit final judgement | explicit final paragraph | final judgement with named conditions |
| **evidence type** | published candidate script and award | published candidate script and award | published candidate script and award | published candidate script and award | published candidate script and award |

Use these as examples of possible approaches, not as a checklist. A reasoned conclusion may synthesize prior evidence; it need not add a new fact or use a particular phrase.

## What each one teaches that the others do not

**1. PPS (Jun 2024) — the only one with a fourth paragraph that is purely a verdict**, and the only one
told by the examiner that it was **too long**: *"This candidate has exceeded the recommended line
allowance for this type of question so therefore could have provided a more concise response."*
It scores 12 anyway. **Length is neither required nor rewarded.**

**2. Irsi (May 2023) — the no-conclusion 12/12.** Its final paragraph *is* the judgement; there is no
*"In conclusion"* anywhere. It also uses two pieces of business knowledge not stated in the case —
**job rotation and job enrichment** — as relevant support. This is one example of appropriate own
knowledge, not a requirement to include those terms or to omit a definition.

**3. Can Culleretes (Jun 2022 P1R) — one script that compares the chosen option with an alternative**
source of finance at the point of judgement, and one where the definition is a full sentence of its own.
It is also a **Justify-shaped** choice (*should the business use venture capital*) rather than an
*importance* question; it illustrates how evaluation adapts to the task.

**4. Café Pinch (Jun 2024 P1R) — a high-scoring response with expression errors.** The roughly
420-word script contains several grammatical slips (*"allows Cafe Pinch with good information"*,
*"when needs are being meet"*, *"may allow to projected"*), yet Pearson awarded 12/12 and the examiner
comment does not cite expression as a weakness. This shows that these errors did not prevent full
credit on this response; it does not prove that clarity can never affect marking.

**5. TBTS (Jun 2022 P1) — the shortest and the only one in this sample whose judgement is framed on a
time axis.** It has no definition or literal *"it depends"* phrase, and it weighs short- and long-term
effects. This is one compact way to evaluate, not a template or a measure of the minimum required.

## Features observed in these five scripts—not universal rules

| Feature checked | In these five 12/12s |
|---|---|
| bullet points or numbered lists | **none in this sample.** The May 2023 report's caution concerns candidates on that paper; this small sample does not establish a universal mark cap. |
| a second definition mid-answer | **none observed** — two scripts define terms near the start |
| repeated links such as *"Also…"* / *"Another point is…"* | **not observed** in these scripts; their transitions vary |
| repeating the extract instead of using it | **not observed** — the cited case details are used in reasoning |
| a summary-only conclusion | **not observed** in this sample; the scripts with final judgements add an evaluation rather than merely restating points |

| May 2023 P2 (IKEA) | **10/12** | *"sound knowledge, application … with limited judgements"* |
| May 2023 P2R (M&M) | **10/12** | well developed, Level 3, docked on judgement |

**In the cited 10/12 and 11/12 examples, examiner comments identify judgement or application as relevant weaknesses.** These cases show why evaluation matters, but do not mean other AOs are unimportant or that one particular conclusion format controls the mark.
