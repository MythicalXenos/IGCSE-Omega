# THE JUNE 2026 P1R PAIRS — five questions, full mark and zero, one page each

**The newest paper prints the most usable set in the archive.** For `1(b)`, `1(c)`, `2(a)`, `2(b)` and `3(a)` the June 2026 Paper 1R report prints **both scripts on one page, with both examiner comments**, and the comments give the **score (`Total: 0 marks` / `Total: 1 mark`) instead of a level and a number** — the tidiest statement of a mark anywhere in the five reports.

**All five are one-mark questions. All five pairs turn on one thing, and it is not what candidates think it is.**

> **1(b)** a definition of the **wrong** concept · **1(c)** a definition that is **one example of it** · **3(a)** a definition of the **related noun instead of the activity**
> **2(a)** and **2(b)** *"not in the context of Spud Bros"* — **a correct answer with no context is worth nothing on a State question**

⚠️ **Report page numbers here come from the report's own printed footer**, which runs **one less than the PDF page index** — `1(b)` is on PDF page 4 and prints *"3"*. **This file cites the printed footer.**

# PAPER 1R

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q1(b) *Define the term secondary sector* — report p. 3

**Question (verbatim):** *"(b) Define the term **secondary sector**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 6):** *"1 (b) · AO1 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of secondary sector.*
> • *The sector of the economy that involves the production/construction of goods (1)*

*(1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Collected data that's already available from the competitor or government."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate does not demonstrate clear knowledge of the term 'secondary sector'. Total: 0 marks"*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"The production process involving the conversion of raw materials into finished and semi-finished goods."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate shows good knowledge of the term 'secondary sector'. Total: 1 mark"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"This was the first 'define' question on the paper. This question saw many candidates provide a clear definition of the term 'secondary sector'. However, **some candidates did confuse the term with secondary market research**. Centres are reminded that any area of the specification can appear in the paper."*

🔬 **⚠️ THE 0-MARK SCRIPT IS NOT A WRONG ANSWER. IT IS THE RIGHT ANSWER TO A DIFFERENT QUESTION — and the examiner names the exact collision:** *"some candidates did confuse the term with secondary market research."*

> **secondary sector** — the part of the economy that makes things.
> **secondary market research** — data someone else already collected.

**Both start with "secondary".** ⚠️ **This is the fourth place in the archive where the same word-family collision has cost a mark** — and it is already in the bank as a rule: **June 2026 P1R `1(b)` is the word-collision table's own row, and June 2024 P1R printed it again.** **The definition is right; the word that was defined is wrong.**

⭐ **The 1-mark script is the model answer to a Define, and it does three things in 15 words:** names the **process** (*"the production process involving…"*) · names the **input** (*"raw materials"*) · names the **outputs, and says there are two kinds** (*"finished **and** semi-finished goods"*). The mark scheme asks for *"the production/construction of goods"* — the script gives that and more.

**One detail worth copying: "semi-finished".** A candidate who writes only *"raw materials into finished goods"* has the right idea and a thinner definition; **adding the half-made goods is what shows the sector has been understood rather than recited.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q1(c) *Define the term e-commerce* — report p. 4

**Question (verbatim):** *"(c) Define the term **e-commerce**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 7):** *"1 (c) · AO1 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of e-commerce.*
> • *Selling goods online/on the internet (1)*

*(1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"It is digital e-mail between business and consumers."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate does not show clear knowledge: **there is some confusion shown because they have discussed digital communication**. Total: 0 marks"*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Selling goods and services online"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate shows good knowledge of the term 'e-commerce'. Total: 1 mark"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"This was a question where candidates responded well, showing accurate knowledge of e-commerce, although **some candidates did confuse the term with digital communication**."*

🔬 **⭐ THE TWO SCRIPTS ARE THE SAME LENGTH TO THE WORD. The difference is ONE VERB.**

| | 0 marks | 1 mark |
|---|---|---|
| script | *"It is **digital e-mail** between business and consumers."* | *"**Selling** goods and services online"* |
| words | 9 | 5 |
| the scheme's words | — | *"**Selling** goods online/on the internet"* ✓ |
| what it describes | a **communication channel** — e-mail | a **commercial transaction** — selling |

**E-mail between a business and a consumer is real, and it is not e-commerce.** ⚠️ **The examiner's own name for the trap: *"confusion… with digital communication."*** — and **the archive's own word-collision table already carries `e-commerce / digital communication` as a row.** **What separates the two scripts is `selling`.** The mark scheme's first word is the discriminator.

⭐ **And the 1-mark script adds `services` where the scheme says only `goods`.** *"Selling goods **and services** online"* — **broader than the scheme's own bullet, and 1/1.** ⚠️ **On a Define, being more complete than the mark scheme is safe; being narrower than it is fatal.** *"digital e-mail"* is narrower. Nothing was lost for the extra word.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q2(a) *State one current asset Spud Bros may have* — report p. 12

**Question (verbatim):** *"2 (a) State **one** current asset **Spud Bros** may have."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 10):** *"2 (a) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a current asset Spud Bros may have.*
> • *Stock of potatoes/toppings (1)*
> • *Cash from the sale of potatoes (1)*

***NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of Spud Bros.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Cash"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"**This candidate provides a current asset, however they do not apply this to the context of Spud Bros.** Total: 0 marks"*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Cash , they make from selling their baked potatoes."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate provides a current asset that is in the context of Spud Bros. Total: 1 mark"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"This was a 'state' question, where candidates responses should be contextualised to gain access to the mark for this **AO2** question. Candidates who responded well to this question provided a clear current asset, whilst ensuring it was in the context of Spud Bros."*

🔬 **⚠️ THE MOST VIVID PROOF IN THE WHOLE BANK THAT A CORRECT ONE-WORD ANSWER EARNS NOTHING. The word `Cash` is not just correct — it is one of the scheme's own two bullets, verbatim.** The examiner does not dispute it: *"This candidate **provides** a current asset."* **One mark, refused.**

> **The whole difference, as the examiner states it: *"however they do not apply this to the context of Spud Bros."***

⭐ **And the 1-mark answer's context is FOUR WORDS: *"they make from selling their baked potatoes."*** It does not add business content, definitions, or length — **it says where the cash came from, and Spud Bros' potatoes are the paper's context.** The scheme's own bullet is *"Cash **from the sale of potatoes**"* — **the script's seven words do the same job with the business's actual product.**

⚠️ **A note on the two scripts' punctuation, because it is the sort of thing that makes candidates distrust their own answers:** the 0-mark is a bare noun; the 1-mark runs a **space before the comma** (*"Cash , they make…"*). **Neither punctuation choice decided anything** — the comment is about context, and only about context (rule 55 family).

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q2(b) *State one reason why Spud Bros motivates its employees* — report p. 13

**Question (verbatim):** *"(b) State **one** reason why **Spud Bros** motivates its employees."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 10):** *"2 (b) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a valid reason why Spud Bros motivates its employees.*
> • *Employees will work hard to sell the potatoes (1)*

***NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of Spud Bros.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"To increase the output results of the business , so the Spud Bros will gain more sales & profits."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"**This is a correct answer but it is not contextualised to Spud Bros.** Total: 0 marks"*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Spud Bros motivates its employees so that they would provide quality services to customers by putting the toppings for the potatoes in a pleasant way."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This is a correct reason why Spud Bros motivates its employees. It is also contextualised to Spud Bros. Total: 1 mark"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"Centres are reminded that 'State' questions must contain context in relation to the given business scenario. This question saw many candidates state one correct reason why Spud Bros motivates its employees. A good proportion was also able to contextualise their response, allowing them to achieve the AO2 mark."*

🔬 **This pair is the reverse of Q2(a) — and it is the harder one, because the 0-mark answer DOES write the business's name twice.** *"so the **Spud Bros** will gain more sales"*. ⚠️ **Naming the business is not context.** The examiner's word is *"contextualised"* — and the archive has five separate confirmations that a name-drop is not it, the first being the June 2019 booklet's *"the only reference made to the scenario is the name of the business"* (`2019-06_P1_Official_Exemplar_Booklet.md:143`).

**What the 0-mark answer actually reasons:** motivation → more output → more sales and profit. **Every step is generic. Any business in the world could be substituted and the sentence would still run.**

⭐ **What the 1-mark answer does instead: it names the job.** *"putting the toppings for the potatoes in a pleasant way"* — **Spud Bros sells baked potatoes, and this candidate has located the motivation in the physical task the business is doing.** Compare the scheme's own bullet, *"Employees will work hard **to sell the potatoes**"* — **the scheme and the script both finish on the potato.**

⚠️ **The 0-mark script is longer, better punctuated and better spelled.** The 1-mark script's *"by putting the toppings for the potatoes in a pleasant way"* is an odd construction. **The mark turned on one adjective's worth of context: `potatoes`.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q3(a) *Define the term niche marketing* — report p. 23

**Question (verbatim):** *"3 (a) Define the term **niche marketing**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 15):** *"3 (a) · A01 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of niche marketing.*
> • *Targeting a small segment of the whole market (1)*

*(1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Small market within a large market"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"**This candidate shows knowledge of the term 'niche market', however they do not show knowledge of niche marketing.** Total: 0 marks"*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"smaller market , **targeting** specific groups of customers with specific products"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate shows good knowledge of the term 'niche marketing'. Total: 1 mark"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"This question saw many candidates provide a clear definition of the term 'niche marketing'; however some candidates showed little knowledge of the term and therefore could not access the question. Centres are reminded that any area of the specification can appear in the paper."*

🔬 **⭐⭐ THE SHARPEST DISTINCTION IN THE FILE, AND THE EXAMINER SPELLS IT OUT: *"knowledge of the term 'niche market'… they do not show knowledge of niche marketing."***

> **niche market** — the *thing*: a small, specific segment of a market.
> **niche marketing** — the *activity*: **targeting** it.

**The 0-mark script defines the noun correctly.** *"small market within a large market"* is a legitimate description of a niche market — and it is scored 0, because **`marketing` in the question is a participle doing work: it asks what the business DOES with the niche.**

⭐ **The 1-mark script's discriminating word is the same one the mark scheme opens with: `targeting`.** Both write *"smaller market"*. **One adds `targeting` and wins; the other stops.**

⚠️ **What to carry out of this page, and it generalises past this question:** ⭐ **for any Define containing an `-ing` word or a concept pair — `niche marketing` vs `niche market`, `market segmentation` vs `market segment`, `e-commerce` vs `digital communication` — the mark scheme is asking for the DOING, not the THING.** **Define the activity with a verb.**

# WHAT THESE TEN SCRIPTS SETTLE

**A five-question sweep of one paper, all of it one-mark questions, and the failures sort into exactly three kinds:**

| Failure | Questions | The examiner's own words |
|---|---|---|
| **Defined the wrong concept** | 1(b), 1(c) | *"did confuse the term with secondary market research"* · *"confusion… with digital communication"* |
| **Defined the thing, not the activity** | 3(a) | *"knowledge of the term 'niche market'… do not show knowledge of niche marketing"* |
| **Correct content, no context** | 2(a), 2(b) | *"do not apply this to the context of Spud Bros"* · *"a correct answer but it is not contextualised"* |

**⭐ And every one of the five full-mark answers is SHORT — 15 words, 5 words, 7 words, 20 words, 10 words.** The longest is *"Spud Bros motivates its employees so that they would provide quality services to customers by putting the toppings for the potatoes in a pleasant way"* (20 words, one mark). **Four of the five zero-mark answers are the same length or longer**, and two of them are better written.

⚠️ **Two of the five zero-mark answers contain the mark scheme's own content verbatim** — `Cash` is one of the scheme's two bullets on 2(a); *"small market within a large market"* describes the scheme's *"small segment of the whole market"* on 3(a). **Both scored nothing.** ⭐ **On a one-mark question, content is not the test: the test is whether the answer is aimed at the question's own word — `sector`, `e-commerce`, `marketing`, `Spud Bros`.**
