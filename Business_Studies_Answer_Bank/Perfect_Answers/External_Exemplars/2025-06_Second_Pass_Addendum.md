# ⭐⭐⭐ A TENTH VERIFIED 6/6 ANALYSE — and it overturns the "multiple points" assumption

## June 2025 P1 (*Four Fitness*, 2 gyms in Italy) — **Q1(g)** — Analyse the effectiveness of the **wide span of control** used by Gianni Polvani — **6/6**

> **Stem:** *"Gianni Polvani's span of control is 14."*
> **Question (6):** *"Analyse the effectiveness of the wide span of control used by Gianni Polvani."*

### ✅ Awarded **6 marks**
> *"Gianni Polvani having a wide span of control means that **he can tell his 14 employees what he wants done and how**. This means that there is **less likely to be miscommunication or misunderstanding** between him and his employees with what he wants done regarding his **two gyms and the physical and social activities** that take part in them with the **gym members**.*
>
> *By having less misunderstandings due to the wide span of control, **both of the gyms are more likely to become more successful** and so the employees who are **highly trained instructors** can **better teach the gym members** what to do and help them if they don't understand something. Therefore this can lead to **the business performing more effectively** and so **the business is more likely to maintain a positive cashflow**."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"6 marks awarded. The candidate has provided **1 point with developed strands**, analysing the effectiveness of the wide span of control. There is **application throughout their response**. Candidates are reminded that **there are no marks for knowledge in this question** and their response must be in the context of *Four Fitness* to access the AO2 marks."*

> ## 🔬 **"ONE point with developed strands" = 6/6.**
> Two paragraphs. **One idea** — a wide span means Gianni communicates directly with all 14, so fewer misunderstandings.
> **Everything after sentence one is consequence, not a new point.** Misunderstandings fall → gyms succeed → instructors teach better → the business performs better → **cashflow is maintained**.
>
> ### 🚨 WHAT THIS SETTLES
> | Assumption | The evidence |
> |---|---|
> | "You need two or three separate points for 6/6" | **No.** This 6/6 has **one** point. |
> | "Longer is better" | This is **~140 words**. It outranks the CCT 6/6 below at similar length. |
> | "Name the theory" | **"There are no marks for knowledge in this question."** |
>
> ⭐ **The lesson: depth of chain beats breadth of coverage.** One point driven four steps to a **business outcome** (*positive cashflow*) is worth more than three points driven one step each.
> ⚠️ **And note the destination: a financial consequence.** Every 6/6 Analyse in the bank ends at something measurable for the business — *stock piles*, *sales*, *cashflow*, *bad reputation needing time to recover*.

### ❌ The 0-mark companion — and why
> *"14 qualified instructor would lead to a good coordination as each would have a specific job, if they were less they would be shared their tasks making them to have fatigue very quickly and lose motivation."*
> **Examiner:** *"**0 marks awarded. The candidate demonstrates no knowledge of a wide span of control.**"*

**Note:** *"the candidate demonstrates no knowledge"* — on a question whose AO list is **AO2 + AO3 with no AO1**. So "no knowledge" here means **no understanding of the concept itself**, which is what makes application impossible. The concept must be correctly understood before it can be applied.

### ⭐ The most common 0-mark response
> *"The most common incorrect response for zero marks referred to **more work getting done as there are more employees to do it**."*
> **Why it fails:** a wide span of control is about **how many people one manager oversees** — not about headcount. It answers *"why are 14 employees good?"* rather than *"what is the effect of Gianni managing 14?"*.

# ⭐⭐⭐ A SEVENTH VERIFIED 9/9 JUSTIFY — June 2025 P2R (*Rabanne*) — Q2(f)

> **Question (9):** *"Rabanne is considering the following two options to help improve communication within the business: **Option 1: communicate all information through text messaging/email. Option 2: hold weekly meetings.** Justify which one of these two options Rabanne should choose."*

### ✅ Awarded **9 marks**

> *"Rabanne should hold weekly meetings to improve communication amongst employees around the world either online or in person. This is because holding weekly meetings **creates & improves personal relations** between manufacturers & costume designers. Holding weekly meetings gives an opportunity for managers & manufacturers to directly communicate their opinions & problems they face. This means that **manufacturers & designers & employees feel heard & understood**. This leads to a **sense of belonging amongst designers in Rabanne**, ultimately **increasing motivation to deliver high quality perfumes & handbags** whilst **minimising miscommunication**.*
>
> *Rabanne should not communicate all information via email & messaging as **not every manufacturer will have the time to check their emails**. This means that **valuable pieces of information gets lost** amongst managers & manufacturers.*
>
> *In conclusion, Rabanne should hold weekly meetings as it gives employees this sense of belonging as **their opinions are physically heard**. Whereas **communication via messages can get lost in inboxes** which leads to manufacturers **missing important business objectives announcements**. **However, the success of this depends on how much employees get to voice their own concerns in business.**"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response gained 9 marks. This response shows **what is expected from candidates in order to gain the higher marks for this type of 'justify' question**. This candidate has chosen to look at **both options** which is acceptable. They have shown an understanding of both options, they have **included context throughout their response with logical chains of reasoning** and have **made a reasonable judgement about their final choice of option**."*

**ResultsPlus Examiner Tip (verbatim):**
> *"Context can be a range of statements as shown here: **fashion, handbags, costume design, perfumes**."*

> ## 🔬 THE ANATOMY — and it is the three-sentence conclusion again
> | Move | Text |
> |---|---|
> | **1. Decide with a reason** | *"Rabanne should hold weekly meetings… as it gives employees this sense of belonging as their opinions are physically heard."* |
> | **2. Name the alternative's drawback** | *"Whereas communication via messages can get lost in inboxes which leads to manufacturers missing important business objectives announcements."* |
> | **3. Concede the counter-case** | *"**However, the success of this depends on how much employees get to voice their own concerns in business.**"* |
>
> ### ⭐⭐ THIS IS THE 9/9 CONCLUSION, EXACTLY AS THE BANK DESCRIBES IT
> **Decides with a reason · names the alternative's drawback · concedes the long-run counter-case.** Five previous 9/9 scripts show the same three moves; this is the sixth. **The template is not a guess.**
>
> ⭐ **The named condition here is unusually good** — *"how much employees get to voice their own concerns"* is **specific to weekly meetings**. It cannot be pasted onto a question about pricing or location. **Apply the paste test: this clause fails it, and that is exactly why it works.**
>
> ### ⭐ AND NOTE THE STRUCTURE CHOICE
> **The candidate argues BOTH options** — one paragraph on meetings, one on messaging, then concludes. The examiner: *"This candidate has chosen to look at both options **which is acceptable**."*
> **So both routes work:** develop only your choice and rebut the alternative, **or** develop both and conclude. **What is not optional is the naming of the alternative's drawback and the concession in the conclusion.**

# ⭐⭐⭐ THE STATE MINIMUM, QUANTIFIED AT LAST

Every report since 2019 has said State answers lose marks for lacking context. **June 2025 P1 is the first to state how much context is enough.**

> **Examiner tip (verbatim), Four Fitness Q2(a):**
> *"**Just one reference to the context of the business is enough for a 'state' question.**"*

### 🚨 AND THE COMPANION RULE — the reason candidates think they *have* contextualised

> **Examiner tip (verbatim), Four Fitness Q1(d):**
> *"**Using the name of the business is not application (AO2) as the name of the business 'Four Fitness' is used in the question.**"*

### The contrast pairs, all from June 2025

| Question | 1 mark | 0 marks |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 Q1(d)** — a reason to motivate staff | *"In order to retain its **gym instructors** and prevent them from going to **other nearby gyms in Italy** that may pay higher."* | *"it is Because it to employees keeping working for **Four**"* — **name only** |
| **P1 Q2(a)** — a location factor | *"close to **instructors**"* / *"close to **customers who want to be healthy**"* | *"locate away from competitors"*, *"locate close to the required supplies"*, *"locate close to the target market"* — **all generic** |
| **P1 Q2(b)** — why branding matters | *"To attract more potential **gym members** who might be looking for a **suitable gym to work out**."* | *"Branding may be important to **your** fitness as they want a **good quality product and brand image**."* — **generic, and note it says "your fitness", not "Four Fitness"** |
| **P1R Q2(a)** — a financial motivation method | *"A bonus for the **tour guides** who achieve…"* | *"Providing bonuses if the employees are achieving their goals and targets."* — **generic** |
| **P1R Q2(b)** — benefit of satisfying customer needs | *"the customers might choose to recommend the **tour company** to people visiting **Shanghai**."* | *"This is a correct answer **but it is not contextualised to CCT**."* |

> ## 🔬 THE TEST THAT SETTLES IT
> **Could this sentence be written about a different business without changing a word?**
> *"branding gives a good quality product and brand image"* → **yes, generic, 0.**
> *"attract more potential gym members"* → **no, this is a gym, 1 mark.**
> ⭐ **And the test Pearson itself suggests (P1R Q2(a) tip):** *"encourage candidates to check if their response is **generic and can be applied to any business** or have they included phrases that link it directly to the given scenario? For this scenario reference to **the service provided**, eg **tour guide, tourist, bicycle company**, would be sufficient."*
>
> ### ⭐ THE FOUR CONTEXT ROUTES, MADE CONCRETE
> | Route | CCT (P1R) | Four Fitness (P1) | Rabanne (P2R) |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **Product / service** | bicycle tour · tour guide | gym members · instructors · suitable gym to work out | perfumes · handbags · costume design |
> | **A competitor** | *"CCT's rival"* · *"rural tour companies"* | *"other nearby gyms in Italy"* | *"Dior"* |
> | **The industry / market** | *"tour guide market in Shanghai"* | *"customers who want to be healthy"* | *"fashion"* |
> | **Type of business** | *"bicycle company"* | *"two gyms"* | *"luxurious and high quality"* |
>
> **One reference is enough for State. That is the cheapest mark in the paper and the most commonly missed.**

# ⭐⭐⭐ THE 3-MARK EXPLAIN — the anatomy confirmed by a matched pair

June 2025 P1R **Q1(f)** — *Explain one benefit to the business owner of operating as a private limited company.*

### ✅ **3 marks**
> *"One benefit is that the owner will have a **limited liability** which means that **he isn't responsible for any debts of the business**, and therefore **won't have to sell his personal possessions to pay the debts**."*
> **Examiner:** *"This has a **clearly identified benefit, with clear interconnected points of development**. 3 marks."*

### ⚠️ **2 marks**
> *"They have a **separate legal identity**. This means that **owners are only required to pay debts on the amount that they have invested** in the business."*
> **Examiner:** *"There is a **clearly identified point, with ONE interconnected point**. 2 marks."*

> ## 🔬 **1 point + 2 developed steps = 3. 1 point + 1 developed step = 2.**
> The 3-mark answer differs from the 2-mark answer by **exactly one further consequence**.
> ⭐ **And the pair comes from the same paper, same question** — so this is not a marker's mood, it is the structure.

**The same anatomy, four more times in June 2025:**

| Paper | Question | 3/3 | Why it worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1R Q2(d)** | a reason governments introduce tariffs | *"**Tariffs would result in a higher tax on imports**, this means the business would need to **spend more on importing goods**, as a result, **profit margins would decrease**."* | reason → cost up → margin down |
| **P1R Q2(e)** | why quality control is important | *"helps business **reduce mistakes**… quality control is **checking the final output**… This leads to **customer satisfaction**, so they will **gain more customers** and **increase their market share**."* | reason → mechanism → two consequences |
| **P1R Q1(f)** | benefit of a private limited company | limited liability → not responsible for debts → **won't sell personal possessions** | reason → meaning → implication |
| **P2 Q2(d)** | a problem of poor communication | *"**Messages being delayed or sent to the wrong receiver** might cause **decision making to slow down**. This can cause the business to **miss out on opportunities such as securing a deal with a new supplier**."* | problem → effect → **concrete missed opportunity** |
| **P2R Q2(e)** | how the statement of comprehensive income helps | *"shows the **profits and losses**… This could help indicate a business **where costs are too high**… to thus **make a decision on how to lower costs / increase sales**"* | what it shows → what it reveals → what you do |
| **P2 Q3(b)** | an outline: proximity of labour (2) | *"Partner would not come late… **would be able to produce more**… hence leading to **more sales revenue**"* | point + development + context |

### ⭐ AND PEARSON'S OWN COUNT OF THE STRUCTURE
> **Examiner tip (verbatim), P2R Q2(e):**
> *"Candidates should be reminded that from the mark scheme **they can only gain 2 further marks for explaining the reason**. **Do not waste time in the examination with longer responses than necessary.**"*

**So: 1 for identifying the reason, 2 for developing it. Nothing beyond that can score.** The 3-mark answer is **three sentences**. Not five.

### 🚨 THE THIRD-MARK FAILURE, named precisely
> **P1R Q2(c):** *"a common issue observed was candidates **failing to reach the third mark**, as their **second strand of development often involved merely restating the initial point**."*
> **P1R chunk 2:** *"Learners need to ensure they sufficiently develop their explanations by providing **distinct, further reasoning or implications**… rather than **reiterating**. This is crucial for accessing all available marks in 'Explain' questions."*

> ### 🎯 **DEVELOPMENT = A NEW STEP DOWN THE CHAIN. NOT A REWORDING.**
> *"…which means the business would have less mistakes"* after already saying *"reduces errors"* is **reiteration**. It looks like development; it is not.

### ❌ And the 0-mark companions — both failing on **incoherence**, not length
| Script | Verdict |
|---|---|
| *"With tariffs, importing goods may be **cheaper**, allowing this business to import more at a lower cost… Therefore, potentially increasing revenue."* | *"This answer **incorrectly addresses** the question. **0 marks.**"* — tariffs raise import costs; the premise is backwards |
| *"Quality control will **increase the competition** for the business, prevent it will be leave out in the market."* | *"This **incorrectly addresses** the question. **0 marks.**"* — quality control does not increase competition |
| *"Total quality management gives a business a better name but increase in customer satisfaction... This will increase profit which increases revenue."* | *"There is **no understanding of TQM demonstrated** in the response therefore the reference to 'increased customer satisfaction/higher productivity' is **not awarded**."* |

> ⭐ **Note the TQM verdict carefully:** the candidate *did* name good consequences, but because the definition embedded in the answer was **wrong**, the consequences were not awarded. **A wrong opening premise poisons everything built on it.**

# ⭐⭐ DEFINING — the two-mark spread on a one-mark question

June 2025 P1R **Q3(a)** — define **share capital**:
- *"raised by selling shares."* → **1 mark** — *"a correct answer for a definition of share capital."*
- *"capital that consists only of shares"* → **0 marks** — *"This is incorrect."*

June 2025 P2R **Q3(a)** — define **just in time (JIT)**:
- *"A method of **lean production** where **stock is bought only when needed** to avoid **waste** materials/resources."* → **1 mark**
- **Examiner tip:** *"**Practise with candidates defining the terms rather than describing what they are. No marks can be awarded for a description of the term in a define question.**"*
- **And on the cohort:** *"Candidates who did not gain the mark **lacked knowledge of Just in time**. Candidates are reminded that **all areas of the specification can appear** in the examination papers **no matter how discrete they are**."*

June 2025 P1 **Q1(b)** — define **trade bloc**:
- ✅ *"a group of states or countries that have an agreement to trade between each other freely without many conditions or fees, **e.g. the trade bloc of the EU**"* → **1 mark**
- ❌ *"When a business might not be able..."* → **0 marks**
- ⚠️ **The most common wrong answer: "trade being blocked/banned"** — the word *bloc* read as *block*.
- ⚠️ *"a small number of candidates only included **either part** of this response so they received zero marks"* — **the definition has two parts: countries working together, AND avoiding tariffs/taxes. Half the definition is no mark.**

June 2025 P1 **Q1(c)** — define **job production**:
- ⚠️ *"A small number of candidates incorrectly referred to a **human resources process**, presumably **because of the word 'job'**. For example, 'creating more job opportunities'."*
- ⚠️ *"making new jobs to provide a benefit for the company"* → **0 marks**

> ### ⭐ THREE MORE WORD-COLLISIONS → the cluster is now **22**
> | Term | Read as | Source |
> |---|---|---|
> | **trade bloc** | *trade being blocked/banned* | Jun 2025 P1 |
> | **job production** | a jobs/HR process | Jun 2025 P1 |
> | **share capital** | *capital that consists only of shares* (circular) | Jun 2025 P1R |
>
> ⭐ **And the define structure for a two-part term:** *trade bloc* needs **countries cooperating** AND **the effect (no tariffs/taxes)**. **"Either part" alone = 0.**

# ⭐⭐ CALCULATIONS — three more precise traps

### 1. Premature rounding (P1R Q1(e), current ratio)
> *"it was observed that those who **rounded their figures up or down prematurely**, affecting the accuracy to two decimal places, did not achieve the full 2 marks."*

⚠️ **NEW: do not round intermediate steps.** Carry full precision and round **only the final answer**.
**And the pair:** correct answer `2.41` = **2 marks**; *"the correct substitution of numbers is evident… However, the answer was **incorrectly rounded up** and not calculated to the two decimal places indicated"* = **1 mark**.
> **Examiner tip:** *"Always check the instructions regarding decimal places as if **two accurate decimal places are not provided then the mark will be lost even with a correct response**."*

### 2. The discount error — a **fifth** confirmation (P2 Q4(a), Starbucks meal)
> *"This question required students to calculate the cost of a meal for a Starbucks employee **after a discount was applied**… Some however **calculated the discount only**."*
> *"This candidate was awarded **1 mark for calculating the discount which was the first step in the calculation. However, they failed to deduct the discount from the full price**."*

### 3. The workings mark, restated three ways
> *"Always show workings as a **mark is always available for correct workings even if the final answer is incorrect**."*
> *"This is **a good example of why we encourage centres to remind candidates to show their working out**. Whilst this candidate has not got the final correct answer, due to their working out, **and as per the mark scheme**, they have gained 1 mark as they have **correctly substituted numbers into the formula**."*
> *"Whilst this candidate has not got the final answer correct, due to their working out… they have gained 1 mark."*

> ### 🎯 AND THE DISCOUNT PATTERN AT FIVE INSTANCES IS NOW CONCLUSIVE
> **2019 P1R · Nov 2023 P1 · Nov 2024 P2 · Jun 2025 P2 · (and Jun 2025 P1's fencepost version) — work out the discount, then FORGET to apply it.**
> ⭐ **It is the single most reliable 1-mark loss in the qualification.** Write the final price **on the answer line** and check it is **not** the discount.

# ⭐ OTHER NEW ITEMS

### P2R Q2(d) — Analyse (6) — **5 marks**, and a warning about the stem
> *"Rabanne will be able to have **less wastage** when resources are used efficiently… therefore customers will feel satisfied with the quality of the perfumes & costumes. As a result this leads to **improved customer satisfaction**, further **increasing repeat sales** and **increases the profits** of the costume & perfume business."*
> **Examiner:** *"scored **5 marks**. The candidate has identified **two reasons** — 'manage costs' and 'produce high quality packaging' — this is then linked with development to 'less wastage of materials' and 'prevent defects'. The response is in context with 'costumes and perfumes'."*
> **Tip:** *"remember **not to simply repeat the information given**. This is to be used and **linked to the question rather than just included in a response without development**."*

⚠️ **Two reasons with development each scored 5, not 6.** Compare with the Four Fitness 6/6: **one reason, driven deeper**. Depth again.

### Rabanne Q3(e) — the second Justify (9) — **script obtained, mark not stated in the retrievable text**
> **Stem:** *Option 1: increasing the number of product trials. Option 2: increasing advertising.*
> *"I recommend Rabanne to choose option 2: increasing advertising. As option 1, increasing product trials, **can lose Rabanne's first mover advantage**, as its competitors like **Dior** might release products quicker than they do. And since Rabanne's customers are global, **it is very hard to gather information / comments about the perfumes that can represent the entire customer base**… Hence increasing advertising is enough.*
> *Increasing advertisements can increase exposure to the general public, as **aftershaves is suitable for the general male population and perfume is suitable for female population**… This means they can **dominate the market** by having more exposure than its competitor…"*

> ### ⚠️ HONESTY NOTE
> The report's surrounding commentary names the failure mode for the cohort — *"Those who **did not reach the top of level 3** often **lacked an evaluative comment as to why their choice was the most appropriate**"* — but **no mark is printed against this script in the retrievable text.** It is **not** recorded as a verified full-mark exemplar. It is filed as a **strong script with an unconfirmed mark**, and it illustrates the named-condition point: **this answer states a recommendation up front and argues for it, but the conclusion does not name a condition.**
> ⚠️ Also: the answer **opens with the recommendation** — recall the standing rule *"Do not repeat the question at the start of your answer."* Stating a verdict in sentence one is not the same as repeating the question, and the 9/9 Rabanne Q2(f) above also opens with its choice. **So opening with the decision is safe; opening by restating the stem is not.**

### ⭐ CCT Q1(g) — the "context variety" instruction, quoted exactly
> *"Context must be **woven throughout the entire response, appearing in all paragraphs**, and should demonstrate **variety rather than mere repetition**."*

**Two requirements, both checkable:** *all paragraphs* · *different routes each time*.

### ⭐ Starbucks Q3(d) — the Level 1 that names a reason but stops
> *"Starbucks wants to promote its existing employees as a **reward and thanks** for working at Starbucks. It also **motivates** the employees to work hard for the promotion."*
> **Examiner:** *"This is a **level 1** response… an example of where a candidate **fails to develop their answer to show causes and consequence**, ie **why will it motivate and what is the consequence to Starbucks of motivating staff by rewarding them?**"*

> ### ⭐ The examiner's own two-question test for a 6-mark chain
> **"Why?" and "So what happens to the business?"** — applied twice, this is the difference between Level 1 and Level 3.

### ⭐ Starbucks Q3(e) — the priced-5-mark one-sided Justify
> *"Opening coffee shops in supermarkets will has many advantages… **Overall I believe** putting coffee shops in the supermarkets will encourage more people to come in and buy something so this will mean more profit for the company."*
> **Examiner:** *"This is a **level 2** response, awarded **5 marks**, which showed **good knowledge in the context of Starbucks**. However, **it is a one sided argument and fails to provide a balanced assessment**."*

⚠️ **This is the single most instructive calibration in the series.** It has context, it has chains, it reaches a conclusion — and it is capped at 5/9 by **one missing element: the drawback of the alternative.** *"Fails to provide a balanced assessment."*

Also when you interview they, they might be more transparent and clear. This helps Starbucks better understand if the candidate is right for the job or not."*
> **Examiner:** *"This response **tried to cover many different points but lacks developed chains of reasoning**. It also **lacks any context**, so it was awarded **level 2** marks."*

> *"One advantage is it **reduces waste**. Checking products along the production line means less waste because they don't have to throw away any products like if they did quality checks at the end."*
