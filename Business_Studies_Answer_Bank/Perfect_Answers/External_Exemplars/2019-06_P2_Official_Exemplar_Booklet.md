## Marking context: the official AO weightings

Stated in the introduction of this booklet:

| AO | Descriptor | % in International GCSE |
|---|---|---|
| **AO1** | Recall, select and communicate knowledge and understanding of business terms, concepts and issues | **27–28**\* |
| **AO2** | Apply knowledge and understanding using appropriate business terms, concepts, theories and calculations effectively in specific contexts | **38–39** |
| **AO3** | Select, organise and interpret business information from sources to investigate and analyse issues | **22–23** |
| **AO4** | Evaluate business information to make reasoned judgements and draw conclusions | **11–12** |

> \* *"A maximum of 15% of total qualification marks will assess knowledge recall."*

**Read that last line again.** AO1 is nominally 27–28% of the qualification, but **recall alone is capped at 15%**. The difference — 12–13% — is *"select and communicate knowledge and understanding"*, i.e. knowledge **used** inside an answer, not knowledge dumped onto a page. This is the quantified version of the rule that runs through this entire bank: **unused knowledge earns nothing.**

**AO2 is the largest single objective at 38–39%.** Application — context — is worth more than knowledge, analysis and evaluation *combined*. That is why the context rule recurs in every report in this folder.

# Q1(f) — Explain (3 marks)

> **Explain **one** impact of technology on the manufacture of vehicles.**

### Official mark scheme — AO1, 3 marks

> *"Award 1 mark for identification of an impact of technology on production, plus 2 further marks for explaining the impact, for a maximum of 3 marks.*
> - *Fewer people required to work in the factories doing repetitive jobs (1) people get bored doing these jobs and make mistakes (1). Less employees bring the cost of goods down (1).*
> - *More robots are manufacturing the vehicles than previously (1) this means that the vehicles can be manufactured in any country (1) therefore the business could move to a cheaper country (1).*
>
> **Answers that list three impacts with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark.**
> *Accept any other appropriate response."*

### ⭐ The listing cap — now confirmed at mark-scheme level

> **"Answers that list three impacts with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark."**

This is the **third independent statement** of the same rule, and the first one written into a mark scheme rather than an examiner report:

| Source | Wording |
|---|---|
| **This mark scheme** (Q1(f), Q2(d)) | *"Answers that list three [impacts/reasons] with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark."* |
| **Paper 1 exemplar booklet** (gross profit margin) | *"This particular response gave three ways… therefore limiting the marks awarded to 1. Had the candidate explained any one of these three ways, then they would have gained further marks."* |
| **Nov 2021 P1 ER** (measure of success) | *"Candidates who list more than one way of measuring business success can only gain one mark."* |

**The rule:** on an *Explain* question, a list is worth **one mark**. Three undeveloped points score the same as one undeveloped point. **One point, developed twice, scores three.**

### Exemplar response A — **3 marks**

> *"technology could have a positive impact on the manufacturing of vehicles as it could speed up the process. This is because machinery created by technology could build cars quicker than a human, with no mistakes or errors. This would have a positive impact as to mean the business would create more cars in a shorter amount of time, and it would have more product to sell."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"The response shows an understanding that using technology means cars could be produced faster, and this point is then developed by the comment 'with no mistakes or errors'. The final mark is given for the comment that technology would have a positive impact on the business and would create more cars in a shorter time. **A number of responses tended to give two impacts of technology as opposed to one, and the consequence of this was that they could not gain the full marks available.**"*

**The chain, decomposed:**

| Mark | Element | Text |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | identify | technology → **speeds up the process** |
| 2 | develop | because machinery **builds cars quicker than a human, with no mistakes or errors** |
| 3 | develop again | the business **creates more cars in a shorter time… more product to sell** |

Note this is a **3-mark explain with no context required** — "the business", "a human". No mention of any named business, and none is needed. Confirmed: AO1 = 3, no AO2 marks.

### Exemplar response B — **1 mark**

> *"The technological impact could be that the company has less expenses as it has to spend less on wages for employees meaning they would be able to afford better quality land and/or machines & potentially giving them an increased profit."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This particular response mentioned that technology would reduce the spending on wages for employees, which is a valid comment. **If the final comment on profit had been developed, further marks could have been awarded.**"*

### ⚠️ The trap in response B — worth studying closely

Response B is a **single sentence**, but it is not short of content. It contains:

1. less expenses
2. less spent on wages
3. afford better quality land and/or machines
4. increased profit

And it scored **1**.

**Why?** Because marks 2 and 3 require **explanation**, not the naming of further consequences. Response B lumps four ideas into one sentence joined by "meaning" and "potentially", so none of them is explained.

**The lesson:** the difference between "developing" and "adding" is the word **because**. Response A says *"This is because…"* and earns the development marks. Response B says *"meaning…"* and *"potentially…"* and earns one.

> **The one-sentence Explain fix:** take your point and append **"…because…"**. If you cannot finish that sentence, you have identified a point but not explained it — which is one mark.

# Q1(g) — Analyse (6 marks)

> **Analyse the importance of good public relations for *Tata Motor Company*.**

### Official mark scheme — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3

| | Indicative content |
|---|---|
| **AO2** | • Tata Motor Company highlights that it helps the local community with building schools/working on the environment.<br>• Tata Motor Company also invests heavily in the health and welfare of people and the community. |
| **AO3** | • The public are more likely to think highly of Tata Motor Company if it is using the profits from its business to help local communities.<br>• If there is any bad publicity about the vehicles sold then it could have a negative effect on the business. |

**Note the structure of the official mark scheme.** AO2 content is *made of the case study details* — building schools, environment, health and welfare. AO3 content is *what follows for the business*.

**Practical rule: on a 6-mark Analyse, your context is not a garnish — it is literally half the mark scheme.** Copy the case-study facts into your answer (AO2), then say what they do to the business (AO3).

### Exemplar response A — **Level 3 – 5 marks** (out of 6)

> *"Customer loyalty will be improved because having good public relation like building schools Tata Motor Company will attract the society because they will think the business care about the society thus improved Customer loyalty will lead repeat buying. Tata Motor Company will have strong brand name because they will be well known for their welfare for local people therefore they will have Market for their vehicle hence they will get high Market share."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response is relevant to good public relations, and does refer back to the scenario in the paper with regard to building schools. The comment is further developed to say that the business cares about society and this does help to improve customer loyalty. **The second paragraph refers to the welfare of local people but the comment could have been developed further to move the response into the top of Level 3.**"*

**Note the linking words the candidate actually used, all underlined by the mark scheme's own logic:** *"because"… "thus"… "therefore"… "hence"*. That is four chains in two sentences. This is precisely the *"linking words/terms such as 'thus', 'therefore', 'because' or 'this leads to'"* technique Pearson recommends on its support page.

### ⚠️ The difference between 5/6 and 6/6 here

5/6 already has: context (building schools) ✓, chain (cares about society → customer loyalty → repeat buying) ✓, second factor (welfare of local people) ✓, brand name → market share ✓.

**It lost the final mark because the second paragraph was not *developed*.** The examiner does not say "add another point" — they say develop the one you have.

This is the **same diagnosis as rule 6d** in `06_External_Source_Library.md` (the 5/6 → 6/6 gap = link/develop, never add more), now independently confirmed on a different paper, a different question and a different tariff.

### Exemplar response B — **Level 2 – 3 marks** (out of 6)

> *"Good public relations is extremely important for Tata Motor Company as it could increase their amount of customers. This is because customers will feel more comfortable and attracted towards Tata Motor Company, which will increase their chance of shopping there. This will create more revenue for Tata as more customers will create more sales."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"A number of the responses seen confused public relations with customer loyalty, also commenting on how Tata could maintain customer loyalty.*
> *If candidates had referred back to the scenario at the front of the paper, it may have helped them to answer this question. **The details about working in the community, and building schools and houses, are all to do with the public**, so this type of work should result in good public relations for the company.*
> *The response mentions the increase in customers, which is a valid point, and this is then developed by reference to the fact that customers are more likely to shop there.*
> *The comment in the second paragraph with regard to exposure of the business is also a valid application.*
> **This was placed at the bottom of Level 2 as the response did not develop or analyse the comments made sufficiently to move into the next level.**"

### ⚠️ The critical failure in response B — and it is not the one you would expect

Response B is **well written**. It has a clear chain: *increase customers → feel comfortable/attracted → increase chance of shopping → more revenue → more sales*. Three links, correctly joined.

**It scored 3/6 — the bottom of Level 2.**

**Why?** It never uses a single **case-study fact**. It names "Tata Motor Company" twice — which, as the support page states outright, *is not context*. There is no "building schools", no "health and welfare", no "local community".

**The examiner gives the fix explicitly:** *"If candidates had referred back to the scenario at the front of the paper, it may have helped them… The details about working in the community, and building schools and houses, are all to do with the public."*

**This is the single most transferable lesson in this booklet.** A fluent, logically chained answer that names the business but never uses the case study is capped at the bottom of Level 2. The chains are worthless without the AO2 half of the mark scheme.

> **Self-check for any 6-mark Analyse:** count the case-study facts you have used. If the answer is zero, you are at the bottom of Level 2 no matter how well you write.

# Q2(d) — Explain (3 marks)

> **Explain **one** reason why a business would use job production.**

### Official mark scheme — AO1, 3 marks

> *"Award 1 mark for a reason to use job production, plus 2 further marks for explaining this reason, for a maximum of 3 marks.*
> - *Job production is used to produce a unique piece of work. (1) It is made to meet the exact needs of the customer (1) that requires time and skills of the employees (1).*
>
> **Answers that list three reasons with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark.**
> **NB No marks are awarded for a definition.**
> *Accept any other appropriate response."*

### ⭐ "NB No marks are awarded for a definition."

A blunt, official rule. On an **Explain** question, defining the term earns **nothing**. This is a very common time-waster: candidates open with *"Job production is when a business makes one item at a time to a customer's specification…"* and get no credit for it.

**Do not define. Start with the reason.**

### Exemplar response A — **2 marks**

> *"Job production is glacible. It is highly responsive to consumers needs and wants. Customers will be able to request specific designs and styles of a product according to their tastes. For obtaining a product unique to the consumer, the consumer will be calling to pay high prices."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"The comment about being flexible is not developed or related to anything in particular, so therefore is not worthy of a mark. The second sentence about being highly responsive to consumer needs and wants is relevant and applicable, and worthy of a mark. The second mark is gained for the development of the comment that the consumer will have to pay a high price for this specific design. **To have gained the final mark available the candidate could have mentioned the time taken to make the items and/or the skill and labour required of one person.**"*

**Note the examiner's attitude to the opening line** — *"Job production is glacible"* (unclear word). It earns **nothing** and is not even discussed. It is the definition opening, and the mark scheme says definitions earn no marks.

**And note the examiner tells you exactly which point was missing** — and it is a point **in the mark scheme**: *"requires time and skills of the employees"*. The candidate got 2/3 by giving a reason plus one development; the third mark was sitting in the mark scheme unused.

### Exemplar response B — **2 marks**

> *"A business would use job production in order to produce high quality products. By producing such products they can meet customer satisfaction which may be an objective for that business in the long run."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"The comment with regard to producing high-quality products is relevant and this is then developed by the comment regarding customer satisfaction, gaining 2 marks. **If the response had then given a further development or comment on cost, it could have gained the final mark.**"*

### ⚠️ Two different responses, both 2/3, for different reasons

| | Response A | Response B |
|---|---|---|
| Points made | flexibility (unexplained), responsiveness, high prices | high quality, customer satisfaction |
| Why not 3 | **no second development** — the third link is missing | **no second development** — the third link is missing |

**Both are 2/3 for the identical structural reason: only two links of the three.**

This is the mechanical meaning of *"identify + 2 further marks for explaining"*. It is **not** "give a point and something extra". It is a **three-link chain**: reason → development → development.

**The build:** *"A business would use job production to produce a unique item (1) designed to the customer's exact needs (1) which takes a great deal of time and skilled labour (1)."* — three links, 3 marks, one sentence.

# Q2(f) — Justify (9 marks)

> *Tata Motor Company uses difference pricing strategies. It is considering **two** options for the launch of a new vehicle.*
> **Option 1:** cost plus · **Option 2:** promotional.
> **Justify which **one** of these two options *Tata Motor Company* should use.**

### Official mark scheme — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3

| | Indicative content |
|---|---|
| **AO2** | • Option 1 – With cost plus pricing Tata Motor Company know that the cost of making the vehicle has been covered.<br>• Option 2 – If Tata Motor Company uses promotional pricing it is a good strategy for selling new vehicles. |
| **AO3** | • Option 1 – Tata Motor Company knows that if the cars are selling well it is making money, which means more profit for the business.<br>• Option 2 – The lower price from the promotional price will result in higher sales. |
| **AO4** | • Option 1 – Although this type of pricing method guarantees a profit on the cost of manufacturer it does not guarantee that sales are going to be made, especially if a competitor is cheaper, therefore selling more vehicles.<br>• Option 2 – Even though the sales may be higher, the profit per vehicle will be lower. |

### ⭐⭐ The mark scheme shows you the AO4 sentence

**Look at the AO4 row.** Every item begins with a **concession**: *"Although…"*, *"Even though…"*. Pearson's own indicative content writes evaluation as **"yes, but…"**.

**This is what AO4 physically looks like on paper.** It is not a summary. It is not a restatement. It is a **concession followed by the downside** — and it is written that way in the mark scheme itself.

### Exemplar response A — **Level 3 – 7 marks** (out of 9)

> *"However, with when using cost plus pricing there are some problems. One is the this pricing strategy ignores market conditions, so if all other cars in the industry are more generously priced then consumers will switch to competitors. Also the ignores the elasticity of demand for cars, it cars are elastic and they charge a high price, as there many substitute substitutes consumers will switch. This will reduce revenue."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"**Comments relating to a description of what each of the two options are should be avoided, as there are no knowledge marks available for this question.** The response clearly shows an understanding of the two methods of pricing and the impact upon the business and its customers. Justification as to why each of the pricing strategies should or should not be used is also given, showing analysis. **In the final paragraph there are some evaluative comments but these are not fully developed, and so the response cannot reach the top of the level.**"*

**Note what the examiner praises and what they penalise:**

✅ *"Justification as to why each of the pricing strategies should or should not be used is also given, showing analysis"* — this is AO3 working.
❌ *"some evaluative comments but these are not fully developed"* — this is why it is 7/9 rather than 9/9.

**7/9 is the bottom of Level 3.** The candidate reached Level 3 on application + analysis, and was held at the bottom of it by undeveloped evaluation.

This matches the established **7/9 → 9/9 gap** (now five confirmations): the missing marks are **evaluative development**, never content.

### Exemplar response B — **Level 2 – 4 marks** (out of 9)

> *"Hunnerer, although cost plus pricing may seem like the best pricing strategy it ignores market conditions. This means that it does not take into account the prices of competition. Therefore if other car company prices are low Tata Motor company may lose cut on customers."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"The response has made sound application of the knowledge of the pricing strategies that could be used. The comment that cost is covered shows application, and a valid point made.*
> *In the third paragraph, the response comments on option 2 and the fact that if the company does not use this method they may lose customers. This demonstrates application.*
> *In the final paragraph, the comment that cost plus pricing seems to be the best pricing strategy because it ignores market conditions is evidence of analysis; this is developed further by the reference to not taking the competition's prices into account. This moves the response to the bottom of Level 2. **To gain a higher level, the response would have to include some consequences of using each method in relation to other businesses. The candidate could also comment on the impact on the business itself, if it continues to use one method as opposed to another.**"*

### ⚠️ Note how little separates 4/9 from 7/9

Response B has a genuine chain: *ignores market conditions → does not account for competitors' prices → if competitor prices are low, may lose customers*. That is real analysis. It still scored **4/9**.

**Why?** The examiner says it is "at the bottom of Level 2". The missing ingredient is **consequence for the business** — *"consequences of using each method in relation to other businesses"*, *"the impact on the business itself"*.

**The lesson:** analysis must terminate in a **business outcome** (profit, sales, revenue, share, cash, survival, reputation), not in a description of the strategy's properties. Response B stops at *"may lose customers"* — close, but it never says what that does to Tata.

# Q3(d) — Analyse (6 marks)

> **Analyse how *Tata Motor Company* could use the financial information in **Figure 2**.**

### Official mark scheme — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3

| | Indicative content |
|---|---|
| **AO2** | • Tata Motor Company's revenue has increased year on year.<br>• Tata Motor Company can use these figures to compare them with other vehicle manufacturers. |
| **AO3** | • Tata Motor Company could invest in designing and manufacturing new cars.<br>• Tata Motor Company could adjust its prices to increase its yearly revenue. |

### Exemplar response A — **Level 2 – 3 marks** (out of 6)

> *"Tata Motor company can use the financial information in Figure 2 to look at the successes as well as areas they need to work on within the business. For example, the business will be able to see that they grew the most between 2013 and 2015. Furthermore, the company can use this financial information to compare to its competitors. This will allow the company to decide on whether they are happy with the products they have or if they need to improve their financial position by launching a new vehicle."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"The comments about looking at the successes and the areas that Tata Motor Company need to [address] demonstrate application. The comments about comparison to competitors and improving the company's financial situation both provide analysis. **This response actually refers to information in Figure 2, which a number of responses did not do.** If the response had used more information from Figure 2, it could have moved into the top level."*

### ⭐ The rule for any question that points at a Figure or table

The examiner's remark — *"This response actually refers to information in Figure 2, **which a number of responses did not do**"* — is a mark-losing failure mode specific to data questions.

**If the question says "in Figure 2", you must quote or use Figure 2.** Not "the financial information" — the actual figures, years or trends.

**And note what the top level required:** *"If the response had used **more** information from Figure 2, it could have moved into the top level."* Response A used exactly one fact — *"grew the most between 2013 and 2015"* — and got 3/6. The route to 5–6/6 is **more facts from the figure**, each turned into a consequence.

### Exemplar response B — **Level 1 – 2 marks** (out of 6)

> *"Tata Motor company could use the financial information to compare with their competition so to see if they are more successful than those which means can use this to plan when to expand and wipe out their competition. This information can also be used to see if a marketing scheme was successful because they could check if their revenue increased which means they can see whether they should keep selling them. This allows them to make decision about continuing to sell products. Which is good as it could reduce costs or wasting money."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"Unfortunately, a number of candidates perhaps did not fully read the question and make use of the financial information given in Figure 2. This would have helped them to develop their responses and perhaps gain a higher level.*
> *The comment about comparing the information to that of the competition is valid, and possibly the most popular response given by candidates. This is then developed by commenting about planning and when to expand.*
> *Candidates could have used the information given with regard to the revenue made from 2011 to 2017 and also used the response to 3(c) to help develop the response."*

### ⚠️ The warning here is about the *shape* of the answer, not its length

Response B is nearly as long as response A. It has **three** chains:

1. compare with competition → see if more successful → plan when to expand → wipe out competition
2. see if a marketing scheme was successful → check if revenue increased → see whether to keep selling
3. → decide about continuing to sell → reduce costs or wasting money

**It scored 2/6 — Level 1 — against response A's 3/6 with one chain.**

**The entire reason: response A names a Figure 2 fact and response B names none.** Every chain in B is generic — it would be true of any business with any set of accounts. Neither "compare with competition" nor "check if revenue increased" is tied to Tata's actual numbers.

> **The data-question rule:** the figure is the application. General reasoning *about* the figure is not. Name the number.

# Q3(e) — Justify (9 marks)

> *Tata Motor Company gathers data to help it promote its vehicles. It is considering **two** options.*
> **Option 1:** primary research · **Option 2:** secondary research.
> **Justify which **one** of these two options *Tata Motor Company* should use.**

### Official mark scheme — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3

| | Indicative content |
|---|---|
| **AO2** | • Option 1 – you are able to obtain the information directly from the people about the cars they want and the price they are prepared to pay.<br>• Option 2 – secondary research is much easier to collate from other car manufacturing websites and reports. |
| **AO3** | • Option 1 – this helps Tata Motor Company gather information that is relevant and can help in designing and pricing the next cars to be manufactured.<br>• Option 2 – and will not take Tata Motor Company as long to gather the information it requires. |
| **AO4** | • Option 1 – although gaining this information is important for Tata Motor Company it is very costly in time and man power.<br>• Option 2 – the data it collects may not provide the information it requires. |

**Again the AO4 row is written as a concession:** *"although gaining this information is important… it is very costly".*

### Exemplar response A — **Level 2 – 6 marks** (out of 9)

> *"Tata should use primary research. This is because it provides up-to-date information about customers wants and needs. It will enable tata to produce market oriented products and hence obtain customers from day one. This will increase its revenue and increase profits, consequently obtaining a higher market share. They will also fulfill their aim of producing In addition, primary research provides confidential information as no other firm knows about it. This enables Tata to sell unique products into the firm. It can use pricing strategies like price skimming and obtain a lot of profits from its awesome cars before competitors like Mitsubishi begin producing. This monopoly power will give them enough finance as retained profits to carry out further market research However, primary research sometimes gives misleading results. Tata can incur large losses after it designs a car and it gains no market especially because huge capital costs are involved. This means it will have reduced cash flow and may even close down due to business failure."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"A number of the responses seen did give a very detailed description of primary and secondary research. **As mentioned, there are no knowledge marks available for this type of question. Commenting on each of the options by giving a description of what they are should be avoided.** The comment on market-oriented cars is application, and the comment on fulfilling the aim of producing according to needs gives further analysis. Another application point made was that the information gained is confidential; the point is further developed by stating that this enables Tata to sell unique products.*
> *The last paragraph comments on how primary research could give misleading information, which is a relevant point.*
> *To move this response to the next level, the candidate could have further developed the implications of primary research and the effects on Tata, **or justified why secondary research was not chosen**."*

### ⚠️⚠️ The most instructive script in this booklet

**Look at this response.** It is long. It contains:

- context — *"Tata"*, *"market oriented products"*, *"competitors like Mitsubishi"*, *"price skimming"*
- chains — revenue → profits → market share; confidential → unique products → monopoly power → retained profits
- a **concession** — *"However, primary research sometimes gives misleading results"*
- a **business consequence** — *"reduced cash flow and may even close down due to business failure"*

**It scored 6/9. Level 2.** Not Level 3.

**This is the single most valuable warning in the file.** Everything in this bank says *"chain, link, evaluate, context throughout"* — and this candidate did all of it, and still landed in Level 2.

**The examiner's instruction for reaching Level 3:** *"the candidate could have further developed the implications of primary research **and the effects on Tata**, or justified why secondary research was not chosen."*

Two diagnostic failures:

1. **The chains explode into generalities.** *"monopoly power → retained profits → further market research"* — this is not about **Tata**; it is a generic economics story. The examiner calls the genuinely Tata-specific moments "application" and lets the rest go.
2. **The evaluation arrives as one undeveloped clause.** *"primary research sometimes gives misleading results"* is a **concession with no destination** — it is not followed by what Tata should therefore do, or why the choice still stands. Compare the mark scheme's AO4: *"although… it is very costly in time and man power"*. There is a specific cost named.

> **The Level-2 ceiling on a 9-marker:** chains that could belong to any business, plus evaluation that concedes but does not conclude.

### Exemplar response B — **Level 2 – 5 marks** (out of 9) — *"the middle of Level 2"*

> *"Tata Motor Company should use primary research. I believe the Tata Motor Company should use primary market research because it allows them to specifically ask certain questions they may want to know about customers preferences in a vehicle and allow them to have a better idea on how to promote its vehicles. Primary research also allows for Tata Motor Company to ask locals which are their customers as to what they want where as secondary research is perhaps done in a different country than where the y self case case usually which could result in issues because the homes customers may have different wants and needs in different places. Primary market research is up to date where as secondary research could be out dated or irrelevant. Though primary research costs much more to have to have a medium For research such as focus groups and surveys than secondary research I feel as though the benefits of primary research heavily out weigh the costs"*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response did not always clarify the points made in sufficient detail to gain the higher level. **Candidates should be aware that there are no knowledge marks allocated for this type of question and therefore comments about how the research is collected, or that secondary research could be outdated, cannot be awarded marks.***
> *In the first paragraph the response shows a clear understanding that by using primary research Tata can get to know about their customers' preferences in vehicles. The next comment, about asking local people for their comments, is also valid and the comment that secondary research may be carried out in a different country is development. A further development point about different wants and needs in different places is also given.*
> *If the response had developed the points further or made reference to the effect of the research and how the businesses could have used the information, it could have moved to the next level."*

### ⭐ "That secondary research could be outdated cannot be awarded marks"

This is a genuinely surprising ruling and it is worth internalising.

**"Secondary research can be out of date" is textbook knowledge, not application.** Because there are no AO1 marks on a Justify question, a factually correct, relevant, well-known point about the *nature of secondary research* earns **nothing**.

The same applies to *"comments about how the research is collected"* — describing the method is knowledge, not application.

**Only take points that attach to Tata's situation.** *"Secondary research is outdated"* ✗ · *"the data would come from other car manufacturers' reports, so it may not reflect what Tata's Indian customers want"* ✓.

Compare response A, where the confidential-information point **was** credited — because the examiner could see it led to *"Tata can sell unique products"*.

### ⭐⭐ The most important fact in this file

**Response A is roughly 400 words long** — by far the longest script in this booklet. It lists: cost of land, pollution, private ownership, raw materials, labour cost, labour skills, proximity to market, transport, rate apportionment, environment, lighting/heating, salaries, rent, taxes, regulations, trade barriers, language barriers, financial incentives.

**It scored 8/12. Response B — about 110 words — scored 7/12.**

Let me state that plainly, because it is the headline finding of this entire booklet:

> ### A 400-word answer scored 8/12. A 110-word answer scored 7/12. One mark apart.

**Look at what the examiner credits in the 400-word answer:** *"knowledge points"* — land cheap and safe, no pollution, availability of labour. *"application"* — labour cheap, skills. And then: *"**However, the response does not evaluate the comments sufficiently to move it into Level 3**."*

**Level 3 begins at 9/12.** The candidate wrote four times as much as the other candidate and could not cross the line — because **300 of those 400 words replaced evaluation they never attempted**.

The examiner is explicit about the mechanism: *"some of them have not been **developed** sufficiently"* and *"often these are not developed or **evaluated** sufficiently"*. The answer does not lack content. It lacks **the half of the marks that come from AO3 and AO4.**

**This is the quantified version of Pearson's own tip:** *"Too often, candidates write far too much for AO3 and leave insufficient space for a sound judgement."* Here a candidate wrote far too much for AO1/AO2 and left nothing for AO3/AO4 — with the same result.

### Exemplar response B — **Level 2 – 7 marks** (out of 12) — full verbatim text

> *"One factor is how good the public transport is. The workers should be able to get there as fast as possible. As a result, there might be less late employees.*
>
> *Another factor is the social type of the region. More old people might be against building the factory. This is bad because the pollution of air would have a negative impact on them. This leads to relocation of the factory.*

>

>

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response again gives lots of different reasons for where the factory should be located, but there is very limited analysis and evaluation, hence Level 2.*
> *The comment about workers getting there fast is relevant and worthy of a mark, and further application is that they would not be late for work.*
> *The comment about air pollution having a negative impact on the people is analysis and worthy of a mark.*
> *Another factor worthy of marks is the comment about the rent not being too expensive for the manufacture of cars.*
> *The final paragraph gives basic evaluative comments about how finding the right location could help Tata Motor Company to reduce its costs.*
> *If the response had further developed each of the points made and related them to how the business would be affected, it could have moved into a higher level."*

### ⚠️ What is wrong with this conclusion — study it word by word

The candidate **did** write a conclusion. It contains *"But the success of them depends on…"* — which is exactly the "it depends" structure that this bank recommends. **It still scored 7/12.**

**Why it did not work:**

| The candidate's conclusion | The examiner's reaction |
|---|---|
| *"could help Tata Motor Company to reduce it costs, save the time on to find new partners"* | *"basic evaluative comments"* — noted, credited, but basic |
| *"But the success of them depends on how many available land for the big factories there are and how the locals would react on it"* | **not mentioned at all** |

**The conclusion names two new factors** — land availability and local reaction — that were never discussed in the body. So the "it depends" hands the judgement to factors the marker has not seen analysed. **It reads as a hedge, not a judgement.**

> **How to write a conclusion that earns AO4.** It must do three things, and this one does none of them:
> 1. **Decide.** Use the words *should*, *most important*, *the key factor*. This candidate never decides.
> 2. **Rank.** Say which factor outweighs which, and why. This candidate adds two more instead.
> 3. **Look backwards.** Base the judgement on the factors already analysed. This candidate bases it on two new ones.

**Compare the mark scheme's AO4 again:** *"Infrastructure is one of many factors affecting the choice of location and other factors may be more important."* Even the model treatment of "it depends" is **a ranking of factors already discussed** — never the introduction of new ones.

# Q1(g) Paper 2R — Analyse (6 marks)

> **Analyse why *Yellow* might encourage its clothing manufacturers to use Kaizen.**

### Official mark scheme — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3

| | Indicative content |
|---|---|
| **AO2** | • The quality of the T-shirts will be better.<br>• Reduction in costs of making the T-shirts. |
| **AO3** | • Yellow's reputation will be enhanced leading to higher sales.<br>• There will be fewer returns of T-shirts, which could lead to increased profitability for the manufacturer. |

### ⭐ Read the question's subject carefully — this is where most marks were lost

**Examiner's opening remark (verbatim):**

> *"Many of the responses that were seen for this question answered from the perspective of the business that manufactures the clothes and then gave the many benefits of Kaizen. **The question asked why Yellow might encourage its clothing manufacturers to use Kaizen, so therefore the response should be in the context of Yellow and not the manufacturer.**"*

**A whole paper's worth of candidates answered about the wrong business.** The question asks why *Yellow* — the retailer — wants its *suppliers* to improve. Not why a factory benefits from Kaizen.

**The rule:** the subject of the question is the business you must apply to. *"Why might Yellow encourage…"* → every consequence must land on **Yellow**: Yellow's reputation, Yellow's sales, Yellow's returns, Yellow's costs.

### Exemplar response A — **Level 3 – 5 marks** (out of 6)

> *"Yellow might encourage its clothing manufacturers to use Kaizen because Kaizen is ultimately waste with continuous improvements. The Company would like continuous improvements because this might help by decreasing costs, by decreasing labour or any other expenses. In addition this will improve the quality of the product even more which will attract customers and gain their loyalty to the business. This would left [help] Yellow by having a good brand image on the market. It will gain popularity and fame as well as that is one of the business objectives to be always improve. This might gain customers satisfaction and make the business successful on the market."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This particular response is related to the benefits of Yellow. The comments about costs decreasing as there would be less waste, and that it would improve the quality of the product, are both relevant application to the question. This is then further developed by noting that the brand image would be improved.*
> *To have moved into the top of Level 3, the response could have included something with regard to how a benefit of using this method might be improved customer satisfaction, which could increase sales."*

**Note the examiner's suggested final link — *"improved customer satisfaction, which could increase sales"* — is a chain in the mark scheme's AO3 row:** *"Yellow's reputation will be enhanced leading to higher sales."*

**Pattern:** 5/6 responses in this bank are consistently missing **one final link**. The candidate has the context and the chain; they stop one step short of the business outcome. **Finish the sentence with sales, profit or reputation.**

### Exemplar response B — **Level 2 – 4 marks** (out of 6) — full verbatim text

> *"Kaizen is the continuous improvement of goods. Yellow might want its clothing manufacturers to use it since it decreases the waste made by the company which would decrease the costs the company spends. Also, customers will be happy since Yellow is continuously improving their products making them better which will lead to customer satisfaction. If customer's are satisfied with the product more people would also want to start purchasing from them which would also lead to more customers being loyal to Yellow & more people would want to buy their new products."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"A number of candidates when answering this question gave detailed descriptions of Kaizen and how it works, and how the business uses it. **Unfortunately there were no knowledge marks available for this question**, as it asked how Yellow would encourage its clothing manufacturers to use Kaizen.*
> *The comments made about decreasing waste and a decrease in costs for the company are application, and worthy of marks. The response then develops the comments by mentioning that if customers are satisfied they are more likely to want to purchase, and this is then further developed by the comment about customers being loyal to Yellow. **To move this response into Level 3, the candidate could have developed the comments made about customers returning to Yellow to purchase more products and telling their friends and family about the products and how good they are.** Alternatively, a comment could have been made about customer satisfaction increasing the status of Yellow as a business that provides quality products."*

**Note the opening sentence again:** *"Kaizen is the continuous improvement of goods."* — a **correct definition**, and the examiner records it as a problem, not a help: *"Unfortunately there were no knowledge marks available for this question."*

**A correct definition can actively cost you a mark here**, because it consumes time you needed for the chain. Response B's chain then runs *waste ↓ → costs ↓* and *quality ↑ → satisfaction → loyalty → buying* — genuinely good — and still stops at 4/6.

**The examiner names the missing links:** *"customers returning… to purchase more products and telling their friends and family"* — i.e. **repeat purchase and word of mouth**. Both are concrete, both follow directly from loyalty, and neither was written.
