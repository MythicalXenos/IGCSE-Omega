# ⚠️ THE "WASTED TIME" FINDING — now confirmed three times

This report contains **two more** instances of an examiner warning that a *full-mark* answer wrote too much. Combined with June 2022 P1, this is a genuine, repeated pattern in the same series:

| Paper | Question | Examiner comment (verbatim) | Mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 2022 P1 | Q2(c) flat structure | *"has explained this benefit with **more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time** when there are only 3 marks available."* | **3/3** |
| **Jun 2022 P1R** | **Q2(c) repeat purchase** | *"has explained this method with **more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time** when there are only 3 marks available."* | **3/3** |
| **Jun 2022 P1R** | **Q2(e) motivation** | *"has **fallen into the trap** of explaining this benefit with **more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time**."* | **3/3** |

## 🎯 What this means for you

**On a 3-mark question, the marks stop after identify + two developments.** Every additional link earns **nothing** — and the examiner names it as a trap.

This does **not** contradict the "keep developing" rule. It is tariff-specific:

| Tariff | Keep going? |
|---|---|
| **3 marks** | ❌ **No.** identify + two links, then **stop and move on** |
| 6 / 9 / 12 marks | ✅ **Yes.** depth of chain is exactly what earns the levels |

**Exam-time value:** in a paper where the 12-marker is routinely left unfinished, over-writing a 3-marker is a direct transfer of marks to no purpose. Write two links, then leave it. In your practice, time yourself: a 3-mark answer should take about **3 minutes**.

# VERIFIED EXEMPLARS — 9 marks (Justify)

## ✅ 9/9 · Q2(f) Justify: increase advertising or open for breakfast to improve revenue

> *"One disadvantage of option 2 however is that employees might be less motivated. This is because they will need to wake up early and work more hours to provide and cook food. This loss of motivation will lead to kitchen staff being lazy and therefore quality of food will be low. As a result, Can Culleretes will lose customers to competitors because food is not [good], leading to a decrease in market growth. **However, this depends on the quality of food at the restaurants nearby**, as if the other restaurants offer food with low quality, then customers will choose to…*
>
> *Overall, Can Cul[le]retes should choose **option 2** because if they were to advertise, [it] is more expensive in the short term. Opening for breakfast in the short term is less expensive and in the long term can boost their reputation even further. **However, this depends on the amount of tourists**, as due to issues such as a global pandemic, there will be less tourists meaning less customers who want to eat."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows what is expected from candidates in order to gain the full marks for this type of 'justify' question. This candidate has shown detailed understanding of the choice they have made, they have included context throughout their response with logical chains of reasoning and has made a good judgement about their choice with a thorough evaluation."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim) — fourth independent statement of the 9-mark rule:**
> *"**Candidates do not need to consider both options.** Candidates need to make a clear choice out of the two options and be able to balance out their response as to why the business should or should not choose that option. Candidates are then expected to provide a clear overall judgement to evaluate their decision."*

### The "it depends" move — used *twice* in one answer

This is the clearest demonstration in the bank of how conditionality earns AO4:

```
"However, this depends on the QUALITY OF FOOD AT THE RESTAURANTS NEARBY…"
"However, this depends on the AMOUNT OF TOURISTS… pandemic → fewer tourists"
```

**Both are tied to Can Culleretes' actual circumstances** — a restaurant whose trade depends on local rival quality and on tourist numbers. Not abstract hedging: a stated condition that would change the recommendation.

> **Cohort note:** *"Around half of candidates accessed level 2 by expanding on their answers but **often missing balance and a conclusion to their thoughts**. More able candidates accessed level 3 by considering their chosen option carefully in the context of Can Culleretes and how this would impact their **financial position**, linking their points together and concluding their answer with balance and justification."*

## ✅ 9/9 · Q3(e) Justify: permanent or temporary contract for a new chef

> *"Can Cul[le]retes should offer the chef a **permanent contract** because it offers stable pay, timings and work conditions for the chef. This means they [have] greater job security, leading to them being more motivated, so they are committed to the restaurant's success. For example, they may put more effort into designing a new dessert menu as well as when they prepare the desserts, leading to higher quality cakes and souffl[és]. Thus [this can] give the restaurant[] a reputation for high quality desserts, this differentiates them from nearby rivals and consequently more people are likely to buy them, increasing revenue.*
>
> *However, offering a permanent contract is more costly than a temporary contract and offers less flexibility, because the chef cannot be laid off or paid less [if] restaurant profitability… [is] not good, so costs can't be lowered. This is particularly risky because there is no guarantee that the new desserts will sell[,] another[ly] especially if other restaurants nearby specialise[] in them, because consumers may trust them more. Consequently, the cost of employment [is] higher than the additional revenue from sales, so profit margin will [fall].*
>
> *Overall, offering a permanent contract is best as it will mean the chef may be more loyal to the restaurant and eas[y] to retain. This leads to lower risk of turnover and therefore recruitment costs are saved. **This depends on the demand for the new desserts** so Can Culleretes should focus on market research to ensure there is a need for them before hiring a new chef."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows what we would expect from candidates when responding to a 'justify' question. This candidate is considering both the benefits and drawbacks of offering a new chef a permanent contract over a temporary contract in order for Can Culleretes to introduce a new range of desserts. This candidate has also offered a conclusion which offers an evaluation **gaining the full available marks**."*

### The chain structure — count the links

```
permanent contract
  → stable pay/hours → job security
    → more motivated → committed to restaurant's success
      → more effort on new dessert menu
        → higher quality cakes and soufflés
          → reputation for high quality desserts
            → differentiates from nearby rivals
              → more people buy → REVENUE RISES
```

**Eight links, one idea, terminating in revenue.** Then a counter-chain running to *"profit margin will fall"*. This is what "detailed interconnected points with logical chains of reasoning" means at 9 marks.

**And note: the answer opens with the decision** — *"should offer the chef a permanent contract because…"*. No preamble, no definitions.

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"Candidates took a number of routes to answer this question and could bring in their own knowledge of the subject… **This response shows a good example of positive and negative impacts on productivity.**"*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim):**
> *"Centres should encourage candidates to include context in 'analyse' questions to ensure they can reach the top levels of this levels based question."*

### 🎯 The technique: run the argument BOTH ways on the same factor

The candidate does not use two different factors. It uses **one factor — technology — and analyses it as both a benefit and a risk**:

| Direction | Chain |
|---|---|
| **Positive** | online ordering → less waiter time → fewer order errors → sent direct to kitchen → shorter wait → more meals out → higher productivity → customer satisfaction → repeat visits |
| **Negative** | unfamiliar customers frustrated → forced to use something unwanted → time lost figuring out the app → customers leave → fewer orders → chefs struggle with the new system → demotivated → productivity falls |

**This is the single most transferable 6-mark technique in the bank.** It means you never need to find two separate points — take one point and follow it in both directions. It also directly supplies the balance the level descriptors reward, without needing AO4.

Compare the same move in **Jun 2022 P1 Q3(d)** (motivation analysed via the implications of *not* motivating) and in **Jun 2023 P1R Q3(d)**.

## ❌ 0 marks · Q2(d) Explain one disadvantage of personal savings as a source of finance

> *"Personal savings is when the owner is required to contribute some money to the business when they first start their business. Personal savings can be lost sometime due to some bank errors. Personal savings can be stolen by fraudulent activities."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This shows a typical response of where a candidate has either **not read the question properly or understood what is required from them**, this candidate has **not been able to relate a disadvantage of personal savings as a source of finance directly to a business**."*

### 🎯 Three defects in three sentences

| Sentence | Problem |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Defines** the term. On an *explain* question, defining earns nothing. |
| 2 | "bank errors" — not a disadvantage of *using personal savings*; it's a risk any money faces |
| 3 | "stolen by fraud" — same problem: true of any money, and nothing to do with funding a business |

**The answer never addresses the question.** It describes what personal savings *are* and then lists generic ways money can be lost. The examiner's phrase — *"not been able to relate a disadvantage… directly to a business"* — is the diagnosis.

**Contrast:** the 3/3 response on Q2(e) works because every sentence is about the *effect on the business* (effort → productivity → customer satisfaction → revenue). This 0-mark response never reaches an effect.
