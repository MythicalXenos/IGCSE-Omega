# THE THREE MAY 2023 EXPLAIN SCRIPTS — found by sweeping the reports for a score line rather than a pattern

**Why this file exists.** Every earlier sweep of the four May 2023 reports looked for the phrasings the bank already knew — *"This response **is** a Level 3"*, *"gained a Level 3"*, *"was Level 3"*. May 2023 prints its Explain and Analyse marks a fourth way: a plain line reading

> **`This response scored 3 marks.`**

**Preceded by a per-question commentary and followed by the script and the ResultsPlus comment.** `2023-05_Series_Irsi_AudeRose_IKEA_MM.md` recorded the three 6/6s and five 9/9s, and a level-phrasing sweep caught two more 6/6s — **but no sweep ever asked the reports for *"scored 3 marks"*, so three full-mark Explain answers printed in full went unread.** They were found on **14 October 2026**, while fixing the register's 3-mark under-count, and they are the last three the bank was missing.

**The lesson, and it is the same one twice.** The bank's sweeps are **pattern-driven**: they find what they were written to look for. **A report that states a mark in a phrasing the bank has not yet catalogued is invisible to every sweep it owns** — and the score lines are the one thing a report always prints. *Sweep for the score, then read the question.* That is now the bank's rule for closing a session.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 3/3 · P1 Q1(f) — *test marketing* — report p. 11

**Question (verbatim from the report):** *"Explain **one** benefit for a business that uses test marketing."* **(3)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, MS p. 8):** *"AO1 - 3 marks. Award 1 mark for identification of a benefit for a business that uses test marketing, plus 2 further marks for explaining the benefit, for a maximum of 3 marks.*

> • *The risk of failure is reduced (1) as the new product is only tested in one area gaining feedback from customers (1) leading to modifications being made before the final launch (1)*

*NB Answers that list three benefits with no explanation will get 1 mark only. Accept any other appropriate response."*

**The script (verbatim — `^…^` is an insertion above the line, `~~…~~` a deletion):**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Using test marketing helps the business to reduce risks of a product having low sales and bad reviews which can potentially damage the reputation of the business. This is because the business gets to test their product ^to a certain^ ~~before its~~ group by giving them to a small group of people. This helps the business to find if there are any potential faults and repair it before it launches."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response scored 3 marks. A good response which gained full marks. Test marketing is clearly understood and the benefit of **reduced risk** has been explained well."*

**The report's own commentary on the question (verbatim):** *"Less than half of candidates were able to explain one benefit for a business that uses test marketing. Candidates who produced a list of benefits only gained 1 mark. The benefit must be developed to gain the further marks. **It is clear from some of the responses that candidates were not aware of the concept of test marketing.**"*

🔬 **Why this one is worth having: it earns its three marks in a different ORDER from every other 3/3 in the bank.** Most open with the mechanism. This one opens with the **consequence** — *reduce risks of a product having low sales and bad reviews which can potentially damage the reputation* — and only then supplies the mechanism, *"This is because the business gets to test their product to a certain group."* **The shape is mechanism → consequence or consequence → mechanism; what matters is that both are present and connected.** That is rule 126 stated from the other end, and it is the reason the rule is written as *"names the mechanism"* rather than *"starts with the mechanism"*.

**It also names the risk in the examiner's own terms.** The MS says *"the risk of failure is reduced"*; the script says which risks — **low sales, bad reviews, damaged reputation**. The report says candidates *"were not aware of the concept of test marketing"*; this one is. **Naming what the risk actually is IS the explanation** — not extra detail.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 3/3 · P1R Q1(f) — *why a business might fail* — report p. 8

**Question (verbatim from the report):** *"Explain **one** reason why a business might fail."* **(3)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, MS p. 8):** *"AO1 - 3 marks. Award 1 mark for identification of a reason why a business might fail, plus 2 further marks for explaining the reason, for a maximum of 3 marks.*

> • *A lack of finance (1) from poor cashflow can lead to its failure (1) leading to an issue of paying for inventory to sell on to customers (1)*

*NB Answers that list three reasons with no explanation will get 1 mark only. Accept any other appropriate response."*

**The script (verbatim):**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"A business might fail if they are unable to innovate. This means that a business will rely on their product as it is instead of figuring out how to improve it, this causes their competitors to get a competitive advantage so the business will lose ~~qq~~ customers as they will go to their competitors. As a result the business won't be able to earn enough revenue to cover their costs and generate profit."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response scored 3 marks. A response which gained full marks. This candidate has been able to identify a clear reason why a business might fail and then been able to explain this reason."*

**The report's own commentary on the question (verbatim):** *"A well answered question where candidates were able to explain one reason why a business might fail and then candidates have been able to develop their answers with an explanation of that reason to be able to gain full marks. As per the mark scheme, candidates who list more than one reason will only gain 1 mark. The reason must be developed to gain further marks. Common responses related to lack of cash, lack of finance, failure to adapt to changing markets and lack of competitiveness."*

🔬 **This is the clearest case in the bank of a chain that runs FOUR links for three marks, and it is the answer that best shows what "developed" means.** The examiner is satisfied by *identify + explain*, i.e. two steps; the candidate supplies:

> **reason** (unable to innovate) → **mechanism** (rely on the product as it is) → **competitive consequence** (competitors gain an advantage, customers leave) → **financial outcome** (revenue cannot cover costs, no profit)

**Note where the examiner's praise lands: *"identify a clear reason… and then been able to explain this reason."*** The two extra links earned no additional marks — and cost none. **Over-developing a 3-marker is not penalised; under-developing is fatal.** The June 2023 P1R Q2(c) script below shows the same thing with the examiner saying so out loud.

**Also note the untouched self-correction:** the candidate wrote and struck out two letters mid-sentence (*"lose ~~qq~~ customers"*) and the script was still marked full. **Handwriting and crossings-out are not marked; content is.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 3/3 · P1R Q2(c) — *ineffective communication* — report p. 13

**Question (verbatim from the report):** *"Explain **one** impact of ineffective communication on a business."* **(3)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, MS p. 12):** *"AO1 - 3 marks. Award 1 mark for identification of an impact of ineffective communication on a business, plus 2 further marks for explaining the impact, for a maximum of 3 marks.*

> • *More mistakes could be made (1) as employees will not know what they are doing (1) leading to low customer satisfaction (1)*

*NB Answers that list three impacts with no explanation will get 1 mark only. Accept any other appropriate response."*

**The script (verbatim — the candidate hyphenates across the line break exactly as shown):**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"It can cause employees to be confus-ed on the task their told. The employee might not do the job properly which can lead to wastage of resources and time for the business. This can lead to unwanted loss for business which can continue if communication is not improved."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response scored 3 marks. A good response which shows a clear impact and then this candidate has been able to expand upon this impact. **This particular candidate has explained this impact with more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time when there are only 3 marks available.**"*

**The report's own commentary on the question (verbatim):** *"This was a well answered question showing good knowledge on the impact of inefffective communication on a business. Candidates were able to identify an impact and then go on the explain this impact. Common responses included increase in costs and increase in mistakes."*

🔬 **⭐ THE MOST USEFUL COMMENT IN THIS FILE: a full-mark answer that the examiner criticises for doing MORE than full marks requires.** *"More than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time when there are only 3 marks available."* **The mark was not reduced — it is 3/3 — but the examiner is telling you the extra work bought nothing and consumed the clock.**

**That is the whole case for writing 3-markers to shape rather than to length**, and it is the same finding the bank recorded from June 2022 P1R (two scripts, *"more than two links of reasoning"*) — **so this is a fourth independent Pearson statement of it, and the first that prints the criticised script beside the comment.**

**The chain, for the record:** impact (employees confused about the task) → mechanism (job done improperly → wastage of resources and time) → business outcome (unwanted loss) → *and a fourth link the examiners did not need* (the loss continues if communication is not improved).

**Note also:** *"the task their told"* — a grammar error in the third word of the sentence — and *"confus-ed"* broken across a line. **3/3.** This is the same finding as C13 in `2022-06_P2R_Cadbury.md`: **content is marked, not SPaG.**
