# ⭐⭐⭐ THE ANSWER-LINE RULE — FINALLY STATED EXACTLY

**June 2025 P2R, Question 1(e)** — *Calculate, to two decimal places, the price of the handbag in US dollars. You are advised to show your working.*

**The candidate's paper:**

> *"1590 / 0.93 **= 1709.87**"* — correct workings
> Answer line: **$1709.67** — wrong

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response scored **1 mark**. This response is a **very rare example of what a 1 mark answer looks like**. The candidate has given the **wrong answer on the answer line** of 1709.67. **Thus, examiners cannot award 2 marks.** Examiners will then go back and look at the workings. In this case we have £1590/0.93 = $1709.67. The response scored 1 mark in total for the correct calculation, **unfortunately the candidate provided the incorrect 2 decimal places for the second mark**."*

**And the rule, stated as plainly as it will ever be:**

> ## 🔴 *"If the answer is correct on the answer line, the examiner will automatically give 2 marks. The workings are only ever looked at if the answer on the answer line is incorrect."*
>
> ### The decision tree — memorise this
> | What is on the answer line | Result |
> |---|---|
> | **Correct, to the required d.p.** | **2 marks** — workings never checked |
> | **Wrong or wrongly rounded** | examiner **then** checks workings → **1 mark** if the method is right |
> | **Blank** | no second chance |
>
> **So the answer line is not a formality — it is the answer.** Workings can only *rescue* a mark you have already lost by writing the wrong thing on the line. **They can never add a mark to a correct line — that mark is already yours.**

**Companion advice from the same report:**
> *"This is a **model approach** to answering this type of question. **It takes very little additional time in the examination to provide the workings** and it also helps the candidate to focus and avoid errors."*

# ⭐⭐⭐ THE 3-MARK EXPLAIN — OFFICIALLY DEFINED AS "ONE POINT + TWO POINTS OF DEVELOPMENT"

**June 2025 P2, Question 1(f) — the definitive statement:**

> *"This is the first of the 'explain' questions. **All 'explain' questions are worth 3 marks. It is important the candidate gives _one_ point and then provides 2 points of development.**"*

### ✅ 3 marks — *one disadvantage of training employees*

> *"Training employees might be cost expenses. This is because, a business might have to employ specialised trainers, or would have to rent a training premises This would likely raise cost for the business. Hence, profit reduces if revenue remains constant."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This was awarded the full 3 marks. **A disadvantage is identified, ie it may be expensive, which is developed to show _both a cause and a consequence_.** The **cause** relates to specialised trainers and training premises that are required. The **consequence** is that this may reduce profits if revenues remain constant."*

### ⚠️ 1 mark

> *"One disadvantage for the business may be that, when a business provides trainings for the new employees. After some time employees may leave and gain rival business, and this is why business gets frustrated, that is why this is a disadvantage for businesses training its employees."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response gained just one mark. The candidate **identified** that the trained employee may go to a rival business **but this was not developed to show any _consequences_ of this to a business**."*

> ### 🎯 THE STRUCTURE, NAMED BY PEARSON
> **Point (1) → cause (1) → consequence (1)**
>
> *"developed to show both a **cause** and a **consequence**."*
>
> This is the clearest the mechanic has ever been stated. **A 3-mark Explain is not three ideas and it is not one idea repeated — it is one idea with a cause AND a consequence.**
>
> **Missing word check before you move on: does my answer contain a _because_ (cause) and a _so/therefore_ that changes something for the business (consequence)?**

**⭐ And a caution from the other direction** (June 2022 P1R, repeated twice in that paper):
> *"This particular candidate has explained this method with **more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time when there are only 3 marks available.**"*
>
> **Two development links is the cap. A third earns nothing and costs time.**

# ⭐⭐ DEFINITIONS — "ANSWERS SHOULD NOT BE LENGTHY BUT ACCURATE"

**June 2025 P2, Q1(b) — Define *social enterprise*:**

> ✅ **1 mark:** *"This is a business that focuses on environmental and human well-being rather than profit."*
> **Examiner:** *"This is a **perfect answer** and awarded the one mark. **Answers should not be lengthy but accurate.**"*

**And the misconception the senior examiner names directly:**

> *"too many candidates are under the impression that **a social enterprise is owned by the government**. Whilst a social enterprise is an **independent organisation that prioritises social and environmental wellbeing**, and **may provide services to the public sector, the governance and ownership are distinct from government entities**."*

> ### 📘 SOCIAL ENTERPRISE — the examinable form
> **A business that operates like a business but exists primarily to achieve a social or environmental purpose**, reinvesting its profits towards that purpose rather than distributing them to owners.
>
> ❌ **It is NOT owned by the government.** It is **independent**. It *may* deliver public services (and be paid for them), but ownership and governance are separate.
> ✅ **The distinguishing feature: purpose over profit.** *"focuses on environmental and human well-being rather than profit."*

**June 2025 P2, Q1(c) — Define *tertiary sector*:**

> ✅ **1 mark:** *"the provision of services"* — *"This is a **short but correct definition** which was awarded the mark."*
> **Examiner tip:** *"**Definitions do not need to be lengthy they just need to be accurate. Examples are not awarded marks.**"*
> *"A few confused the tertiary sector with the **secondary sector** and some candidates provided **examples** of businesses or jobs within the tertiary sector rather than giving a definition."*

**June 2025 P2R, Q1(b) — Define *autonomy*:**

> ✅ **1 mark:** *"Allowing employees to make their own decisions."* — *"provided a correct definition of autonomy 'to make their own decisions'."*
> **Tip:** *"For 'Define' questions candidates are required to show they have a clear understanding of the term."*
> ℹ️ Report note: *"most candidates relate autonomy to a **motivational technique used for employees**, although some candidates made generic reference to **freedom in the workplace or robots and an automated process** hence not accessing the mark."*

**June 2025 P1R, Q1(c) — Define *autonomy* (same term, different paper):**

> ❌ **0 marks:** *"Autonomy refers to the independence of something"* — *"This is not a correct definition."*
> ✅ **1 mark:** *"when the business gives employees the **authority to make decisions about how they work**."*
> **Report:** *"Centres are reminded that **any area of the specification can appear in the paper**."*

**June 2025 P2R, Q1(c) — Define *qualitative data*:**

> ✅ **1 mark:** *"data - that represents in behaviour, attitude and emotions, that is written in words."*
> **Examiner:** *"a valid definition of qualitative data with reference to '**emotions and attitudes**'. Please note though that **'written in words' is not considered a definition**."*
> ⚠️ **So: qualitative data is defined by WHAT IT IS (opinions, attitudes, emotions, feelings) — not by its FORMAT.**

**June 2025 P1R, Q1(b) — Define *branding*:**

> ✅ **1 mark:** *"Branding is when a business uses logos or slogans to be differentiated from other businesses and be unique"* — *"a clear definition of branding, **including an example in the form of 'logo'**."*
> ❌ **0 marks:** *"The product or service has a logo of the company."* — *"This is an **example** of branding, **rather than a definition**."*

**Report guidance — the required substance:**
> *"To earn full marks, candidates needed to define branding as something that makes a product, service, or business **distinguishable, different, or stand out from its competitors**."*
> *"a significant number provided **examples of branding rather than a precise definition**."*

> ### 📘 DEFINITION RULES — the complete set, from four papers
> | Rule | Evidence |
> |---|---|
> | **~10 words is enough** | *"the provision of services"* → 1 mark |
> | **Do not use the word you are defining** | Cadbury Q1(b), LEGO Q1(b) |
> | **Do not copy the question into the box** | Cadbury |
> | **A definition is not an explanation** | *"is the response a definition or an explanation?"* |
> | **Examples are NOT definitions** | *"Examples are not awarded marks"* — logo, tertiary-sector jobs |
> | **Definitions need the whole concept, not just the category** | *"a group of people"* (crowdfunding) incomplete; *"a group of countries"* (trade bloc) incomplete |
> | **Every word must do work** | *"business owner"* broke *"personal"* savings → 0 |
> | **Format ≠ definition** | *"written in words"* is not a definition of qualitative data |

# ⭐⭐ THE STATE QUESTIONS — still the biggest systematic loss

**June 2025 P2 (Starbucks) — Q1(d):**

> ❌ **0 marks:** *"to save money, because looking for new employees costs can be expensive."*
> **Examiner:** *"a prime example of the candidate showing **good knowledge but failing to contextualise** their response. No marks awarded."*
> *"**Non-contextual responses are awarded 0 marks.**"*
>
> ✅ **1 mark:** *"Starbucks wants to retain its employees as if it does not it will have to spend time and money recruiting and training new **baristas, coffee masters and coffee shop managers**."* — *"This shows good knowledge in the context of Starbucks."*
>
> **Tip:** *"Candidates should provide context to any question that refers to a specific business."*

**June 2025 P1R (CCT) — Q1(d):**

> ❌ **0 marks:** *"Using market reports to identify what their customers need and want to adapt their tours and increase customer satisfaction."*
> **Examiner:** *"**While this is correct as a method of secondary research, it is not contextualised to CCT.** 0 marks."*
>
> ✅ **1 mark:** *"CCT could use government reports to compare the revenue earned from [firms] providing **bicycle tours**."* — *"a correct definition that is related to CCT with the context as '**bicycle tours**'."*
>
> **Examiner tip (verbatim):**
> > *"For all 'State' questions, candidates must include context in their response. Encourage them to **check if their answer is generic and applicable to any business**, or if it includes phrases that link it directly to CCT. For this scenario, simply referencing CCT's specific offerings like **bicycle tours, the tourists they serve, or locations such as China and Shanghai**, will be sufficient contextualisation."*

**June 2025 P2R (Rabanne) — Q1(d):**

> ✅ **1 mark:** *"Rabonne may release its new handbags in a smaller market to get feedback on how to improve the handbag before releasing it into the real bigger market."*
> **Examiner:** *"applied this to the context of Rabanne — '**the handbag**'."*
> **Tip:** *"Simply adding '**the perfume or the aftershave**' could also have been enough context to gain access to the mark."*

> ### 🎯 What all three read as, side by side
> | Paper | ❌ 0 marks | ✅ 1 mark |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Starbucks** | *"looking for new employees costs can be expensive"* | *"recruiting and training new **baristas, coffee masters and coffee shop managers**"* |
> | **CCT** | *"adapt their **tours** and increase customer satisfaction"* | *"revenue from firms providing **bicycle tours**"* |
> | **Rabanne** | — | *"get feedback on how to improve **the handbag**"* |
>
> **In every case the 1-mark answer differs by ONE NOUN taken from the case study.** Note the CCT pair especially: both mention *tours*. The passing one says *"**bicycle** tours"* — the case study's actual product.

**And June 2025 P1R's introduction states the rule for the newest cohort:**
> *"a persistent challenge for a significant number of candidates, as observed in previous examination series, lies with **'State' and 'Outline' questions**… candidates should **avoid simply rephrasing parts of the question** in their responses."*

# 🚨 EXTRA PAPER — the strongest statement yet

**June 2025 P2, from the Introduction:**

> *"**An increasing number of candidates are using extra paper which is not recommended. There are sufficient lines given to allow candidates to provide a thorough answer. There is absolutely no evidence at all to suggest that candidates using extra paper score higher marks.**"*

**Cross-references across the bank:**
> *"the lines available are **more than adequate** for such a question but often learners are using additional papers **without gaining additional marks**"* (Summer 2019 P1)
> *"**Sufficient lines are given** for candidates to answer questions… **Examiners are unable to guess if a candidate has continued the answer somewhere else.**"* (Summer 2019 P2R, and repeated in later series)

> ### What this means, precisely
> **The paper is calibrated so the printed lines are enough for full marks.** Extra paper is not a route to more marks — and it costs time you need for the 12-marker.
>
> **If you must continue, from the ePen guidance:** mark it clearly — *"continued"*, *"contd page ___"*, or *"see separate sheet"* — and **label the question number on the extra sheet**. Otherwise the examiner cannot connect the pages and it is lost.

# Q1(e) — the decimal-place rule, 13th and 14th instances

**June 2025 P2 (Starbucks):**
> *"165.70 + 38.25% → 165.70 × 1.3825 = **229.1 billion**"*
> **Examiner:** *"This candidate **rounded to 1 decimal place**. However, they were **awarded 1 mark for correct workings**."*
> **Tip:** *"**Always round to the required number of decimal places otherwise full marks cannot be attained.**"*
> *"some candidates are still failing to round answers to two decimal points, which is **specifically stated in the question**."*

**June 2025 P1R (CCT):** Q1(e) *"Calculate, to two decimal places, the current ratio for 2024"* — *"Candidates displayed a variety of response styles in their working out"*, well answered.

> **Running count: 14 instances of the decimal-place rule across 8 papers.** It is the single most repeated calculation error in the qualification — ahead of step-counting **in frequency**, though step-counting is more damaging.

# Cross-cutting findings from the newest series

### ⭐ The relevance rule — answering a different question

**June 2025 P2 (Starbucks) Q2(f):**
> *"question 2f where candidates were required to **justify if financial or non-financial incentives would help Starbucks to _retain its employees_**. Unfortunately, many candidates wrote about how the incentives would **impact the revenue or profits of Starbucks**, which is **not answering the question set**."*

⭐ **Note the wording: "not answering the question set."** The outcome the question asked about was **employee retention**; the candidates wrote about **profit**. The instruction at the top of the paper said: *"the main one being that **candidates do not always read the question thoroughly enough**."*

> **The protective habit: after writing your plan, re-read the question and ask "which outcome did it name?" Then check every paragraph delivers THAT outcome, not a neighbouring one.**
