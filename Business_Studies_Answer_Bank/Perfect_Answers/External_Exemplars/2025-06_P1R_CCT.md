## ⭐⭐⭐ Q3(e) — *Justify which one of these two options CCT should choose* — **9/9**

**The question, as printed on the paper** (May–June 2025 P1R QP, p. 14):

> *"Feedback from some customers suggested they became tired during the bicycle tours. To overcome
> this problem CCT is planning to purchase electric bicycles. To fund the cost of purchasing the
> electric bicycles, CCT is considering two options:*
> **Option 1: selling assets**
> **Option 2: retained profit.**"

> ⚠️ **Bank correction, 13 Nov 2026.** `10_Chain_Bank.md` entry 181 said the paper asked
> *"Option 1: selling off unused bicycle equipment · Option 2: retained profit"*. **Those are the
> mark scheme's words, not the paper's.** The paper says **"selling assets"**. The difference is not
> cosmetic: *"unused bicycle equipment"* hands the candidate the AO2 anchor, and the paper does not.

### The answer, in full

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a
> transcription, not a copy]**
>
> *"CCT should choose option of selling assets to fund their purchasing of the electric bycicles. By
> this, CCT would be able to conserve their retained profit for future emergencies where they'd need
> quick access to capital. And by selling assets, CCT is still able to gather funds. This leads to
> CCT not having to risk spending their emergency funds just to purchase electric bycicles. As this
> important because CCT is still a small business, so they might need emergency funds anytime and
> would also have difficulties arranging capital immediately. So therefore, CCT can ensure
> ~~satisfying~~ tourists' need by still being able to purchase the electric bycicles and maintaining
> their financial security for emergencies. Additionally, selling assets*
>
> *However, CCT wouldn't be able to gain immediate access through selling assets, as they'd have to
> wait for some time for their asset to sell off before they receive the capital, and also waits some
> time before they place order for the electric bycicles and before they arrive. This can be
> frustrating for the tourists and they could get annoyed, which could lead to reduced customer
> satisfaction and the tourist's might halt their uses with CCT and seek other competitor tourist
> businesses in Shanghai. Therefore CCT would lose ~~their~~ some of their customers as well*
>
> *In conclusion, CCT should choose option 1: selling assets as it doesn't risk putting CCT in a risk
> by using retained profit to purchase electric bycicles. The success of this decision depends on how
> long CCT has to wait for their asset to sell and for them to receive capital back, in order to not
> make the tourists wait for a long time. In the short-term, things seem a bit worrying to CCT as
> they'd have to permanently sell some of their assets away, but in ~~the~~ long term, CCT can
> conserve their emergency funds and use it in cases of unpredictable economic downturns or
> ~~unpredictable~~ during seasons where tourists are less."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim, from the report's text layer):**
> *"This is a good response that fully meets the mark band descriptors. It demonstrates detailed
> application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts and issues to the business context
> throughout to CCT (AO2). The candidate effectively deconstructs business information and issues,
> finding detailed interconnected points with logical chains of reasoning (AO3). Furthermore, the
> response makes a clear judgement, providing a clear justification based on a thorough evaluation of
> business information and issues relevant to the choice made (AO4). Level 3 - 9 marks."*

## 🔬 What actually earned the 9 — and what did not

| Requirement (MS p. 20, 3(e)) | hit? | The candidate's words |
|---|---|---|
| **AO2 · Option 1** — selling equipment CCT does not use provides cash for the bicycles | ✅ | *"selling assets to fund their purchasing of the electric bycicles"* |
| **AO2 · Option 2** — retained profit gives immediate access to the funds | ✅ | *"conserve their retained profit… quick access to capital"* — used **against** Option 2 |
| **AO3 · Option 1** — making good use of assets no longer used | ⚠️ half | *"still able to gather funds"*; the words *"no longer used"* are never written |
| **AO3 · Option 2** — adapt quickly to changing wants and needs | ⚠️ half | *"fulfilling tourists' need"*, not adaptation |
| **AO4 · Option 1** — at £550 each, there may not be enough surplus assets | ❌ **absent** | the candidate's objection to Option 1 is **time**, not **value** |
| **AO4 · Option 2** — spending all the retained profit leaves nothing for emergencies | ✅ | *"not having to risk spending their emergency funds"* — the spine of paragraph 1 |
| a judgement | ✅ | opening sentence and conclusion |
| a named condition | ✅ | *"depends on how long CCT has to wait for their asset to sell"* |

> ### 🚨 THE LESSON, AND IT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE IN THE BANK
> **This answer scores 9 while missing the one AO4 bullet the scheme prints in most detail** — the
> £550 arithmetic. It never mentions £550, never counts the bicycles, never questions whether the
> assets will cover the cost.
>
> **A mark scheme is a list of acceptable points, not a checklist of required ones.** The AO4 marks
> were earned by the *quality of the judgement*: a named condition, a drawback of the chosen option,
> and a stated trade-off between the short and long term. Had the candidate written the £550
> argument and no condition, the examiner's comment would have been a 7 — which is exactly what
> happened to Starbucks 7/9 on the same series (`2025-06_Series_CCT_Starbucks_Rabanne.md`).
>
> **What to copy:** the conclusion does three jobs in three sentences — decide, name the condition,
> weigh short against long term.
> **What not to copy:** *"As this important because"* has a missing verb, *"bycicles"* is misspelled
> four times, and *"doesn't risk putting CCT in a risk"* is a doubled phrase. **Nine marks anyway.**

## ⚠️ One conflict inside the bank, stated rather than hidden

`02_Verified_Exemplars.md` presents *"the success of this depends on…"* as the **shape** of a full-mark
conclusion, and it is — but note the difference between this script and the Rabanne 9/9. Rabanne's
condition qualifies **the option it chose** (*how much employees get to voice concerns in meetings*).
CCT's condition qualifies **the risk in the option it chose** (*how long the sale takes*). Both are
specific to the business and the option. **A condition that would fit any question is not a
condition** — that test is unchanged.

## ⭐⭐ Q3(d) — *Analyse the possible effects on CCT of recruiting externally* — **6/6**

**The question, as printed:** *"(d) Analyse the possible effects on CCT of recruiting externally."*
(6) — led in by *"CCT wants to extend its services by offering family tour holidays and team-building
days for businesses. It plans to recruit an external Marketing Manager to promote these new
services."*

### The answer, in full — two short paragraphs, and it opens AGAINST

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a
> transcription, not a copy]**
>
> *"Recruiting a Marketing manager externally could have negative repurcussions for CCT. This
> individual wouldn't know company culture. ~~and~~ This means that they would take time to adapt and
> get along with other tour guides and managers. Therefore productivity decreases. As a result, family
> tour holidays and team building days may not be marketed and advertised as well. Hence, it could
> fail as many are unaware of these new introductions.*
>
> *However, recruiting externally allows new input ~~on~~ and skills. This means that the new
> introduction of services may do even better than it could've done. Therefore, CCT makes more revenue
> not just on their bicycle tours. As a result, CCT can compare to other large companies such as
> Hajar bike tours in China."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim, from the report's text layer):**
> *"The response fully satisfies the mark band descriptors, showcasing a balanced discussion with two
> distinct arguments—one in favour and one against. These arguments are thoroughly developed with
> multiple interconnected points. Crucially, application is integrated well, demonstrated through
> diverse examples (such as tour holidays, China, and bicycle tours) across every paragraph. Level 3 -
> 6 marks."*

### 🚨 THIS BREAKS THE "ONE POINT IS ENOUGH" READING — AND THE "YOU NEED A CONCLUSION" READING

| | Four Fitness 6/6 (June 2025 P1 1(g)) | **CCT 6/6 (this script)** |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | **one** point, ~140 words | **two** points, opposite directions |
| Order | the effect, then its consequence | **against first, then for** |
| Conclusion | none | **none** |
| Verdict | none | **none** |
| Context | *"14 employees"*, *"two gyms"*, *"gym members"* | *"company culture"*, *"tour guides"*, *"family tour holidays"*, *"bicycle tours"*, *"China"* |
| What the examiner credits | *"1 point with developed strands… application throughout"* | *"two distinct arguments… application integrated well… across every paragraph"* |

> **Both are 6/6 and they have almost nothing in common structurally.** The one thing they share is
> what the examiner actually pays for: **a chain that ends on a business outcome, with the case woven
> through it.** *"Productivity decreases → the new services are not marketed → they fail because
> nobody knows about them"* is a chain. *"Gianni tells his 14 employees directly → less
> miscommunication → instructors teach better → cash flow maintained"* is a chain.
>
> **So: two points or one, in either order, with or without a conclusion — all 6/6.** The trade is
> always **depth for breadth**, and the examiners accept either side of it.
>
> ⚠️ **The one thing this answer does NOT do:** it never says which effect is more likely. On a
> 6-mark Analyse that is not required (there are no AO4 marks), and this is the proof.

## ⭐⭐ Q4(c) — *Evaluate the impact on CCT of an increase in the Chinese interest rate* — **11/12**

**The question, as printed:** *"(c) Evaluate the impact on CCT of an increase in the Chinese interest
rate. You should use the information provided as well as your own knowledge of business."* (12)
The paper prints: **"In 2024 the interest rate in China was 3% but is now predicted to increase to
3.2% in 2025."**
The candidate drew an arrow from *"an increase in the Chinese interest rate"* to
**"less disposable income"** before starting — a plan in six words.

### Why this script is in the bank: Pearson says exactly what the twelfth mark was

> **Full transcription:** `../../audits/corpus/transcripts/4BS1_01R_2506_4c.md`.
> Pearson prints a full comment for this script on report p. 54, and it is quoted verbatim there.

**It does everything the mark scheme asks:**

| | |
|---|---|
| chain through the **customer** | higher mortgages → less disposable income in China → fewer tours → price cuts that may not cover costs |
| chain through the **business** | CCT's loans cost more → average total costs ↑ → profit ↓ → no reinvestment → no expansion to Beijing |
| **two** counter-arguments | foreign tourists hold a stronger currency than the CNY, so tours are *"a steal"* for them; **and** the rise is only **0.2 percentage points**, which the candidate computes from the case |
| a judgement | *"increased interest rates have a big impact on CCT"* |

**And it loses a mark anyway — and Pearson says why:**

> ### ✅ PEARSON'S OWN DIAGNOSIS
> *"This is a very strong response that meets most of the Level 3 descriptors… The conclusion is
> particularly effective, making a clear judgement for AO4. **To achieve the very top marks, more
> sophisticated application of the 'it depends rule' would be beneficial.** Level 3 - 11 marks."*
>
> **Note what is praised: the conclusion.** Whatever a reader thinks of paragraph 6, the examiner
> calls it *"particularly effective"*. The missing mark is not a contradiction in the last paragraph —
> it is the absence of the **conditional**: the answer decides, but never says **when it would decide
> the other way**.
>
> **The comparison Pearson prints two pages earlier, on the 10-mark script on the same question:**
> *"making a clear and well justified judgement that uses the 'it depends rule' for strong AO4. To
> achieve the very top marks, more sophisticated application of the 'it depends rule' earlier and
> throughout the arguments would be beneficial."* — **the 10 uses the rule and is asked to use it
> earlier; the 11 does not use it and is asked to use it better.** Naming the rule is not the mark.

⚠️ **Correction, 13 November 2026.** An earlier version of this section said Pearson printed no
comment for the script and argued that paragraph 6 contradicted paragraph 5. **The comment exists and
it praises that paragraph.** The invented diagnosis has been withdrawn and the real one put in its
place — see `KNOWN_DEFECTS.md` D19.

**What to copy:** both chains, both counters, and the arithmetic on the paper's own figure.
**What not to copy:** a verdict that decides without saying on what it depends.

## ⭐ Q4(c) — same question, same paper, **10/12** — and this is the pair that settles the "it depends" argument

**Full transcription:** `../../audits/corpus/transcripts/4BS1_01R_2506_4c_10.md`.

Five paragraphs: cost of borrowing → demand falls in China → revenue falls → risk of failure; then
investment costs rise → production costs rise → profit falls → less finance to expand; then two
counters (the rise is small; CCT sells to foreign tourists who want to see Shanghai); then a
conclusion that names the condition.

**Pearson's comment, verbatim (report p. 51):**

> *"This is a very strong response that fully meets the Level 3 descriptors… The conclusion is
> particularly effective, making a clear and well justified judgement that **uses the 'it depends
> rule'** for strong AO4. To achieve the very top marks, **more sophisticated application of the 'it
> depends rule' earlier and throughout the arguments** would be beneficial. Level 3 - 10 marks."*

### 🎯 PUT THE TWO COMMENTS SIDE BY SIDE, BECAUSE THEY SETTLE A QUESTION THE BANK HAS BEEN ASKING

| | **11/12** | **10/12** |
|---|---|---|
| Pearson's first line | *"meets **most** of the Level 3 descriptors"* | *"**fully** meets the Level 3 descriptors"* |
| On the conclusion | *"particularly effective, making a clear judgement for AO4"* | *"particularly effective, making a clear and well justified judgement that **uses the 'it depends rule'**"* |
| On the missing mark | *"more **sophisticated** application of the 'it depends rule'"* | the rule *"**earlier and throughout** the arguments"* |

> **Read the right-hand column of each row.** The 10 is the one Pearson says uses the rule, and it is
> the one that scores **lower**. **The phrase is not the mark.** What the examiner wants is the rule
> *as a way of arguing* — applied earlier, throughout, and more sophisticatedly — and **neither of
> these two candidates does that.**
>
> The 11 asks for a plain judgement to become conditional; the 10 asks for a conditional to become
> structural. **Both are being told the same thing: put the condition where the argument is, not
> where the conclusion is.**

**One more difference, and it is a factual one:** the 10/12 writes *"only a 2% increase in interest"*
when the paper prints 3% → 3.2%, which is **0.2 percentage points**. The 11/12 works it out correctly
(*"only been a 0.2% increase"*). **The 10 still earns AO4 for arguing from magnitude — but it earns
two marks less.**

**What to copy from the 10:** the size of the rise used as a counter, and the foreign-tourist line as
a second, independent counter.
**What not to copy:** *"2%"* — and a conclusion that carries the whole of the "it depends" thinking on
its own.
