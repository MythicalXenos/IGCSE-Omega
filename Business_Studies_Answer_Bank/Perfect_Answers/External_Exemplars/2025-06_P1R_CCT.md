## ⭐⭐⭐ Q3(e) — *Justify which one of these two options CCT should choose* — **9/9**

**The question, as printed on the paper** (May–June 2025 P1R QP, p. 14):

> *"Feedback from some customers suggested they became tired during the bicycle tours. To overcome
> this problem CCT is planning to purchase electric bicycles. To fund the cost of purchasing the
> electric bicycles, CCT is considering two options:*
> **Option 1: selling assets**
> **Option 2: retained profit.**"

> ⚠️ **Bank correction, 13 Nov 2026.** `10_Chain_Bank.md` entry 181 said the paper asked
> *"Option 1: selling off unused bicycle equipment · Option 2: retained profit"*. **Those are the
> mark scheme's words, not the paper's.** The paper says **"selling assets"**. The difference is not
> cosmetic: *"unused bicycle equipment"* hands the candidate the AO2 anchor, and the paper does not.

## 🔬 What actually earned the 9 — and what did not

| Requirement (MS p. 20, 3(e)) | hit? | The candidate's words |
|---|---|---|
| **AO2 · Option 1** — selling equipment CCT does not use provides cash for the bicycles | ✅ | *"selling assets to fund their purchasing of the electric bycicles"* |
| **AO2 · Option 2** — retained profit gives immediate access to the funds | ✅ | *"conserve their retained profit… quick access to capital"* — used **against** Option 2 |
| **AO3 · Option 1** — making good use of assets no longer used | ⚠️ half | *"still able to gather funds"*; the words *"no longer used"* are never written |
| **AO3 · Option 2** — adapt quickly to changing wants and needs | ⚠️ half | *"fulfilling tourists' need"*, not adaptation |
| **AO4 · Option 1** — at £550 each, there may not be enough surplus assets | ❌ **absent** | the candidate's objection to Option 1 is **time**, not **value** |
| **AO4 · Option 2** — spending all the retained profit leaves nothing for emergencies | ✅ | *"not having to risk spending their emergency funds"* — the spine of paragraph 1 |
| a judgement | ✅ | opening sentence and conclusion |
| a named condition | ✅ | *"depends on how long CCT has to wait for their asset to sell"* |

> ### 🚨 THE LESSON, AND IT IS THE MOST IMPORTANT ONE IN THE BANK
> **This answer scores 9 while missing the one AO4 bullet the scheme prints in most detail** — the
> £550 arithmetic. It never mentions £550, never counts the bicycles, never questions whether the
> assets will cover the cost.
>
> **A mark scheme is a list of acceptable points, not a checklist of required ones.** The AO4 marks
> were earned by the *quality of the judgement*: a named condition, a drawback of the chosen option,
> and a stated trade-off between the short and long term. Had the candidate written the £550
> argument and no condition, the examiner's comment would have been a 7 — which is exactly what
> happened to Starbucks 7/9 on the same series (`2025-06_Series_CCT_Starbucks_Rabanne.md`).
>
> **What to copy:** the conclusion does three jobs in three sentences — decide, name the condition,
> weigh short against long term.
> **What not to copy:** *"As this important because"* has a missing verb, *"bycicles"* is misspelled
> four times, and *"doesn't risk putting CCT in a risk"* is a doubled phrase. **Nine marks anyway.**

## ⚠️ One conflict inside the bank, stated rather than hidden

`02_Verified_Exemplars.md` presents *"the success of this depends on…"* as the **shape** of a full-mark
conclusion, and it is — but note the difference between this script and the Rabanne 9/9. Rabanne's
condition qualifies **the option it chose** (*how much employees get to voice concerns in meetings*).
CCT's condition qualifies **the risk in the option it chose** (*how long the sale takes*). Both are
specific to the business and the option. **A condition that would fit any question is not a
condition** — that test is unchanged.
