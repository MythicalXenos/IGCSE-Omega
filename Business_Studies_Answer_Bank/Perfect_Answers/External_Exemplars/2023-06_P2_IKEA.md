# THE CONTEXT TRAP — a second direct examiner statement

## ❌ 0 marks · Q1(d) State

**Question:** State why IKEA provides induction training for new employees.

> *"Ikea provides induction training for new employees to welcome and familiarize the employees with the new job."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"Although the response understands the purpose of induction training for employees **it is not in context and therefore does not gain a mark**."*

## ✅ 1 mark · the same question

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Then IKEA can introduce all the things for employees such as targets and aims of the business and the staff to work familiar with them which will increase the productivity **when selling furniture**."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"The candidates clearly understands the importance of induction training by getting to know the staff they will work with and the comment at the end about **furniture** clearly puts the response in context."*

### 🎯 "furniture"

**One word.** The 0-mark response does everything else correctly — it understands induction training perfectly. The 1-mark response adds the word **"furniture"** at the end.

This is the **fourth independent confirmation** of the same rule, across four different papers and five years:

| Paper | 0/low mark | Full mark | The word that did it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 2019 P1R | "product" | "food" | **food** |
| Jun 2024 P1 | "products" | "dog coats and dog leads" | **dog coats** |
| Jun 2024 P1R | "products" | "filled Ciabattas" | **ciabattas** |
| **Jun 2023 P2** | *(generic)* | *(generic)* + "**furniture**" | **furniture** |

**Before you hand in any paper, scan every answer for the words "product", "products", "the business", "customers" and replace each with the actual noun from the case.** It is a two-minute edit worth a mark on almost every question.

> *"This response clearly gives a definition of person specification."*

## ❌ 0 marks ×2 · Q1(c) Define *quality control*

The examiner's framing on this one is unusually blunt:

> *"Again this question was not well answered as a number of candidates **included either quality or control in their response**. Defining quality control as control of the products made is not clear and not a specific definition. **Less than a quarter of cohort gained the mark available.**"*

Response A:
> *"how a business controls the quality of its product."*
> *"Again the word **quality** is used which is therefore not giving a clear definition of the term."*

Response B:
> *"This is a measure that ensure[s the] quality of particular goods or services are always maintained to be at its best."*
> *"The response does not give a clear definition of quality control and therefore was not awarded a mark."*

### 🎯 The rule: **you may not use the word you are defining**

> *"In definition questions try and avoid using the term, it may help to develop a response."*

Both responses above are *circular* — "quality control is controlling quality". Note that response B is longer, more sophisticated and better written than the 1-mark response to Q1(b), and still scores **0**, because it explains quality rather than defining quality control.

**Note also the examiner's benchmark:** fewer than a quarter of candidates got Q1(c). This is a *hard* question, not an obscure one — quality control is core specification content.

# VERIFIED EXEMPLARS — 3 marks (Explain)

## ✅ 3/3 · Q1(f) Explain one reason why businesses use resources effectively in manufacturing

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"To reduce the total cost of the resource and to lower average cost. If the resources are able to be stretched [and] used to make more products, that would mean the company will need to buy less resources to make more product. That will also decrease the average cost because less money is being spent. This will result in a higher [added] value and more profit."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"A very detailed answer on the reason why businesses want to use resources effectively, gains full marks for this response."*

## ⚠️ 2/3 · the same question — the difference is one missing step

> *"One reason [w]hich is because if resources are left over[,] materials can be reused[,] this helps the business save a bit of money."*

> *"In this response the candidate clearly understood the benefits of using left over material that can be reused. **However there was limited development of the response and therefore only gained two marks.**"*

**Compare the chain lengths:**

| | Identify | Develop 1 | Develop 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3/3** | reduce total cost / lower average cost | stretch resources → more products from less | → lower average cost → higher value → **more profit** |
| **2/3** | reuse leftover materials | → saves money | *(stops)* |

**The 3/3 pushes one step further: to profit.** That final consequence — profit — is what completes the third mark. This is why "so what?" should be asked twice on every 3-marker.

## ✅ 3/3 ×2 · Q2(c) Explain one reason why cash flow forecasting is important

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Cash flow forecasting is important for the business because it is good to predict the cash flow in the up[coming] months. This can allow for changes which prevent decline of cash flow and can prevent business failure."*
> *"The candidate clearly understood the question and gave one reason why cash flow forecasting helps a business, with further details to gain the marks available."*

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Cash flow forecasting allows a business to predict upcoming cash shortages, and take out an appropriate form of external finance to meet the running costs of the business during the shortage, so the business does not fail due to cash flow shortage[s] problems, such as seasonal factors."*
> *"Another response that clearly shows the candidate fully understands the purpose of a cash flow forecast and how it can affect a business."*

> **Examiner guidance on this question, verbatim:** *"An explain question which **does not have to be in context** of IKEA. The Assessment Objective being tested is **AO1 (knowledge)**… This question is asking for one reason and **it does not have to be in context**."*

### ⚠️ Correction to a rule you may have been taught

**Not every explain question is AO1, and not every explain question is context-free.** Compare across the papers:

| Question | Examiner states |
|---|---|
| Jun 2023 P2 Q2(c) | *"does not have to be in context… AO1 (knowledge)"* |
| Jun 2023 P2 Q2(d) | *"does not need to be in context"* |
| Jun 2022 P1R explain questions | *"No context is required for explain questions"* |
| **Jun 2019/2023 Mark Schemes** | **`1 (f)  AO1 - 3 marks`** |

But the **mark schemes for some papers list explain as AO1 and others as AO2**, and the examiner reports show explain questions where context *is* required.

**The safe rule: check the command word AND the AO in the mark scheme. If the MS says `AO1`, context is not required. If it says `AO2`, it is.** Do not assume.

# CALIBRATION — knowledge with no application

## ❌ 0 marks · Q1(g) Analyse the benefit to IKEA of being a franchise (6)

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"It is multi-national meaning that it is located all over the world so millions even billions of people could have seen it and bought from it. Limited liability meaning any owner is only liable for amount invested. Unincorporated business meaning that there is no difference between the owner and the business[] legal identity"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"In this response there is **a very detailed knowledge of what a franchise is but without any application to the benefits of this type of business, hence no marks were awarded.**"*

> **Examiner guidance on this question, verbatim:**
> *"An **'Analyse' question which is testing two of the Assessment Objectives… AO2 'Application' and AO3 'Analyse'**. Thus, to be successful in gaining the marks, candidates need to demonstrate both of these skills within their answer…*
> *To gain the marks available candidates need to apply the information and then analyse. **Candidate[s] should avoid describing what a franchise is.***
> *Examiners will look at the response 'holistically'… **Candidates must be reminded that there are no marks available for knowledge, and that the response must be in context of IKEA.**"*

### 🎯 "There are no marks available for knowledge" — on an ANALYSE question

This is a stronger statement than the bank previously recorded. We already knew there are **no knowledge marks on justify** (9 marks). This report says it of an **analyse** question too.

**Why:** on the 6-mark tariff the AO split is `AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3`. AO1 contributes **zero**. So three technically-correct definitions of "multi-national", "limited liability" and "unincorporated business" — all accurate — earn **0 out of 6**.

**The lesson:** the words "knowledge" and "content" are not your friend on 6, 9 and 12 mark questions. Only *applied* knowledge scores.

# CALIBRATION — the "State" catastrophe

**This is the most striking data in the report.** Over four fifths of the cohort missed two consecutive 1-mark State questions.

## ❌ 0 marks · Q2(a) State one reason why IKEA would employ temporary staff (×2)

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Because [IKEA] needs to get new ideas from time to time to keep the products up to date therefore they [hire] new employ[ees] in order to get new ideas."*
> *"As previously mentioned just putting IKEA in the response is not putting it in context as it is in the question. This response is valid but did not gain the mark."*

> *"one reason is if the full time employees are sick and cannot do their Job so temporary employees are [hired] to [do] their Job"*
> *"Again a very valid response that IKEA would need to have temporary employees to cover employees who are sick, **however there is no context. If the candidate had included something along the lines of to cover a sick employee who look[s] after the sales of tables and chairs, this would have gained the mark.**"*

> **Examiner note on the cohort:** *"**Over four fifths of the cohort did not get the mark on this question** because they did not put it in context, although they may have given a very valid response. It is well worth practising these types of questions to ensure that candidates fully understand what is required."*

**"tables and chairs."** Two words. That is the entire difference between 0 and 1 mark.

## ❌ 0 marks · Q2(b) State a financial objective IKEA might have

> *"Increase in the sales and increase in the market share"*

> *"A very valid point of wanting to increase its sales and market share, but **the response does not relate back to IKEA**."*

> **Examiner note:** *"nearly **four fifths** of the cohort [did] not gain the mark available."*

## 🎯 Why this matters more than the big questions

Four fifths of candidates lost a mark they knew the answer to. On Paper 2 these State questions are worth 1 mark each — but there are many of them, and they are the **cheapest marks on the paper**.

**The State-question protocol:**
1. Write the correct business point (you already know it).
2. **Add one concrete noun from the case** — a product, a customer type, an ingredient, a location, a competitor.
3. Stop. One piece of context suffices.

> *"one piece of context is sufficient for a state question"* — Jun 2024 P1 examiner report

# Calculation traps — third and fourth confirmations

## ⚠️ 1/2 · Q1(e) Calculate the gross profit margin to two decimal places

**Data:** Revenue €37,368 · Cost of sales €25,643 · Gross profit €11,725

The candidate correctly computed `11725 / 37368 × 100 = 31.3771` and then wrote **31.4%**.

> *"The candidate has clearly understood how to work the calculation out and but gave the percentage as a whole number as opposed to what the question asked – 2 decimal places."*
> *"Again the candidate has carried out the correct calculation but rounded the answer to one decimal place."*

**Correct answer: 31.38%**

### The running tally of this exact trap

| Paper | Question | What went wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Jun 2022 P2R | Calculate to 2 d.p. | *"only gained one mark as the working out is correct but the answer was missing the second decimal place"* |
| Jun 2023 P1R | % decrease to 2 d.p. | answered to 1 d.p. |
| Jun 2024 P1R | operating profit margin | correct method, wrong figure used |
| **Jun 2023 P2** | **gross profit margin to 2 d.p.** | **31.4% instead of 31.38%** |

**This is the single most reliable mark-losing trap on the 4BS1 paper.** It recurs in every session examined. **Write the answer to the decimal places the question states, every time, and check it before moving on.**

> **Related rule, stated here and in three other reports:** *"Candidates should be encouraged to show their workings as on all calculation questions **they could gain a mark for the working, even if the actual answer written in the box is incorrect**."* And: *"write their answer on the dotted lines at the bottom of the box so that the examiners can clearly see what their answer is."*
