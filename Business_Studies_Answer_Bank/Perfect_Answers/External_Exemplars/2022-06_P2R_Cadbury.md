# VERIFIED EXEMPLARS — 6 marks (Analyse)

## ✅ 5/6 and 6/6 · Q1(g) Analyse two factors that could influence Cadbury when locating a new factory

### The 5/6 response — and precisely why it stopped short

> *"A factor in opening a new factory is to locate it near its natural resources of the product the firm is producing, this causes less time to [p]roduce the product… Since Cadbury is a chocolate producing firm they should locate their factories in countries where **cocoa seeds** are highly common…"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows a clear understanding of Cadbury locating near to where its natural resources are and **it is applied by mentioning cocoa seeds**. This is then developed with the comment about less time to produce. The second reason of locating away from cities as it will annoy the population is also a valid reason. **However, the response did not gain full marks because it lacked the logical chains of reasoning. Perhaps the candidate could have linked the two factors together**, by moving near to supplies might be difficult to get employees to work at the factory, or locating near to a city provides workers who have the skills. **Level 3 – 5 marks**"*

### 🎯 The gap between 5 and 6 — the examiner names it exactly

The response has:
- ✅ application ("**cocoa seeds**" — the perfect context anchor for Cadbury)
- ✅ two valid factors
- ❌ **no link between the factors**

**The fix the examiner supplies:** rather than listing factor A, then factor B, **connect them.** *Moving near supplies* makes it *harder to recruit skilled workers*; *locating near a city* supplies workers with the skills. Both factors, in one chain.

**This is the highest-value sentence in this report.** It defines the difference between "two points with reasons" (5) and "detailed interconnected points" (6) — the AO3 word in the level descriptor is **"interconnected"**. A 6/6 does not have more points; it has points that **bear on each other**.

### The 6/6 response

> *"Proximity to employees. This is because the factory being close to employees would reduce Cadbury's costs that would be used for transport. This leads to them being able to save time that would be used in the journey, therefore, Cadbury would increase their efficiency."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"A well answered response, clearly identifying the two factors of location and giving justifiable reasons. **Level 3 – 6 marks**"*

### ⚠️ "There are no marks available for knowledge" — on an analyse question, again

> **Examiner guidance (verbatim):** *"'Analyse' questions are testing two of the Assessment Objectives… AO2 'Application' and AO3 'Analysis'. Thus, to be successful, candidates need to demonstrate both of these skills within their answer… To gain the marks available candidates need to apply the information and then analyse. **Unfortunately, many candidates tend to describe one factor of locating a new factory, which is not answering the question.** Most candidates gained a Level 2 mark.*
>
> *Examiners will look at the response 'holistically'… **Candidates must be aware that there are no marks available for knowledge, and that the response must be in context of Cadbury.**"*

**This is now the third report to state it of a 6-mark analyse** (also Jun 2023 P2, Jun 2022 P1). It is a firm rule, not a one-off:

| Tariff | AO split | Knowledge marks |
|---|---|---|
| 3 (explain, AO1) | AO1 = 3 | **All 3** — knowledge *is* the question |
| **6 (analyse)** | AO2 = 3, **AO3 = 3** | **Zero** |
| 9 (justify) | AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 | **Zero** |
| 12 (evaluate) | AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 | 3 |

# VERIFIED EXEMPLARS — 3 marks (Explain)

## ✅ 3/3 and ⚠️ 2/3 · Q1(f) Explain one advantage to a business of lower interest rates

**3/3:**
> *"If banks reduce their interest rates, business[] will not be giving as much money to the bank as they once did. This leads to the business saving money and instead of putting the money into the bank it can be used to help further improve their business therefore they may have more chance of survival."*
> *"The response includes the one advantage [that] they are not going to be paying the bank as much. The development is leading to them saving money, which could further improve their business. **3 marks**"*

**2/3:**
> *"They can pay their debts easier, as interest rates being decreased means they will have to pay less to borrow, which improves the liquidity of the business."*
> *"This particular response gained 2 marks, the comment with reference to paying less to borrow this is then developed which improves the cash flow of the business. **The comment about paying their debts easier is not developed.**"*

### The difference

| Mark | 3/3 response | 2/3 response |
|---|---|---|
| Identify | *"not giving as much money to the bank"* | *"pay their debts easier"* |
| Develop 1 | *"saving money"* | *"pay less to borrow"* |
| Develop 2 | *"…can be used to help further improve their business → more chance of survival"* | *(stopped)* |

The 2/3 response actually contains **two** candidate points — "pay debts easier" and "improves liquidity". The examiner credits one and marks the other *"not developed."* **Naming two things is not the same as developing one.**

> **Examiner note:** *"Many candidates misread advantage for disadvantage and gave detailed account [of what happens] if the banks reduced the interest rates to customers and not businesses."* — **Read whether the question says advantage or disadvantage.** This is the same misreading flagged in Jun 2023 P1R (*"Many candidates misread advantage for disadvantage"*).
>
> **Also:** *"Candidates are reminded that the question asked for one advantage not several advantages."*

> **Examiner Tip (verbatim):** *"**To avoid wasting time do not repeat the question in the first sentence.**"*

## ✅ 2/3 and ✅ 3/3 · Q2(c) Explain one method a business could use to motivate its employees

**2/3:**
> *"A business could use **job rotation** to motivate its employees. Job rotation will provide a change in the workplace of its employees, as a result it could motivate them."*
> *"The method of job rotation is valid and worthy of a mark, with a justifiable reason of giving employees a change in the workplace. **However, the response was not further developed to gain the extra mark available.** 2 marks"*

**3/3:**
> *"A business could use non[-]financial methods to motivate employees. This means that employees will be more challeng[ed]. This leads to the employees feeling trusted and valuable [to] the business as a large amount of work needed for the business is from them. As a result the employee will feel wanted and also the business would be increasing its productivity."*
> *"This response gives a method of **Job Enrichment**, followed by development of employees being more challenged, together with employees feeling trusted and valued. **3 marks**"*

### ⚠️ An important precision point

The 2/3 response names **job rotation** — a valid method, correctly explained, and it still only scores 2. The 3/3 goes one step further into **the employee's state of mind and then the business outcome** (*"feel trusted and valued" → "increasing its productivity"*).

**Note also:** the 3/3 candidate wrote "non-financial methods" generally, and the examiner identified it as **job enrichment** — so a slightly imprecise label is not penalised, provided the development is sound.

> **Examiner guidance (verbatim):** *"This was a straightforward question… **for those candidates that did not get the full allocation of marks this might be because they gave more than one method.** Again care should be taken to ensure that the question is thoroughly understood and it is advisable to read it through **at least twice** before attempting to answer."*

## ✅ 3/3 ×2 · Q2(d) Explain one reason why a business has fully trained employees

> *"To avoid mistakes and maintain quality, this means that if [all] employees a[re] trained to a standard across the business this avoids mistakes, as a result maintaining quality, leading to more satisfied customers, therefore building a qualified workforce."*
> *"This response gained the full marks available. **The response avoided repeating the question in the answer which is helpful.** The reasons for fully trained staff are to avoid mistakes, maintaining quality which leads to more satisfied customers. **3 marks**"*

> *"To decrease safety risks / hazards. This is because if employees are trained they're less likely to make… mistakes and be more safe and have safe working conditions since they know [h]ow to do their job well. This may lead to none of the employees [suing] the business for unsafe working conditions meaning they'll reduce their legal costs."*
> *"The response refers to decreased safety risks and hazards as the reason for being fully trained. This is then further developed by mistakes are less likely and employees will be safe. **3 marks**"*

> **Examiner Tip (verbatim):** *"**Try to avoid repeating the question in your response, it wastes valuable time and is not needed.**"*

**Two different routes to 3/3 on the same question:**
- Route A: mistakes → quality → satisfied customers
- Route B: safety risks → fewer mistakes → safer conditions → **fewer lawsuits → lower legal costs**

Route B is the stronger answer because it terminates in a **financial outcome for the business** — the rule established by the "final three words" finding in `2022-06_P1_TBTS.md`.

## ✅ 3/3 ×2 · Q2(e) Explain why manufacturing businesses use Kaizen

> *"To increase employee loyalty, this is because Kaizen is a Japanese term used to mean 'continuous development'; this leads to employees feeling more valued as their opinions and suggestions are used to create improvements in the firm, there[]for[e] more productive employees."*
> *"A well developed response referring to employees becoming more loyal which is a valid reason and this is then developed by employees['] opinions being valued to increase productivity. **3 marks**"*

> *"[T]o improve their product. This means that employees are given the chance to decide on how to improve the product since they are the ones making them. As a result businesses can continuously improve if they use Kaizen in their factories."*
> *"Another well developed response indicating that employees are involved in the decision on how to improve the manufacture of the products. **3 marks**"*

> **Examiner guidance (verbatim):** *"many candidates understood why manufacturing businesses use Kaizen in their factories but then did not include the reasons why… Most responses included the comment about **reducing waste**, which is relevant but then did not develop the comment further, perhaps including **involving employees more to give their opinion as they know how the machinery works**."*

**Note:** the examiner supplies the missing development. *Reducing waste* is only the identify step — you must say *how* and *what follows*. Both 3/3 responses reach a **human or productivity outcome**.

# CALIBRATION — 9 marks (Justify)

## ⚠️ 7/9 · Q2(f) Justify: job share or part-time work

> *"[Job share means] to be shared, which may not be enough for their daily needs… As skilled employees in the long term would motivate them and would ensure that [they] stay at Cadbury due to the reduced burden and more flex[i]bility, ensuring the[y] also produce better chocolates and sweets, leading to higher revenues."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"The first response has a clear understanding of what job share is and how it works, encouraging staff to work together which motivates them to work harder. The response is mainly for the option chosen. There are comments with regard to how job sharing affects the employee, it is less stressful and that they have the flexibility of working. The evaluative comments about staying at Cadbury is relevant as it reduces the burden on themselves. **Perhaps if the response had included more evaluative comments with regard to Cadbury and more logical chains of reasoning it could have moved into the top of Level 3. Level 3 – 7 marks**"*

**Diagnosis — two named gaps:**
1. *"more evaluative comments **with regard to Cadbury**"* — the evaluation was generic, not applied
2. *"more logical chains of reasoning"* — the chains were short

## ⚠️ 6/9 · the same question (Level 2)

> *"Working part-time will benefit employees and the business as employee[s] ha[ve] lesser working hours and business spends lesser on employee[s] (due to working hours) so… Cadbury, in turn, can spend more on part-time workers (large work-force, small pay)… Cadbury can have an improved cash flow which allows them to return [to] employees at a cheaper pri[ce]."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response chose Option 1 – part-time work and gave reasons as to why working part-time would be beneficial, the shorter hours, etc but the negative side for Cadbury [is] that they will have to employ more employees to ensure that the work is covered.*
>
> *The response showed **sound application** of knowledge, found interconnected points with chains of reasoning and **some** judgement was made. Gaining the top of Level 2. **Level 2 – 6 marks**"*

**Note the descriptors in play:** "sound application" (Level 2) vs "detailed application" (Level 3); "some judgement" (Level 2) vs "clear justification based on thorough evaluation" (Level 3). Same content, different quality of execution.

## 🎯 The 9-mark knowledge rule — stated again, verbatim

> *"Candidates should be careful to avoid giving detailed knowledge of the two options available **as there are no knowledge marks for this question**.*
>
> *The 'Justify' questions are marked in the same way as the 'Analyse' questions which is Levels, but candidates now have to include 'Evaluative' (Assessment Objective 4) comments. **Candidates do not have to consider both options in their response. Candidates can achieve the full marks only considering the one option.**"*

> *"There were a number of candidates who **did not achieve any marks** on this question, as some gave very detailed knowledge of the two methods of working which was not asked for. There were also a number of candidates who **confused the two methods of working** which also meant that they did not gain the marks available."*

**Two distinct zero-mark routes:**
1. Giving detailed knowledge instead of justification → **0**
2. Confusing the two options (e.g. job share vs part-time) → **0**

# CALIBRATION — 1 mark (Define)

## ❌ 0 marks and ✅ 1 mark · Q3(a) Define *public relations*

**0 marks:**
> *"The attempt to communicate with interested parties."*
> *"The comment about trying to communicate with interested parties is **not relevant** and therefore not worthy of a mark."*

**1 mark:**
> *"public relations mean the business has a good relation with the community they are located in."*
> *"Although the response includes relation, it is developed with reference to the business having a good relation with the community they are located in."*

**Note the tension the examiner identifies:**
> *"This was not as well answered as expected. Many candidates did not understand fully what the term actually meant. **A number of candidates used the actual words in their definition which does not always help.**"*

**The rule, resolved:** you must not *circularly restate* the term (as in "quality control is controlling quality"), but using the root word **as part of a genuine explanation** — *"public relations mean the business has a good **relation** with the community"* — is acceptable **because it says what the relationship is with**. The test is not "did you avoid the word" but **"have you told us something specific?"**

> **Cohort note:** *"This was not as well answered as expected."*
