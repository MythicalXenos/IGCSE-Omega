## ⭐⭐ Q1(d) — STATE a reason for paying above the minimum wage — AO2, 1 mark

> **Question:** State **one** reason why *Cadbury* might pay its UK employees above the minimum wage.

**This is the most instructive pair in the whole bank — the same question, one mark and zero marks, side by side.**

### ✅ 1 mark
> *"To retain its employees. In this case, Cadbury would want to retain its employees so they don't switch to competitors like Lindt or Royce."*

**Examiner comment:** *"This response is **in context** by commenting on Cadbury not wanting its employees to switch to **competitors like Lindt**."*

### ❌ 0 marks
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"This is to improve brand image and reputation in the market, as it has lots of competition, it needs good image and reputation to attract workers to work for them and to attract customers to buy from them."*

**Examiner comment:** *"This response has made **very valid comments** about there is lots of competition and Cadbury wants to maintain its reputation. **However, there is nothing that is related back to Cadbury and its workers.**"*

### 🎯 Read those two side by side

| | 1-mark answer | 0-mark answer |
|---|---|---|
| Length | **18 words** | **43 words** |
| Names Cadbury | once | **twice** |
| Names a **competitor** | **Lindt, Royce** | *"lots of competition"* |
| Names the **employee group** | *"its employees"* | *"workers"* (generic) |
| Verdict | **in context** | *"very valid… however, nothing related back"* |

> **The 0-mark answer is longer, more fluent, and says nothing wrong.** It even correctly identifies competition as the reason — and earns zero.
>
> **The 1-mark answer is barely more than a sentence.** What earns the mark is **the competitor's name (Lindt) and the employee group** — two specific facts from the case that could not appear in a generic answer.

**⭐ The rule, stated by the examiner:** *"**Just putting Cadbury in the response does not mean it is in context as Cadbury is in the question.**"*

**⭐ The examiner tip — the single best piece of technique in the bank:**

> *"**Try and avoid using the name of the scenario but include something that is related to the business. In this example perhaps including chocolate cake designers would put the response in context.**"*

> **Pearson is telling you to do the opposite of what most candidates do.** Do not write *"Cadbury"*; write *"chocolate cake designers"*, *"competitors like Lindt"*, *"the Bournville factory"*. **The business name is already printed on the question paper — it proves nothing. A specific detail proves you read the case.**

**Also from the report:** *"'State' questions are **AO2 (Application)** hence there must be some evidence of application or context within the response."*

## ⭐⭐ Q1(f) — EXPLAIN one advantage of a fall in interest rates — AO1, 3 marks

> **Question:** Explain **one** advantage… **Many candidates misread advantage for disadvantage.**

### ✅ 3 marks
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"If banks reduce their interest rates, business' Will not be giving as much money to the bank as they once did. This leads to the business saving money and instead of putting the money into the bank it can be used to help further improve their business therefore they may have more chance of Survival."*

**Examiner comment:** *"The response includes the **one advantage** they they are not going to be paying the bank as much. **The development** is leading to them saving money, **which could further improve their business**."*

### ⭐ 2 marks
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"They can pay their debts easier, as interest rates being decreased means they will have to pay less to borrow, which improves the liquidity of the business."*

**Examiner comment:** *"This particular response gained **2 marks**, the comment with reference to **paying less to borrow** this is then developed which **improves the cash flow** of the business. **The comment about paying their debts easier is not developed.**"*

**⭐ Examiner tip:** *"**To avoid wasting time do not repeat the question in the first sentence.**"*

> ### 🎯 The 3-mark answer vs the 2-mark answer
>
> Both candidates understood the advantage. **The difference is entirely in whether the final consequence lands.**
>
> **2-mark answer:** advantage (pay less to borrow) → development (improves cash flow). **Stops.**
> **3-mark answer:** advantage (not paying the bank as much) → development (saving money) → **consequence for the business (it can improve, more chance of survival)**.
>
> **The 3-mark Explain has three rungs: the point, the mechanism, the outcome for the business.** The 2-mark answer stops one rung short — and the examiner names the missing rung precisely.

**Also:** *"Candidates are reminded that the question asked for **one advantage not several advantages**."*

## ⭐⭐⭐ Q1(g) — ANALYSE two location factors — AO2 + AO3, **6 marks**

> **Question:** Analyse **two** factors that could influence *Cadbury* when locating a new factory.

**Report guidance — the clearest statement of the Analyse tariff anywhere:**

> *"'Analyse' questions are testing two of the Assessment Objectives, or AO's. Namely **AO2 which is 'Application' and AO3 which is 'Analysis'**. Thus, to be successful, candidates need to demonstrate **both** of these skills within their answer."*
>
> *"Unfortunately, many candidates tend to **describe** one factor of locating a new factory, **which is not answering the question**. Most candidates gained a Level 2 mark."*
>
> *"Examiners will look at the response **'holistically'**, considering: **have they applied their knowledge to the scenario** and **have they analysed the information**, they will then make a decision as to where the response **'best fits'** into the Levels."*
>
> *"Candidates must be aware that **there are no marks available for knowledge**, and that the response **must be in context of Cadbury**."*

### 🏆 Level 3 — **6 marks (FULL MARKS)** — 43 words, three sentences

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Proximity to employees This is because the factory being close to employees would reduce Cadbury's costs that would be used for transport. This leads to them being able to save time that would be used in the journey, therefore, Cadbury would increase their efficiency."*

**Examiner comment:** *"A well answered response, clearly identifying the two factors of location and giving justifiable reasons."*

> ### 🔴 STOP AND READ THIS AGAIN
>
> **A full-marks 6-mark Analyse answer is THREE SENTENCES.**
>
> It contains no introduction, no definition, no listing, no conclusion. It is:
> **factor (proximity to employees/labour) → why it cuts costs (transport) → what that does for Cadbury (saves time → raises efficiency).**
>
> **This settles the single biggest misconception about length on level-marked questions.** Candidates fail Analyse not by writing too little but by writing *descriptions*.
>
> **Pattern to copy:** `[Factor] → This is because [mechanism applied to the business] → This leads to [business outcome] → therefore [Cadbury] would [result].`

### ⚠️ Level 3 — **5 marks**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"A factor in opening a new factory is to locate it near its natural resources of the product the firm is producing, this causes less time to Produce the product with the firm Cadbury being really successful money limitations isn't an issue to opening new factories, Since Cadbury is a chocolate producing firm they should locate their factories in countries where cocoa seeds are highly common these."*

**Examiner comment:** *"This response shows a **clear understanding** of Cadbury locating near to where its natural resources are and **it is applied by mentioning cocoa seeds**. This is then developed with the comment about less time to produce… **However, the response did not gain full marks because it lacked the logical chains of reasoning.** Perhaps the candidate could have **linked the two factors together**, by moving near to supplies might be difficult to get employees to work at the factory, or locating near to a city provides workers who have the skills."*

> ### 🎯 WHAT SEPARATES 5 FROM 6
>
> The 5-mark answer has **better knowledge** and **better application** than the 6-mark answer — it names *cocoa seeds*, which is genuinely applied chocolate-industry detail.
>
> **It still lost a mark, for one reason: "it lacked the logical chains of reasoning."**
>
> **The fix the examiner suggests: link the two factors together.** Not "say more about factor one" — *connect* them. *"moving near to supplies might be difficult to get employees to work at the factory"* is a link between two location factors.
>
> **So on a two-part Analyse: do not treat the parts as two separate mini-answers. End with a sentence that joins them.**

## ⭐⭐ Q2(c) — EXPLAIN one method of motivating employees — **AO1**, 3 marks

> **Question:** Explain how a business could motivate its employees. *(one method only)*

**Report guidance:** *"An 'Explain' question which does **not** have to be in context of Cadbury. The Assessment Objective being tested is **AO1 (Knowledge)**."*
> *"for those candidates that did not get the full allocation of marks this might be because they **gave more than one method**."*

### ⭐ 2 marks
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"A business could use job rotation to motivate its employees. Job rotation will provide a change in the workplace of its employees, as a result it could motivate them."*

**Examiner comment:** *"The method of job rotation is valid and worthy of a mark, with **a justifiable reason** of giving employees a change in the workplace. **However, the response was not further developed to gain the extra mark available.**"*

### ✅ 3 marks
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"A business could use non financial methods to motivate employees This means that employees will be more challenging. This leads no the employees feeling trusted and valuable no the business as a large amount of work needed for the business is from them As a result the employee will feel wanted and also the business would be increasing its productivity."*

**Examiner comment:** *"This response gives a method of **Job Enrichment**, followed by **development** of employees being more challenged, together with employees feeling trusted and valued."*

> ### 🎯 THE 3-MARK EXPLAIN, DECOMPOSED
> | Rung | 2-mark answer | 3-mark answer |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Method named** | job rotation | job enrichment |
> | **Why it works** | *"provide a change in the workplace"* | *"employees will be more challenged… feeling trusted and valuable"* |
> | **Business outcome** | ❌ missing | ✅ *"the business would be increasing its productivity"* |
>
> ⭐ **Note both 3-mark answers in this paper end on a business outcome: productivity, satisfied customers, employee loyalty.** **The final rung is always *what the business gets*.**

**⭐ Note also:** the candidate's answer contains **grammatical errors** (*"This leads no the employees"*, *"valuable no the business"*) and still scored **3/3**. **Examiners mark business content, not spelling and grammar.** Do not lose time proofreading for style.
