# MAY 2023 PAPERS 2 AND 2R — four Explain answers that scored below full marks

**The four are every below-full-mark 3-mark script the May 2023 reports print.** They were found on 14 Oct 2026 by **`audit_below_fullmarks.py` (check 25)**, which asks the five archived reports for every below-full-mark script they print and compares the answer with the bank — the third class of printed script the bank had been reading without a guard.

**Between them they carry one question scored two ways and the single commonest way to lose a mark on `Explain one…`.**

⚠️ **Read the spread.** Three of the four sit on a page whose counterpart is the facing one; Paper 2's `Q1(f)` script is on report **p. 16** while its score line is on **p. 15** — the page that appears blank below the commentary is where the script lives.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/3** · P2R Q2(c) *Explain one reason why branding is important to businesses* — report p. 19

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):** *"**(c)** Explain **one** reason why branding is important to businesses."* **(3)**

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"Branding increases customer awareness about brand which means more customers will get attracted [to the] business a business can experience high profits."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"The candidate has identified the benefit of branding by increasing customer awareness and that customers will be attracted to the business. The comment about high profits is another reason and therefore not awarded any marks."*

🔬 **THREE LINKS — awareness → attraction → profit — AND THE THIRD ONE COSTS NOTHING AND EARNS NOTHING.**

The examiner pays **two marks** for a complete two-step chain: *increases customer awareness* (identification) → *more customers will get attracted* (development). **Then he says, in terms, that the last clause is a *different* reason and is therefore worth zero** — even though it is true, on-topic and businesslike.

⚠️ **This is the *"Explain **one**"* trap in its purest form: the question caps the answer at one reason, and a second reason cannot be absorbed into the first one's chain.** The candidate wrote a three-step chain and was paid for two of the steps, because the third step started a new argument rather than continuing the old one.

⭐ **The fix is a single word.** *"…more customers will get attracted to the business, **which means** it can experience high profits"* makes the profit clause the **consequence of the attraction** rather than a second reason — **and it is the same information.** ⚠️ **Nothing about the content needed to change; only the connector did.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/3** · P2R Q2(d) *Explain one way businesses ensure that all employees have equal opportunities* — report p. 20

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):** *"**(d)** Explain **one** way businesses ensure that all employees have equal opportunities."* **(3)**

**The script (verbatim — the crossed-out mark before "employees" is the candidate's own correction):**

> *"One way a business can ensure all the employees have equal opportunities is by having team meeting's and fixing or helping any issues the employees have. This could be offering a promotion or a new position if the employee wants more responsibility."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response shows an understanding of how a business could ensure that all employees have equal opportunities by having team meetings and this is then further developed by fixing any issues. The comment about offering promotion is another way and the question asks for one way only."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, printed above the script):** *"Another 'Explain' question which requires knowledge and does not need to be in the context of M&M. The question asked for one way businesses ensure that all employees have equal opportunities. This question proved quite difficult for a number of candidates with well over half of the cohort not gaining any marks on the question and only a tenth gaining the full marks available. Some candidates referred to equal opportunities as being methods of retaining employees such as job rotation. Again candidates should read the question through at least twice before attempting to answer it to ensure they know what they have to do."*

🔬 **THE SAME RULING, IN THE SAME WORDS, ONE QUESTION LATER — AND THIS TIME THE SECOND IDEA IS THE OBVIOUS ONE.**

*"The comment about offering promotion is another way and the question asks for one way only."* **The mark scheme's own content for this question is not printed in the report's summary — but whatever it is, the examiner has already paid the candidate for *team meetings → fixing issues*, and promotion cannot be added to it.**

⚠️ **Both 2/3s in this file are the same error with different words: *another reason* (p. 19) and *another way* (p. 20).** Two candidates, two questions, one ruling — **and both answers read as *more thorough* than a 3/3 would.**

⭐ **The cohort data makes this the most expensive habit on the paper:** *"well over half of the cohort not gaining any marks… only a tenth gaining the full marks available."*

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **0/3** · P2R Q2(d), the same question — report p. 21

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"They work on businesses and depend on salary. They also have other needs such as promotion, Job opportunities and Job enrichment."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response does not answer the question and therefore did not gain a mark."*

🔬 **⭐ THE SHARPEST ZERO IN THE TIER — AND THE ONE THAT TEACHES THE MOST ABOUT WHAT AN ANSWER IS.**

**Look at what this script contains: three correct syllabus terms — *promotion*, *job opportunities*, *job enrichment* — used with their right meanings.** It would pass as "knowledge" in a conversation about motivation. **It scores nothing, and the examiner's whole comment is seven words: *"does not answer the question."***

⚠️ **What the question asked for is a WAY a business ENSURES equal opportunities.** The script says what employees *want* — *"they also have other needs"* — which is a different question entirely (*how do you motivate staff?*), and the first sentence is about employees as a category rather than about anything a business *does*.

⭐ **Put next to the 2/3 on the facing page, the pair is the tier's clearest calibration:** the 2/3 answers the question and adds an unnecessary second way; **the 0/3 answers a question nobody asked.** *Both are about equal opportunities and employee needs.* **The difference is not knowledge, length or English — it is whether the sentence is doing the job the command word set.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/3** · P2 Q1(f) *Explain one reason why businesses want to use resources effectively in manufacturing* — report pp. 15–16

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script, p. 16):** *"**(f)** Explain **one** reason why businesses want to use resources effectively in manufacturing."* **(3)**

**The script (verbatim — a struck-through draft sits before *"left our materials"*):**

> *"One reason whis is because if resources are left our materials can be reused this helps the business save a bit of money."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim, p. 16):** *"In this response the candidate clearly understood the benefits of using left over material that can be reused. However there was **limited development** of the response and therefore only gained two marks."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 15):** *"This is the first of the 'Explain' questions. The question requires candidates to explain 'one reason …'. A number of candidates misread the question and gave several reasons why businesses would want to use resources effectively in manufacturing. Candidates must give a reason and then the explanation to gain the full 3 marks available. Nearly half of the cohort were able to gain the full marks available."*

🔬 **THE FOURTH MARK-LOSING PATTERN, AND THE ONLY ONE THAT IS ABOUT DEPTH RATHER THAN DISCIPLINE.**

> **reason** (leftover resources → materials can be reused) → **consequence** (saves money) → **…and stops.**

**The examiner credits what is there — *"clearly understood the benefits"* — and names the gap with the bank's own term: *"limited development."*** The chain reaches *"save a bit of money"*, which is a business outcome, **but it is a hedged, small one — *"a bit of"*.** ⚠️ **Compare the 3/3s in this same tier: their last link is *increased sales*, *less wastage*, *positive cashflow* — a specific named result.** *"Saves money"* is where a Level-2 chain terminates; **a 3/3 says what the money saves *for*.**

⚠️ **And note the cohort commentary repeats the *"one reason"* rule as the paper's own headline finding: *"A number of candidates misread the question and gave several reasons… Candidates must give a reason and then the explanation to gain the full 3 marks available."*** **Three of the four scripts in this file are the same ruling from three directions.**

# WHAT THESE FOUR ESTABLISH

1. **⭐⭐ On `Explain one…`, a second reason is a CAP, not extra credit.** The examiner says it twice in two questions: *"another reason… not awarded any marks"* (p. 19) · *"another way and the question asks for one way only"* (p. 20). **Neither answer was penalised for being wrong — both were penalised for being right twice.**
2. **⚠️ The connector is what decides whether the extra clause counts.** *"…more customers attracted… **a** business can experience high profits"* = two reasons (2/3). *"…more customers attracted, **which means** it can experience high profits"* = one chain (3/3). **Same facts, different conjunction.**
3. **⚠️ Length is not the variable.** The 0/3 and one 2/3 are the *longest* answers of the four. **The two shortest readings — the ones the examiner calls complete — score highest.**
4. **⭐ Vocabulary is not an answer.** The 0/3 names *promotion*, *job opportunities* and *job enrichment* correctly and scores zero because none of them is doing what the command word asked.
5. **⚠️ "Limited development" is the fourth failure mode and the only subjective one.** Its test, from this tier's own 3/3s: **does the last link name a result — *increased sales*, *less waste*, *positive cashflow* — or just *"save a bit of money"?***
