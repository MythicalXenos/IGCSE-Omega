# VERIFIED EXEMPLARS — 6 marks (Analyse)

## ✅ 6/6 · Q1(g) Analyse the impact of external factors on business decisions made by TBTS

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"External [factors], such as customer demand[, are] likely to impact TBTS' decisions as depending on how high customer demand is, they will need to make the effective decision as [to] how many children's play items to produce. If they produce too many toys, then TBTS will lose money as they won't sell what they have produced resulting in loss of profit and money wasted.*
>
> *Another external factor, like the environment[,] may affect their decisions. This is because TBTS may want to be environmentally friendly to attract more customers. If global warming is increased, laws and regulations may be put in place to protect the world. Meaning that TBTS won't have the freedom to produce in [a] certain way. For example factories may be shut down due to pollution rates and TBTS will have to change the way they produce kid's play time toys."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"Candidates chose to take many routes to answer this question and could bring in their knowledge of the subject… This candidate has chosen customer demand and the environment to analyse the likely impact on business decisions made by TBTS. This candidate demonstrates a response where clear context has been provided linked to the given business scenario."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim):**
> *"Centres should encourage candidates to include context in 'analyse' questions to ensure they can reach the top levels of this levels based question."*

> **Important cohort note:** *"Candidates must be reminded that **there are no AO1 marks for this question, there are AO2 marks** for this level-based question as often candidates would provide an extended answer but not apply this answer to the context of TBTS therefore not allowing themselves to move into the top of level 2. **Most marks gained by candidates were within the level 2 band** for this particular question."*

### Two chains, both anchored

| Chain | Steps |
|---|---|
| 1 | customer demand → how many to produce → too many toys → unsold stock → **loss of profit, money wasted** |
| 2 | environment → want to appear environmentally friendly → global warming → laws/regulations → factories shut down for pollution → **change how they produce toys** |

Both chains terminate in a **consequence for TBTS**. Neither defines anything. This is the same technique as the verified 6/6 on profitability ratios — *do not describe the factor; follow it through to its effect*.

## ✅ 6/6 · Q3(d) Analyse the importance of TBTS motivating its employees

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"One reason [why] it is important TBTS motivates its employees is to ensure they are efficient and appeal to customers. The employees at TBTS's 3 stores in Singapore must stay motivated in order to ensure their customers have a good experience when they enter the store, and in order to maintain customer loyalty through building a good relationship with the shop by being impressed by employees['] responsiveness. Hence, in order to retain customer loyalty it is important that employees stay motivated in all their stores.*
>
> *Another reason TBTS should motivate their employees is to ensure that they don't lose their employees and have employee retention. If workers don't feel satisfied in their jobs they may want to leave, wh[ich] may cause an issue for TBTS as they won't have sufficient people to help run their business, wh[ich] may lead to business failure. So in order to ensure that employees don't leave the business, TBTS must keep them motivated so the employees are satisfied with their jobs and continue to work across their stores in Singapore."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This candidate shows how we expect candidates to respond to an 'analyse' question. This response shows detailed application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts **with context throughout** as well as interconnected points with logical chains of reasoning.*
>
> *This response shows that the candidate has thought about **the positives of TBTS motivating its employees and the implications of not motivating its employees**."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim):**
> *"Unless responses for 'analyse' questions are in the context of the given business scenario candidates will find it difficult to access to higher marks."*

### The technique to copy

Chain 2 is run **in reverse** — the candidate argues from the *absence* of motivation:
```
workers not satisfied → may leave → insufficient staff to run business → business failure
```
Same content, framed negatively. This doubles your usable material on any analyse question: for every point you can argue *"and if they did not, then…"*.

**Context anchors used:** "TBTS's **3 stores in Singapore**", "**customers** have a good experience when they enter the store", "continue to work **across their stores in Singapore**". The figure **3** and the location come from the case.

# VERIFIED EXEMPLARS — 3 marks (Explain)

## ✅ 3/3 · Q2(c) Explain one benefit of a flat organisational structure

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"A flat organisation structure has a short chain of command. This is usually less formal and employees can feel more at ease in the work place. This may increase productivity as well as motivation in the employees. The employees will then work harder, increasing the business' output."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"A good response which shows a clear benefit of '…has a short chain of command…' This candidate has then been able to expand upon this benefit and draw out why this is a benefit. **This particular candidate has explained this benefit with more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time when there are only 3 marks available.**"*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim):**
> *"Centres are reminded to refer back to the mark scheme to how explain questions should be answered. **No context is required for explain questions but time and time again we see candidates applying context unnecessarily.**"*

### ⚠️ A rare warning in the *other* direction

Every other report warns candidates to write *more*. This one warns a full-mark candidate to write *less*: the chain ran past the two development marks available, wasting exam time.

**Both rules coexist:**
- **3-mark explain:** identify (1) + exactly **two** developments (2). Then **stop**.
- **6/9/12-mark:** keep chaining — depth is what earns the levels.

Knowing **when to stop** is itself an exam skill, and this is the only place in the whole bank where the examiner flags it.

## ✅ 3/3 · Q2(d) Explain one method a small business might use to increase its profit

> *"Finding a new supplier who will sell materials cheaper. This means reduced cost of production, so more gain from profit, as revenue increases."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim) — this is the most precise mark-by-mark breakdown in the entire bank:**
> *"A good response which is concise and to the point. This candidate correctly identifies **find a new supplier who will sell materials cheaper** as a method a small business might use to increase its profit. This candidate has then successfully explained this method by stating **'…means reduced cost of production…' gaining a second mark** and gaining the final mark for **'…more gain from profit, as revenue increases.'*** **If the candidate omitted the final three words then this response would've gained just two marks** but adding in that profit would only increase as revenue increases seals the final mark."*

### 🎯 "the final three words"

The examiner states that deleting three words — *"as revenue increases"* — would have dropped this from 3/3 to 2/3.

| Mark | Text | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | "Finding a new supplier who will sell materials cheaper" | identifies the method |
| **2** | "This means reduced cost of production" | explains *how* it helps |
| **3** | "…as revenue increases" | links to the **financial outcome** |

**The third mark is the business consequence.** Not more detail about suppliers — the *result*. This is exactly what the June 2023 P2 Q1(f) 2/3 response was missing (*"helps the business save a bit of money"* → stopped short of profit), and what the 3/3 response there supplied (*"→ higher value and more profit"*).

**Practical rule for every 3-marker:** identify → how → **what it does to the business's money**. Three steps, then stop.

# VERIFIED EXEMPLARS — 9 marks (Justify)

## ✅ 9/9 · Q2(f) Justify: retained profit or bank loan to fund selling a wider variety of toys

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"If TBTS chooses to use retained profit in order to finance its buying of new toys such as cars, it can quickly order [the] toys as it[']s a quick and easy process, which means the new ordered toys (cars and barbies) will be marketed before the rivals start introducing their products[,] which means they will get more revenue and have a higher market share than other toy shops in the shopping mall. Secondly, TBTS will not need to pay interests or regular instalments, which in turn will mean their cash flow will be stable… If TBTS chooses to get a bank loan they will be able to reach the adequate amount needed to finance the buying of new toys. Secondly, bank loans are a long-term [source] of finance therefore TBTS will hold on to its cash made for longer, which will improve their cash flow.*
>
> *[Retained] profit it will be an opportunity cost as after usage it can't be used again. Which is a bad thing as if there is a [slow] day TBTS will not be able to respond fast[]ly. Also, retained profit may not be adequate as TBTS is only a small business, therefore it can't finance the toys TBTS wants to buy. On the other hand, if TBTS chooses a bank loan, it will be obliged to pay interests, which might ruin their cash flow. Secondly, a bank loan may need collateral, so TBTS may [risk] losing its most valuable assets like its company car."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows what is expected from candidates in order to gain the full marks for this type of 'justify' question. This candidate has shown detailed understanding of the choice they have made, they have included context throughout their response with logical chains of reasoning and has made a good judgement about their choice with a thorough evaluation."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim) — the third independent statement of the 9-mark rule:**
> *"**Candidates do not need to consider both options.** Candidates need to make a clear choice out of the two options and be able to balance out their response as to why the business should or should not choose that option. Candidates are then expected to provide a clear overall judgement to evaluate their decision."*

> **Cohort note on this question — worth reading as a target:** *"Those candidates who gained no marks on this response simply showed knowledge of one or both options with no application, analysis or evaluation. Candidates working at level 1 showed good knowledge of both options… Around half of candidates accessed level 2 by expanding on their answers but **often missed balance and a conclusion to their thoughts**. More able candidates accessed level 3 by considering their chosen option carefully in the context of TBTS and how this would impact their financial position, **linking their points together and concluding their answer with balance and justification**."*

### Two techniques visible here

**(1) Concrete named products.** *"new toys such as **cars**"*, *"**(cars and barbies)**"*. These come from the case and are the context anchors that earn AO2.

**(2) Arguing both sides of the *same* option.** The candidate weighs retained profit *against* the bank loan — but note this is not "covering both options" in the sense of splitting the answer. It uses the rejected option as the counterweight, exactly as the verified 9/9 in `../02_Verified_Exemplars.md` did.
