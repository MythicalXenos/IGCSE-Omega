# ⚠️ THE FORMULA WARNING — check what is actually given

> **Q4(a):** *"a calculation question which has been well answered by over a third of candidates. **As the break-even formula is not given in the paper it is one that candidate need to know.** This question showed that **not all candidates had an understanding of how to calculate the break-even point** for Loaf."*

### 🎯 The formulae sheet is *not* a complete list

This report resolves a genuine ambiguity in Pearson's own guidance.

| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| Pearson support page | *"some formulas are **provided** at the front of the paper"* |
| Nov 2020 P1 | *"some formulas are provided at the front of the paper, whilst most candidates did use these **there are still some that are not**"* |
| **Nov 2020 P1R (this report)** | *"**As the break-even formula is not given in the paper it is one that candidates need to know.**"* |

**So: the formula sheet covers some formulae; break-even is explicitly NOT on it.**

> **Memorise these outright — do not rely on the sheet:**
> **Break-even output = Total fixed costs ÷ Contribution per unit**
> **Contribution per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit**
> **Total costs = Fixed costs + Variable costs**
> **Profit = Revenue − Total costs**

**And a second, subtler warning:** *"over a third of candidates"* answered it well. **Under half.** A single memorised formula would have moved the majority of the cohort up.

### The two definition confusions — both neighbour-term errors

> **1(b):** *"about two thirds… correctly define the term **capital intensive production**. Some candidates did not gain the mark as they **confused this with labour intensive production**."*
>
> **1(c):** *"just over half… were able to correctly define the term **quantitative data**. Some candidates **confused this with qualitative data** thus gaining no marks."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Capital intensive** | production relying **mainly on machinery** — e.g. soft drinks |
| **Labour intensive** | production relying **mainly on workers** — e.g. clothing |
| **Quantitative data** | data that is **numerical** and can be analysed statistically |
| **Qualitative data** | data about **opinions and feelings**, which *"cannot easily be converted to data"* |

**Sixth and seventh neighbour-term errors in this bank.** The pattern is consistent: candidates know the topic but not the *distinguishing feature*.

> **The one-line fix:** before writing a definition, ask **"what does this have that the other one does not?"** For capital intensive, the answer is **machinery**. Put that word in the sentence.

### ⭐ Q1(e) — the step-counting error, 7th instance

> *"over two thirds of candidates gained full marks for the correct calculation of **revenue for 2017 with an increase of 3% from 2016**. **Some candidates worked out the 3% increase but did not go on to add this to the revenue of 2016 thus only gaining one mark.** It is good practice for candidates to show working out as they could gain a mark for substituting numbers into the formula even if their final answer is incorrect."*

**Work out the increase, forget to add it.** Identical to Nov 2020 P1 Q4(a) (2.5% increase to minimum wage) and Nov 2020 P1 Q3(c) (net cash flow instead of closing balance).

> **This is now SEVEN recorded instances across five papers.** It is the most repeated calculation error in the bank — more common than the decimal-place error.
>
> **The step-counting drill:** name the **final** thing asked for out loud ("revenue **for 2017**"), then name every operation needed to reach it. **An "increase of 3%" is an input, not an answer.**

### ⭐ Q1(f) — the listing cap, 5th confirmation

### Q1(g) — a content gap: environmental factors

> *"candidates showed a **lack of knowledge of environmental factors** that a business could face. Candidates must be reminded that **there are AO2 marks for this level-based question**… often candidates would provide an extended answer but not apply this answer to the context of Loaf therefore **not allowing themselves to move into the top of level 2**."*

### 📘 ENVIRONMENTAL FACTORS — a named spec point (1.7 External factors)

*"Environmental factors"* in the 4BS1 sense means the **natural environment and sustainability pressures** on a business, not "the environment it operates in". Learn a short list:

- **Climate and weather** — affects supply, seasonality, transport
- **Sustainability and carbon** — regulations, carbon taxes, emissions targets
- **Waste and packaging** — single-use plastics, disposal costs, consumer pressure
- **Raw material availability and sourcing** — sustainable/ethical supply
- **Energy costs and renewables** — rising utility bills, investment in efficiency
- **Reputation and consumer sentiment** — customers boycotting polluters

> **Apply it to Loaf:** *"If **Loaf** has to pay a levy on **non-recyclable bakery packaging**, its **costs rise on every loaf** — so switching to compostable wrapping protects its margin **and** its reputation with the **local, environmentally conscious customers** the report mentions."*

### ⭐ 2(e) — HALF the cohort scored zero on JIT

> *"**half of the candidates demonstrated a lack of knowledge of just in time method of stock control and therefore gained no marks for this question.** One fifth of candidates gained full marks and correctly identified a way in which just in time method of stock control might benefit a business and then developing the answer."*

**One in five full marks; one in two zero.** The knowledge is either there or it is not.

> **JIT — the complete, examinable version.**
>
> **What it is:** holding **as little stock as possible**, with materials arriving **just as they are needed for production**.
>
> **Benefits:** *"lower warehousing costs"*; *"improved liquidity since cash is no longer tied up in stock"*; less **wastage/spoilage** (crucial for a **bakery** — bread is perishable); less risk of **obsolescence**.
>
> **Drawbacks:** *"cannot cope with surges in demand"*; *"requires very strong supplier relationships"*; **a supply delay halts production entirely**.
>
> **Best used:** *"in a dynamic market where inventory depreciates rapidly"* — **which is exactly Loaf's situation: fresh bread and cakes.**

> **Apply it to Loaf:** *"Using JIT means **Loaf** only takes delivery of flour and ingredients as each batch of bread is baked, so it is not paying to store stock or throwing away **unsold perishable loaves** — which improves its **cash flow** and reduces **waste costs**."*

### 2(c) — the listing cap, 6th confirmation, and the Explain reminder

> *"over one third of candidates showed a good understanding of e-newsletters and how they can benefit a business. **Some candidates listed three benefits to a business of using e-newsletters with no explanation thus limiting themselves to one mark.** A reminder that **an 'explain' question does not need to be in context**."*

### 2(d) — a content gap in HR

> *"almost half of candidates gained 2 or more marks… **Unfortunately, over one third of candidates gained no marks on this question due to a lack of knowledge of the human resources function.**"*

> **The HR function** — recruitment and selection · training and development · **motivation and rewards** · **employee welfare** · discipline and grievance · contracts and legal compliance (health & safety, equality) · workforce planning (staffing levels, redundancies).

### 2(f) — Justify, and the closing advice

> *"Where candidates gained no marks on this question **there was no evidence of application, analysis or evaluation and they have simply shown knowledge of the two promotional activities**. Candidates working at level 1 showed good knowledge of both options, making statements on one or both options with some relation to Loaf but this was not always expanded upon. More able candidates accessed level 3 by considering their chosen option in the context of Loaf and **how this would impact on the customer numbers for its bakery and cookery course**, linking their points together and concluding their answer with balance and justification."*

**Note the case-specific outcome the examiner names: *"customer numbers for its bakery and cookery course."*** Not "sales" — **the actual revenue lines of this business.**

### ⭐ Q3(a) — only a fifth could define job share

> *"**only one fifth of the candidates were able to correctly define the term job share.** Candidates have often **confused this term [with] other types of employment**."*

### 📘 THE EMPLOYMENT PATTERNS — the full set, kept apart

| Term | What it actually means |
|---|---|
| **Job share** | **One full-time job is split between two (or more) people**, who each work **part of the hours** — e.g. 2.5 days each. |
| **Part-time** | One person works **fewer hours than a full-time contract**. |
| **Zero-hours contract** | The employee is **not guaranteed any hours**; work is offered as needed. |
| **Casual / seasonal** | Employed **short-term for peak periods** (e.g. Christmas). |
| **Flexible hours / flexitime** | Full-time hours, but **the employee chooses when** to work them within limits. |
| **Temporary / fixed-term** | A **contract with an end date**. |

> **The distinguishing feature of job share:** **the job itself is divided between people** — not the *hours* of one person, and not the *duration* of the contract. **Two people, one job.** That is the sentence that earns the mark.

**Eighth neighbour-term confusion in this bank.**

### Q3(b) — Outline and the context requirement

> *"those candidates who gained full marks on this question were able to **outline a reason why higher levels of customer satisfaction might be important to Loaf and develop their answer all in the context of Loaf**. **Candidates who do not use the context of the given business are not able to access the full marks.**"*

**An Outline (2 marks) is AO2 — Application.** One reference to context is required. Point **(1)** + development *and* context **(1)**.

### ⭐ Q3(c) — step-counting, 8th instance

> *"Those candidates who calculated the **operating profit** were then able to successfully calculate the **operating profit margin** thus gaining the full 2 marks available. **Some candidates only calculated the operating profit figure therefore limiting themselves to 1 mark.**"*

**Two operations were required; half the work earned half the marks.**

### Q3(d) — Analyse: the Level 2 ceiling once more

> *"one fifth of candidates did not gain any marks due to a lack of knowledge of the operating profit margin and the usefulness of the figures provided. **Where candidates showed no application to Loaf or any analytical points did not gain any marks.** Those who accessed level 3 **carefully considered how the information presented would or would not be useful to Loaf**. Candidates were disadvantaged when they did not apply their answer to Loaf as **they cannot access the top of level 2 without any application**."*

**Note the Level 3 wording: *"would or would not be useful."*** Even on a 6-mark Analyse — **no AO4** — the examiner's description of the best answers contains a **two-sided assessment of usefulness**. That is what *"detailed"* application looks like in practice.

### Q3(e) — the Justify route, templated

> *"Some candidates who gained no marks on this response simply showed knowledge of one or both options with no application, analysis or evaluation… More able candidates accessed level 3 by considering their chosen option in the context of Loaf and how this would impact the business, they have linked their points together and **concluded their answer with balance and justification**."*

# Question 4

| Part | Command | Topic | Cohort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4(a)** | **Calculate** | break-even point | **"over a third"** — formula NOT given |
| **4(b)** | Analyse (6) | benefits of employing casual staff | see below |
| **4(c)** | **Evaluate (12)** | factors for locating a second bakery | *"over one third… level 2"* |

### Q4(b) — the context failure, stated once more

> *"Those candidates who analysed the benefits of [employing casual] staff **did not apply it to Loaf therefore could not access the top of level 2**. Those who accessed level 3 did so by detailed analysis of the benefits of employing casual staff **in relation to Loaf and the day to day running of the business**."*

> **Note the phrase: "the day to day running of the business."** Application does not have to be a number or a proper noun — it can be **an operational reality of this business**. For a bakery: *"casual staff can be called in for the **early-morning bake** when a large order comes in."*

### Q4(c) — Evaluate: the two reasons, and a new failure mode

> *"Those who performed less well than expected on this question did so for **one of two reasons**.*
> *Firstly, candidates provided a **generic answer with no application to Loaf**, or candidates would **go into detail outlining a number of factors with no real application to the given situation**.*
> *Secondly, candidates did not provide a **thorough judgment** relating back to any issues that may occur for Loaf when considering the location for its second bakery, or they **did not balance their conclusion** therefore limiting their evaluation."*

**The second failure mode is worth isolating:**

> *"go into detail outlining **a number of factors** with **no real application** to the given situation"*

**A list of location factors — cost of land, proximity to market, labour supply, transport links — earns nothing on a 12-marker.** This is the **listing cap arriving at the top of the paper**, and it is now recorded in **three** reports (also Nov 2020 P1 Q4(c), Nov 2021 P1 Q4(c)).
