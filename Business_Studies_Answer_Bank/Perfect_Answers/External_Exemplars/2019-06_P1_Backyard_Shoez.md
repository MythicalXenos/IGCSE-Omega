### 📘 HOW TO INCREASE GROSS PROFIT MARGIN — the content gap

Gross profit margin = (gross profit ÷ revenue) × 100. To raise it you must either **raise revenue without raising cost of sales** or **lower cost of sales**.

| Route | How |
|---|---|
| **Raise selling price** | *"charge more per pair of shoes"* — margin rises if costs are unchanged, **but demand may fall** |
| **Find cheaper suppliers** | *"source shoes from a lower-cost manufacturer"* — direct cut to cost of sales |
| **Buy in bulk** | bulk discounts **lower unit cost**, so each sale keeps more profit |
| **Reduce wastage / returns** | fewer damaged or unsold shoes means **less cost per unit sold** |
| **Change the product mix** | **push the high-margin lines** — handbags beside shoes |

> ⭐**The 3-mark shape:** identify the method **(1)** → explain the mechanism **(1)** → land the effect on margin **(1)**.
> *"Backyard Shoez could **negotiate bulk discounts with the supplier (1)**, which **lowers the cost of each pair of shoes it buys (1)**, so **the gap between the selling price and cost of sales widens and the gross profit margin rises (1)**."*

### 📘 PROMOTION — above the line vs below the line

**Above the line** = **paid, mass-media advertising**, where you pay an agency or media owner and cannot measure exactly who responded:

| Method | Note |
|---|---|
| **Television** | wide reach, high cost |
| **Radio** | local reach, cheaper |
| **Newspapers / magazines** | targeted by readership |
| **Billboards / posters** | location-based, good for local shops |
| **Cinema** | younger audiences |
| **Internet display / social media ads** | now typically counted here |

**Below the line** = **everything else, targeted and measurable**:

| Method | Note |
|---|---|
| **Sales promotions** | discounts, 2-for-1, coupons |
| **Direct mail / email** | addressed to individuals |
| **Sponsorship** | e.g. sponsoring a local sports team |
| **Public relations / press releases** | earned, not paid-for coverage |
| **Point-of-sale displays** | in-store promotion |
| **Loyalty schemes** | repeat-customer rewards |

> **The 4BS1 examiner's distinction that matters: above the line = MASS MEDIA you PAY FOR.** Anything you can aim at a named individual, or that involves a discount or a prize, is **below the line**.

### ⭐ Q3(b) — an OFFICIAL 2-MARK MODEL, with the marks shown

The question asks for **an impact of an increase in interest rates on Backyard Shoez**. The report explains the failure:

> *"This question **does need to be in the context of Backyard Shoez to gain the full marks**. Some candidates went on to **explain what interest rates are**, the question is asking candidates for an **impact** as **there are no knowledge marks available**."*

**And the mark scheme supplies a worked answer** (in `By_Paper/4BS1_May-June_2019_P1.md`):

> **Award 1 mark for identifying an impact of an increase in interest rates on *Backyard Shoez*. One further mark for outlining it in the context of the business.**
>
> > *"Backyard Shoez could see a decrease in sales of shoes **(1)** because their customers who have debts have less income to spend on luxury items because they are paying more back to lenders **(1)**."*
>
> **NB Do not accept a reason that is not in context of *Backyard Shoez*.**

> ### 🏆 THIS IS A GENUINE OFFICIAL TWO-MARK MODEL
> Not a constructed answer — **Pearson's own words, with the mark boundaries printed in.** Every 2-mark Outline in the bank should be built to this shape:
>
> **Mark 1 = the impact, named and applied to the business.**
> **Mark 2 = the outline — a chain that explains how it happens, still applied.**
>
> **And the NB is absolute: no context, no mark — either mark.**

### ⭐ Q4(a) — a NEW error type: working it out backwards

> *"Centres should encourage candidates to **include their working out as often candidates gained a mark for their calculation despite their final answer being incorrect**. Candidates should also be encouraged to **double check their working out** as **some candidates calculated their working the wrong way around on this question thus not gaining any marks**."*

**Backyard Shoez bought children's shoes for 2,400 Ks and sold them for 3,200 Ks.**

> **Markup = (selling price − cost) ÷ COST × 100 = 800 ÷ 2,400 = 33.33%**
> **Margin = (selling price − cost) ÷ SELLING PRICE × 100 = 800 ÷ 3,200 = 25%**
>
> **"The wrong way around" = dividing by the wrong base.** The **cost** is the denominator for markup; the **selling price** is the denominator for margin.
>
> **Memory hook: mark**up asks *"how much did I add on top of what I paid?"* → **divide by cost.** **Margin** asks *"what slice of the sale price is profit?"* → **divide by price.**

### ⭐⭐ Q4(b) — the "link it to other information" technique

> *"**a poorly answered question due to the lack of knowledge of profitability ratios**, this has led to **one third of candidates not gaining any marks**. Those candidates who analysed the usefulness of profitability ratios often **did not apply it to Backyard Shoez therefore could not access the top of level 2**. Those candidates who accessed level 3 did so by **detailed analysis of the usefulness of profitability ratios in relation to Backyard Shoez with some candidates linking it to other information given about the business throughout the paper**."*

> ### 🎯 THE CROSS-PAPER LINKAGE TECHNIQUE
> The best candidates did not confine an answer to the numbers in the question. They **drew on information printed elsewhere in the paper** — other paragraphs of the case study, other questions' data — to strengthen the application.
>
> **Why this is a Grade 9 habit:** it is *impossible* on a knowledge-only answer. It demonstrates that you have actually read and understood **the whole case study**, which is precisely what AO2 rewards.
>
> **Habit:** before starting Question 4, skim back over Questions 1–3 and note **one extra fact** the paper gave you that you could use.

### ⭐⭐ Q4(c) — the 12-marker: every rule, in the examiner's words

> *"centres are reminded that this is **the only level-based question in the paper which is equally weighted across all four assessment objectives**."*
>
> *"**Answers were varied and one third of candidates accessed level 2** on this question."*
>
> *"Candidates at **level 1** made some attempt at the question and [were] able to make **basic statements about primary market research often without any reference to Backyard Shoez** or any development of their answer."*
>
> *"Those candidates who accessed **level 3** considered the importance of primary market research **in the context of Backyard Shoez** and **how the business can use primary market research**. Candidates have **successfully linked their points together in a logical order** and been able to **conclude their answer with a well balance and justified argument**."*
>
> *"Those who performed less well than expected on this question did so for **one of two reasons**.*
> **Firstly**, candidates provided a **generic answer with no application to Backyard Shoez** or candidates would **go into detail outlining a number of primary market research methods**.
> **Secondly**, candidates did **not provide a thorough judgment** relating back to any issues that may occur for Backyard Shoez if they were to use primary market research or they **did not balance their conclusion** therefore limiting their evaluation."*

> **These "two reasons" are repeated almost word-for-word in Nov 2020 P1 and Nov 2020 P1R.** They are the fixed diagnosis for 12-mark underperformance across the whole qualification.
>
> **Reason 1 = no application (or worse: a LIST of methods).**
> **Reason 2 = no thorough judgement, or an unbalanced conclusion.**
>
> **Note what is NOT on the list: not knowing enough about the topic.**
