## The case study

*Spud Bros* sells hot baked potatoes. It has **millions of followers on social media** and *"customers travel from all over the world to taste its famous potatoes."* The brothers **Jacob and Harley** want to expand throughout the UK and to other countries. It takes part in **charity events** supporting the local community and **sick children across the UK**, and **every Friday provides free meals for homeless people in the local area**.

# ⭐⭐⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **11/12 — 12-mark Evaluate** (full transcript)

**Q4(c)** — *"Evaluate the extent to which social objectives may contribute to the success of Spud Bros. You should use the information provided as well as your own knowledge of business."* **(12)**

> **Social objectives are aims to improve human or environmental well-being**, and Spud Bros could build a better brand image and attract investors by having social objectives.
>
> Firstly, Spud Bros takes part in different charity events to support the local community and help sick children. This kind act could build a better brand image of Spud Bros. Their kind act of charity or providing **free baked potatoes to the poor** could be spread throughout **social media platforms like Tiktok or Instagram**, people who received their help would also comment online, which builds a good reputation of Spud Bros and allows the public to easily **recognise Spud Bros from other potato businesses**, and increases their **competitiveness and sales** as people would be interested in purchasing Spud Bro's potatoes as they appreciate their kind act and charity work.
>
> Moreover, Spud Bros could **attract new investors** by having these social objectives. Some large business also with social objectives like **Red Cross** may like to support Spud Bros to continue to take part in charity events and support those in need by investing Spud Bros, which increases Spud Bro's **source of finance**, where they could have more money to recruit more well-trained staff, invest in new toppings or to advertise on TV, or reduce their cost as they provide free meals for homeless. People may also be more willing to contribute in the **crowdfunding** and support this kind business to expand and help more people, allowing Spud Bros to gain more revenue.
>
> **However**, as they provide free meals each Friday to the homeless, it may cause a **huge increase their cost** significantly, as they need to purchase many potatoes or toppings like vegetables to make the meals. This huge cost causes Spud Bros to **gain less profit** which do not benefit Spud Bros from **expanding globally**. Workers may also have an **increase in workload** as they do not just need to produce potatoes to be sold, but also to make free meals each Friday, which may lead to **demotivation** as they get tired.
>
> **In conclusion**, the social objectives could bring more benefits to Spud Bros as it benefits it to have a better reputation, attracting customers to recognise and support their business, raising sales, also attracting new investors and receiving more finance. **Although** it causes a huge cost to provide free meal, the increase in sales due to support of customers may over the cost in long term and **although** it adds workload for workers, they may also feel motivated as they take part in doing charity, **thus being more motivated to work with more productivity**.

### Examiner comment — verbatim
> *"This response shows that the candidate understands how social objectives have contributed to the success of Spud Bros. **They offer balance in their argument with appropriate levels of context throughout the response.** Total: **11 marks**"*

> ### 🔬 Why 11, and why this is the best 12-mark model in the bank
> | AO | What the script does |
> |---|---|
> | **AO1** | Opens with the definition — *"aims to improve human or environmental well-being"* |
> | **AO2** | **Every paragraph is anchored in Spud Bros**: free baked potatoes · Friday free meals for the homeless · sick children · TikTok/Instagram · *"other potato businesses"* · expanding globally · crowdfunding |
> | **AO3** | Full chains, each **ending on a business outcome**: charity → social media → reputation → *"recognise Spud Bros from other potato businesses"* → competitiveness → sales · investors → finance → staff/new toppings/TV advertising |
> | **AO4** | A genuine *"However"* paragraph (cost of free meals, worker workload → demotivation) **and a conclusion that concedes and then outweighs** — *"Although it causes a huge cost… the increase in sales… may over the cost in long term"* |
>
> ### ⭐ And note the two things it does NOT do
> 1. **No "it depends" formula.** The weighing is explicit — *"Although X… Y is bigger because…"* — rather than a conditional clause. **Outweighing is what earns AO4, not a magic phrase.**
> 2. **No bullet points, no headings.** Continuous prose throughout.
>
> ### ⭐⭐ The AO3 chain-craft is the transferable skill
> Look at the density of the fourth sentence of paragraph 2. One chain: *charity → posted on TikTok/Instagram → recipients comment online → good reputation → public recognises them **from other potato businesses** → competitiveness → sales.* **Six links, and the context is inside the chain, not bolted on.** That is what *"interconnected, logical"* means in the Level 3 descriptor.

# ⭐⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **8/9 — a 9-mark Justify that loses only on its conclusion** (full transcript)

**Q2(f)** — *"The brothers, Jacob and Harley, want to expand Spud Bros throughout the UK and to other countries. They are considering two types of finance: **Option 1: venture capital · Option 2: crowdfunding.** Justify which **one** of these two options the brothers should choose."* **(9)**

> **Crowdfunding is when people donate for a reason such as~~/~~help expanding a business.**
>
> One advantage of crowdfunding is that **you don't need to pay interest**. This means that because the people are giving the money ~~will as~~ free will they don't get paid interest **[or anything back]**. This leads to less [sums] needing to be payed by Spud Bros and therefore leads to Spud Bros having **more money to spend on things such as expanding, renovating the van or research and development of new baked potato recipes that people like**.
>
> Another advantage of crowdfunding is that it is **quick and easy**. Setting up a crowdfund in websites such as **gofundme** can be very simple and straightforward. This leads to **less time being spent to set up the funding** and more time can be used on other things such as **improving baked potato recipes and tweaking sauces** that is put on the baked potato.
>
> One disadvantage of crowdfunding is that perhaps **it can be hard to raise money** as the people who donate are **not getting anything in return** leading to less donations. This means that Spud Bros will get less money and **less money means they probably can't do the expanding** throughout UK plan leading to **loss of potential customers and loss of potential new markets**.
>
> I believe that Spud Bros should use crowdfunding because they **won't have to pay anyone in return** unlike in *[venture capital]* where you have to repay the money and pay interest as well.

### Examiner comment — verbatim
> *"This candidate **chooses to look at one option, which is acceptable** and demonstrates effectively the advantages and disadvantages of crowdfunding in the context of Spud Bros. The response shows **logical chains of reasoning that are contextualised**. **There is a judgement made that repeats the financial implication that was argued previously in the answer.** Total: **8 marks**"*

### ⚠️ Examiner Tip — verbatim
> *"**Candidates should avoid only repeating their previous arguments in their conclusion.**"*

> ### 🔬 The AO4 ladder is now complete — three verdicts, one insight
>
> | Mark | Report | Examiner's exact complaint |
> |---|---|---|
> | **7/9** | June 2025 P2 (Starbucks) | *"**fails to provide a sound judgment** to provide a clear justification based on a thorough evaluation"* — **no judgement at all** |
> | **8/9** | June 2025 P1R (CCT) | could *"incorporate something more complex, like the **'it depends' rule**"* — **judgement present, no new dimension** |
> | **8/9** | **June 2026 P1R (Spud Bros)** | *"a judgement made that **repeats the financial implication that was argued previously**"* — **judgement present, but recycled** |
> | **9/9** | June 2025 P2R (Rabanne) | ✅ *"a **reasonable judgement** about their final choice"* + *"**the success of this depends on** how much employees get to voice their own concerns"* |
>
> ### ⭐⭐ THE RULE THIS YIELDS
> **A conclusion is worth marks only if it contains something that is not already on the page.**
> There are three ways to satisfy that, and this pair shows two of them:
> 1. **Weigh** — *"Although it causes a huge cost… the increase in sales may over the cost in long term"* (**the 11/12 above**).
> 2. **Condition** — *"the success of this depends on…"* (**the 9/9 above**).
> 3. **Decide** — name the choice and the single decisive reason.
>
> **Restating your own three paragraphs in a summary sentence is not a judgement — it is a conclusion-shaped space where a judgement should be.** This 8/9 is the proof.

⭐ **Fifth independent confirmation of the one-option rule** — *"chooses to look at one option, which is acceptable."* (Cadbury 2022 · Café Pinch 2024 · TUI 2024 · Starbucks 2025 · **Spud Bros 2026**.)

> ⚠️ **Small but instructive:** in his closing sentence the candidate writes *"unlike in crowdfunding where you have to repay the money and pay interest"* — he means **venture capital**. **One wrong term in a Level 3 answer, and it still scored 8.** Examiners mark the argument, not the slips.

## ⭐⭐⭐ HOW AO4 IS ACTUALLY AWARDED — verbatim, from the June 2026 report

> *"Candidates may access **AO4 marks by offering drawbacks to the same option for which they have given the advantages.** AO4 may also be provided by offering **direct contrasting points between the options**."*

**Two routes. That is the whole of AO4.** Either:
1. **Advantage → drawback of the SAME option** (what the 8/9 above does: free samples give accurate feedback **/ but** samples are costly), or
2. **Direct contrast between the two options.**

> ⭐ **This is the single most useful sentence in the report for the 9- and 12-mark questions.** Candidates who write only advantages have written no AO4 at all, no matter how long the answer — which is exactly why the 6/12 benefits-only Evaluate and this paper's 3/9 both stop where they do.

> ### ⭐ THE 6-MARK CONTEXT LADDER, FROM THE TWO SCRIPTS SIDE BY SIDE
> 1. *"Spud Bros"* — the name, lifted from the stem. **Worth nothing.** (This is what the 2/6 does.)
> 2. *"Spud Bros operate on TikTok and Instagram"* — a fact from the scenario. **Unlocks AO2.**
> 3. *"expand internationally into the US and Canada"* — a fact from the scenario that **answers the question's own growth premise**. **This is what carries the answer to 6.**
>
> **A 6-mark Analyse is written by finding the TWO most specific facts in the scenario and building one chain out of each.** The chain is: *fact → consequence → business effect*. Every 6/6 in this bank has that shape, and no 6/6 in this bank has anything else.
