# ⭐⭐⭐ THE BIGGEST REPEATING ERROR IN 4BS1: THE DISCOUNT

Four reports, four separate mentions — it is the most-failed calculation in the qualification.

| Report | Question | Examiner's words |
|---|---|---|
| **2019 P1R** | 15% discount on 25 cakes | *"Some candidates worked out the 15% discount but **did not go on to take the discount from the cost of 25 cakes** thus only gaining one mark."* |
| **Nov 2023 P1** | new price of a jumper in a sale | *"Some candidates gained a mark for **calculating the discount** but they did not then **go on to apply the discount** therefore not gaining the second mark."* |
| **Nov 2024 P2** | discounted meal | *"Whilst many students were able to **calculate the discount** they failed to **subtract the discount from the full price**."* |
| **Jun 2025 P2** | (see `06_`) | *"Some however calculated the discount only."* |

> ## 🎯 THE RULE
> **On a percentage-discount question, the discount is NOT the answer. The final price is.**
> Before writing, name the thing the question actually asks for — *"the new price"*, *"the price the customer pays"* — and check your last operation produces **that**, not the percentage removed.
> ⚠️ **This is the single most valuable rule in this file**, because it is a 1-mark loss on a question most candidates otherwise get right, and it recurs in **every** series examined.

# ⭐⭐⭐ RELEVANCE: ANSWERING ABOUT THE WRONG PARTY

Four clear instances, each costing whole answers. **The question names who benefits; candidates routinely answer about the other one.**

| Report | The question | What candidates did |
|---|---|---|
| **Nov 2024 P2 Q1(g)** | how new Premier Inn hotels in **India** would benefit **India** | *"Many students wrote about how the new hotels would benefit **Premier Inn and not India**. This is a **prime example of students not reading the question carefully**."* |
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q1(g)** | benefits for **Huawei** of locating factories near labour sources | *"a large number of candidates gave **very detailed response of the benefits for its employees not Huawei** and therefore did not gain the marks available."* |
| **Nov 2024 P2 Q2(d)** | benefits **to a business** of sponsoring an event or person | *"Many candidates wrote about **the benefits of being sponsored** rather than the benefits to a business when sponsoring."* |
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q1(f)** | advantage of sponsorship for a business | *"a number of responses seen mentioned that it enables **more money to be put into the business**. Perhaps giving the benefits of those who are sponsored."* |
| **Nov 2023 P1 Q2(a)** | a **non-financial** method of motivation | *"candidates offered a **financial** method of motivation, whether in context or not, **this cannot be awarded a mark as it does not answer the question**."* |

> ## 🎯 THE RULE
> **Circle the subject of the question — the party and the qualifier — before you plan.** *Benefits to X*, *reasons for Y*, *non-financial Z*.
> ⚠️ **And note the Nov 2021 Q1(g) detail: an answer can be long, accurate, detailed and contextual — and still score nothing because it argues for the wrong person.**

# ⭐⭐ KNOWLEDGE IN PLACE OF APPLICATION — the Outline/Analyse trap

| Report | Examiner's words |
|---|---|
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q3(b)** | *"Candidates had to outline one reason why Huawei would use robotics… A number of candidates **described what robotics was, this is knowledge and therefore did not gain them any marks, even though the description was accurate**. The response needed to **include the scenario** as the marks allocated are for **AO2, application**."* — *"nearly three quarters of the cohort not gaining a mark"* |
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q2(e)** | *"a number of candidates **described what secondary research was, rather than how it would be used**."* |
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q3(d)** | *"others again **described in depth what off-the-job training was**."* |
| **Nov 2023 P1 Q2(f)/Q3(e)** | *"Learners showed clear knowledge of the two options for AWG but often were **unaware there are no AO1 marks available**."* |
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q2(f)** | *"a large number of candidates gave **very detailed descriptions of the two options, which was knowledge and therefore they did not gain any marks**."* |
| **Summer 2019 P1R Q01f** | *"Some candidates **explained what a franchisee was but did not provide any advantage** of being a franchisee therefore not gaining any marks."* |

> ## 🎯 THE RULE
> **On Outline, Analyse, Justify and Evaluate questions, a description is worth nothing — however accurate.** The test: does the sentence say how it **helps this business**, or does it say what the thing **is**?
> ⚠️ *"even though the description was accurate"* — accuracy is not the criterion. **Direction of use is.**

# ⭐⭐ DEFINING BADLY — four distinct ways to lose a 1-mark Define

| Report | Question | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Nov 2024 P2 Q1(b)** | define **job description** | *"Many gave **vague** definitions and **simply stated it was a description of a job** which was inadequate for the mark."* → **self-referential** |
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q1(c)** | define **demographics** | *"A number of candidates answered the question by **turning the term around**, such as 'the graphics made to check a profit or loss'."* → **self-referential** |
| **2019 P1R Q01b** | define **short-term finance** | *"Some candidates did **not** gain the mark as they **simply stated methods of short-term finance** as opposed to providing a definition."* → **examples instead of a definition** |
| **2019 P1R Q03a** | define **current liabilities** | *"Candidates have often **confused this term with current assets** or **listed current liabilities** as opposed to providing a definition."* → **collision + listing** |
| **Nov 2021 P2 Q3(a)** | define **revenue** | *"some candidates referred to revenue as **money received**."* → **imprecise wording** |

> ## 🎯 THE TEST
> **Can your definition be read back as the term itself?** *"A job description is a description of a job"* defines nothing. **Name the function, not the word.**
> **And a list is not a definition** — three methods of short-term finance is not the term.

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**

>

# ⭐⭐ THE "OTHER" TRAP — read the qualifier in the stem

**Summer 2019 P1R Q04(c):**
> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"The stem of the question states that **Lavender already uses a website** to promote its products so it is unfortunate that a handful of candidates misunderstood the question and **detailed the benefits of a website to Lavender** when promoting its products **thus gaining no marks as this does not answer the question**."*

> ## 🎯 THE RULE
> When a question says **"other"**, **"another"**, or **"different"**, the stem has told you what does **not** count. Writing about the excluded option scores **zero**, not partial credit.
> **Check:** is the thing I am describing the one the stem already ruled out?

# ⭐⭐ ROUNDING — the second variant nobody expects

**Summer 2019 P1R Q04(a):**
> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"Centres must remind candidates that where it is stated in the question the answer is **'to 2 significant figures'** then this is the expectation of the answer. Candidates have often provided a lengthy answer and **not gained full marks as their answer is not to 2 significant figures**."*

**Nov 2021 P2 Q1(e):**
> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"Candidates should be aware that if the question asks for **two decimal places** and their response has **three decimal places** they will not gain all the marks available."*

> ## 🎯 THE RULE
> **Read the rounding instruction — and it is not always decimal places.** *2 decimal places* · ***2 significant figures*** · *whole number* · *nearest £*.
> ⚠️ **2 s.f. and 2 d.p. give different answers.** Writing extra precision is a mark lost, not a mark earned.

**And the companion (Summer 2019 P1R Q04(a), Nov 2023 P1 Q4(a), Nov 2021 P2 Q3(c)/Q4(a)):**
> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"they could gain a mark for the formula, even if the actual answer is wrong"* · *"often candidates have gained a mark for their calculation despite their final answer being incorrect"* · *"**In some cases the writing of candidate's number 4 and number 9 could be confused by the examiner.**"*
