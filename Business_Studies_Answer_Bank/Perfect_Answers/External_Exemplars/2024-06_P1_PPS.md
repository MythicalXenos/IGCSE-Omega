## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 3/3 · Q2(c) Explain

> *"One impact of ineffective communication is the workers may do mistakes as the instructions are unclear to them. This means, once mistakes are done, there will be wastage. Revenue cost may increase and profit decreases."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This candidate correctly identifies the impact is 'workers may do mistakes' they have then gone on to explain this well with the links that 'instructions are unclear to them' and 'there will be wastage.'"*

**Examiner tip — important and counter-intuitive:**
> *"No context is required for explain questions but often we see candidates applying context unnecessarily. This candidate has then unnecessarily further explained this one impact but as we can see from the mark scheme candidates can only gain 2 further marks for explaining this reason."*

**Two lessons.** (1) The 3-mark explain chain is identify → develop → develop. (2) **Explain questions do NOT need context** — adding it wastes time. (3) Once you have identify + 2 developments, **stop**; extra explanation earns nothing.

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 6/6 · Q1(g) Analyse

**Question:** Analyse the usefulness of the gross profit margin.

**Figure 2 data:** Gross profit margin 2022 = **25.6%**; 2023 = **29.4%**

> *"The gross profit margin can be used by P[s]S to monitor the costs of selling dog coat[s] and dog leads. By monitoring the cost of selling these dog[s] products they will be able to identify if the cost of sales is increasing, and if it is they can change the information to increase profitability. This will lead to increased financial returns for Rachel Goutorbe. Additionally it can be used by Rachel Goutorbe to assess [and] monitor the performance of P[s]S. The more dog leashes they sell means that the gross profit margin will increase. For example as shown by Figure 2, P[s]S's gross profit margin has increased from 25.6% in 2022 [to] 29.4 in 2023 indicating that they have performed better in 2023."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This candidate shows they understand what the gross profit margin is and the links to costs of sales. They have contextualised their response by making reference to not only the type of products sold 'dog products' but also made use of the statistics provided in the stem of the question."*

**The technique — identical to the verified 6/6 profitability-ratios answer:** convert the abstract tool into *a decision the business takes*, and **use the figures given** ("25.6% → 29.4%"). The examiner specifically praises use of the statistics in the stem — many candidates never touch the data.

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 9/9 · Q2(f) Justify

**Question:** Justify which option PPS should choose to improve its revenue — trialling a new brand of dog food, or advertising.

> *"[Trialling] a new brand of dog food in the shop and advertising it can make customers interested and loyal because you are introducing a new brand of dog food, and the pet owners might be interested in buying it because they will be curious if their pet likes it and if it is better than their current one. Therefore, because of this profit increases and so does the revenue[,] all customers are kept interested. But, this may also be bad because PPS would have to invest money in advertising, manufacturing and how it looks and that would bring revenue down.*
>
> *In my opinion, given the business situation of PPS the best choice is introducing a new dog food brand because it increased brand image greatly."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows what is expected from candidates in order to gain the full marks for this type of 'justify' question. This candidate has chosen to look at **both** options which is acceptable. They have shown detailed understanding of both options, they have included **context throughout** their response with logical chains of reasoning and have made a good judgement about their choice with **a thorough evaluation**."*

**Two critical corrections to technique, straight from the examiner:**

1. **"Candidates can choose to weigh up the advantages and disadvantages of ONE option and then provide a conclusion, OR they could choose to weigh up BOTH options and then provide a conclusion."** And from the June 2022 P1R paper summary: ***"'Justify' questions do not need to consider both options."*** You do not have to cover both. But you **must** conclude.

2. The examiner says *"There are no knowledge marks."* On this 9/9, the candidate never defines anything — every sentence is application, chain or judgement.

# CALIBRATION — the "context" rule, demonstrated

## ❌ 0 marks · Q2(a) State

> *"Will increase their sales their local customers will continue to buy their products."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"A good response but for the lack of context cannot access the AO2 mark available. **Had this candidate replaced the term 'product' with an actual product PPS sells then they would have achieved the mark.**"*

## ✅ 1 mark · Q2(a) State

> *"Customers could engage in repetitive purchase of dog coats and dog leads."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"A good response with clear context that directly links back to PPS. **Just one piece of context will suffice.**"*

**This pair is the single cleanest illustration of the context rule in the entire bank.** Same question, same tariff. The only difference between 0 and 1 is that one candidate wrote "dog coats and dog leads" instead of "products".

Compare the June 2019 P1R 3/6 comment — *"Had the candidate replaced terms such as 'product' with 'food'…"* — **the same trap, five years apart, and the same one-word fix.**

## ❌ 1 mark · Q2(e) Explain

> *"Seasonal employees are used depending on when the business needs them which will have the benefit of less wages and also motivation for the employee"* `[sic — transcribed from a table layout]`

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This candidate included their knowledge of a seasonal employee – this is not answering the question so therefore awarded no marks at this point. This candidate has identified the benefit as 'less wages' gaining the mark. To expand on this response the candidate could have explained why wages would be less."*

**Lesson:** defining the term earns nothing on an *explain*. Only the identified benefit (1) plus two developments (2) score.

## ⚠️ 1 mark vs 2 marks · Q3(c) Calculate closing balance

**Data:** Opening balance €16,000 · Cash inflow €22,000 · Cash outflow €20,000

**1 mark —**
```
cash inflow - outflow = 22000 - 20000 = 2000 → closing balance
16000 - 2000 = 14000
€14000
```
> *"Whilst this candidate has not got the final answer correct, due to their working out, and as per the mark scheme, they have gained access to 1 mark."*

**2 marks —**
```
16000 + 22000 = 38000
38000 - 20000 = 18000
€18000
```
> *"This candidate has good recall of the formula for the closing balance, has identified the correct answer therefore gains access to the full available marks."*

**Rule:** always show working. A wrong answer with correct method still banks a mark. (And note: closing balance = opening + inflow − outflow. The 1-mark candidate subtracted instead of adding.)
