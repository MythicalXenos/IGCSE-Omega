# MAY 2023 PAPERS 1 AND 1R — the 1- and 2-mark sets, transcribed in full

**Twenty-two marked scripts.** Paper 1R prints ten of its 1-/2-mark questions in full (one script each); Paper 1 prints twelve (two of them pairs), and **every single one carries the examiner's ResultsPlus comment**.

**Why this tier is worth a file of its own.** *The mark scheme is the perfect answer* for a one- or two-mark question — so the bank needs **no model** here. What it cannot get from a mark scheme is **what a zero looks like**, and that is exactly what these reports print: the credited answer and the rejected one, often on facing pages, with a sentence from the examiner saying which is which and why.

⚠️ **Read the spread.** Paper 1R prints its script for `1(b)` on p. 4 and `1(c)` on p. 5 — one question per page, with the cohort commentary for that question above it. Paper 1 puts some pairs two pages apart.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1** · Q1(b) *Define the term economies of scale* — report p. 4

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):** *"**(b)** Define the term **economies of scale**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 6):** *"1 (b) · AO1 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of economies of scale.*

> • *A fall in average costs as output increases (1)*

*(1)"*

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"Reduction in average costs when the firm is producing in large quantities."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response shows a good example of a response demonstrating accurate knowledge of economies of scale."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"This question saw around just over half of candidates correctly define the term economies of scale with some candidates confusing the term with diseconomies of scale."*

🔬 **The 1-mark definition formula, and it is three elements long: WHAT falls (average costs) · the DIRECTION (reduction) · the CONDITION (producing in large quantities).** The mark scheme's own wording is *"a fall in average costs as output increases"* — **the script swaps *output increases* for *producing in large quantities*, and it is paid.** ⚠️ **The definition does not have to use the scheme's causal clause word for word; it has to name the same two moving parts.**

⚠️ **And note the trap the commentary names: candidates define the opposite term.** *"some candidates confusing the term with diseconomies of scale."* **In this tier a definition is one mark and there is no partial credit — writing the mirror image of the right answer scores the same as writing nothing.**

**Not required in this answer:** any business name, any context, any example. **A `Define` question is AO1 and the script names no business at all.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1** · Q1(c) *Define the term stakeholder* — report p. 5

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):** *"**(c)** Define the term **stakeholder**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 6):** *"1 (c) · AO1 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of stakeholder.*

> • *Individuals or groups with an interest in the operation of a business (1)*

*(1)"*

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"Stakeholders are individuals or group of individuals who are interested in the managing business."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response demonstrates accurate knowledge of a stakeholder."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"Around two thirds of candidates were able to correctly define the term stakeholders although some candidates took the route of providing examples of stakeholders which does not provide a clear definition so would not have gained the available mark."*

🔬 **This script is the mark scheme with three words changed** — *individuals or group of individuals* (for *individuals or groups*) · *who are interested in* (for *with an interest in*) · *the managing business* (for *the operation of a business*). **1/1.**

⚠️ **⭐ AND THE COHORT COMMENTARY NAMES THE FAILURE MODE THAT COSTS THIS MARK, BECAUSE IT IS THE ONE THAT LOOKS LIKE AN ANSWER: *"providing examples of stakeholders which does not provide a clear definition."*** *"Employees, customers and suppliers"* is a **list**, not a definition — it shows you know some stakeholders, not what a stakeholder is. **The definition needs the RELATIONSHIP (*an interest in*), and a list of three examples never contains it.**

⭐ **Note also the opening: *"Stakeholders are…"* — the term repeated as the subject, exactly as the Paper 2R Q1(b) 1/1 does (*"Public corporation is businesses that are owned and run by the government"*).** The two papers print the same habit in the same series and both pay it. **Repeating the term is allowed; it is the LIST that is refused.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1** · Q1(d) *State one benefit to Aude Rose of using just-in-time (JIT) production* — report p. 6

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):** *"**(d)** State **one** benefit to **Aude Rose** of using just-in-time (JIT) production."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 7):** *"1 (d) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a benefit to Aude Rose of using just-in-time production in the context of the business.*

> • *Aude Rose will have less waste as the flowers will be fresh when delivered to the Paris shop (1)*

***NB Do not accept a benefit that is not in the context of Aude Rose.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"No excess stocks are produced so Aude Rose's flower production will not be wasted."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate has stated a benefit of 'no excess stocks are produced' and then further explained this with the use of context with the word 'flower' as this makes direct reference to a product Aude Rose sells."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"Generally candidates responded well to this question and were able to identify a benefit of just-in-time (JIT) production and add context in relation to Aude Rose in their response. Where candidates stated a benefit but no context they could not access the A02 mark."*

🔬 **⭐⭐ THIS IS THE MOST PRECISE STATEMENT OF THE *STATE* RULE IN THE WHOLE BANK, BECAUSE THE EXAMINER IDENTIFIES THE EXACT WORD THAT CARRIES THE MARK — AND IT IS ONE WORD.**

The benefit (*"no excess stocks are produced"*) is **generic business knowledge and is worth nothing on its own** — it would be true of any JIT user. **The mark is paid for *"flower"*:** *"the use of context with the word 'flower' as this makes direct reference to a product Aude Rose sells."*

> ⚠️ **That is the whole requirement on a `State` question, stated as narrowly as it can be: the answer must contain something only THIS business has.** A florist sells flowers; *"flower production will not be wasted"* cannot be said of a car plant.
>
> ⭐ **The cheapest way to earn it — and the one the script demonstrates — is to name the business's own product, service or activity inside the sentence.** The name *"Aude Rose"* sits in the sentence too, **but the examiner credits the word *flower*, not the name.**

**This is the fifth independent confirmation in the bank that naming the business is not context**, and it is the first that shows the examiner choosing a *common noun* over the *proper noun* when explaining where the mark came from. **Rule: on a `State` question, put the business's product in the sentence, not just its name.**

⚠️ **Note the AO on the scheme: `A02 - 1 mark`.** **Sixth confirmation that `State` is an application question.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2** · Q1(e) *Calculate how many bunches of flowers Aude Rose will have to sell to break-even* — report p. 7

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):**

> *"Aude Rose has fixed costs of €50 000 a year with a selling price of €50 and a variable cost of €10 per bunch of flowers sold."*
> *"**(e)** Calculate how many bunches of flowers **Aude Rose** will have to sell to break-even. You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 7):** *"1 (e) · A02 - 2 marks*

> *50 000/(50 – 10) (1)*
> *= 1 250 (1)*

*Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula. Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working. (2)"*

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"Break even = Fixed Cost / Variable cost – selling price*
> *= 50 000 / 50 − 10"*
>
> ***(answer line:)*** *"1250 … bunches of flowers"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This is an example of how a candidate has responded well to the question, showing their working out and correctly answering the question.*
>
> *This candidate has found it helpful to write down the formula to be used but this is not a requirement and will not gain the candidate any marks."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"A well answered question where over two thirds of candidates gained the full marks for the correct calculation of how many bunches of flowers Aude Rose needs to sell in order to break-even. Some candidates found they could not access the question as they could not recall the formula for break-even; it is important that centres ensure they are aware of the formulas provided in the paper as well as those that are not."*

🔬 **⭐⭐⭐ THE HEADLINE FINDING OF THIS FILE: THE CANDIDATE WRITES THE FORMULA DOWN INVERTED, AND SCORES 2/2.** Read the first line:

> *"Break even = Fixed Cost / **Variable cost − selling price**"*

**The denominator is written the wrong way round** — it should be *selling price − variable cost*. **As written it would produce a negative division and a nonsensical answer.** **Then, on the very next line, the candidate substitutes the numbers CORRECTLY (50 − 10) and reaches 1 250 — and the examiner pays both marks.**

> ⭐ **What the examiner credits is the SUBSTITUTION, not the formula.** **The scheme's own reward line is *"50 000/(50 – 10) (1)"* — that is precisely what the script's second line does.** The mis-transcribed formula line above it is not marked, not penalised, and not mentioned in the comment.
>
> ⚠️ **This is the single most reassuring fact in the tier for a candidate who is not confident with algebra: you are not being marked on recalling the formula correctly. You are marked on *"correctly substituting numbers into formula"* and on the answer.** **A correct substitution reaches the top mark even under a wrong formula heading.**

⚠️ **And the examiner uses the opportunity to correct the OTHER half of the same habit:** *"This candidate has found it helpful to write down the formula to be used but this is not a requirement and will not gain the candidate any marks."* **Writing the formula earns nothing — and, as this script shows, gets you nothing taken away either.**

⭐ **Note also what the answer line carries: just the number and the unit — `1250 … bunches of flowers`.** The question's own unit is printed on the dotted line after the answer; **the candidate writes the digits, and the mark is for `1 250`.** The scheme's *"Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working"* means the working above is insurance rather than requirement — **but the working is what saved this script, since the formula above it was wrong.**

**Cohort data:** *"over two thirds of candidates gained the full marks."* ⚠️ **And the commentary's diagnosis of the rest is worth carrying: *"Some candidates found they could not access the question as they could not recall the formula for break-even; it is important that centres ensure they are aware of the formulas provided in the paper as well as those that are not."*** **Break-even is one of the formulae the paper does NOT print** (see the specification's Appendix 4 list) — so this is exactly the question where the answer is either known or lost, **and the fix the examiner recommends to centres applies to you as a candidate: know which formulae the paper gives you and which it expects you to recall.**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 11):** *"2 (b) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a valid reason why branding is important to Aude Rose in the context of the business.*

> • *It makes the business stand out against other flower shops in Paris (1)*

***NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of Aude Rose.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

### ⚠️ THE 0/1 SCRIPT — and this is the finding of the file

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"Branding is important for Ade Rose because it can increase the image of Ade Rose and may Attract customers."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate has not quite understood how to respond to a 'state' question. Branding allowed candidates to bring in their own understanding of the importance of branding and this candidate chose to base their response around increase the image of the business to attract customers, unfortunately with no context no marks can be gained. **To improve this response the candidate could've replaced the business name with the owners name of Aude Anglaret or added '[attract customers] to the flower shop' which would bring the response into context.**"*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim, in its own box):** *"Centres should remind candidates that 'state' questions have to have context in the response otherwise they will not gain the mark."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"Another 'State' question which was, again, only well answered by just over one third of candidates. Candidates were able to correctly identify why branding is important but did not always apply this way to Aude Rose. Candidates should remember that 'State' questions need to be in the context of the given business, a lot of candidates are currently being disadvantaged by not doing this."*

### 🔬 THE BANK'S SHARPEST DEMONSTRATION THAT THE BUSINESS NAME IS NOT CONTEXT

⚠️ **This script says *"Aude Rose" TWICE — the second time it is the SUBJECT of the clause — and scores 0.** Read what is left when the name is removed:

> *"Branding is important because it can increase the image of the business and may attract customers."*

**That is a true statement about every business that has ever existed. It is the definition of a generic answer, and the examiner says exactly that: *"no context no marks can be gained."***

⭐⭐ **AND THE EXAMINER DOES NOT JUST DIAGNOSE — HE PRESCRIBES TWO SPECIFIC EDITS, AND THEY ARE THE TWO MOST USEFUL REPAIRS IN THE WHOLE BANK:**

| His edit | Why it works |
|---|---|
| *"replaced the business name with the **owners name of Aude Anglaret**"* | The owner is a **specific human being** — the answer now contains a fact about *this* business that is not printed on the question paper |
| *"added '*[attract customers] **to the flower shop**'*" | The business's **activity** enters the sentence — a bank does not have a flower shop |

⚠️ **Both repairs use the same principle: replace something that is true of ANY business with something true of ONLY this one.** ⭐ **And note the examiner's own phrase: *"which would bring the response into context"* — one clause is enough.** He is not asking for a longer answer; **he is asking for the same answer with the generic noun swapped for the case's own noun.**

🔬 **⭐ CONTRAST THE TWO SCRIPTS ON THIS PAPER AND THE RULE BECOMES VISIBLE.** Q2(a)'s **1/1** is *"Company Car for the florists to drive around Paris"* — **six words with no business name in them at all.** Q2(b)'s **0/1** is thirteen words with the business name written twice. **The mark tracks the case's own subject-matter, not its proper noun.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1** · Q3(a) *Define the term variable costs* — report p. 19

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):** *"3 · **(a)** Define the term **variable costs**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 16):** *"3 (a) · A01 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of variable costs.*

> • *Costs that vary as output levels change (1)*

*(1)"*

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"Cost that change when level of output change."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This is a good example of how we would candidates to respond to this question, this candidate has provided a thorough definition of variable costs."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim):** *"For 'Define' questions candidates are required to show they have a clear understanding of the term, where candidates have given examples of variable costs that a business may incur they have not gained the mark."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"A question where candidates were asked to define the term variable costs. Just over half of candidates showed clear knowledge of the term. Where candidates did not gain the mark either gave examples of variable costs, did not understand the term or simply stated 'costs that change' which is not a sufficient definition as costs can change for a number of reasons."*

🔬 **⭐⭐ THIS SINGLE PAGE CONTAINS THE COMPLETE, EXACT BOUNDARY OF A ONE-MARK DEFINITION — THE SMALLEST CHANGE THAT LOSES THE MARK AND THE SMALLEST CHANGE THAT KEEPS IT.**

> *"Cost that change when level of output change"* → **1 mark**
> *"Costs that change"* → **0 marks**

**The difference is three words: *when level of output change*.** And the examiner explains precisely why they are load-bearing: *"'costs that change' is not a sufficient definition **as costs can change for a number of reasons**."*

⭐ **A definition is one mark and there is no partial credit — so the margin between 1 and 0 on this question is a four-word CAUSAL CLAUSE.** The scheme wants the *trigger* named: **costs vary *as output levels change*.** Without the trigger, "costs that change" could describe inflation, a rent review, a new tax — none of which make a cost *variable*.

⚠️ **And the third failure mode is named in the tip: *"where candidates have given examples of variable costs that a business may incur they have not gained the mark."*** *"Ingredients, packaging, delivery"* is a list of examples — **exactly the same trap the Q1(c) stakeholder script's commentary describes.** **On a `Define` question in this tier: two failure modes, both of them lists or half-clauses, and no business name is needed anywhere.**

⚠️ **Note the grammar: *"Cost that change when level of output change"* — no plural agreement on either verb, and *"levels"* singularised.** The examiner calls it **"a thorough definition"** and pays it. **Rule 55 again, in the tier where a single mark is at stake.**

***NB Do not accept a way that is not in the context of Aude Rose.** Accept any other appropriate response. (2)"*

**The script (verbatim — with a correction visible mid-sentence):**

> *"Aude Rose can change the packaging by adding extra ribbons to it or changing the colour of the wrapping paper so that people are still likely going to buy the ~~product~~ flower bunches."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This is the only outline question in the paper and requires candidates to outline a way that Aude Rose could extend the product life cycle of one of its products. 'Outline' questions require candidates to use context within their responses and this shows how we would expect candidates to response to this type of question as this has achieved the full available marks."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim):** *"Centres are encouraged to remind candidates how to respond to the command words in order to access all of the available marks."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"The response for an Outline question requires candidates to answer in the context of Aude Rose to gain the full available marks. 🔬 **THE CANDIDATE STRIKES THE WORD *"product"* AND WRITES *"flower"* — AND THAT SINGLE EDIT IS THE SECOND MARK.** Follow it:

| | text | worth |
|---|---|---|
| way | *"change the packaging … adding extra ribbons … changing the colour of the wrapping paper"* | **the identification — 1 mark** |
| consequence | *"so that people are still likely going to buy the flower bunches"* | **the context link — 1 mark** |

⭐ **Note what the identification IS: a real extension strategy (packaging change), not a price cut.** The scheme's own bullet is *"lower the prices of its least popular flowers"* — **a completely different strategy, and the script is still paid in full, because the requirement is "a way", not "the way".**

| | **Q3(c) — 1/2** | **Q4(a) — 2/2** |
|---|---|---|

| result | **1 mark** — *"cannot access the second mark"* | **2 marks** |

**The two scripts differ in nothing except the digit the question asked for.** ⚠️ **Same paper, same series, same instruction in the stem: *"to two decimal places"*.** **This is the single cheapest mark in the qualification to lose, and the examiners flagged it three times on this one paper.**

⭐ **The candidate even corrects the formula mid-line — *Gross Profit / ~~Revenue~~* → *Sales* — and the examiner pays it without comment.** We may state with certainty only that **no deduction was made for the correction**; the comment praises the working rather than the formula itself, so **what the correction is worth cannot be read off this script.** It is consistent with every other script in this bank where an in-line correction has cost nothing, but note the comment is silent.

⚠️ **The examiner's own tip is the one-line diagnosis of this whole pair: *"read the question carefully to fully understand what is required."*** On a calculation question, **the instruction is not decoration — it is half the mark.**

⭐ **Note the cohort commentary names the aid: *"The formula for gross profit margin is given at the start of the exam paper."*** Gross profit margin **is** on the specification's printed formulae list (unlike break-even, in Q1(e) above, which is not) — **so this question is a gift to a candidate who reads the front of the paper, and the examiner says centres are now seeing more candidates use it.**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1 MS p. 6):** *"1 (b) · AO1 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of limited liability.*

> • *When the business owner is only responsible for the original amount of money invested in the business (1)*

*(1)"*

**The script (verbatim — the candidate writes *"owner"*, crosses it out, and writes *"shareholder"* above the line):**

> *"limited liability is when the ~~owner~~ shareholder of a business and the business have separate legal identities."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response does not state a clear definition of limited liability as the business and owner could have a separate identity for a number of reasons."*

🔬 **⭐ THIS IS THE MOST INSTRUCTIVE ZERO IN THE WHOLE 1-AND-2-MARK HARVEST, BECAUSE THE ANSWER LOOKS LIKE A TEXTBOOK DEFINITION AND IS NOT ACCEPTED.**

*"Separate legal identities"* is **true of every limited-liability business** — and the examiner's objection is that it is **not exclusive to it**: *"the business and owner could have a separate identity for a number of reasons."* **The mark scheme wants the CONSEQUENCE that gives the concept its name: the owner is *only responsible for the original amount invested*.**

> ⚠️ **The distinction to carry: *separate legal identity* is what makes limited liability POSSIBLE; *limited to the amount invested* is what it IS.** The scheme pays for the second.
>

⚠️ **The candidate's own correction — *owner* struck through, *shareholder* substituted — is a real improvement in accuracy and earned nothing**, because the fault was in the logic, not the vocabulary. **This is the tier's clearest evidence that self-correction is free but is not a substitute for the right idea.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 AND 0/1** · Q1(d) *State one way Irsi Chocolatier could use the Boston matrix* — report pp. 7–8

**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):** *"**(d)** State **one** way **Irsi Chocolatier** could use the Boston matrix."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1 MS p. 7):** *"1 (d) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a way Irsi Chocolatier could use the Boston matrix*

> • *Irsi Chocolatier could use the dog category to see the chocolates that are not worth making anymore (1)*

***NB Do not accept a way that is not in the context of Irsi Chocolatier.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim, report p. 7):**

> *"Irsi Chocolatier can use the Boston matrix to identify which of it's choclates to invest in. As it's more useful investing in a star than a dog."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate has stated one way Irsi Chocolatier could use the Boston matrix and it is in the context of the given business scenario allowing them to access the mark for this question."*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim, report p. 8):**

> *"The boston Matrix allows Irsi Chocolatier to see whether the business has low/high Market share or low/high market growth to try and improve their business."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate shows knowledge of the Boston matrix but without context in the response in relation to the given business scenario they cannot achieve the mark for this question."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim, in its own box below — the most usable self-check in the bank):** *"All 'State' questions require candidates to include context to their response – **encourage candidates to check if their response is generic and can be applied to any business or have they included phrases that link it directly to the given scenario? For this scenario reference to the product sold e.g. chocolate would be sufficient.**"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 7):** *"Centres are reminded that 'State' questions must contain context in relation to the given business scenario. This question saw about one quarter of candidates correctly state one way Irsi Chocolatier could use the Boston matrix. Those who did not gain the mark either did not have any knowledge of the Boston matrix or if they did then it was not in the context of the business thus not achieving the A02 mark."*

### 🔬 THE 0-MARK SCRIPT NAMES THE BUSINESS — AND STILL SCORES ZERO

⚠️ **Read the two scripts against the examiner's own test — *"can be applied to any business?"***

| | 1/1 — p. 7 | 0/1 — p. 8 |
|---|---|---|
| what the matrix is used **for** | *"identify which of it's **choclates** to invest in"* | *"see whether the business has low/high market share or low/high market growth"* |
| the business's own material | ✅ **chocolates** | ❌ **nothing** |
| the sentence | a **decision** being made | the **tool being described** |

⭐⭐ **THE 0-MARK SCRIPT CONTAINS THE BUSINESS'S FULL NAME — *"Irsi Chocolatier"* — IN THE FIRST SIX WORDS, AND THE EXAMINER SAYS IT IS STILL NOT IN CONTEXT.** This is the third script in this harvest to prove the same thing (P1R Q2(b) wrote the name twice and scored 0), **and it is now beyond argument: a proper noun is not application.**

⭐ **The 1/1's context is the word *"choclates"* — the product** — exactly the word the Examiner Tip names: *"reference to the product sold e.g. chocolate would be sufficient."* ⚠️ **And note the misspelling: *"choclates"*, once, inside an answer that also misuses *"it's"* twice. 1/1.** Rule 55.

> ⭐⭐ **THE STRUCTURAL DIFFERENCE, and this is the lesson to take from the pair:**
> * **0/1 EXPLAINS THE TOOL** — the axes, market share and market growth. It answers *"what is the Boston matrix?"*
> * **1/1 USES THE TOOL** — *"invest in a star rather than a dog"*. It answers *"how would Irsi use it?"*
>
> **The command word is `State one way … could use`. The 0-mark answer teaches the examiner what the matrix is; the 1-mark answer tells him what the business would do on Monday morning.** ⚠️ **A candidate can lose this mark while knowing FAR more about the matrix than the candidate who gains it.**

⭐ **One more detail: the 1/1 uses the matrix's own vocabulary — *"a star than a dog"* — without ever explaining what those categories are, and the examiner pays it without asking for the explanation.** On a `State` question there is no room for a definition, and no mark for one.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2 AND 1/2** · Q1(e) *Calculate, to two decimal places, the cost of these chocolates after the discount has been applied* — report pp. 9–10

**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):**

> *"A customer buying five boxes of chocolates for a total of €75.50 is given a 6% discount."*
> *"**(e)** Calculate, to two decimal places, the cost of these chocolates after the discount has been applied. You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1 MS p. 7):** *"1 (e) · A02 - 2 marks*

> *75.50 x 0.06 = 4.53 (1)*
> *75.50 – 4.53 = 70.97 (1)*

*Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula. Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working. (2)"*

**The 2-mark script (verbatim, report p. 9 — note the abandoned first line):**

> *"~~75.50 x 5 =~~ 75.50 − (75.50 x 0.06) = 70.97"*
>
> ***(answer line:)*** *"€ 70.97"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"A good response where the candidate has gained the full available marks for the correct answer."*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim, report p. 10):**

> *"75.50*
> *1% = 0.755 x 6 = 4.53*
> *75.50 + 4.53"*
>
> ***(answer line:)*** *"€ 80.03"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This is an example of a candidate who has not achieved the full available marks due to miscalculation. **They have added the discount amount to the cost of the chocolates as opposed to subtracting**, however, as this candidate has shown clear working out a mark was awarded, as per the mark scheme, for the calculation of the discount amount of €4.53."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim):** *"Candidates should be encouraged to include their working out. As you can see from the example, there are occasions where candidates have the incorrect final response but the correct working out so have been able to gain a mark."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 9):** *"This is a calculation question where two thirds of candidates gained the full available marks for calculation of the cost of the chocolate after the discount has been applied. Some candidates who did not gain the marks failed to apply the discount to the cost of the chocolates or miscalculated the discount amount."*

### 🔬 A "+" INSTEAD OF A "−" COSTS EXACTLY ONE MARK — AND THE WORKING IS WHY IT COSTS ONLY ONE

⚠️ **The 1-mark script does the DISCOUNT arithmetic perfectly: *"1% = 0.755 × 6 = 4.53"* — the scheme's own intermediate figure to the penny.** Its error is the operator on the last line: **it adds the discount to the price instead of taking it off, and answers €80.03 — a price RISE of €4.53 on a question about a discount.** One character, one mark.

⭐ **Read the examiner's reasoning, because it is the mark scheme applied literally: *"as per the mark scheme, for the calculation of the discount amount of €4.53."*** The scheme's first reward line is *"75.50 x 0.06 = 4.53 (1)"* — **that is a standalone step, and the candidate earned it.** ⭐ **Marking is per-line, not per-idea: a candidate who computes the right intermediate value gets its mark even when the answer they build from it is wrong.**

⭐⭐ **AND THIS PAIRS WITH Q1(E) OF PAPER 1R — SAME SERIES, SAME QUESTION NUMBER, SAME SKILL — TO GIVE THE COMPLETE PICTURE OF CALCULATION MARKING IN THIS QUALIFICATION. Three routes through one 2-mark calculation, all of them now on the record:**

| | the answer | the working | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1R Q1(e)** | **correct** (1 250) | **formula headed UP-SIDE DOWN**, but the substitution right | **2/2** |
| **P1 Q1(e)** here | **wrong** (€80.03) | **both lines right, one operator wrong** | **1/2** |
| **P1R Q3(c)** | **wrong** (`22.5` for `22.55`) | **formula and substitution both right** | **1/2** |

⭐ **The constant is not the formula and not the final answer — it is the SUBSTITUTION. Write the numbers doing something, and the marking finds a line to pay.** ⚠️ And the examiner says it in as many words, twice on this one paper: *"Candidates should be encouraged to include their working out … there are occasions where candidates have the incorrect final response but the correct working out so have been able to gain a mark."*

⭐ **Note also the 2-mark script's abandoned first line: *"75.50 x 5 ="* is struck through.** The candidate began by multiplying by five boxes — **the stem does not ask for that; €75.50 is already the total for five boxes** — then crossed it out and worked it correctly. **A struck-out misreading of the stem cost nothing.** ⚠️ Read the stem carefully anyway: *"a customer buying five boxes of chocolates **for a total of** €75.50"* is precisely the sentence a hasty candidate multiplies.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **0/1 on a 1 mark question** · Q2(b) *State one way Irsi Chocolatier might extend the life cycle of one of its products* — report p. 15

**Question (verbatim, as printed on the script):** *"**(b)** State **one** way **Irsi Chocolatier** might extend the life cycle of one of its products."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1 MS p. 10):** *"2 (b) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a valid way Irsi Chocolatier might extend the life cycle of a product in the context of the business.*

> • *Irsi Chocolatier can lower the price of its chocolates (1)*

***NB Do not accept a way that is not in the context of Irsi Chocolatier.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"By advertising the product, they will be able to extend its life-cycle as their customers would want to try it out."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate has correctly identified a way to extend the life cycle of a product by advertising, unfortunately, they have omitted any context making the response generic and can be related to any business as opposed to Irsi Chocolatier specifically."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"Another 'State' question which saw just over one third of candidates able to correctly identify a way Irsi Chocolatier might extend the life cycle of one of its products. Centres should remind candidates that 'State' questions need to be in the context of the given business, a lot of candidates are currently being disadvantaged by not using this skill."*

### 🔬 THIS SCRIPT HAS THE BUSINESS'S PRODUCT IN IT — *"the product"*

⚠️ **And that is exactly why it fails.** Follow the two words:

> *"By advertising **the product**… as **their customers** would want to try it out."*

**"The product" and "their customers" are the generic article and the generic possessive.** They belong to whichever business the reader has in mind. **The answer is anatomically perfect — way (*advertising*), mechanism (*would want to try it out*), and a clean chain — and it names nothing.**

⭐⭐ **SET THIS SCRIPT BESIDE THE TWO PAGES BEFORE IT AND THE TIER'S RULE IS COMPLETE.** On this same paper:

| | script | did it name anything of the business's own? | mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Q1(d)** | *"the business has low/high Market share…"* — business named in the sentence | ❌ no product | **0/1** |
| **Q2(b)** here | *"advertising **the product** … **their** customers"* | ❌ no product | **0/1** |
| **Q1(d)** | *"which of it's **choclates** to invest in"* | ✅ **the product** | **1/1** |
| **Q2(a)** | *"the prices it charges for its **chocolates**"* | ✅ **the product** | **1/1** |

⚠️ **A candidate reading only the business name in the question can believe *"the product"* refers to it. The examiner cannot.** **The article is what makes it generic — write the noun.**

⭐ **Compare the same command word and the same topic on Paper 1R, where the candidate DID name it:** Q3(b)'s 2/2 wrote *"so that people are still likely going to buy the **flower bunches**"* **after striking out *"product"*.** **The two papers of one series print the same instruction being followed and missed, and the fix is six characters long.**
