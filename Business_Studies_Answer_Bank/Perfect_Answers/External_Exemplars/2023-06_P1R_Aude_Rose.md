## ⭐ THE 9-MARK RULE — stated outright by the examiner

> **ResultsPlus Examiner Tip (verbatim):**
> *"**Candidates do not need to consider both options.** Candidates need to make a clear choice out of the two options and be able to balance out their response as to why the business should or should not choose that option. Candidates are then expected to provide a clear overall judgement to evaluate their decision."*

**Note what *is* required: balance *within your chosen option*, plus a clear overall judgement.** "Balance" does not mean "cover both options" — it means weigh the chosen option's strengths against its weaknesses. This finally explains why the verified 9/9 in `02_Verified_Exemplars.md` turned *against its own choice* mid-answer.

## ⤴ 9/9 · Q2(f) Justify — **not counted here** (duplicate of the series file)

**Question:** Choose between sponsoring a local event or distributing leaflets to increase sales.

> *"…[this] means that Aude Rose may have a larger customer base, and customers may be more attracted to Aude Rose rather than other flower shops. **However, this entirely depends on the success of the local event**, and whether it was held effectively or not, meaning that Aude Rose may not be able to increase its sales if the number of attendees of the local show is low.*
>
> *In conclusion, I believe that sponsoring at a local event is better for Aude Rose in order to increase its sales. This is because leaflets may be hard to modify and alter once printed, and may require Aude Rose to have experience in graphics and presentations, or it may have to hire specialists to create the leaflets in an attractive way, which is expensive. Moreover, despite sponsorships being expensive, if the local event was successful, then the costs may be covered easily."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows what is expected from candidates in order to gain the full marks for this type of 'justify' question. This candidate has shown detailed understanding of the choice they have made, they have included context throughout their response with logical chains of reasoning and has made a good judgement about their choice with a thorough evaluation."*

**Note the conditional judgement:** *"this entirely depends on the success of the local event"* — the same **"it depends"** move the 12/12 used. Conditionality is a mark-earning technique at both 9 and 12 marks, not just 12.

## ⚠️ Define — 1 mark, and the recurring trap

**Question:** Define variable costs. **Response:** *"Cost that change when level of output change."* → **1 mark**

> *"'This candidate has provided a thorough definition of variable costs."*
>
> **Examiner Tip:** *"where candidates have given **examples** of variable costs that a business may incur they have not gained the mark."*

**The threshold is exact:** *"costs that change"* alone is **not** sufficient. *"Costs that change **when level of output changes**"* is. The definition must name the **driver**.

## ⚠️ Calculation — the decimal-place trap again

**Question:** Calculate, to two decimal places, the percentage decrease in retained profits (2021: €16,260 → 2022: €12,594).

The candidate recalled the formula and worked correctly **but answered to one decimal place** → **1/2**.

> *"the candidate cannot access the second mark as they have not fully read the question stating 'to two decimal places'… without this working out this candidate would not have achieved any marks but because of this working out they have gained a mark even though the final answer is to one decimal place."*

**Two rules in one:** (1) read the decimal requirement and obey it exactly; (2) show working — it converted a total failure into 1 mark. **This exact trap appears in June 2022 P2R, June 2024 P1R and June 2023 P1R. It is the most reliably repeatable mark loss on the paper.**
