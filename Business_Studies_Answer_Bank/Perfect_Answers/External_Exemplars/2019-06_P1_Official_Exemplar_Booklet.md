# ⭐⭐ THE OFFICIAL LEVEL DESCRIPTORS

**This is the most important block of text in the entire bank.** Every other file tells you what successful answers *did*. This tells you what the examiner is **looking for** — the rubric itself, in Pearson's own words.

Two sets exist: the **6-mark** descriptors (AO2 + AO3 only) and the **9-mark** descriptors (AO2 + AO3 + AO4).

## 6-mark Analyse — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3

| Level | Marks | Descriptor (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| | **0** | No Rewardable material. |
| **Level 1** | **1–2** | • **Limited** application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts and issues to the business context (AO2).<br>• **Attempts** to deconstruct business information and/or issues, finding **limited** connections between points (AO3). |
| **Level 2** | **3–4** | • **Sound** application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts and issues to the business context **although there may be some inconsistencies** (AO2).<br>• Deconstructs business information and/or issues, finding **interconnected** points with chains of reasoning, **although there may be some logical inconsistencies** (AO3). |
| **Level 3** | **5–6** | • **Detailed** application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts and issues to the business context **throughout** (AO2).<br>• Deconstructs business information and/or issues, finding **detailed interconnected** points with **logical** chains of reasoning (AO3). |

## 9-mark Justify — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3

| Level | Marks | Descriptor (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| | **0** | No Rewardable material. |
| **Level 1** | **1–3** | • **Limited** application… to the business context (AO2).<br>• **Attempts** to deconstruct…, finding **limited** connections between points (AO3).<br>• Makes a judgement, providing a **simple** justification based on **limited** evaluation of business information and issues relevant to the choice made (AO4). |
| **Level 2** | **4–6** | • **Sound** application… although there may be some inconsistencies (AO2).<br>• Deconstructs…, finding **interconnected** points with chains of reasoning, although there may be some logical inconsistencies (AO3).<br>• Makes a judgement, providing a justification based on **sound** evaluation of business information and issues relevant to the choice made (AO4). |
| **Level 3** | **7–9** | • **Detailed** application… to the business context **throughout** (AO2).<br>• Deconstructs…, finding **detailed interconnected** points with **logical** chains of reasoning (AO3).<br>• Makes a judgement, providing a **clear justification based on a THOROUGH evaluation** of business information and issues relevant to the choice made (AO4). |

## How to read these descriptors — the whole strategy in one table

The descriptors are built on **one escalating adjective per AO**. Find the column you are stuck in, and you have found the single word you must satisfy.

| | Level 1 | Level 2 | **Level 3 — target this** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AO2 — context** | *Limited* application | *Sound* application | **Detailed** application **throughout** |
| **AO3 — chains** | *Limited* connections | *Interconnected* points | **Detailed interconnected** points with **logical** chains |
| **AO4 — judgement** | *Simple* justification, *limited* evaluation | *Sound* evaluation | **Clear justification** based on a **thorough** evaluation |

### ⭐⭐ Three findings that fall straight out of the rubric

**1. AO2 Level 3 requires "throughout" — not "used".**
Level 2 is *"sound application… although there may be some inconsistencies"*. Level 3 is *"detailed application… **throughout**"*. **The single word "throughout" is the difference between the 3–4 band and the 5–6 band.** One good contextual point in the opening line is a Level 2 answer. This is the rubric behind Pearson's support-page tip: *"Context must be woven throughout the entire response, appearing in all paragraphs."*

**2. AO3 Level 3 requires "logical" — Level 2 permits "logical inconsistencies".**
Level 2 explicitly forgives broken reasoning: *"although there may be some logical inconsistencies"*. Level 3 does not. **So a chain that does not actually follow is a Level 2 chain, even if it is long.** This is what "logical chains of reasoning" is testing.

**3. AO4's Level 3 word is "thorough" — and this is where almost everyone stops.**
Compare Level 2 (*"sound evaluation"*) with Level 3 (*"clear justification based on a **thorough** evaluation"*). **"Thorough" is the highest bar in the entire qualification**, and it sits on the objective worth only 11–12%. Candidates routinely reach Level 3 on AO2 and AO3 and are held at the bottom of the band by an AO4 that is merely "sound".

> **This is the mechanical explanation of the 7/9 → 9/9 gap, confirmed five times now in this bank.** A 7/9 is a Level 3 answer whose AO4 word is not yet "thorough".

### The cut-off numbers to memorise

| Question | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6-mark Analyse** | 1–2 | 3–4 | **5–6** |
| **9-mark Justify** | 1–3 | 4–6 | **7–9** |
| **12-mark Evaluate** | 1–4 | 5–8 | **9–12** |

**The Level 3 threshold is the target: 5/6, 7/9, 9/12.** Scoring 4, 6 or 8 means you are one mark below the band that matters — and every one of those near-misses in this bank is diagnostic: the examiners say *"limited to the top of level 2"*, *"limited to the bottom of level 3"*.

# Q1(f) — Explain (3 marks)

> **Explain one way a business could increase its gross profit margin.**

### Mark scheme — AO1, 3 marks (verbatim)

> *"Award 1 mark for identification of a way, plus 2 further marks for explaining how this way will affect a business for a maximum of 3 marks.*
> *A business could negotiate deals with suppliers at a cheaper rate (1) this will keep costs as low as possible (1) meaning that the business can look to reduce the prices of their products/services to encourage more sales (1).*
>
> **Answers that list three ways with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark.**"

### Exemplar A — **3 marks**

> *"A business could reduce it's cost of sales to increase its gross profit margin. It could reduce it's cost by focusing on lean production which is reducing waste to save money by using less raw materials."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response shows an understanding of how a business could increase gross profit margin by reducing the cost of sales. It continues to explain how this way can help a business to increase its gross profit margin by focusing on lean production leading to the use of fewer raw materials, thus gaining the full available marks."*

**Chain:** reduce cost of sales **(1)** → lean production, reducing waste **(1)** → using less raw materials **(1)**. Three links. No context needed (AO1 only).

### Exemplar B — **1 mark**

> *"A business can increase its gross profit margin by reducing cost of sales. Also, another way is to reduce unnecessary expenses of the business. And nising [raising] the price of products of the business. That way gross profit margin would increase."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This particular response gave three ways in which a business could increase its gross profit margin therefore limiting the marks awarded to 1. **Had the candidate explained any one of these three ways, then they would have gained further marks.**"*

### ⭐ The cleanest demonstration of the listing cap in the bank

Three ways → **1 mark**. One way explained twice → **3 marks**.

**The candidate did the harder work** — they knew three valid ways to raise gross profit margin — and scored a third of the marks available. **The examiner's fix is explicit: *"Had the candidate explained any ONE of these three ways, then they would have gained further marks."*

> **Drill:** when you finish an Explain answer, count your points. If the count is more than one, delete everything except one and spend the freed space on *"this is because…"* and *"which means…"*.

# Q2(d) — Explain (3 marks)

> **Explain one method of above the line promotion a business could use.**

### Mark scheme — AO1, 3 marks (verbatim)

> *"Award 1 mark for identification of method, plus 2 further marks for explaining this method, for a maximum of 3 marks.*
> • *A business could use television advertising (1). This can be seen by large numbers of people raising awareness (1) resulting in an increase in sales for a business (1).*
>
> **Answers that list more than one method with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark.**"

### ⭐ The repeat-the-question rule — now confirmed on a 3-marker

### Exemplar A — **3 marks**

> *"Above the line promotion is using the media. A television advert may be used in order to advertise products and influence people to buy them. As a result the business can experience a rise in sales and revenue."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response clearly states television as the method of above the line promotion. **The first sentence is not needed in this response. The candidate has simply repeated the question, therefore wasting time and space.** The candidate has then explained this method and how it can influence people to buy products and further developed this by explaining that this would results in the business experiencing a rise in sales and revenue, thus gaining the full marks available."*

**Note: it still scored 3/3.** The wasted sentence was survivable **here** only because the answer was otherwise complete. On a longer question, the same habit is what produces the time pressure that costs the conclusion — and with it, AO4.

**The rule now has confirmations at every tariff:**

| Tariff | Source | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **This question** | *"simply repeated the question, therefore wasting time and space"* |
| all | Pearson support page | *"Do not repeat the question at the start of your answer. It does not score any marks and wastes valuable time."* |
| 12 | Nov 2024 / Jun 2022 P1R | *"wastes valuable time"* |

### Exemplar B — **1 mark**

> *"Social media can be used to promote its products. For example: using platforms such as facebook and instagram to advertise your products."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response clearly states one method of above the line promotion a business could use. **The candidate has then gone on to give examples of social media rather than develop on the given method, thus gaining only one mark for the method stated.**"*

### ⚠️ "For example…" is not development

This is a distinct failure mode from listing, and it deserves its own warning. The candidate **did not list a second method** — they stayed on one method, exactly as instructed. They still scored 1/3, because what followed the method was an **illustration** (*"platforms such as facebook and instagram"*) rather than an **explanation**.

**The test:** *"For example"* adds an instance. *"This means"* adds a consequence.

> **Rule: after naming your method, the next word should be "This means…" or "This allows…" — never "For example…".** Examples earn nothing; consequences earn the 2 marks.

# Q2(f) — Justify (9 marks)

> *Backyard Shoez wants to motivate its employees. It is considering two options.*
> **Option 1:** bonus payments · **Option 2:** job enrichment.

### Mark scheme — AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 (verbatim, abridged)

| | Indicative content |
|---|---|
| **AO2** | • Option 1 – Backyard Shoez could give bonuses for the person who has achieved the highest shoes sales within a month.<br>• Option 2 – Backyard Shoez could use job enrichment to give their employees greater responsibilities within the shoe shop. |
| **AO3** | • Option 1 – The employees may be encouraged to work harder and compete against each other to gain the highest shoe sales for the bonus.<br>• Option 2 – This will motivate employees as they can see they are being given the opportunity to use their abilities within the shoe shop as they are trusted by Backyard Shoez. |
| **AO4** | • Option 1 – **However**, if an employee cannot continually achieve the highest shoes sales then they may become demotivated and this could have the opposite effect.<br>• Option 2 – **However**, some employees may resent not being chosen for job enrichment as therefore may become demotivated. |

**Again the AO4 row is written as a concession — and here the mark scheme actually prints the word *"However"*.** Three separate mark schemes in this bank now show AO4 as "yes, but…".

### Exemplar A — **Level 3 – 7 marks** (bottom of Level 3)

> *"Backyard Shoe Should use job enrichment to motivate workers. Firstly, job enrichment is a cheaper way to motivate employees than bonus payments because they don't have to pay anything to the workers. Therefore it helps to save the money for the business. Backyard Shoes. In addition, job enrichment can motivate workers in long-term because superiors can give different jobs everyone to the employees. For example instead of just selling handbags and shoes, businesses can let them involve in marketing, promotions maybe to make their job more interesting. Whereas for bonuses, Backyard Shoes can only give one or two bonuses per year, and not everyone can get it, so some workers that didn't get it might be motivated because they might think they did a good job selling handbags and shoes, but still can't get one. Also for the people who got the bonuses, they won't be motivated for very long because they know within a year there is not going to be another bonus. Therefore, workers might not be motivated again. Backyard Shoes will always have different work to do such as importing goods like handbags or shoes, also doing promotions. So they always have jobs that they can give to subordinates to enrich their job. So the workers will be motivated for longer. So job enrichment is the one they should use."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response clearly shows an understanding of the two motivational methods. The candidate has shown **detailed application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts throughout the response**. This response shows **detailed interconnected points with logical chains of reasoning**. There is **good justification of why each method should or should not be used** by the business.*
> *The candidate has started to make a judgement based on both options, which moves the response into level 3. **Had this candidate provided a thorough evaluation of the information presented, then they would have gained further marks.***
> **This response is limited to the bottom of level 3.**"

### ⭐ Note the examiner is quoting the rubric back

*"detailed application… **throughout**"* — the Level 3 AO2 descriptor.
*"detailed interconnected points with **logical** chains of reasoning"* — the Level 3 AO3 descriptor.
*"**started** to make a judgement"* — short of Level 3's *"clear justification based on a thorough evaluation"*.

**7/9 is a perfect Level 3 on AO2 and AO3 and a Level 2 on AO4.** The candidate did the hard part — the answer has genuine context (*"handbags and shoes"*, *"importing goods"*), a real comparison between the options, and it turns against its own choice (*"for the people who got the bonuses, they won't be motivated for very long"*). **It lost two marks purely on the conclusion.**

> **This is the highest-value script in the file.** It shows that a well-argued, well-contextualised, balanced answer can still finish at the *bottom* of Level 3. Everything except the final judgement is already right — and the final judgement is two marks.

### Exemplar B — **Level 2 – 4 marks**

> *"Bonus payments may be better, as staff will feel rewarded for any additional hard work, and will have more disposable income. If staff have more disposable income, they can treat themselves causing them to be generally happier and more motivated. They will also want to work harder, as they know they will be rewarded for doing so. Job enrichment may not be for everyone. Not everyone is in it for promotion, and they will be unable to reward every member of staff, as they can not promote everyone. Some people are generally financially motivated, and will definitely be willing to work harder and better in order to recieve their bonus."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response shows an understanding of both methods of motivation. The candidate has gone on to explain the impact each of these methods can have on the business as well as the employee being rewarded.*
> *Some justification of each method of motivation been given albeit with some inconsistencies.*
> ***There is no attempt to make any judgement or evaluation of either method.***
> *To move towards level 3, this response would require application to given business in the scenario with regards to their **business activity** and how either method would impact this activity. Each point made needs to be fully developed. This candidate would also need to **make a clear decision on which method they would advise Backyard Shoez to use.**"*

### ⚠️ Two separate fatal gaps, both named by the examiner

1. **No judgement at all.** *"There is no attempt to make any judgement or evaluation."* — AO4 is a **required**, not optional, component. A Justify answer without a decision cannot leave Level 2 however good the argument.
2. **No business activity in the context.** The comment *"with regards to their business activity"* is the rubric's *"detailed application… throughout"* failing. Note that the candidate **did** write "Backyard Shoez" once — and still failed AO2. **Sixth confirmation that naming the business is not context.**

Note also response B **makes a decision in its first two words** — *"Bonus payments may be better"* — yet the examiner says there is no judgement. **A one-line claim is not a judgement.** A judgement requires *justification*, and the rubric asks for it to rest on a *thorough evaluation*. **Decide, then justify the decision.**

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> **This response is limited to the top of level 1.**"

### ⚠️ An important qualification to the "one factor in both directions" technique

This bank has established, with two verified 6/6 scripts, that **one factor analysed in both directions can score full marks** (Jun 2022 P1R Q1(g) and Q3(d)).

**Here the examiner asks for "at least two main benefits".** Both are true, because the two situations differ:

| Question shape | What full marks needs |
|---|---|
| *"Analyse the importance/impact/benefits **of X to Business Y**"* — a factor question | **one factor, developed in both directions** can reach 5–6 |
| *"Analyse the **benefits** of using [a tool/technique] to do [a task]"* — a benefits question | the examiner expects **at least two distinct benefits**, each developed |

**The reconciliation:** what never works is **two undeveloped points**. What always works is **depth**. If one factor gives you enough material to run four or five logical links (benefit → mechanism → business consequence → counter-direction), that is a Level 3 answer. If you cannot find that depth in one factor, take two and develop both.

> **Practical rule: depth over breadth — but if you stall, add a second factor and develop it fully. Never add an undeveloped one.**

# Q4(b) — Analyse (6 marks)

The candidate wrote **"suppliers"**. The fix is **"shoe suppliers"**. Two words — and the examiner says it is the difference between Level 1 and Level 2/3.

# Q4(c) — Evaluate (12 marks)

> ***Evaluate** the importance of primary market research to Backyard Shoez. You should use the information provided as well as your own knowledge of business.*

### Mark scheme — AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 (verbatim)

| | Indicative content |
|---|---|
| **AO1** | • Market research can be conducted by the business itself in the form of a questionnaire.<br>• The business could chose to use other methods that suit their business like focus groups. |
| **AO2** | • Backyard Shoez could question customers in the mall on their needs and wants for shoes.<br>• Backyard Shoez will be able to speak directly to customers who buy their shoes who will then feel valued. |
| **AO3** | • Therefore, using primary market research Backyard Shoez can find out exactly what they need and work out ways of meeting those needs.<br>• Focus groups will give Backyard Shoez qualitative data which can help with the design of new shoes. |
| **AO4** | • **However**, it can be time consuming to find out what customers' needs are and if these are already being met by the competitors in the local area then no amount of market research will help them to target their new audience.<br>• **However**, without doing secondary market research as well then Backyard Shoez cannot find out wider research such as information on competitors and suppliers. |

**Note the AO1 row.** At 12 marks AO1 is worth 3 — and the AO1 content is **the most basic, textbook material on the page**: *a questionnaire*, *focus groups*. **The 12-marker pays 3 marks for knowledge you already have.** Do not over-invest in it; the AO4 row is where the answer is decided.

### Exemplar A — **Level 3 – 10 marks**

*(Full response in `02_Verified_Exemplars.md`.)*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response demonstrates clear and accurate knowledge of primary market research throughout. The candidate understands different business concepts and issues using primary market research. This response shows good application to the business scenario presented to the candidate. **Context is shown through terms such as 'designs of shoes and clothes' and 'rivals in shopping centre.'***
> *This response shows the **counterargument** of the initial arguments presented in the first part of the answer. The points made by this candidate show logical chains of reasoning to each point presented.*
> *This candidate has **started** to make a judgement with some justification based on the evaluation of the business information given.*
> *To move further into level 3, this response needs to have a **thorough evaluation placing importance, or not, on primary market research to Backyard Shoez**.*
> **This response is limited to the middle of level 3.**"

### ⭐ The examiner names the exact phrases that earned context

> *"Context is shown through terms such as **'designs of shoes and clothes'** and **'rivals in shopping centre'**."*

**This is the examiner showing you, concretely, what a context mark looks like on the page.** Not "Backyard Shoez". Not "products". *"Designs of shoes and clothes"* and *"rivals in the shopping centre"* — two specific facts from the case, used as the subject of a chain.

> **Copy these two phrases as templates:**
> — **designs of [the case study's product]** (what it makes/sells)
> — **rivals in [the case study's location/market]** (who it competes with)

**Note also the word "started":** *"has started to make a judgement."* 10/12 is a strong answer that has **not yet** delivered the *thorough* evaluation Level 3's top requires. **The last two marks on a 12-marker are the hardest two marks in the paper** — and this is the same finding as the 7/9 scripts.

### Exemplar B — **Level 1 – 4 marks** (top of Level 1) — full verbatim text

> *"Primary market research is research done in person or face to face. Knowledge gained by asking or seeing in person.*
>
> *Primary research is important as it would help Backyard Shoez in making decisions. It would help them know what to improve, how to improve, new development ideas. Primary research can be done by questionnaires. They can interview their loyal and frequent customers. It will help Backyard Shoez to improve their customer services and production. Backyard Shoez can conduct primary market research before launching their new product eg the shoes of men and children. The research will provide them answers about whether it will be a profitable idea or result in loss. It will surely help the business for their new product launch.*
>
> *Therefore primary research would be quite important to Backyard Shoez."*

**Examiner's comment (verbatim):**

> *"This response provides plenty of knowledge of primary market research through the use of questionnaires and interviews. There is **very limited analysis** throughout this response with **little reference to the business**, therefore showing limited application of the knowledge shown. **There is a very basic judgement made at the end** of the response.*
> *To gain further marks, this response would need to have **more context other than the reference to the launch of the new products**. Each point made about the importance of primary market research needs to be **expanded upon with chains of reasoning**, referring back to the point made. It would also be helpful if a **sound evaluation** was provided to summarise how important primary market research would be to a business, like Backyard Shoez.*
> **This response is limited to the top of level 1.**"

### ⚠️ Study this response — it is the most complete catalogue of failure in the bank

The candidate did **all the things students are told to do**:

| What they did | Why it did not work |
|---|---|
| opened with a definition | AO1 content, and at 12 marks AO1 is only 3 — and *"research done in person"* is not even the mark-scheme AO1 item (*questionnaire*, *focus groups*) |
| named the business (×4) | *"little reference to the business"* |
| used a case fact (*"the shoes of men and children"*) | the examiner allows only *"the reference to the launch of the new products"* — **one** reference, for a question that demands application *throughout* |
| reached a profit consequence (*"profitable idea or result in loss"*) | undeveloped — attached to nothing |
| wrote a conclusion (*"Therefore primary research would be quite important to Backyard Shoez"*) | *"a very basic judgement"* — it states a verdict with **no justification and no balance** |
| wrote 130 words | the 10/12 answer is roughly three times as long |

**Result: 4/12 — the top of Level 1.** Not because of a knowledge gap, and not because of bad English, but because **not one chain was developed and the conclusion contained no reasoning**.

> **The 12-marker is decided by AO3 and AO4 — 6 of its 12 marks.** This candidate wrote a competent, well-organised, factually sound answer containing **no chains and no real judgement**, and scored a third of the marks. Every bullet in the table above is a habit worth breaking.

### What a "basic judgement" is, precisely

The examiner's phrase *"a very basic judgement"* is a technical description. Compare:

| | Verdict | Justification | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **"Therefore primary research would be quite important to Backyard Shoez."** (4/12) | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ |
| **"To move further into level 3, this response needs to have a thorough evaluation placing importance, or not, on primary market research"** (the 10/12 answer's needed step) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

**A judgement is not a verdict.** The rubric asks for *"a clear justification based on a thorough evaluation"* — a verdict, plus the reasoning that produced it, plus the case against.

The examiner again names the exact words that earned the context marks: **"small cakes"** and **"fine dining"**. Not "Lavender". **The product and the type of business.**

**And note the second paragraph:** *"The final points made in this response are not necessary."* **You can write too much on a 6-marker** — once the chains are complete, extra material adds no marks and costs time.

> *To gain further marks, candidates must write in the context of the business scenario set.*

### ⭐⭐ The "one word replaced" test

The examiner's diagnosis is extraordinarily specific:

> *"Had the candidate replaced terms such as **'product'** with **'food'**, it would have helped them move further into level 2 with the potential of a **level 3** response."*

**One word.** "Product" → "food". **A two-level jump, potentially.**

And note what the examiner **concedes**: *"The candidate has demonstrated the effects… outlined two valid points… has been able to show logical chains of reasoning."* **The AO3 work is done and credited.** The answer has:

- ✔ two valid points
- ✔ logical chains
- ✔ names the business ("Lavender", twice)
- ✔ reaches revenue and profit
- ✗ **zero contextual nouns**

**3/6 — bottom of Level 2.** This is now the **seventh** independent confirmation of the same rule, and the cleanest, because the examiner reduces the entire fix to a single lexical substitution.

For each one, ask whether the case study supplies a more specific word. **Wherever it does, use it.** "Product" becomes "food". "Suppliers" becomes "shoe suppliers".
