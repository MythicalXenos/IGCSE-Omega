**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):** *"**(b)** Define the term **person specification**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2 MS p. 6):** *"1 (b) · AO1 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of person specification.*

> • *Details of the qualifications, experience in the person appointed (1)*

*(1)"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 4):** *"This question provided to be more difficult for some candidates to answer, by not being able to define the term person specification. Less than a quarter of the cohort gained the mark available. **Candidates should also try and avoid using the term in their response, this will help them ensure that they have answered the question asked.** It is worth practising these questions to allow candidates to understand the difference between a definition and an explanation of the term."*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim, report p. 5 — the opening word is heavily cancelled in the original):**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"… when a business takes lots of ~~m~~aplicants and ~~separates~~ them into smaller groups. Then chooses a select few that the business believes would be of greater good for the business."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response is not giving a definition of person specification but what a business does with the applications received. This is not a definition and therefore not worthy of a mark."*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim, report p. 6):**

> *"Person specification means the specific requirements a business needs when hiring a new employee this could be level of experience, education, ~~qual~~ etc:"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response clearly gives a definition of person specification."*

### 🔬 THE 0-MARK ANSWER DESCRIBES A PROCEDURE; THE MARK IS FOR THE LIST

⚠️ **Read the 0-mark script against the scheme's single word — *"Details"*.** The candidate has written **the recruitment process**: applicants arrive, are *"separates[d] into smaller groups"*, a *"select few"* are chosen. **Nothing in it is a specification.** ⚠️ **It is also describing the wrong document — that chain is a shortlisting or selection procedure, not a person specification, which is the sheet of requirements written *before* anyone applies.**

⭐⭐ **AND HERE IS THE OTHER HALF OF THE REPORT'S TIP, WHICH LOOKS CONTRADICTORY AND IS NOT.** The tip says *"avoid using the term in their response"*; **the 1-mark script opens with the term** — *"Person specification means…"*. **Both are right.** The tip is against **using the term to define itself** — *"quality control is when you control quality"* (see Q1(c) below, twice) — **not against naming the term as the subject of your sentence.** *"X means …"* is a definition; *"… is the control of quality"* is a circle.

⭐ **And look at the shape of the paid definition: a category (*"requirements"*), a qualifier (*"specific"*), a recipient (*"a business"*), a moment (*"when hiring a new employee"*) — and then two examples, *"level of experience, education"***, before a trailing colon where a third was abandoned. **Examples here are safe because the definition is already complete** — the scheme's own bullet is a list of *"details"*. ⚠️ **Contrast P1R Q1(c), where examples of stakeholders with no definition earned nothing.** **Examples appended to a definition are evidence; examples INSTEAD of a definition are a substitution — and the examiner says so on the next question, twice.**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2 MS p. 7):** *"1 (e) · A02 - 2 marks*

> *= 31.38 (1)*

*Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula. Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working. (2)"*

Candidates had to calculate to 2 decimal places the gross profit marking for 2020. This question was well answered by candidates and the majority were able to gain the two marks available."*

**The first 1/2 script (verbatim, report p. 13):**

> *"Gross profit margin / Revenue × 100"*
> *"11 725 / 37368 × 100 = 31.377"*
>
> ***(answer line:)*** *"31 …        %"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"The candidate has clearly understood how to work the calculation out and but gave the percentage as a whole number as opposed to what the question asked – 2 decimal places."*

**The second 1/2 script (verbatim, report p. 14 — the dotted answer line is EMPTY):**

> *"gross profit margin = 11 725 / 37368 x 100"*
> *"31.3771"*
> *"31.4"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"Again the candidate has carried out the correct calculation but rounded the answer to one decimal place."*

### 🔬 TWO CANDIDATES REACH 31.377 AND 31.3771 — AND BOTH SCORE 1/2 FOR THE SAME REASON

⭐⭐ **THIS IS THE BANK'S CLEANEST PAIR ON THE PRECISION RULE, AND NEITHER SCRIPT FAILS AT ANYTHING MATHEMATICAL.**

| | first script — p. 13 | second script — p. 14 |
|---|---|---|
| formula | ✅ *"Gross profit margin / Revenue × 100"* | ✅ *"gross profit margin = gross profit / revenue × 100"* |
| substitution | ✅ *"11 725 / 37368 × 100"* | ✅ *"11 725 / 37368 x 100"* |
| raw value | ✅ **31.377** | ✅ **31.3771** |
| the answer the question demanded | **31.38** | **31.38** |
| what was written | ❌ **31** — *"a whole number"* | ❌ **31.4** — *"one decimal place"* |
| where it was written | **on the answer line** | **in the box; the answer line is blank** |
| result | **1/2** | **1/2** |

⭐⭐ **THE EXACT SAME ONE-MARK LOSS AS P1R Q3(c), IN THE SAME SERIES — `22.5` where `22.55` was demanded.** ⚠️ **That is now three scripts in May 2023 losing one of two calculation marks purely to premature rounding, each with the correct value already written on the line above.**

⭐ **And the pair answers the question every candidate asks about the dotted line.** ⚠️ **The second script leaves the answer line EMPTY and is not penalised for that at all — the examiner finds *"31.4"* in the box and comments only on its precision.** The cohort commentary *recommends* the dotted line (*"so that the examiners can clearly see what their answer is"*), and Paper 2R proves the corollary outright — a correct answer written anywhere in the box scores full marks. **What costs a mark is not WHERE the number is written; it is how many decimal places it has.**

> ⭐ **THE THREE-LINE METHOD THAT PASSES BOTH SCRIPTS, AND COSTS NOTHING:**
> 1. write the raw value the calculator gives — **31.3771**;
> 2. round it **on its own line**, as P1 Q4(a)'s 2/2 script does — **= 31.38**;
> 3. copy the rounded value to the answer line, **with the unit printed on the paper** — **31.38 %**.
>
> ⚠️ **Both candidates did step 1 perfectly. Neither did step 2. The arithmetic in this pair is not the skill being tested — the last ten seconds are.**

⚠️ **One more detail: the first script's formula line is written *"Gross profit margin / Revenue"* — the candidate has put the WORDS from the question above and below the fraction bar and then multiplied by 100, which is exactly the formula sheet's structure.** *"It is pleasing to see candidates are making more use of the formulas given at the start of the paper"* is this report's refrain (P1 Q3(c), Q4(a)). **The formula sheet cannot prevent a rounding error, but it is why these two scripts have a mark at all.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **0/1 on a 1 mark question** · Q3(a) *Define the term overdraft* — report p. 38

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2 MS p. 16):** *"3 (a) · A01 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of overdraft.*

> • *An agreement to draw more money from a bank account that it has in it (1)*

*(1)"*

> *"Overdrafts are ~~a~~ loans that business may use from the bank."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"Quite a number of candidates confused overdraft with loan and therefore did not gain the mark available."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 38):** *"Another 'Define' question where candidates were asked to define the term 'overdraft'. ### 🔬 THIS IS NOT A CIRCULAR DEFINITION — IT IS A CATEGORY ERROR, AND THE SCHEME'S WORD IS *"MORE"*

⚠️ **Look closely: the script does not recycle the word *"overdraft"* to define itself — it swaps the term for a NEIGHBOUR.** *"…are loans that business may use from the bank."* ⭐ **The scheme's whole distinction is in one word: *"draw MORE money from a bank account that it has in it".*** **An overdraft is not a sum borrowed and lent; it is permission to take the balance BELOW ZERO on an account you already hold.** ⚠️ **A loan is a new lump of money; an overdraft is a negative balance. The candidate has the bank right and the mechanism wrong.**

⭐ **And the cohort statistic is the most interesting number on this paper: *"Most candidates understood what the term meant"* — nearly two thirds still failed.** ⚠️ **An examiner is not marking whether you understand. He is marking whether the words on the page could be used by a reader who does not.**

⭐ **Both this and the two Q1(c) zeros are the same instruction read from opposite sides:**
> * **Q1(c) 0/1 #1 and #2 reused the term** — knowledge likely present, definition circular.
> * **Q3(a) refused the term altogether and substituted a cousin** — knowledge approximately present, definition wrong at the decisive word.
>
> ⚠️ **Rule 42 catches the first; only reading the scheme's exact noun catches the second. Neither failure looks like ignorance in the exam hall — which is why *"most candidates understood"* and *"nearly two thirds … not gaining"* are both true at once.**

> • *If a government reported that it was going to reduce the amount of money people have to spend by increasing income tax (1) this could mean that people might not purchase more furniture for their home (1)*

***NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of IKEA.** Accept any other appropriate response. (2)"*

**The script (verbatim):**

> *"To find keep updated and to expect any changes to legislation / law so they can adopt quickly and not face a set-back."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"There is no context in this response and therefore did not gain a mark."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 40):** *"An 'Outline' question which is similar to a 'State' question where candidates have to put their response in the context of the business. 'Outline' questions are AO2 (Application) questions. **To score the marks available there must be a development in the response as well as being applied to the scenario.** In most cases candidates will give a correct response but it will not be in context. Nearly half of the cohort did not gain a mark on this question."*

### 🔬 A COMPLETE TWO-MARK CHAIN — DEVELOPMENT AND ALL — THAT SCORES ZERO

⚠️ **Read the structure of the script, because structurally it is exactly what an Outline answer should look like:**

| step | the candidate's words |
|---|---|
| **the reason** | *"To find keep updated"* |
| **the development** | *"to expect any changes to legislation / law"* |
| **the consequence — the second half of the chain** | *"so they can adopt quickly and not face a set-back"* |

⭐⭐ **THE CHAIN IS THE RIGHT LENGTH, THE CONSEQUENCE IS REAL, AND THE SCORE IS 0/2 — because the whole of it could have been written about any business on earth.** ⚠️ **A government report on legislation affects every employer; the scheme's marked route runs instead through the one thing IKEA sells: *"increase income tax (1) … people might not purchase more furniture for their home (1)".*** **The examiner's award is for the LINK — and here the link is missing at both ends.**

⭐ **And note how much of the two-mark question is answered by the MS's *"furniture for their home"*** — five words, on a question *"nearly half of the cohort did not gain a mark"* on. ⚠️ **Compare the two 2/2s already banked from this series: P1 Q3(b) earned its second mark with *"chocolates"*, P1 Q4(a) with nothing but a correctly rounded number. This paper's tier needs a NOUN from the case study or a NUMBER from the formula — there is no third way.**

⚠️ **One last observation about the script's own order of operations: it also does what no chain should do — it puts the action first and the reason second in the wrong sequence.** *"To find keep updated"* — two verbs without an object — is the weakest phrase in the answer, **and the examiner's comment does not mention it. On an AO2 question, everything else is invisible once context is absent: the examiner never reaches the analysis, because the mark for it is in the same place as the first mark — the scenario.**

## 🔬 THE PAPER IN ONE PARAGRAPH — and what it adds to the bank

**Thirteen scripts; four marks in total awarded out of a possible eighteen.** Paper 2 is the archive's study of **the answer that is right and unplaced**: twelve of the thirteen printed responses are described by the examiner in the language of adequacy — *"a very valid response"*, *"understands the purpose"*, *"clearly understood how to work the calculation out"*, *"carried out the correct calculation"* — and eleven of the thirteen scored 1 or 0. **The exception is the pair at Q1(e), and its adequacy is also what spoils it: both candidates reached the right number and neither reached the right precision.** ⚠️ **Taken with Paper 1R — where a candidate named the business twice and scored 0/1 — this paper's five context failures settle a rule the bank can now state without qualification: on a `State` or `Outline` question IKEA is in the question, so naming IKEA is worth nothing; the product (*furniture*, *tables and chairs*, *flat packs*), the venue (*"the flower shop"*, *"the florists … Paris"*), or the number on the answer line is worth the mark.**
