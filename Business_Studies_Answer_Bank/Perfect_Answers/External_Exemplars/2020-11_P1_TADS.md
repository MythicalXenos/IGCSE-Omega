> *"Learners showed clear knowledge of the two options for TADS but often were unaware there are no AO1 marks available for these questions and they are being tested on application (AO2), analysis (AO3) and evaluation (AO4) skills… **Learners should practice writing more concise answers to these questions as the lines available are more than adequate for such a question but often learners are using additional papers without gaining additional marks.** When additional paper is used candidates must indicate they are doing so as marking is completed online and examiners may not be aware additional paper is being used."*

## ⭐ THE CONCISE-ANSWER RULE

> **"the lines available are more than adequate for such a question but often learners are using additional papers without gaining additional marks."**

**Extra paper earned nothing.** Candidates wrote longer answers than the space provided and scored the same as they would have scored inside it.

This is a **practical, exam-hall rule** and it is the same finding as the 400-word/8-mark finding in the June 2019 Paper 2 booklet — stated here even more plainly:

| The belief | The reality |
|---|---|
| More is safer | **More is neutral at best and costly at worst** |
| Running out of lines is a problem | The lines are *"more than adequate"* |
| Extra paper shows effort | Extra paper earns *"additional marks"*: **none** |

> **Rule: if you are reaching for extra paper on a Justify or Evaluate question, you have almost certainly not planned your answer.** The marks you are missing are in the conclusion you have not written yet, not in another paragraph of analysis.

**And the administrative point recurs:** *"marking is completed online and examiners may not be aware additional paper is being used."* If you do continue, **label it**.

### ⭐⭐ Q1(d) — the applied answer, quoted by the examiner

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"not a well answered question; **only one third of candidates gained a mark** for this question. Candidates were able to identify a benefit of investing computer technology but **few understood the answer needed to be in the context of TADS**. **Those who answered it well were able to link to the improvement in the quality of musical sound.**"*

**The examiner gives you the model answer.** A benefit of computer technology in a **drum school** is *"the improvement in the quality of musical sound"* — a fact that exists **only because the business is a music school**.

**That is what application looks like, and it is one clause long.**

> **Ninth confirmation of the context rule** — and the first where the examiner supplies the ideal wording rather than only criticising. Copy the *shape*: **[correct generic point] → [the case-study-specific consequence].**

### ⭐ Q1(b) — defining the broader term

> *"half of candidates correctly define the term **fixed costs** although **some candidates just defined costs as opposed to fixed costs**."*

**Defining "costs" is not defining "fixed costs".** The definition must contain the distinguishing feature — that the cost **does not change with output**.

> **Fixed costs:** costs that **do not vary with the level of output** — such as rent, insurance and salaries.
> **The test:** does your definition distinguish it from the other members of its family? If it would also be true of variable costs, it is not a definition of fixed costs.

### ⭐ Q1(c) — the confusion pair

> *"two thirds of candidates were able to correctly define the term **stakeholder**, although **some candidates are still confusing this with the term shareholder**."*

| | Who it is |
|---|---|
| **Shareholder** | an **owner** — someone who holds shares in the company |
| **Stakeholder** | **anyone affected by or interested in** the business — employees, customers, suppliers, the local community, **and shareholders** |

**Note the relationship:** every shareholder is a stakeholder; not every stakeholder is a shareholder. Writing "someone who owns shares" for *stakeholder* is the single documented error on this question. **Fourth instance in this bank of a candidate defining a neighbouring term.**

### Q1(e) — the decimal rule (8th confirmation)

It would also be helpful for centres to remind candidates that **some formulas are provided at the front of the paper**, whilst most candidates did use these there are still some that are not."*

### ⭐ Q1(f) — the listing cap (4th confirmation)

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"**One third of candidates gained a mark for identifying a disadvantage** of being in a partnership but then they would not develop their answers. Those who were able to identify a disadvantage of being in a partnership and go on to explain this disadvantage saw them achieve **all of the 3 marks** available. **Candidates who list more than one way can only gain one mark.**"*

### ⚠️ Q2(e) — a genuine content gap: interest rates

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"just over half of the candidates gained 2 or more marks… **There are still a lot of candidates who did not know what the impact of an increase in interest rates on a business would be due to lack of knowledge in this area of the specification.**"*

**The fourth instance in this bank of interest rates being a cohort weak point** (also Nov 2021 Q1(g), Nov 2023, and the Nov 2021 report). It is examined repeatedly because it is a **government policy affecting business** (spec 1.6).

> **Learn the direction of travel, both ways:**
> **Interest rates ↑** → borrowing becomes **more expensive** → repayments on existing loans **rise** → costs **increase** → profit **falls** → firms **postpone investment/expansion** → consumers have less disposable income → **demand falls**.
> **Interest rates ↓** → borrowing **cheaper** → investment **more affordable** → **expansion cheaper** → but savers earn less → *disposable income may fall*.
>
> **Do not leave this as "it affects them". Name the mechanism.**

More able candidates accessed **level 3** by **considering their chosen option in the context of TADS and how this would impact the retention of the teachers, linking their points together and concluding their answer with balance and justification.**"*

**Note the phrase this time: *"how this would impact the retention of the teachers."*** The case-study purpose is not decoration — **retention is the thing the question is about**, and the chains must land on it.

| Part | Command | Topic | Cohort |
|---|---|---|---|

### Q3(a) — liquidity, and the confusion

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"just over one third of candidates were able to define the term **liquidity**. Those who did not gain the mark **often confused liquidity with making money** or were not able to answer the question."*

> **Liquidity:** the ability of a business to **pay its short-term debts as they fall due** — i.e. whether it has enough **cash and current assets** to meet its **current liabilities**.

**"Making money" is profitability, not liquidity.** A profitable business can be illiquid (all its money tied up in stock and debtors) and an unprofitable one can be liquid. **That distinction is the whole point of the term** — and it is the *fifth* instance in this bank of defining a neighbouring concept.

### ⭐⭐ Q3(b) — the single most useful rule in this report

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"**one fifth of candidates gained the full 2 marks** available. Those who did gain the full marks correctly identified a financial objective that TADS may have. Those who did not often **confused financial objectives with non-financial objectives** whilst **others echoed the question by suggesting revenue**. **Some candidates went on to explain what financial objectives are — the question is asking candidates for a possible financial objective as there are no knowledge marks available.**"*

### ⚠️ Three distinct failures, each worth internalising

**1. Confusing financial with non-financial objectives.**
**Financial objectives:** survival · **profit** · **sales** · **market share** · financial security.
**Non-financial:** social objectives · personal satisfaction · challenge · independence · control.

**2. "Others echoed the question by suggesting revenue."**
The question asks for an objective. **"Revenue" is not an objective** — it is a measure. An objective is something the business *wants to achieve*: *"to increase revenue by 20% next year"*. This is the **echoing-the-question** failure — giving back the question's own vocabulary.

**3. "Some candidates went on to explain what financial objectives are."**
**A two-mark Outline asks for the objective, not the concept.** The examiner is explicit: *"there are no knowledge marks available."* **Do not define the thing you were asked to give.**

### 🎯 The Outline (2-mark) template

```
[the specific item asked for] + [it attached to the case study]
```

*"A financial objective for TADS could be **to increase profit** by filling more places in its **drum lessons**."* — objective **(1)**, context **(1)**.

**Not:** *"A financial objective is a goal relating to money…"* — knowledge, **0 marks**.

### Q3(c) — the multi-step calculation, again

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"a calculation question where **one third of candidates did not gain any marks** due to the lack of knowledge of calculating the closing balance on a cash flow forecast. Candidates who did gain full marks **successfully calculating the net cash flow therefore being able to calculate the closing balance**. **Some candidates limited themselves to 1 mark by only calculating the net cash flow.**"*

**Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow.** Candidates computed the net cash flow and stopped.

> **Sixth instance in this bank of the same error:** Q4(a) Nov 2020 · Q1(e) Nov 2021 · Q4(a) Nov 2021 · Q4(a) Jun 2022 P1R · **Q3(c) here** · Q1(e) Jun 2022 P2R.
>
> **The rule: count the steps the question asks for, then check you have taken all of them.** If the question says "closing balance", "total pay **once the bonus is added**", "cost **of delivering**", or "the discounted cost **of booking both**", there are two steps and the second one is worth the mark.

### Q3(d) — Analyse: two ways to be stuck at 2/6

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"**one quarter of candidates did not gain any marks** due to a lack of knowledge… or they have shown knowledge of what cash is but **with no application to TADS or any analytical points**. Those who assessed level 3 carefully considered the importance of cash to TADS whether that is **in relation to payment to various stakeholders** or **to prevent business failure**. Candidates were disadvantaged when they did not apply their answer to TADS as they **cannot access the top of level 2 without any application**."*

**Two model Level 3 routes:** *payment to stakeholders* (suppliers, workers) and *preventing business failure* (insolvency).

### Q3(e) — the Justify route, restated

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"More able candidates accessed level 3 by considering their chosen option in the context of TADS and how this would impact the business when deciding **which method of increase revenue to choose**, they have linked their points together and **concluded their answer with balance and justification**."*

# Question 4

| Part | Command | Topic | Cohort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4(a)** | Calculate | new minimum wage after a 2.5% increase | **"just one quarter"** got 2/2 |
| **4(b)** | Analyse (6) | benefits of **below-the-line** promotion | **"more than one third… did not gain any marks"** |
| **4(c)** | **Evaluate (12)** | TADS becoming a **private limited company** | level 1 / 2 / 3 |

### ⭐⭐ Q4(a) — half the cohort stopped one step early

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"a calculation question which saw **just one quarter of candidates gaining the full 2 marks** available. **Almost half of candidates have been able to successfully calculate the increase of 2.5% which saw them gain 1 mark but limited themselves to this as they did not calculate the new minimum wage as specified in the question.** Centres should encourage candidates to include their working out as often candidates gained a mark for their calculation despite their final answer being incorrect or missed out."*

**Nearly half the cohort computed the increase and never added it to the original.** They were asked for the **new** minimum wage. One mark of two.

> **The step-counting drill, formalised:**
> 1. Read the question.
> 2. Ask: **"what is the final thing being asked for?"**
> 3. Ask: **"what must I do to get there?"** — usually two operations.
> 4. **Do them both**, then write the final figure on the answer line.
>
> *"calculate the increase of 2.5%"* = step 1. *"calculate the new minimum wage"* = the answer.

### Q4(b) — a content gap: below-the-line promotion

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"a **poorly answered** question due to the **lack of knowledge of below-the-line promotion** — this has led to **more than one third of candidates not gaining any marks** on this question. Those candidates who analysed the benefits for TADS of using below-the-line promotion **often did not apply it to TADS** therefore could not access the top of level 2. Those who accessed level 3 did so by detailed analysis… **with some candidates linking it to other information given about the business throughout the paper**."*

### 📘 BELOW THE LINE vs ABOVE THE LINE — a genuine knowledge gap to close

| | **Above the line** | **Below the line** |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | promotion through **paid mass media** | promotion **not** through mass media — **direct, targeted** activity |
| Examples | television, radio, **newspapers/magazines**, cinema, outdoor/posters, internet display advertising | **sales promotions, special offers, discounts, coupons, point-of-sale displays, direct mail, sponsorship, product trials, packaging, merchandising** |
| Reaches | a **wide, undifferentiated** audience | a **targeted** audience |
| Cost | high absolute cost | cheaper, more measurable |

> **Memory hook:** *above the line* = you pay an agency to place it in **media**; *below the line* = you do it **yourself, directly, to a specific customer.** If it needs a media slot, it is above. If it is a discount, a sample, a display or a mailing, it is below.
>
> **The examiner's own Nov 2021 note confirms the pair:** the June 2019 P1 Q2(d) question asked for *"one method of **above the line** promotion"* and the mark scheme's example was **television advertising**.

**Note also the Level 3 route:** *"linking it to other information given about the business throughout the paper."* **At Level 3 on a 6-marker, you are expected to connect the point to other facts from the case** — not just to develop it in isolation. This is the rubric's *"detailed application… throughout"* in practice.

### Q4(c) — Evaluate: the two documented reasons, again

> *"centres are reminded that this is **the only level-based question in the paper which is equally weighted across all four assessment objectives**…*
> *Those who performed less well than expected did so for **one of two reasons**.*
> *Firstly, candidates provided a **generic answer with no application to TADS** or candidates would **go into detail outlining the various types of ownership**.*
> *Secondly, candidates did not provide a **thorough judgment** relating back to any issues that may occur for TADS **if they were to become a private limited company**, or they **did not balance their conclusion** therefore limiting their evaluation."*

**Identical wording to the Nov 2021 report.** Useful as confirmation rather than news — but note the second failure mode again: **laying out all the types of ownership**, i.e. dumping AO1 knowledge on a question that is testing judgement.

## The two content gaps this paper exposes
