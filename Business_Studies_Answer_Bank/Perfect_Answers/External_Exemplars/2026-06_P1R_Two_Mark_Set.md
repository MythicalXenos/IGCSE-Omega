# PAPER 1R

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q1(d) *State one reason why brand image is important to Spud Bros* — report pp. 5–6

**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):** *"**(d)** State **one** reason why brand image is important to Spud Bros."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 7):** *"1 (d) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a reason why brand image is important to Spud Bros.*

> • *So that people will buy more potatoes*

***NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of Spud Bros.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"It could increase amount of customers"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim, over the ResultsPlus logo):** *"This candidate only offers a reason why brand image is important to Spud Bros: there is no applied context. Total: 0 marks"*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim — the insertion sits above the line):**

> *"Having ~~high~~ ^High^ brand image can lead to ^new^ customers interest to try the potato from Spud Bros."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate provides a reason why brand image is important and also provides clear context within their response. Total: 1 mark"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"The question was the first 'State' question of the paper. The command word 'State' requires candidates to provide a contextual response to gain access to the available one mark, which is an AO2 mark. Therefore, for this question, simply stating a reason why brand image is important is not enough to receive 1 mark. It has to be a reason why brand image is important in the context of Spud Bros."*

🔬 **This is the cleanest 0-versus-1 demonstration of a rule the bank has now confirmed fifteen times — and it is the best one, because the losing answer is not wrong.** Brand image → more customers is **a perfectly good business reason.** It would earn the mark on 1(a) of any paper if the question had no case study in it. **It earns nothing here, and the examiner's reason is one clause: *"there is no applied context."***

| | 0/1 | 1/1 |
|---|---|---|
| the reason | *"increase amount of customers"* | *"customers interest to try the potato"* |
| business named? | **no** | yes |
| **the product named?** | **no** | **yes — *the potato*** |

⭐ **The mark scheme's own bullet is *"So that people will buy more potatoes"*.** Both the scheme and the candidate finish on the potato. **That is the whole difference: the 1-mark answer names the thing Spud Bros actually sells, and the 0-mark answer names nobody and nothing.**

⚠️ **And note the handwriting:** the 1-mark candidate crossed out *"high"* and inserted *"High"* above the line for a capital, **and inserted *"new"* — omitting it is a missing word, and the answer is still not a grammatical sentence.** 1 mark. Both corrections, and a missing word, in one marked answer — rule 55 again.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2 and 1/2** · Q1(e) *Calculate the retained profit left after expansion* — report pp. 6–7

**Question (verbatim, as printed):**

> *"A business had retained profits of $320 000.*
> *It used 20% of the retained profit for expansion of the business.*
> ***(e)** Calculate the retained profit left in the business following the expansion. You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 8):** *"1 (e) · A02 - 2 marks. Award 2 marks for the correct response of **$256 000** OR Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula.*
> *320 000 × 20% = 64 000 (1)*
> *320 000 × 80% (1)*
> *320 000 − 64000 (1)*

*(2)"*

**The 2/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 6):**

> *= 320,000 × 20%*
> *= 64000*
> *= 320,000 − 64000*
> *= 256000*
>
> *answer line: $ 256000*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This is the correct answer and is calculated accurately. Total: 2 marks"*

**The 1/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 7):**

> *320 000 × 20% = 64000*
>
> *answer line: $ 64000*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response illustrates the calculated 20% used retained profit. However the question requires candidates to calculate the remaining retained profit. Total: 1 mark"*

🔬 **⚠️ THIS IS THE MOST INSTRUCTIVE 1-MARK CALCULATION IN THE ARCHIVE, AND IT IS A MIRROR OF THE MAY 2023 PAIRS.** Both candidates did the same first step: *"320 000 × 20% = 64000"* — **and the 2/2 candidate wrote it first, too.** The difference is not the arithmetic. It is that one stopped.

> **The question asks for what is LEFT.** $64 000 is what was **spent**. It is *"the calculated 20% **used** retained profit"* — the examiner's own phrase, and the word *used* is doing all the work in that comment.

**The mark scheme actually provides two independent routes to the correct answer, and neither involves the subtraction being obvious:** *"320 000 × 80%"* reaches $256 000 directly; *"320 000 − 64000"* reaches it via the subtraction. **The 1-mark script took neither.**

⭐ **Read it against the scheme's own reward line: *"Award 2 marks for the correct response."*** The first mark is for *substituting numbers into a formula* — which this candidate did, correctly — and **the second is for the answer to the question that was asked, which this candidate never wrote.** ⚠️ **So the danger is not ignorance; it is answering a slightly easier question than the one on the page.**

**For contrast, rule 60 in this same set:** two June 2026 scripts did the arithmetic **correctly** and lost the second mark purely on the **answer line** (`13280.2` for `13280.25` — May 2023 P2 Q3(c)). **Here the answer line is right and the answer is wrong.** Both errors cost exactly one mark, and both are the same error: *the final line must state the value the question asked for.*

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2 and 1/2** · Q3(b) *Outline one reason why cash is important to Spud Bros* — report p. 24

**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):** *"**(b)** Outline **one** reason why cash is important to Spud Bros."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 16):** *"3 (b) · A02 - 2 marks. Award 2 marks for two linked points explaining one reason why cash is important to Spud Bros. Award 1 mark if the points are not linked or the answer is lacking context.*

> • *Spud Bros needs cash to pay for the ingredients (1) to ensure it can keep up with demand (1)*
> • *Spud Bros need cash to pay the employees (1) to enable them to continue to serve their hot baked potatoes (1)*

***NB For the full 2 marks the points must be linked AND include context of Spud Bros.** Accept any other appropriate response. (2)"*

**The 1/2 script (verbatim):**

> *"To pay their suppliers on time , so they retain trusts them."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response indicates a clear point with development, however there is no contextual link to Spud Bros. Total: 1 mark"*

**The 2/2 script (verbatim — two struck words are shown):**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Cash helps Spud Bros in paying back their ~~suppliers~~ ^suppliers^ on time which ~~can~~ can lead to a reliable and constant supply of "potatoes" ~~and~~ and "toppings"."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response includes an appropriate reason why cash is important, with context in the form of 'potatoes' and 'toppings', which is also developed. Total: 2 marks"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"This was an 'outline' question that assessed AO2. To receive 1 mark candidates must identify why cash was important to the business. For 2 marks the response needed to show development, whilst also linking back to the context of Spud Bros. Without a developed contextualised response candidates could access the full marks."* *(the report's own sentence is garbled; it reads as a missing "not" — read it as "could not access")*

🔬 **This is the sharpest matched pair in the tier, because the two answers are almost the SAME ARGUMENT.** Both say: cash → pay suppliers on time → a consequence.

| | 1/2 | 2/2 |
|---|---|---|
| chain | *pay suppliers on time* → *retain **trust*** | *pay suppliers on time* → *reliable and constant supply* |
| the two points linked? | **no — the second is about trust, not supply** | **yes — payment causes the supply** |
| context | **none** | *"**potatoes**"* and *"**toppings**"* |

⭐ **The mark scheme states two independent conditions and the 1/2 satisfies one**: *"Award 1 mark if the points are not linked **or** the answer is lacking context."* **The 1/2 fails both — the points do not link, and there is no context — but the examiner credits it the single mark for the "clear point with development".**

⚠️ **The 2-mark answer's context is TWO WORDS IN QUOTATION MARKS.** The candidate put *"potatoes"* and *"toppings"* in inverted commas — **literally marking out the context the examiner was looking for.** The scheme's own bullets end *"to continue to serve their hot baked potatoes"*: **the mark for context is bought by naming what the business buys or sells, and nothing else.**

⚠️ **The 2/2 script has two struck words and a grammatical error** (*"in paying back their suppliers"* — you pay a supplier, you do not pay them back). **2/2.** Rule 55 holds again.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2 and 1/2** · Q3(c) *Calculate the percentage increase in the minimum wage* — report pp. 25–26

**Question (verbatim, as printed):**

> *"**Figure 2** shows the minimum wage for workers aged 21 and over in the UK in 2024 and 2025.*
>
> | Year | Minimum wage (£) |
> |---|---|
> | 2024 | 11.44 |
> | 2025 | 12.21 |
>
> ***Figure 2***
>
> *Using Figure 2, calculate, to **two** decimal places, the percentage increase in the minimum wage between 2024 and 2025. You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 16):** *"3 (c) · A02 - 2 marks. Award 2 marks for the correct response of **6.73%** OR Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula.*
> *12.21 − 11.44 = 0.77 (1) OR*
> *0.77 / 11.44 × 100 (1)*

*(2)"*

**The 1/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 25 — its comment is on p. 26):**

> *"2024 = 11.44* ⟶ *(bracketed together, labelled)* *6.73*
> *2025 = 12.21* ⟶
>
> *12.21 ⊗ 11.44 = 0.77 ^difference^*  *(the struck mark is ambiguous)*
>
> *6.73 × 100 = 77.1*  *(or × /00 — the operator's first stroke is double-struck)*
>
> *answer line: 77 %*
> *⚠️ the division sign in the scheme's method and this stroke are both cramped; the marker's own comment settles what happened — the candidate did not calculate the percentage change, and the answer line reads **77**.*

**Examiner comment (verbatim, p. 26):** *"This candidate has calculated correctly the £0.77 difference in minimum wage between 2024 and 2025. However, this candidate has not been able to calculate the percentage change. Total: 1 mark"*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim, immediately beneath):** *"Remember to show your working-out."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 25):** *"This was a 'calculate' question: candidates were required to calculate the percentage increase in the minimum wage between 2024 and 2025. This is a good example of why centres should remind candidates to show their working-out. Whilst this candidate has not calculated the final answer correctly, due to their working out, and as per the mark scheme, they have gained access to 1 mark."*

**The 2/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 26):**

> *(12.21 − 11.44) / 11.44 × 100%*
> *= 6.73%*
>
> *answer line: 6.73 %*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response calculates effectively the percentage change, to 2 decimal places. Total: 2 marks"*

🔬 **⭐ THIS IS THE ARCHIVE'S CLEANEST PROOF OF WHY THE WORKINGS EXIST, AND THE EXAMINER SAYS SO IN TERMS:**

> *"Whilst this candidate has not calculated the final answer correctly, **due to their working out, and as per the mark scheme, they have gained access to 1 mark**."*

**The 1/2 candidate got the figure on the answer line WRONG and was paid anyway.** The mark scheme's first reward line is *"correctly substituting numbers into formula"*, and **12.21 − 11.44 = 0.77** is exactly one of its two listed substitutions. **Writing that line down converted a 0 into a 1.**

⚠️ **It cuts the other way too, and this is the point.** The same script **had already written the answer without knowing it**: its own left-hand column derives **6.73** from the two wages — the correct constant — and the candidate then multiplied that number by 100 and wrote **77**. **The examiner credited the step the SCHEME lists and not the one the candidate happened to have right in the margin.** The scheme is the thing being marked against.

⭐ **And the 2/2 is the same formula written as one line** — *(12.21 − 11.44) / 11.44 × 100%* — with the subtraction before the division, which is the whole of the difference. **One operation's placement, one mark.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2 and 1/2** · Q4(a) *Calculate the total costs for the business in 2025* — report pp. 36–37

**Question (verbatim, as printed):**

> *"**Figure 3** shows the costs and output of a business in 2025.*
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | Total fixed costs | $60 000 |
> | Variable costs per unit | $10 |
> | Quantity of units produced | 5 000 |
>
> ***Figure 3***
>
> *Using Figure 3, calculate the total costs for the business in 2025. You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P1R MS p. 20):** *"4 (a) · A02 - 2 marks. Award 2 marks for the correct response of **$110 000** OR Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula.*
> *10 × 5000 = 50 000 (1)*
> *or*
> *50 000 + 60 000 (1)*

*(2)"*

**The 1/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 37):**

> *5000 × 10 = 50000*
> *60 000 − 50000 =*
>
> *answer line: $ 10 000*
> *⚠️ the answer line reads **10 000**; the only operation the script completed before it is a subtraction.*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"Because the final answer is incorrect, **examiners are instructed to look through the working out to see if a mark can be allocated for working out.** This candidate has calculated the total variable costs correctly. Total: 1 mark"*

**The 2/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 37):**

> *Total Variable Cost = $10 × 5 000*
> *= $50 000*
> *Total Cost = $60 000  +  $50 000*
> *= $110 000*
>
> *answer line: $ 110 000*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response has calculates the total costs for the business in 2025 correctly. Total: 2 marks"*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim, directly beneath):** *"Show your working, as demonstrated here."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 37):** *"This was a 'calculate' question that required candidates to calculate the total costs for the business in 2025."*

🔬 **⭐ THE 1/2 SCRIPT IS THE MIRROR IMAGE OF Q1(e), AND PUTTING THE TWO SIDE BY SIDE TEACHES THE WHOLE TIER.**

| | Q1(e) 1/2 | Q4(a) 1/2 |
|---|---|---|
| the arithmetic | **right** | **right** — the scheme lists `10 × 5000 = 50 000` and the script has it |
| the operation performed | × then **−** (the right shape) | **−** on the answer line (the wrong operator) |
| what went wrong | **answered a different question** — the % used, not what is left | **the final quantity is not the total** |
| the mark | 1 of 2 | 1 of 2 |

**In both, the first reward line of the scheme was earned and the second was not** — and in both, the examiner explicitly reached back to the working to pay for it. **That is what *"Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula"* means in practice: half the marks on a calculation are available for showing the right numbers in the right places, before any answer is right.**

⚠️ **Note what the 1/2 candidate wrote: `60 000 − 50000`.** The scheme wants **+**. Subtracting the variable costs from the fixed costs is a plausible-looking line that produces a meaningless number, **and the following line is left blank — the candidate did not compute it.** The answer line nevertheless carries a value. **Whatever produced that value, the examiner's instruction is the same: ignore a wrong answer, go looking in the working, pay what the scheme lists.**

⚠️ **And the 2/2 handles the trap properly by LABELLING.** *"Total Variable Cost ="* … *"Total Cost ="* — **the two stages are named, so the marker can see that the second line adds the first to the fixed costs.** The scheme's own second substitution line is *"50 000 + 60 000"*. **Name the quantities and the like-for-like line is unmistakable.**

# WHAT THESE FIVE ADD TO THE FIRST BATCH

**The companion file's lessons were: context is the answer on a State question, match the mark scheme's wording on a Define, and put the final value on the dotted line.** These five add the other half of the tier:

1. **⭐ A wrong answer can still earn half the marks — if the scheme's own substitution line is on the page.** Q3(c) is the examiner stating it outright: *"due to their working out… they have gained access to 1 mark"* — **with the wrong figure on the answer line.** Two more scripts (Q1(e), Q4(a)) show the same rescue in action.
2. **⚠️ …but the workings are paid against the SCHEME's listed steps, not the candidate's good arithmetic.** Q3(c)'s candidate derived the correct 6.73 in the margin, multiplied it by 100, wrote 77, and scored 1 — **because the credited step was the subtraction, not the figure they had.** **Learn the scheme's substitution lines, not a lucky route.**
3. **⭐ The other 1-mark loss is not ignorance.** Q1(e)'s candidate did *"320 000 × 20% = 64000"* **exactly as the 2/2 candidate did**, then stopped — **they answered "how much was used" instead of "how much is left".** The examiner's word for it is *"used retained profit"*. **Re-read what the question actually asks for before writing the final line.**
4. **A context mark can cost two words.** Q3(b)'s 2/2 bought it with *"potatoes"* and *"toppings"*, in quotation marks the candidate wrote themselves; Q1(d)'s 1/1 bought it with *"the potato"*. **The business's own product, named, is the cheapest context in the qualification.**
5. **Struck-out words and broken grammar are everywhere in these full-mark scripts** — Q1(d)'s 1/1 corrects a capital and drops the word *"new"* mid-sentence, Q3(b)'s 2/2 carries two strikes and a wrong phrasal verb, Q4(a)'s 2/2 comment reads *"has calculates"*. **Rule 55, now confirmed on this tier as well.**
