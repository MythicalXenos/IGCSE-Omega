# THE REMAINING MAY 2023 FULL-MARK SCRIPTS — all eleven, transcribed

**Why this file exists.** `audit_er_fullmarks.py` asked all five archived examiner reports a question every report can answer — *which scripts did you award full marks to?* — and compared the answers with the register. **May 2023 prints eleven full-mark scripts the bank had never read**: nine three-mark Explain and two six-mark Analyse, all printed in full with the question, the mark scheme's own answer, the script and the examiner's comment.

They were invisible because **every sweep the bank owned searched for a phrasing it had already catalogued** — *"This response **is** a Level 3"*, *"gained a Level 3"*, *"was Level 3"* — and May 2023 states its marks in a plain line no sweep knew:

> **`This response scored N marks.`**

⚠️ **A layout warning for anyone re-deriving these.** In the May 2023 reports the commentary and the score line appear at the **foot of the page before the script**, not beside it — the P1 Q3(d) script straddles report pages *27 and 28* with its comment at the top of 28, and P2's Q2(e) **splits across 31 and 32** with a blank area where the script would be. **Read the pages around a script, not the page that prints the score.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 6/6 · Q3(d) — *limitations of break-even charts for Irsi Chocolatier* — report pp. 27–28

**Question (verbatim from the report):**

> *"Irsi Chocolatier will need to consider many factors if it wants to introduce its new product of chocolate lollipops."*
> *"**(d)** Analyse the limitations for **Irsi Chocolatier** of using break-even charts to decide whether it should sell the new product."* **(6)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, MS):**

> *"AO2 = 3 marks     AO3 = 3 marks*
> **AO2**
> • *Break-even charts make the assumption that all of the new chocolate lollipops will be sold*
> • *The chart shows the costs for the chocolate lollipops as a straight line not taking into account other costs that may occur along the way*
> **AO3**
> • *This could lead to false data as it does not take into account any wasted or damaged stock not sold*
> • *This could lead to overspending if Irsi Chocolatier have not taken into account other costs or if customers do not like the new product"*

**The script (verbatim, pages 27–28 joined):**

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"A disadvantage of break-even charts to decide whether it should sell a new products will be that in break even charts it is assumed that all the output produced it's always sold but sometimes it isn't. Considering that Irsi chocolate is considering new chocolate lollipops and chocolate loses it's quality in just a few days, it would probably lead to some being thrown away and the break even chart wouldn't be realistic and reliable.*
>
> *Another disadvantage of break-even charts to make the decision is that in break even charts the price remains constant all the time. This is a problem because prices don't always remain the same and they can sometimes change. For Irsi Chocolatier this would be a problem because as they have many competitors there they may be forced to raise or lower their prices depending in their sales so the break-even chart wouldn't be reliable."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim, top of p. 28):** *"This candidate shows how we expect candidates to respond to an 'analyse' question. This response shows detailed application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts **with context throughout** as well as **interconnected points with logical chains of reasoning**. **Examiner Tip (verbatim, same page):** *"Unless responses for 'analyse' questions are in the context of the given business scenario candidates will find it difficult to access to higher marks."*

**The report's per-question commentary (verbatim):** *"A question where candidates who accessed level 3 carefully considered the limitations for Irsi Chocolatier of using break-even charts to decide whether it should sell the new product of chocolate lollipops. **Candidates were disadvantaged when they did not apply their answer to Irsi Chocolatier as they cannot access the top of level 2 without any application.** Common responses included effects of external factors on the break-even point, the assumption that all stock would be sold, the effect on costs over time."*

🔬 **This is the bank's cleanest transcription of how AO2 and AO3 are physically separated in a 6/6.** Read the script against the mark scheme column by column:

| The mark scheme's AO2 (application) | What the script does with it |
|---|---|
| *assumption that all… will be sold* | *"it is assumed that all the output produced it's always sold but sometimes it isn't"* |
| *costs… as a straight line* | *"the price remains constant all the time"* |

| The mark scheme's AO3 (analysis) | What the script does with it |
|---|---|

| *overspending if… customers do not like the new product* | *"they may be forced to raise or lower their prices depending in their sales so the break-even chart wouldn't be reliable"* |

**The script supplies TWO limitations, not four points** — and each is a full chain (assumption → why it fails → **what that does to the decision**, *"wouldn't be realistic and reliable"*). ⚠️ **Note where both chains terminate: on the reliability of the chart as a decision tool.** That is the question's own command — *to decide whether it should sell the new product* — being answered.

**And the context is real, not a name-drop:** *"chocolate loses it's quality in just a few days"* and *"they have many competitors"* are facts about a chocolatier and its market. The examiner's *"context throughout"* is exactly this — **the business's own characteristics used AS the reasoning**, not mentioned beside it.

## What these eleven close

**The audit that found them is now clean.** Before: **33 printed full-mark scripts, 22 banked — 11 gaps.** After: **33 printed, 33 banked.** `audit_er_fullmarks.py` is therefore promoted from a report to **check 23**, and the target it prints is **zero unbanked**.

**Five findings worth carrying forward, each from a script here:**

1. **A grammar error is not a mark deduction — demonstrated again and again, in one series, in six of these eleven scripts** (*"business's can"*, *"This is also result to"*, *"one reason why Business maintain"*, *"depending in their sales"*, *"it can use it for a long time it will broken"*). Rule 55.
2. **Candidates who corrected themselves in mid-sentence scored full marks** — P1 Q2(e) abandoned and restarted a sentence, P1R Q2(d) struck a phrase, P2R Q1(f) and Q2(c) both struck words. **The examiner marks the chain that reaches the page, not the route taken to it.**
3. **The same idea carries opposite requirements at different tariffs, in the same series:** *product orientation* at 3 marks needs no context (AO1), *motivating employees* at 6 marks needs nothing else (AO2/AO3 only). **Read the tariff, not the topic.**
4. **The mark scheme's bullets are examples, not the answer.** P2R Q1(f) earned 3/3 on *expanding its market* while the scheme's own content is *a global supplier base* and *reduced trade barriers* — *"Accept any other appropriate response"* is printed on every scheme.
5. **A question is only answered on its own angle.** P2R Q2(e) shows a candidate lose 2 marks for supplying a true consequence of the *opposite* condition.
