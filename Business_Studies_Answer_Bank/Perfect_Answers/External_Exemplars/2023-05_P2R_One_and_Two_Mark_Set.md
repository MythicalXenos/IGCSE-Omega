# MAY 2023 PAPER 2R — the whole 1- and 2-mark set, thirteen scripts

**The single richest 1-/2-mark batch in the archive: eight questions, thirteen marked scripts, and every one of them printed with the examiner's comment.** Three of the four one-mark questions are **0-and-1 pairs on facing pages**; four of the five two-mark questions are **calculation questions** and they carry the tier's hardest lesson — the fifth is the Q3(b) pair below, where the failure is not arithmetic but aim.

> **⚠️ THE HEADLINE FINDING IS A CORRECTION TO A RULE THIS BANK HAS BEEN TEACHING FROM ONE EXAMPLE.**
>
> The bank's answer-line rule was drawn from May 2023 **Paper 2** — where two candidates wrote correct values incompletely or one step short, and lost a mark. **Paper 2R's Q3(c) proves the rule is about PRECISION and COMPLETENESS, not about the dotted line.** Its 2/2 script has **nothing at all on the dotted line** and was paid in full: *"Although the answer is not on the dotted lines it is correct and therefore gained the two marks available."* **A correct answer with the required number of decimal places, written anywhere in the box, scores.**
>
> **What actually costs the mark is the format the question demanded.** All four two-mark questions on this paper say *"to 2 decimal places"* or *"to two decimal places"*, and **three of the four 1/2 scripts lost their mark on precisely that** — an answer given to the wrong precision.

⚠️ **Two of these questions span a page break** — `1(b)`, `1(d)`, `2(a)`, `3(b)`, `3(c)` and `4(a)` all print their 0-or-1-mark script on one page and the other script on the next. **Read the spread.**

# ONE-MARK QUESTIONS

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q1(b) *Define the term public corporation* — report pp. 4–5

**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):** *"**(b)** Define the term **public corporation**."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2R MS p. 6):** *"1 (b) · AO1 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a correct definition of public corporation.*

> • *A business owned and controlled by the state/government (1)*

*(1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"The publics involvement together into a business"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response did not gain a mark it is not a definition of public corporation."*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Public corporation is businesses that are owned and run by the government."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response demonstrates accurate knowledge of a definition of public corporation."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim, immediately beneath — and it is the point of the pair):** *"Putting public corporation at the beginning was not needed. For a 'Define' question candidates are required to show that they have a clear understanding of the term."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"A define question of public corporation, was well answered by over half of the cohort gaining the mark available. Candidates should be reminded to try and avoid using the term in their response, this helps to ensure that they have answered the question asked. Candidates are encouraged to practice these types of questions to ensure that they understand the difference between a definition and an explanation of the term."*

🔬 **THE 1-MARK ANSWER OPENS BY REPEATING THE TERM, AND THE EXAMINER EXPLICITLY SAYS THAT WAS NOT NEEDED — AND PAYS IT ANYWAY.** This is the tier's most useful single ruling, because *"X is…"* is how nearly every candidate writes a definition. **It is allowed. It is not required. It costs nothing.**

⚠️ **And the 0-mark script fails for a reason worth naming: it defines the word *public*, not the concept.** *"The publics involvement together into a business"* is a guess at what *public* means in the phrase — **the mark scheme wants who OWNS and CONTROLS it.** The tool the commentary gives for catching this: *"try and avoid using the term in their response, this helps to ensure that they have answered the question asked."* **If your definition cannot be written without the term you are defining, you are probably describing the words rather than the thing.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q1(d) *State one reason why it is important for M&M to motivate its employees* — report pp. 7–8

**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):** *"**(d)** State **one** reason why it is important for **M&M** to motivate its employees."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2R MS p. 7):** *"1 (d) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a valid reason why it is important for M&M to motivate its employees in the context of the business.*

> • *M&M motivating its employees means they will work harder and take a pride in building tractors (1)*
> • *More motivated employees might be willing to accept new working practices after training such as the introduction of robots on the production line (1)*

***NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of M&M.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"to avoid labour turnover"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"The response is very valid that a business would want to try and avoid labour turnover, however it is not in the context of M&M and therefore does not gain a mark."*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Motivated employees may work more efficient and increase output , such as vehicle for M&M."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response is worthy of a mark as it includes reference to increasing output of vehicles. The response is therefore in context."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim, immediately beneath — the most quotable sentence in the tier):** *"Reminder all 'State' questions require candidates to include context to their response – it is worth encouraging candidates to check their response on these types of questions. **Is there response generic? Can it be applied to any business? If it is then they will not gain the marks available.**"*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"The first 'State' question where candidates must relate their response to the business of why it is important for M&M to motivate its employees. Only just over a third of the cohort gained the mark available. As mentioned in previous series many candidates will give a correct answer, however it will not be in the context of M&M and will therefore not gain the mark available. **It is also worth remembering just adding M&M in the response does not mean it is in context as M&M is in the question.**"*

🔬 **⭐ THIS PAIR IS THE BANK'S BEST ONE-MARK EVIDENCE, BECAUSE THE 0-MARK ANSWER IS FOUR WORDS AND PERFECTLY TRUE.**

*"to avoid labour turnover"* is a textbook reason to motivate employees. **It scores zero.** The examiner's own words: *"very valid."* **Then: *"however it is not in the context of M&M."***

**And the cohort commentary closes a loophole candidates rely on:** *"just adding M&M in the response does not mean it is in context as M&M is in the question."* ⚠️ **Writing the business's name buys nothing — the name is printed on the question paper.** Compare `2026-06_P1R_One_Mark_Pairs.md`, where the 0-mark answer wrote *"Spud Bros"* twice and still failed.

**The 1-mark answer's context is two words: *"such as vehicle for M&M"*.** Not a sentence, not a paragraph — **the word *vehicle*.** ⭐ And note the scheme's own bullet names *"building **tractors**"* and *"robots on the **production line**"*: **the context is the thing the business makes.** Compare Q2(a) of the same paper, where the 1-mark is bought with *"designing quality cars."*

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/1 and 0/1** · Q2(a) *State one non-financial objective for M&M* — report pp. 15–16

**Question (verbatim, as printed):**

> *"**M&M is constantly working to maintain the standards and quality of its products.***
> ***(a)** State **one** non-financial objective for M&M."* **(1)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2R MS p. 10):** *"2 (a) · A02 - 1 mark. Award 1 mark for a valid non-financial objective for M&M in the context of the business.*

> • *M&M may want to continue to help the environment by planting even more trees (1)*
> • *M&M want to maintain the support it gives to the schools it runs, helping disadvantaged children (1)*

***NB Do not accept a non-financial objective that is not in the context of M&M.** Accept any other appropriate response. (1)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim — struck words shown):**

> *"~~job~~ ~~enric~~ ~~retention~~ for ~~e~~ protecting the environ-ment"*
> *⚠️ the first three words are heavily struck out; the surviving legible text is **"for"** and **"protecting the environment"**. The examiner credits the point as valid and fails it on context, so the reading is consistent which-ever struck word was intended.*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"The response that this candidate gave was valid and very relevant to M&M however it was not in context and therefore did not gain a mark."*

**The 1-mark script (verbatim):**

> *"Customer satisfaction may be one of M&M's objectives as they want to maintain a good reputation and relationship with them (by designing quality cars)."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidate has identified a non-financial objective and ensured that it is in context to the scenario by the comment of designing quality cars."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"Another 'State' question where candidates were asked to give one non-financial objective for M&M. Unfortunately as in previous series over two thirds of the candidate did not gain a mark for this question. The main reason being that they did not relate the non-financial objective to M&M. Candidates should ensure that they always relate their response back to the scenario to gain the mark available."*

🔬 **⚠️ THE 0-MARK COMMENT SAYS *"valid and very relevant to M&M"* AND STILL AWARDS NOTHING — THIS IS THE SHARPEST STATEMENT OF THE RULE IN THE WHOLE ARCHIVE.** The examiner is not saying the objective was wrong. **He is saying it was not tied to a fact about this business.**

⭐ **AND THE MARK SCHEME SHOWS WHY THE ITEM EXISTS: both its bullets are facts ONLY about M&M** — *planting trees* and *the schools it runs*. **A candidate who has read the case study can answer this question; one who has only revised the theory of non-financial objectives cannot.** That is the design.

**The 1-mark's context is a bracketed clause at the END of the sentence: *"(by designing quality cars)."*** ⭐ **Note where it sits — after the full stop in meaning, in brackets, as an afterthought.** The examiner quotes it verbatim as the thing that earned the mark.

⚠️ **THE 1-MARK ANSWER IS 22 WORDS. THE 0-MARK ANSWER IS FIVE.** Length has nothing to do with it; **one bracketed fact does.**

# TWO-MARK CALCULATIONS

**All four of this paper's two-mark questions are calculations and all four say *"to 2 decimal places"*.** The mark scheme for each is the same shape: *"Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula. **Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working.**"* **The four pairs below show four different ways to spend exactly one of the two marks — and one script that loses the dots and keeps the marks.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2** · Q1(e) *Percentage increase in borrowing, 2019→2020* — report p. 9

**Question (verbatim, as printed):**

> *"In 2019 **M&M** borrowed **448 million Indian Rupees.** In 2020 it borrowed **900 million Indian Rupees.***
> ***(e)** Calculate, to two decimal places, the percentage increase from 2019 to 2020. You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2R MS p. 7):** *"1 (e) · A02 - 2 marks.*
> *900 – 448 = 452 (1)*
> *452/448 x 100*
> *= 100.89 (1)*
>
> *Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula. Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working. (2)"*

**The 2/2 script (verbatim):**

> *(900 − 448) / 448*
>
> *answer line: 100.89 %*
> *⚠️ the working is written into the paper's own printed fraction skeleton — the numerator inside the top box, **448** beneath the line.*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"A good response to this question gaining the full marks available."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim):** *"This is a calculation question where nearly half of the cohort gained the two marks available… **Workings out should be included, as there are occasions where candidates have the incorrect final response but the correct working out, and this would enable them to gain one mark.**"*

🔬 **This is the paper's only 2/2 on a calculation, and it is worth noting how it is written: the candidate fills in the formula skeleton the paper prints, writes no equals sign, no interim values, and puts the rounded percentage on the answer line.** **The examiner's comment is four words. The scheme's two reward lines are both satisfied by one fraction.**

⚠️ **Note the numerator was corrected mid-writing** — *"448"* has a struck glyph through it and has been rewritten. **2/2.** Rule 55, on a calculation, in the same paper where the rule about precision does the damage.

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/2** · Q3(c) *The current ratio for 2020* — report p. 32

**Question (verbatim, as printed):**

> *"In 2020 **M&M** had current assets of **3 400 million Indian Rupees** and current liabilities of **900 million Indian Rupees.***
> ***(c)** Calculate, to 2 decimal places the current ratio for 2020. You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2R MS p. 18):** *"3 (c) · A02 - 2 marks.*
> *3 400/900 (1)*
> *= 3.78 (1)*
>
> *Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula. Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working. (2)"*

**The 1/2 script (verbatim):**

> *3400 / 900 = 3.7777*
>
> *answer line: 3 :1*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response gained one mark for the formula as the answer on the dotted lines is not to two decimal places as the question asks. **Candidates should take care to ensure that they follow what is asked for.**"*

🔬 **⚠️ THE ANSWER IS ON THE DOTTED LINE AND STILL COSTS THE MARK — BECAUSE THE PAPER ASKED FOR *2 DECIMAL PLACES* AND THE LINE READS *3*.**

**The script's own working has the answer to four decimal places: `3.7777`.** To two places that is **3.78** — the scheme's own figure. ⭐ **The candidate had the value and lost the mark writing it down.**

⚠️ **And note the trap in the question's own phrasing: *"the current ratio"*.** A ratio is conventionally written *"3 : 1"*, and **that is exactly what this candidate wrote on the line.** It is a reasonable thing to write and it is not what the paper asked for. **The instruction *"to 2 decimal places"* overrides the convention.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2** · Q3(c) *the same question, full marks* — report p. 33

**The 2/2 script (verbatim):**

> *Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities = 3400 / 900 = 3.78*
>
> *answer line: blank — [the dotted line carries nothing but the printed ":1"]*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"**Although the answer is not on the dotted lines it is correct and therefore gained the two marks available. Candidates should be encouraged to put their answer on the dotted lines.**"*

🔬 **⭐ THIS IS THE CORRECTION TO THE BANK'S ANSWER-LINE RULE, AND IT MATTERS MORE THAN ANY OTHER LINE IN THIS FILE.**

The rule was written from May 2023 **Paper 2** — two scripts, both of which did the arithmetic correctly and lost the second mark on the final line (`13280.2` for `13280.25`, and `0.6` where the question wanted a percentage). **The bank generalised that to *"the answer on the dotted line is the mark."***

**Here a script leaves the dotted line entirely empty and is paid in full.** So the lesson must be restated:

> **The mark is for a CORRECT answer at the REQUIRED PRECISION. The examiner finds it wherever it is written.**
> **The dotted line is where they are told to look first — and leaving it blank puts your mark at the mercy of a marker who has to hunt.**

⭐ **And the tips point the same way, from both sides.** This examiner: *"Candidates should be **encouraged** to put their answer on the dotted lines."* **Encouraged — not required.** Contrast the Paper 2 examiner at Q3(c) of the same series: *"Care should be taken to ensure that the correct answer is placed on the dotted lines."* **Two examiners, one series, one instruction is a recommendation and one is a warning. The behaviour that satisfies both is the same: write it on the line.**

⚠️ **The 2/2 script also does something the 1/2 does not: it writes the formula out in words first — *"Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities"*.** The scheme's own first reward line is *"3 400/900"*, and that fraction appears in the middle of this script. **Naming the formula is what the back of the paper prints, and this candidate has used it.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **1/2 and 1/2** · Q4(a) *The tractor's price in pounds sterling* — report pp. 41–42

**Question (verbatim, as printed):**

> *"**M&M** sells its tractors to the UK. Its tractors sell for **14 000 000 Indian Rupees.** The exchange rate is **103 Indian Rupees = £1 (GBP).***
> ***(a)** Calculate, to 2 decimal places the cost of the tractor in pounds sterling (GBP). You are advised to show your working."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2R MS p. 22):** *"4 (a) · A02 - 2 marks.*
> *14 000 000 ÷ 103 (1)*
> *= 135 922.33 (1)*
>
> *Award 1 mark for correctly substituting numbers into formula. Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working. (2)"*

**The first 1/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 41):**

> *14 000 000 ÷ 13*
> *= 1 076 923.08 (2 decimal place)*
> *= £1 076 923.08*
>
> *answer line: £ [blank]*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidates only gained one mark for the correct formula."*

**The second 1/2 script (verbatim, printed p. 42):**

> *14,000,000 / 103 × 1*
> *= 135922*
>
> *answer line: £135,922*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This candidates also gained one mark for using the correct formula. The question asked to calculate to 2 decimal places."*

**Examiner Tip (verbatim, immediately beneath):** *"Centres are reminded to encourage candidates to read the question carefully to fully understand what is required and therefore being able to access all of the available marks."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 41):** *"Another calculation question where over four fifths of the cohort gained the full marks available and only a very small percentage of candidates did not gain a mark."*

🔬 **⭐ THIS IS A PAIR ABOUT ONE INSTRUCTION, AND IT PRINTS TWO DIFFERENT WAYS TO FAIL IT — INCLUDING ONE THAT IS NOT ABOUT PRECISION AT ALL.**

| | script 1 | script 2 |
|---|---|---|
| formula | **14 000 000 ÷ 13** — the rate is **103**, and the candidate has **dropped a digit** | **14,000,000 / 103 × 1** — the scheme's own line, verbatim |
| intermediate | **1 076 923.08**, correctly rounded to 2 d.p. **but from the wrong divisor** | **135922** — the right value, **unrounded** |
| answer line | **£ [blank]** | **£135,922** |
| the mark | 1 — *"one mark for the correct formula"* | 1 — *"one mark for using the correct formula. The question asked to calculate to 2 decimal places."* |

⚠️ **Script 1 is the cautionary tale: the candidate followed the precision instruction perfectly — *"(2 decimal place)"* is written in their own hand — and got the arithmetic wrong by one digit.** **The examiner still finds the formula and pays it.** *"This candidates only gained one mark for the correct formula."*

⚠️ **And note WHAT the examiner credits in script 1: not a correct substitution — the divisor is wrong — but the structure.** The scheme's first reward line is *"14 000 000 ÷ 103 (1)"*, and the examiner reads the candidate as having attempted the division the question demands. **The precision instruction they obeyed bought nothing, and the formula they fumbled bought the mark anyway.**

⭐ **Script 2 passes the formula cleanly — and then rounds to the nearest rupee, writing `£135,922` on the line where the scheme wants `£135,922.33`.** **Its own working is accurate; the line discards the two decimal places the question asked for, for the third time on this paper.**

## ⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — **2/2 and 0/2** · Q3(b) *Outline one reason why M&M might locate a new factory near to a labour force* — report pp. 30–31

**Question (verbatim, as printed on both scripts):** *"**(b)** Outline **one** reason why **M&M** might locate a new factory near to a labour force."* **(2)**

**Official mark scheme (verbatim, P2R MS p. 17):** *"3 (b) · A02 - 2 marks. Award 1 mark for identifying one reason why M&M might locate its factories near to a labour force, plus 1 further mark for linking it to the context.*

> • *To have access to a wide pool of labour when the car plant opens (1) that pool to include production line workers and skilled technicians (1)*

***NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of M&M.** Accept any other appropriate response. (2)"*

**The 0-mark script (verbatim, report p. 30):**

> *"One reason is to lower costs. Closer to a labour force means cheaper means of transport to the factory which means cheaper labour."* *(a correction is visible before "Closer" — a struck mark — but the sentence reads on cleanly)*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response is not in context because the comment transport to the factory could apply to any business."*

**The 2-mark script (verbatim, report p. 31):**

> *"Because there would be a high number of highly skilled workers e.g. mechanics or car engineers which means M&M can hire labour at a low cost due to the competition of wanting a job."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"The response refers to access to more skilled workers of mechanics and engineers and the development is that they would want a job."*

**Cohort commentary (verbatim, p. 30, printed above the 0-mark script):** *"An 'Outline' question which is similar to a 'State' question where candidates have to put their response in the context of the business. To gain the two marks available there must be a development in the response as well as the response being applied to the scenario. Nearly two thirds of the cohort did not gain a mark on this question. From some of the clips seen, candidates may have misread the question or did not finish reading the question with regard to locating a new factory near to a labour force. A number of comments made reference to easier to transport vehicles and tractors, which does not have a benefit for the labour force."*

🔬 **⭐ THIS IS THE TIER'S CLEANEST DEMONSTRATION OF WHAT THE SECOND MARK IS FOR, BECAUSE BOTH SCRIPTS HAVE A STRUCTURE — ONE IS POINTED AT THE WRONG THING.**

| | 0/2 — p. 30 | 2/2 — p. 31 |
|---|---|---|
| reason | *"to lower costs"* | *"a high number of highly skilled workers"* |
| mechanism | *"cheaper means of transport to the factory"* | *"e.g. mechanics or car engineers"* |
| further step | *"which means cheaper labour"* | *"can hire labour at a low cost due to the competition of wanting a job"* |
| why it fails / pays | **the benefit is to the BUSINESS's transport bill, not to the labour force** | **the benefit flows from the labour force itself — and the employer takes advantage of it** |

⚠️ **The 0-mark answer is not short, not badly expressed, and has a chain of the right LENGTH — three links.** It reads as a reasonable business answer. **It fails because it answers *why locate near anything* rather than *why locate near a labour force*** — and the examiner names the test in one clause: *"could apply to any business."* **Swap M&M's name in and it would still be just as generic**, which is why *NB Do not accept a reason that is not in the context of M&M* is printed on the scheme.

⭐ **The examiner credits the 2/2 in two steps and says which is which:** *"The response refers to access to more skilled workers"* — **the identification** — *"and the development is that they would want a job"* — **the link**. **The development is a COMPETITION argument: many skilled workers nearby means the employer can pay less because they are competing with one another.** The mark scheme's own indicative content (*"that pool to include production line workers and skilled technicians"*) is a different second step; *"Accept any other appropriate response"* is what admits the candidate's.

⚠️ **Note the order of the 2/2's chain: identification → example → consequence → the employer's gain.** The example (*"e.g. mechanics or car engineers"*) sits **inside** the chain, exactly as the 3-mark tier's rule requires — it is not the development.

**Cohort data to carry:** *"Nearly two thirds of the cohort did not gain a mark on this question."* And the commentary records the commonest failure by name — *"easier to transport vehicles and tractors, which does not have a benefit for the labour force"* — **candidates who read the question as *locate near to* rather than *near to a LABOUR FORCE*.** On a two-mark Outline, half the question is the noun at the end of it.

# WHAT THE THIRTEEN ESTABLISH

1. **⭐ THE ANSWER-LINE RULE IS NOW CORRECTLY STATED — and this file corrects the bank's own version of it.** *"The answer on the dotted line is the mark"* is **wrong as an absolute**; Q3(c)'s 2/2 leaves the line blank and is paid in full. **The mark is a correct answer at the required precision, wherever it is written — and the line is where examiners are told to look.** Encouraged to use it, not required: write on it anyway.
2. **⚠️ What actually costs the mark on this paper is the format the question demanded.** All four two-mark questions ask for *2 decimal places*; **three of the four 1/2 scripts lose their mark to precision or rounding** — *3* instead of *3.78* · *135,922* instead of *135.922,33* · and one whose precision is perfect but whose divisor is not.
3. **⚠️ A wrong intermediate answer can still be worth a mark, but not for the reason candidates hope.** Q4(a) script 1 writes a value that is wrong from the first line, and is paid *"one mark for the correct formula"*, while the precision it obeyed is not mentioned. **The examiner is reading the structure of the attempt against the scheme, not the arithmetic.**
4. **⭐ On a one-mark `State` question the context is the mark, and the examiner says it in a form you can self-check:** *"Is there response generic? Can it be applied to any business? If it is then they will not gain the marks available."* **A valid, relevant, textbook-true answer scores 0 if any business could have said it.**
5. **⚠️ Naming the business is not context.** *"just adding M&M in the response does not mean it is in context as M&M is in the question."* **The context is the thing it makes or does** — *"vehicle"*, *"designing quality cars"*, *"building tractors"*.
6. **⭐ Repeating the term in a `Define` is allowed, and a good definition does not need it.** *"Public corporation is businesses that are owned and run by the government"* = 1 mark, and the examiner adds: *"Putting public corporation at the beginning was not needed."* **The test is whether the rest of the sentence would stand alone.**
7. **Corrections, struck words and messy handwriting remain free.** Q1(e)'s 2/2 corrects a figure mid-fraction; Q2(a)'s 0/1 is a wall of corrections. **Rule 55 holds on the 1- and 2-mark tier too.**
