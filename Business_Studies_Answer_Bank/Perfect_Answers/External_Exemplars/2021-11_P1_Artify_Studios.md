# November 2021 — Paper 1 — Artify Studios — Examiner Report

**Case study:** Artify Studios, Singapore.

## ⚠️ Read this first — what this report is and is not

This is a **Principal Examiner Feedback** report: the **summary** document, not the full examiners' report. It contains a question-by-question commentary and cohort statistics. **It contains no candidate scripts and no exemplar responses.**

So this file is a different kind of asset from the others in `External_Exemplars/`. Its unique value is the thing no other source in this bank has: **an approximate pass rate for every question on the paper**, written by the Principal Examiner.

That makes it directly usable as a **revision priority list** — see the last section.

> **One contradiction inside the report, flagged honestly.** The General section states: *"'State in question 1 (d) required learners to 'State one financial method of motivation Artify Studios could use to motivate its employees'."* But the question-by-question section says Q1(d) *"was a well answered question with around two thirds of candidates gaining the mark for this question by being able to correctly **define the term job production**."* These cannot both be right; the General paragraph is very likely carried over in error from another paper. **The question-specific section is the reliable one.** No question text is quoted verbatim in this report, so question wording below is described, not transcribed.

### ⭐ Q1(e) — the two-step calculation rule, again

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"Some candidates gained a mark for calculating the discount but **they did not then go on to apply the discount** therefore not gaining the second mark."*

Fifth confirmation of a rule already established in this bank (Jun 2022 P1R Q4(a): `12 × 38.40 = 460.80`, then `× 0.875 = €403.20`; the 1-mark answers stopped after the discount).

> **Rule: if the question requires two steps, stopping after step one is worth exactly half. Finish the calculation.**

### ⭐⭐ Q1(f) — the listing cap, stated a third way

> *"**Candidates who list more than one way of measuring business success can only gain one mark.**"*

Combined with the two mark-scheme statements in the June 2019 Paper 2 booklet (*"Answers that list three impacts with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark"*), the rule is now settled beyond argument.

**On an Explain question, listing is worth one mark — no matter how many items you list.**

### Q1(g) — Analyse: the AO1 trap on a level-marked question

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"Candidates must be reminded that **there are no AO1 marks for this question**, there are AO2 marks for this level-based question as often candidates would provide **an extended answer but did not apply this answer to the context of Artify Studios therefore not allowing themselves to move into the top of level 2**."*

Note the exact phrasing — this is copied almost word for word onto Pearson's support page for the Analyse command word, which confirms the support page is drawing on these reports.

### ⭐⭐ Q2(a) and Q2(b) — the two worst-answered questions on the paper

> **Q2(a):** *"a 'State' question which was **not well answered** by candidates. Candidates were sometimes able to generically offer a reason of why high-quality service may be offered but did not apply this reason to Artify Studio. **Less than one fifth of candidates** were able to correctly identify a reason why Artify Studio would want to offer a high-quality service and put this in context to the business."*
>
> **Q2(b):** *"again, another 'State' question which was **not well answered** by candidates. **Only one tenth of candidates** were able to correctly identify an advantage for Artify Studio of using part-time employees. Centres must remind candidates that **'State' questions need to be in the context of the given business, a lot of candidates are currently being disadvantaged by not being taught this skill**."*

**A single 1-mark State question defeated nine candidates in ten.**

This is the highest-return revision target in the entire bank and it costs nothing: **one correct point plus one concrete noun from the case study**. That is the whole answer.

> **Note the examiner's language: *"a lot of candidates are currently being disadvantaged by not being taught this skill."*** Pearson is saying the skill is teachable and the cohort is not being taught it. That is free marks.

### Q2(c) and Q2(d) — what limits an Explain to 2/3

> **Q2(d):** *"Where those candidates did not gain the higher marks was due to identifying a way social media can be used by a business but **not then developing the answer**, or **providing a list of ways** of how social media can be used by a business."*

Two failure modes, both structural: **no development**, or **a list**. Same two as everywhere else.

### Q2(e) — one third of the cohort scored zero on TQM

> *"Some candidates found it difficult to extend their answer beyond listing benefits and therefore were unable to gain full marks."*

Note: the gap was not knowledge for the other two thirds — it was **listing instead of explaining**.

### Q2(f) — Justify, and the sixth statement of the zero-mark route

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"Those candidates who **gained no marks on this response simply showed knowledge of one or both options with no application, analysis or evaluation**. Candidates working at level 1 showed good knowledge of both options, making statements on one or both options with some relation to Artify Studios but this was not always expanded upon… Around half of candidates accessed level 2 by expanding on their answers but often **missing balance and concluding their thoughts**. More able candidates accessed level 3 by considering their chosen option in the context of Artify Studio and how this would impact its image, **linking their points together and concluding their answer with balance and justification**."*

### ⭐ Level 3's recipe, in the Principal Examiner's own words

> **"considering their chosen option in the context of Artify Studio and how this would impact [the business]… linking their points together and concluding their answer with balance and justification"**

Hmm — note what is **absent**: there is no requirement to discuss the second option. Compare Pearson's support page: *"Candidates do not have to consider both options."* The route to Level 3 is **context + linked chains + a balanced, justified conclusion**.

**And half the cohort stopped at Level 2 for exactly one reason: *"missing balance and concluding their thoughts."*** They had the chains. They ran out of answer before the judgement.

> **Time-management conclusion:** if you routinely reach Level 2 on justify questions but not Level 3, the fix is not more analysis — it is **budgeting the last third of your time for the conclusion.**

## Question 3

| Part | Command | Topic | Cohort performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **3(a)** | Define | price skimming | **only *"around a third"*** — *"showed a gap in knowledge of pricing strategies with some candidates [confusing] this strategy with other pricing strategies"* |
| **3(b)** | Outline (2) | why cash might be important to Artify Studio | *"only around one fifth of candidates went on to outline the importance of this reason"* |
| **3(c)** | Calculate | — | *"the majority of candidates have gained at least one mark with around two thirds gaining all of the marks available"* |
| **3(d)** | Analyse (6) | how information provided can be used to assess performance | **around one quarter scored zero** |
| **3(e)** | **Justify (9)** | crowdfunding **vs** retained profit | one fifth scored zero — see below |

### Q3(a) — a two-thirds failure on a definition

**"Around a third" on a 1-mark definition question.** The examiner gives the cause: candidates confused price skimming with **other pricing strategies** — penetration pricing, price skimming, competitive pricing, price discrimination.

> **The distinction to hold:** price skimming is a **high price at launch, lowered later** — it works because early adopters will pay more.

This is a **Define** question, so it is also worth restating the three documented ways to lose a definition mark:

1. Giving an **example** instead of a definition
2. **Using the word being defined** in the definition
3. Defining a **related but different term** (exactly what happened here)

### Q3(c) — the 2-decimal-place rule, restated

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"It is pleasing to see candidates are making more use of the formulas given at the start of the paper. Centres must remind candidates that **if the question states 'to 2 decimal places' then this is the expectation to gain full marks** for the question."*

**Sixth confirmation.** And note Pearson's support page adds the mechanised version of this rule: the answer **on the answer line** is what is marked, and *"if the final answer is not to 2 decimal places the candidates will not gain access to the full marks even if the answer to 2 decimal places is in the working out."*

### Q3(d) — two ways to score zero on a 6-marker

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"around one quarter of candidates did not gain any marks due to **a lack of knowledge of how the information provided can assess performance** or because **they repeated the figures from Figure 1 with no explanation**."*

The second failure mode is the interesting one, and it is the mirror image of the June 2019 finding:

| Failure | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Ignoring the figure** (Jun 2019 P2 Q3(d), 2/6) | reasoning about "financial information" in the abstract |
| **Repeating the figure** (Nov 2021 P1 Q3(d), 0) | copying the numbers out with no consequence attached |

**Both fail. The mark is in the number *plus* what it means for the business.**

### Q3(e) — Justify, and the seventh statement of the zero-mark route

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"**One fifth of the candidates who gained no marks on this response simply showed knowledge of one or both options with no application, analysis or evaluation.**"*

## Question 4

| Part | Command | Topic | Cohort performance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4(a)** | Calculate | cost of delivering 1,000 leaflets over 3 hours | *"Over half of the candidates"* correct |
| **4(b)** | Analyse (6) | why Artify Studio benefits from on-the-job training for new employees | **around one fifth scored zero** |
| **4(c)** | **Evaluate (12)** | factors influencing location decisions | level 1 / 2 / 3 distribution |

### Q4(a) — working out pays, and finish the whole calculation

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"It is clear to see centres have encouraged candidates to include their working out as often **candidates have gained a mark for their calculation despite their final answer being incorrect**. Candidates should be encouraged to [read] the requirements of the question as **some candidates worked out parts of the question and missed the top marks because they did not complete all of the calculations required**."*

**Two distinct calculation rules in one paragraph:**

1. **Show working** — it rescues a mark when the answer is wrong.
2. **Finish the calculation** — partial working earns partial marks at best. (Same as Q1(e) above, and Jun 2022 P1R Q4(a).)

### Q4(b) — the misread question

> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"around one fifth of candidates did not gain any marks on this question or they did not gain any marks as they **misread the question and analysed the benefits for employees rather than the business**."*

**This is the advantage-vs-disadvantage misread in a different costume** — now flagged in three papers (also Jun 2022 P2R, Jun 2023 P1R).

> **Rule: read exactly who the question asks you to analyse for.** *"Benefits for employees"* ≠ *"benefits for the business"*, even when the topic is the same.

### Q4(c) — Evaluate: the two documented reasons for underperformance

> *"Those who performed less well than expected on this question did so for **one of two reasons**.*
> *Firstly, candidates provided a **generic answer with no application to Artify Studio or its business activity**, or candidates would **go into detail outlining a number of factors** influencing location decisions.*
> *Secondly, candidates did not provide a **thorough judgment relating back to any issues that may occur for Artify Studio** if it did decide to relocate, or they **did not balance their conclusion** therefore limiting their evaluation."*

**These two paragraphs — written in November 2021 — are reproduced almost word for word on Pearson's current assessment support page for the Evaluate command word.** They are the official diagnosis of why candidates lose 12-mark questions, and they have not changed in five years.

**Note the first reason contains a second, less obvious trap:** *"candidates would go into detail outlining a number of factors."*

**Listing many factors on a 12-marker is itself a reason for underperformance.** This is the same listing cap, arriving at the top of the paper instead of the bottom.

# 🎯 The revision priority list — this report's unique contribution

Ranked by cohort failure rate. **The lower the cohort's score, the more a prepared candidate gains** — these are the questions where a grade 9 is made.

| Priority | Question | Command | Success rate | Why it pays |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **State questions generally** (Q2(a), Q2(b)) | State (1) | **1 in 10** to **1 in 5** | cheapest marks on the paper; the skill is a formula, not knowledge |
| **2** | Q1(f) — measure of business success | Explain (3) | **1 in 4** got 3/3 | one point developed twice = 3 marks |
| **3** | Q3(a) — define price skimming | Define (1) | **1 in 3** | know the pricing strategies apart |
| **4** | Q4(b) — on-the-job training benefits | Analyse (6) | **1 in 5** scored zero | via misreading *for whom* |
| **5** | Q3(d) — assessing performance from data | Analyse (6) | **1 in 4** scored zero | number + consequence |
| **6** | Q2(e) — TQM benefit | Explain (3) | **1 in 3** scored zero | content gap — learn TQM |
| **7** | Q3(e), Q2(f) — justify questions | Justify (9) | **1 in 5** scored zero | via writing knowledge, not judgement |
| **8** | Q4(c) — location factors | Evaluate (12) | Level 1/2 heavy | via generic answer + no judgement |

**The pattern in that table is the whole strategic finding:** across seven questions, the cohort's failure is caused by **four mechanical errors**, and not one of them is a content problem:

1. no context (State questions)
2. listing instead of developing
3. writing knowledge where there are no knowledge marks
4. no conclusion, or a conclusion with no balance

**A candidate who fixes only those four things beats the majority of the cohort on this paper without learning any new business content.**
