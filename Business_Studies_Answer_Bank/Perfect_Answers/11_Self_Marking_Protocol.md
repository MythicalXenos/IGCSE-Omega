# 11 — THE SELF-MARKING PROTOCOL

**What this file is for.** Everything else in this folder tells you what a perfect answer looks like. **This one is the sheet you run over your own answer to find out whether it already is one** — for the ninety minutes in the exam hall you cannot consult anything, so what you actually need is a marking routine small enough to hold in your head.

**Where every line comes from.** Nothing here is new evidence. **Each rule is evidence already banked in this folder**, cited by its rule number: **rules 41–63 live in `01_Mark_Tariff_Playbook.md`, rules 1–48 and 64–126 in `06_External_Source_Library.md`.** Where a rule comes from a script or a report, the pointer says so. **If you want the proof, the rule number takes you to it; if you want the rule, this page is enough.**

> ⚠️ **One thing this file does NOT do: tell you what you would have scored.** It tells you where marks are lost. **No one except Pearson marks a script**, and the bank never claims a score for an answer that was not examiner-marked.

---

## THE FIVE PASSES — run them in this order, on every answer

**The order is the point.** Each pass is cheaper than the one after it, and the earlier ones kill more marks. **A candidate who runs only passes 1–3 usually beats a candidate who writes more and runs none.**

| Pass | Question you ask | Why it is first |
|---|---|---|
| **1. RELEVANCE** | *Did I answer the question that was SET?* | A perfect answer to a different question scores zero (rules 68 · 102 · 106 · 126) |
| **2. THE COMMAND WORD** | *What does the verb owe — knowledge, application, analysis, judgement?* | The tariff card below is built on it (rules 41 · 42) |
| **3. CONTEXT** | *Is the BUSINESS in the sentence — its people, its products, its numbers?* | Applying is where the most marks are lost, and the cause is never knowledge (rule 108) |
| **4. THE TARIFF** | *Does it have the shape my tariff card requires?* | The five shapes are fixed; the topics are not (rules 110 · 88 · 82) |
| **5. THE ARITHMETIC** | *Is the answer line right, at the required precision?* | The examiner reads the answer line, not your working (rules 59 · 51 · 107 · 113) |

## PASS 1 — RELEVANCE: the four ways to answer the wrong question

**This pass is first because its failures are total.** Four separate traps, all banked from real reports:

1. **The "other" trap** *(rule 106)* — a stem qualifier flips the question. *"the **other** method"*, *"the **non-financial** way"*, *"**other than** the one you chose"*: **a good answer to the un-qualified question scores zero.** Circle the qualifier before you write.
2. **Relevance by PARTY** *(rule 102)* — *who* is the answer about? There are five instances in the bank: an answer about the customer when the question asked about the business, about the employer when it asked about the employee. **Underline the party noun.**
3. **"Not answering the question set"** *(rule 68)* — the 2025 form of the same fault, named by the examiner in those words.
4. **⭐ Vocabulary is not an answer** *(newest evidence: `External_Exemplars/2023-05_Series_P2_and_P2R_Two_Mark_Explain.md`, the **0/3**)* — that script names *promotion*, *job opportunities* and *job enrichment* — **three correct syllabus terms, used correctly — and scores nothing**, because it describes what *employees want* when the question asked what a *business does*. **The examiner's whole verdict: *"does not answer the question."***

⚠️ **And the trap that reads like thoroughness** *(the same file, the two 2/3s)*: on a question that says **`one`**, a second reason is **a cap, not extra credit** — *"The comment about high profits is **another reason** and therefore not awarded any marks"*; *"the comment about offering promotion is **another way** and the question asks for **one way only**."* **Same information, extra clause, two marks instead of three.** The cure is a connector, not deletion: *"…more customers attracted, **which means** it can experience high profits"* keeps it one chain.

## PASS 2 — THE COMMAND WORD

**The tariff tells you the marks; the command word tells you what the marks are FOR.** The examiner's own taxonomy *(rule 41)*:

| Command word | What it owes | Tariff it usually sits on |
|---|---|---|
| **Define** | **AO1** — knowledge, and nothing else | 1 |
| **State / Give / Identify** | **AO1 + AO2** — one fact, applied | 1 |
| **Outline** | **AO2** — application, briefly | 2 |
| **Explain** *(why / one reason)* | **AO1** — and *"no marks are awarded for a definition"* (rule 119) | 3 |
| **Explain** *(how / one way / method)* | **AO1**, and a DIFFERENT technique from why (rule 62) | 3 |
| **Analyse** | **AO2 + AO3** — no AO1 marks at all (rule 95) | 6 |
| **Justify** | **AO2 + AO3 + AO4** — a choice, and a condition | 9 |
| **Evaluate** | **3 / 3 / 3 / 3** — knowledge, application, analysis, judgement (rule 42) | 12 |

**⚠️ The one that surprises candidates: on a 3-mark Explain, a definition earns nothing** — Pearson prints *"NB No marks are awarded for a definition"* in the scheme box itself, confirmed on six series *(rule 119)*. **And on a 6-mark Analyse there are no AO1 marks either (rule 95)** — so an answer made of definitions and description can be entirely accurate and score zero.

## PASS 3 — CONTEXT

**Rules 60 · 61 · 111 · 108 · 117, in one paragraph.** Context is **the business's own nouns** — its people, its products, its places, its numbers — **never its name.** The examiner says it outright: *"The name of the business does not provide context"* *(rule 60)* and *"Using the name of the business is not application (AO2) as the name of the business 'Four Fitness' is used in the question"* *(rule 111)*. **The newest confirmation: `External_Exemplars/2023-05_P1_and_P1R_One_and_Two_Mark_Set.md` — a **State** answer scored 1/1 because the examiner credits *"the use of context with the word **'flower'"***, not the business name, which was in the same sentence.**

**Three calibration points:**

- **One reference is enough on a state question** — *"Just one reference to the context of the business is enough for a 'state' question"* *(rule 111)*.
- **And one is REQUIRED to get paid at all on Explain — yet the tier's failure is never knowledge:** success rates across reports run **10–20%** *(rule 108)* — *"Candidates were able to identify a reason… but **not relate it to the business**."*
- **Context must be woven, with variety** — *"woven throughout the entire response, appearing in all paragraphs… variety rather than mere repetition"* *(rule 117)*, followed by the warning **not to simply repeat the information given.**

⚠️ **The generic test, from a real examiner ruling:** *"could apply to any business."* **Run every chain through it.** If the sentence would be true of any firm in any country, it has not been applied. **One chain aimed at the wrong thing** — the P2R Q3(b) 0/2 whose benefit flowed to the transport bill instead of the labour force — fails exactly this test.

## PASS 4 — THE TARIFF CARDS

### **1 mark — DEFINE and STATE**

**What the examiner pays, in order:** for **Define**, the term's **three elements** — **what falls · which direction · the condition** *(verified on a 1/1: `2023-05_P1_and_P1R_One_and_Two_Mark_Set.md` Q1(b) — the wording can be looser than the scheme's and still be paid)*. For **State**, **one applied fact**, with the **product in the sentence** *(rule 50)*.

**60-second check — Define**
- [ ] **Do I use the word itself in the definition?** *(rule 54)* — *"a description of a job"* for **job description** is self-referential and scores nothing *(rule 104)*.
- [ ] **Have I turned the term around instead of defining it?** *(rule 104)* — *"the graphics made to check a profit or loss"* is one of the four named ways to lose a 1-mark Define.
- [ ] **Have I given an example instead of a definition?** *(rule 66)* — *"providing examples … does not provide a clear definition"*; examples earn nothing. **The 1/0 pair: `2023-05_P1_and_P1R_One_and_Two_Mark_Set.md` Q1(c).**
- [ ] **Is the term a two-parter?** *(rule 115)* — if it is, both halves must be there.

**60-second check — State**
- [ ] **Is the product/thing in the sentence, not just the business?** *(rules 50 · 111)*
- [ ] **⚠️ Am I about to lose the easiest mark on the paper?** *(rule 108)* — 10–20% success, and **the examiners name the cause every single time: not related to the business.**

### **2 marks — OUTLINE and CALCULATE**

**What the examiner pays, in order:** for **Outline**, one applied point — **no development needed, and one context reference is enough** *(rule 111)*. For a **calculation**, the **substitution** and the **final answer**.

**60-second check — the calculation rules, all five of them**
- [ ] **Answer line** — *"examiners are advised to take the answer provided by candidates on the **final answer line**"*: **if it is not to 2 d.p. you do not gain the full marks even if the correct answer is in your working** *(rule 59)*.
- [ ] **Precision as asked** — 2 d.p. or **2 significant figures** when the question says so *(rules 51 · 107)*.
- [ ] **No premature rounding** — rounding up or down mid-way loses the second mark *(rule 113)*.
- [ ] **The discount error — the most-failed calculation in 4BS1** *(rule 101)*: **calculate the discount, then APPLY it.** *"calculated the discount … but did not go on to take the discount from the cost"* — four separate reports say it.
- [ ] **The formula in words earns nothing** *(rule 69)* — and, in the other direction, **a wrong formula heading does not cost you the marks: the scheme pays the substitution.** ⭐ The clearest case in the bank is the **inverted formula that still scored 2/2** (`2023-05_P1_and_P1R_One_and_Two_Mark_Set.md` Q1(e)): the candidate writes *"Break even = Fixed Cost / Variable cost − selling price"* and then substitutes correctly on the next line — **1 250, full marks.** The examiner even notes that without the working the candidate *"would not have achieved any marks"*.

### **3 marks — EXPLAIN**

**What the examiner pays, in order — three rungs** *(rules 53 · 65 · 73 · 112 · 126)*:

> **identify** the reason → the **mechanism** (what it does) → the **business outcome** (what follows for the business)

**And the newest evidence in the bank says the missing rung is always the same one:** four June 2026 P1R scripts scored **0/3 for four different questions with one identical fault — a missing mechanism**, not a short answer *(rule 126)*.

**60-second check**
- [ ] **Three rungs present, and the third one names a result?** — *increased sales*, *less waste*, *positive cashflow*; **"save a bit of money" is where a 2-mark chain stops** *(the 2/3 at `2023-05_Series_P2_and_P2R_Two_Mark_Explain.md`)*.
- [ ] **Did the question say `one`?** — then exactly one reason, one chain, one method *(Pass 1, above)*.
- [ ] **"why" or "how"?** — advantage/disadvantage questions need a different technique from method/way questions *(rule 62)*.
- [ ] **Have I written a definition first?** — it earns nothing here *(rule 119)*.
- [ ] **Length is not the variable.** The four June 2026 3/3s run **35–70 words**; the shortest full-mark answer in the bank is **35** *(08_Completion_Map.md)*.

### **6 marks — ANALYSE**

**What the examiner pays, in order:** **one point, with developed strands** *(rule 110)* — not several points. **AO2 + AO3 only: there are no AO1 marks** *(rule 95)*, so definitions are neither required nor helpful *(rule 98)*.

**The examiner's own two-question test for a chain** *(rule 116)*:
> ***Does it say what happens? Then does it say what follows from that?***

**60-second check**
- [ ] **One point, or a list of points?** — a list is the commonest Level-2 shape *(rule 96)*.
- [ ] **Does every connective have a mechanism behind it?** — *"limited interconnected points"* is the examiner's name for **a connective with no mechanism** *(rule 97)*; **connectives alone are not analysis** *(rule 63)*.
- [ ] **Would the chain survive on any business?** — rule 81's *"generic analysis"* is context-free.
- [ ] **A full-marks answer can be three sentences** *(rule 52)* — 6/6 is a shape, not a length.
- [ ] **Context in all paragraphs, and varied** *(rule 117)*.

### **9 marks — JUSTIFY**

**What the examiner pays, in order: a choice, both sides, and a NAMED CONDITION.** Six required features, assembled from four verified 9/9 verdicts, are listed in **rule 88**; the single rule that explains every mark band is **rule 91 — *the named condition***.

**The things candidates believe that are not true:**
- **You do NOT have to discuss both options.** *"Candidates can achieve the full marks only considering the one option"* *(rules 49 · 71 — four confirmations)*. ⚠️ **Discussing the second option BADLY costs you:** *"the counterbalance should be as equally developed as the points of analysis"* *(rule 105)* — a one-sided answer done well beats a two-sided answer done shallowly. The 5/9 one-sided script makes the trade visible *(rule 114)*.
- **"It depends" is not enough.** An **8/9** used the phrase and still lost a mark *(rule 80)* — **what is missing is the CONDITION: what it depends ON.**
- **Level 2 is the default** *(rule 89)* — the tier is designed so a competent description lands mid-band.

**60-second check**
- [ ] **Is there a clear choice, stated?** *(rule 88, requirement 1)*
- [ ] **Is the conclusion three sentences doing three jobs?** *(rule 92)*
- [ ] **⭐ Is there a NAMED CONDITION?** — *"could be limited because it depends on factors such as **natural**…"* is the 12/12's shape; **the condition is the band** *(rule 91)*.
- [ ] **AO4 ladder:** the four verdicts across three sessions reduce to one rule *(rule 75)*.
- [ ] **Named people and named materials are the CEILING of AO2** *(rule 94)* — *Gianni Polvani's 14 employees*, not *"the staff"*.
- [ ] **The 7/9's gap is ALWAYS the conclusion** *(rule 72)*, and **two routes to zero** exist on this tier *(rule 87)*.

### **12 marks — EVALUATE**

**What the examiner pays, in order: 3 / 3 / 3 / 3** *(rule 42)* — knowledge, application, analysis, **judgement** — and **the judgement is woven through the answer, not bolted on at the end** *(rules 82 · 83)*.

**60-second check**
- [ ] **Bullet points or numbered items?** — *"this will not allow them to gain the higher levels"* — **confirmed three times** *(rule 84)*. **Prose only.**
- [ ] **Is the counterbalance developed as fully as the analysis?** *(rule 105)*
- [ ] **Two routes to AO4, stated outright by the examiner** *(rule 79)* — use both.
- [ ] **10/12 means "limited judgements" and nothing else** *(rule 83)*; **the 4/12 diagnosis names four missing points** *(rule 85)*.
- [ ] **Context woven, not repeated** *(rule 117)* — and **do not restate the stem** as your opening line.

## PASS 5 — THE LAST THIRTY SECONDS

**Read your own answer line.** Then check, in this order:

1. **The answer line** carries the right number, at the required precision *(rule 59)*.
2. **Significant figures** if the question asked for them *(rule 107)*.
3. **Units** — SEK, £, %, units of output.
4. **Nothing rounded mid-way** *(rule 113)*.
5. **The last sentence of a 9- or 12-marker names a condition** *(rule 91)*.

## WHERE THE MARKS ACTUALLY ARE — a ranked list

**Every tariff count here is derived from the 38 mark schemes** (988 parts, 3040 marks; see `00_`).

| Rank | Where marks die | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **State questions not applied to the business** — 10–20% success, cause never knowledge *(rule 108)* | **456 one-mark parts** — the largest tier in the qualification |
| **2** | **The 3-mark mechanism gap** — four 0/3s, one fault *(rule 126)*; a second reason on a `one` question *(Pass 1)* | **152 parts** |
| **3** | **Calculations: answer line, precision, the discount error** *(rules 59 · 101 · 113)* | **152 two-mark parts** |
| **4** | **Analyse as a list** instead of one developed chain *(rules 96 · 97 · 110)* | **114 parts** |
| **5** | **Justify without a named condition** — the whole band hangs on it *(rule 91)* | **76 parts** |
| **6** | **Evaluate without woven judgement** — and bullets disqualify the top levels *(rules 82 · 84)* | **38 parts · 12 marks each — a quarter of the paper on one question** |

⚠️ **Note the arithmetic of the paper:** the six 12-mark questions in 4BS1 are worth 12 of the 80 marks each. **One weak 12-marker can cost more than ten state questions.**
