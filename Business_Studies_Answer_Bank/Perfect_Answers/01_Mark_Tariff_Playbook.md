## The assessment objectives

From the official June 2019 Paper 1/1R exemplar booklet, p. 2:

| AO | Skill | % of IGCSE |
|---|---|---|
| **AO1** | Recall, select and communicate knowledge and understanding of business terms, concepts and issues | **27–28%** |
| **AO2** | Apply knowledge and understanding using appropriate business terms, concepts, theories and calculations effectively in **specific contexts** | **38–39%** |
| **AO3** | Select, organise and interpret business information from sources to investigate and **analyse** issues | **22–23%** |
| **AO4** | **Evaluate** business information to make reasoned judgements and draw conclusions | **11–12%** |

> *A maximum of 15% of total qualification marks will assess knowledge recall.*

**Read that last line as a warning.** AO1 recall is capped. AO2 application is the single biggest bucket in the qualification. An answer that is knowledgeable but generic caps out low — this is exactly what the examiner comments repeat over and over.

## Tariff by tariff

### 1 mark

| Command word | AO | What earns the mark |
|---|---|---|
| **Define** | AO1 | Correct definition. Nothing else. |
| **State / Identify** | AO1 or AO2 | One correct point. One. |
| **MCQ / Select one** | AO2 | The right letter. |

Mark scheme wording, verbatim:

> `1 (b)  A01 - 1 mark`
> `Award 1 mark for a correct definition of fringe benefits.`

> `2 (a)  A02 - 1 mark`

Note the AO can flip even at 1 mark. A *define* is AO1. A *state one method **for this business*** is AO2.

**Trap:** don't waste time. One line. Move on.

---

### 2 marks

| Command word | AO | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Outline one** | AO2 | Identify **(1)** + link to the business context **(1)** |
| **Calculate** | AO2 | Method/workings **(1)** + correct answer **(1)** |

> `3 (b)  A02 - 2 marks`
> `Award 1 mark for identifying a way Irsi Chocolatier could keep customer loyalty, plus 1 further mark for linking it to the context of the business.`

**The second mark is a context mark.** It is not a second idea. If you give two generic points you get **1 mark**. You must attach the point to the named business.

> `1 (e)  Award full marks for correct numerical answer without working.`

For a calculation: show the substitution anyway. It is worth a mark if you slip on the arithmetic.

### 3 marks — EXPLAIN

**AO1 = 3 marks.** (Verified: `1 (f)  A01 - 3 marks`)

> `Award 1 mark for identification of a benefit for a business that uses test marketing, plus 2 further marks for explaining the benefit, for a maximum of 3 marks.`
>
> `NB Answers that list three benefits with no explanation will get 1 mark only.`

**The single most expensive mistake in the paper.** The examiner states the penalty explicitly: three named benefits, no development → **1 mark**. The mark scheme's own worked example shows the required shape:

> `• The risk of failure is reduced (1) as the new product is only tested in one area gaining feedback from customers (1) leading to modifications being made before the final launch (1)`

That is **one idea, three linked steps**:

```
IDENTIFY (1)  →  BECAUSE… (1)  →  WHICH MEANS / LEADING TO… (1)
```

**Literal opening sentence:**

> "One benefit of test marketing is that the risk of failure is reduced, because…"

**Test your own answer:** cover the middle. If your answer still works with the chains removed, you wrote a list. Redo it.

---

### 6 marks — ANALYSE

**AO2 = 3 marks, AO3 = 3 marks. There is NO AO4.**

Verified: `1 (g)  AO2 = 3 marks  AO3 = 3 marks` · `3 (d)  AO2 = 3 marks  AO3 = 3 marks` · `4 (b)` equivalent.

| Level | Marks | Descriptor (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–2 | *Limited* application of knowledge and understanding of business concepts and issues to the business context. (AO2) · *Attempts* to deconstruct business information and/or issues, finding *limited* connections between points. (AO3) |
| 2 | 3–4 | *Sound* application … although there may be some inconsistencies. · Deconstructs business information and/or issues, finding *interconnected* points with chains of reasoning, although there may be *some* logical inconsistencies. |
| 3 | 5–6 | *Detailed* application … **throughout**. · Deconstructs … finding *detailed* interconnected points with **logical chains of reasoning**. |

Read the upgrade pattern in the descriptors — it is mechanical:

| | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| AO2 word | Limited | Sound | **Detailed, throughout** |
| AO3 word | Limited connections | Interconnected | Detailed, **logical** chains |

**What this means in practice:**
- "**throughout**" — context cannot be one mention of the business name in the opening line. Weave the context through every chain.
- "logical chains" — each step must follow from the one before it.

**No conclusion needed or rewarded.** AO4 is worth zero here. Save the judgement for 9 and 12 marks.

### 9 marks — JUSTIFY

**AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3.**

Verified: `2 (f)  AO2 = 3 marks  AO3 = 3 marks  AO4 = 3 marks` · `3 (e)` identical.

| Level | Marks | AO4 requirement (verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–3 | Makes a judgement, providing a *simple* justification based on *limited* evaluation of business information and issues relevant to the choice made. |
| 2 | 4–6 | Makes a judgement, providing a justification based on *sound* evaluation … |
| 3 | 7–9 | Makes a judgement, providing a **clear** justification based on a **thorough** evaluation of business information and issues relevant to the choice made. |

**The examiners' most repeated complaint — there are no knowledge marks here.** From the June 2019 booklet, on a 3/9 response:

> *"A number of responses seen have demonstrated knowledge of both options; there are no knowledge marks available for this question."*

And from the May 2023 P2 examiner report on Q3(e):

> *"A number of candidates described both methods … there are no knowledge marks available for this type of question. … It may help some candidates to gain the higher level if they give an evaluative comment as to the chosen option and why it would be more suitable."*

**So: do not spend your words defining or describing the two options.** Assume the examiner knows what crowdfunding is. Everything you write must be doing AO2 (context), AO3 (chains) or AO4 (judgement) work.

**Structure that matches the descriptors:**

```
1. STATE THE CHOICE IMMEDIATELY
   "Backyard Shoez should choose Option 1, because…"
2. JUSTIFY with applied chains (AO2 + AO3)
3. COUNTER-ARGUE — acknowledge the weakness of the option you chose
4. FINAL JUDGEMENT, still in context (AO4)
```

Note step 3. In the verified 9/9, the candidate turns against their own choice mid-answer — *"However, if the retailer uses crowdfunding then every investor would own a share of the business. This could result in a loss of control…"* — and then resolves it in the conclusion. That balance is what builds the AO4 marks.

### 12 marks — EVALUATE

**AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3.** *The only tariff that pays AO1 separately.*

| Level | Marks | Band |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **1–4** | |
| 2 | **5–8** | |
| 3 | **9–12** | |

From the June 2024 P1 examiner report:

> *"Centres are reminded that this is the only level-based question in the paper which is equally weighted across all four assessment objectives… Candidates at level 1 made some attempt at the question and were able to make basic statements about marketing, often with very little or no context. Those candidates who accessed level 3 considered the importance of marketing in the context of PPS and the impact this can have on the business, they have then successfully linked their points together in a logical order and been able to conclude their answer with a well-balanced and justified argument."*

And from the May 2023 P2 examiner report:

> *"Some candidates have used bullet points or numbered items, this will not allow them to gain the higher levels."*

**Two hard rules:**
1. **Write prose. Never bullet points.** The report says bullets cap you below the higher levels.
2. **You must conclude.** A well-balanced, justified conclusion is what the level 3 descriptor rewards.

**The one 12-marker that is different.** The June 2024 P1 Q4(c) examiner comment notes the 12/12 used *"the 'it depends rule'"*. Weaker responses assert flatly; the top response says it depends on X and Y, then commits anyway. See the verified 12/12 in `02_Verified_Exemplars.md` for exactly how that reads.

> **AO1 is tested on only FOUR command words: Define, State, Explain, Evaluate.**
>
> **On Outline, Analyse and Justify — 17 of the paper's 80 marks — knowledge earns exactly nothing.**
>
> **The single biggest self-inflicted loss on the paper is writing a definition where a development was asked for.**

## 42. ⭐⭐ THE 12-MARKER IS 3 / 3 / 3 / 3 — and it is the ONLY one

> *"centres are reminded that this is **the only level-based question in the paper which is equally weighted across all four assessment objectives**."* (Summer 2019 P1 Q4(c))

**AO1 3 · AO2 3 · AO3 3 · AO4 3.** No other question on the paper is weighted that way.

**Implication for answer shape — a quarter of the marks are AO4.** Since ~80% of candidates stop at Level 2, and the two recorded causes are *no application* and *no thorough judgement*, the practical split is:

> **~3 sentences of textbook-correct knowledge · ~4 sentences of applied chain · a paragraph of both sides · a conclusion that DECIDES and RANKS.**
>
> **The conclusion is worth up to 3 marks on its own.** It is not an optional flourish.

## 49. ⭐⭐⭐ JUSTIFY — YOU DO NOT HAVE TO DISCUSS BOTH OPTIONS

> *"**Candidates do not have to consider both options in their response. Candidates can achieve the full marks only considering the one option.**"* (June 2022 P2R Q2(f))

**Nine marks are available for one option, thoroughly developed.** There is no requirement to argue both sides.

**Why this matters so much:** candidates who "cover both" routinely produce **two shallow halves instead of one deep argument**. *"The response is mainly for the option chosen"* is recorded by the examiner as a **neutral-to-positive** observation.

> **So: pick the option you can develop furthest and commit to it.** Use the other only as a one-sentence comparison inside your conclusion.

**And the Justify structure is exactly Analyse plus judgement:**

> *"The '**Justify**' questions are marked in the same way as the '**Analyse**' questions which is **Levels**, but candidates now have to include '**Evaluative**' (**Assessment Objective 4**) comments."*

## 50. ⭐ THE STATE RULE, WITH A MARKED 1/0 PAIR

June 2022 P2R Q1(d) — *State one reason why Cadbury might pay its UK employees above the minimum wage.*

| | **1 mark** | **0 marks** |
|---|---|---|
| Response | *"To retain its employees. In this case, Cadbury would want to retain its employees so they don't switch to **competitors like Lindt or Royce**."* | *"This is to improve brand image and reputation in the market, as it has **lots of competition**, it needs good image and reputation to attract workers to work for them and to attract customers to buy from them."* |
| Length | 18 words | **43 words** |
| Examiner | *"in context by commenting on Cadbury not wanting its employees to switch to competitors like Lindt"* | *"has made **very valid comments**… **However, there is nothing that is related back to Cadbury and its workers.**"* |

**The 0-mark answer is longer, more fluent, and says nothing wrong.** It correctly identifies competition as the reason — and earns nothing.

**The rule:** *"Just putting Cadbury in the response does not mean it is in context **as Cadbury is in the question**."*

**And the examiner's own remedy — the most useful technique in this bank:**

> *"**Try and avoid using the name of the scenario but include something that is related to the business. In this example perhaps including chocolate cake designers would put the response in context.**"*

> **Do the opposite of what most candidates do.** Do not write the business's name. Write **a specific noun from the case that could not appear in a generic answer** — *"chocolate cake designers"*, *"competitors like Lindt"*, *"the quality of the chocolate packaging"*.
>
> **Also confirmed:** *"'State' questions are **AO2 (Application)** hence there must be some evidence of application or context within the response."*

## 51. ⭐ THE DECIMAL-PLACE RULE, PROVEN ON A REAL SCRIPT

June 2022 P2R Q1(e), *calculate the selling price of a chocolate cake* (**2 marks**):

| Response | Mark |
|---|---|
| *"6.5 x (1+20%) = 6.5 x 1.2 = **£7.8**"* | **1 mark** — *"working out is correct but the answer was **missing the second decimal place**"* |
| *"6.50 × 1.2 = £**7.80**"* | **2 marks** — *"A clear response and easy to read."* |

> **Correct method. Correct arithmetic. One character missing. One mark gone.** 10th recorded instance of the d.p. rule, and the first shown on a script.
>
> **Tip:** *"Check the question carefully to see if one or two decimal places are required in the answer."*

**Two neighbouring calculation rules, same report:**
> **Workings are insurance.** *"they could gain a mark for the working, **even if the actual answer written in the box is incorrect**."*
> **Put the answer on the line.** *"write their answer on the **dotted lines at the bottom of the box**."*
> **Make digits legible.** *"their **figures are easily recognisable**, to avoid any confusion by the examiner."*

## 52. ⭐⭐ A FULL-MARKS 6-MARK ANALYSE IS THREE SENTENCES

June 2022 P2R Q1(g) — *Analyse two factors that could influence Cadbury when locating a new factory.* **Awarded 6/6:**

> *"Proximity to employees This is because the factory being close to employees would reduce Cadbury's costs that would be used for transport. This leads to them being able to save time that would be used in the journey, therefore, Cadbury would increase their efficiency."*
>
> **Examiner:** *"A well answered response, clearly identifying the two factors of location and giving justifiable reasons."*

**43 words. No introduction. No definition. No listing. No conclusion.**

**Skeleton to copy:**
> `[Factor] → This is because [mechanism applied to the business] → This leads to [business outcome] → therefore [Business] would [result].`

**And the 5-mark answer on the same question was *better informed* — it named "cocoa seeds" — and still lost a mark:**

> *"the response did not gain full marks because **it lacked the logical chains of reasoning**. Perhaps the candidate could have **linked the two factors together**…"*

> **On a two-part Analyse: do not write two disconnected mini-answers. Finish with one sentence that JOINS them.**

**Also from this question:** *"there are **no marks available for knowledge**, and the response **must be in context**"* · *"Examiners will look at the response **'holistically'**"*.

## 53. ⭐ THE 3-MARK EXPLAIN — three rungs, and the third one is the business outcome

Four Explain questions on June 2022 P2R, each with a **2/3 and a 3/3** answer.

| Rung | Earns | Q2(c) example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The point** — named | 1 | *"job enrichment"* |
| **2. The mechanism** — why it works | 1 | *"employees will be more challenged… feeling trusted and valuable"* |
| **3. The business outcome** | 1 | *"the business would be increasing its **productivity**"* |

**The examiner's 2-mark verdict, verbatim:** *"the response was **not further developed to gain the extra mark available**."*

> **The third rung is almost always one of: productivity · costs · quality · customer satisfaction · revenue · survival · loyalty.**
> **If your answer ends on "this motivates them" or "this improves cash flow", you are one rung short.**
>
> **And: *"the question asked for one advantage not several advantages."*** The listing cap, again.

| Rule | Evidence (June 2022 P2R) |
|---|---|

| **Do not copy the question into the box** | *"avoid wasting valuable time **writing the question in the answer box**"* |

## 55. ⭐ LANGUAGE IS NOT MARKED — verified on scripts

Every full-mark answer in the June 2022 P2R report contains **spelling and grammar errors**:

| Mark awarded | What the candidate actually wrote |
|---|---|
| **3/3** | *"This leads **no** the employees feeling trusted and valuable **no** the business"* |
| **3/3** | *"if **call** employees and trained to a standard"* · *"they **'ll** reduce their legal costs"* |
| **3/3** | *"there **for** more productive employees"* |
| **7/9** | *"reduced burden and **move tembility**"* |
| **6/6** | *"would increase **their** efficiency"* — no full stop structure, one long sentence |

> **Examiners mark business content. They do not deduct for spelling, grammar, or sentence structure.**
>
> **Consequence for technique: do not spend exam time proofreading for style.** Spend it on the third rung, on context, and on the conclusion. **Clarity matters because the examiner must be able to find the mark — nothing more.**

## 57. ⭐⭐ THE FORMULA QUESTION — finally resolved

Three sources, previously in tension, now reconciled:

| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| Pearson support page | *"some formulas are provided at the front of the paper"* |
| Summer 2019 P1 | *"some formulas are provided at the front of the paper, whilst most candidates did use these **there are still some that are not**"* |
| Nov 2020 P1R | *"**As the break-even formula is not given in the paper it is one that candidates need to know.**"* |

**The Specification resolves it.** Content point **3.5.1** lists the ratios and states:

> *"**Calculating and analysing accounting ratios:** gross profit margin · operating profit margin · markup · return on capital employed (ROCE) · current ratio · acid test ratio. **(Formulae for use in this qualification can be found in Appendix 4. Learners will be provided with these formulae in the examination.)**"*

**Break-even sits in a different content point, 3.3.2, which gives no such promise:**

> *"The concept of break-even and calculation of break-even (**from formula or diagram**): break-even level of output."*

> ### ✅ THE RULE, DEFINITIVELY
> **The formula sheet gives you the SIX ACCOUNTING RATIOS.** Gross profit margin · operating profit margin · markup · ROCE · current ratio · acid test ratio.
> **It does NOT give you break-even, contribution, total costs, or profit.**
>
> **Memorise these outright:**
> **Break-even output = Total fixed costs ÷ Contribution per unit**
> **Contribution per unit = Selling price − Variable cost per unit**
> **Total costs = Fixed costs + Variable costs**
> **Profit = Revenue − Total costs**
>
> ⚠️ **And note the trap in 3.3.2: "from formula _or diagram_"** — the specification allows *either*, but the paper's chart questions ask you to **read** a break-even chart, never to draw one. **Never draw a break-even diagram in the exam** (see `07_Official_Exam_Scope.md`).

## 59. 🚨 THE ANSWER-LINE RULE — the d.p. requirement, at full strength

> *"**Centres should be aware that examiners are advised to take the answer provided by candidates on the final answer line** therefore if the final answer is not to 2 decimal places the candidates **will not gain access to the full marks even if the answer to 2 decimal places is in the working out. The answer provided by a candidate on the answer line is viewed as their final response to the question so that is what is marked.**"* (June 2024 P1R)

**This is stricter than anything recorded before.** Workings are insurance **for the method mark only**. The number written on the final line **is** the answer.

> **So the sequence is: work it out → round it as the question demands → write it ON THE LINE.** The `.80` on the line is worth more than the `.80` in the working.

## 60. ⭐ THE FOUR CONTEXT ROUTES — Pearson's official list

> *"**The name of the business does not provide context.** Candidates can choose to: **name the products / services the business offers**, **name competitors in the same industry**, or **state the industry the business operates in**… to provide a contextualised response."* (June 2024 P1R Q3(b))

| Route | Café Pinch | Cadbury | LEGO |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Products / services** | *"filled Ciabattas"*, *"breads and teas"* | *"chocolate cake"*, *"chocolate packaging"* | *"Harry Potter Legos"*, *"pineapple pencil holders"* |
| **2. A competitor** | *"other Café shops in Berlin"* | *"competitors like Lindt or Royce"* | *"other rivals in the toy market"* |
| **3. The industry** | *"the Café industry"* | *"the chocolate market"* | *"the toy market"* |
| **4. The type of business** | *"its café in Berlin"*, *"a small trader"* | *"a chocolate producing firm"* | *"a toy manufacturer"* |

**The test Pearson supplies:** *"check if their response is **generic and can be applied to any business** — or have they included phrases that link it directly to the given scenario?"*

> **On a regional paper (01R / 02R) the LOCATION is free context** — *"its café in Berlin"*, *"compared to other cafés in surrounding areas"*.

## 61. ⭐ WATCH FOR: the testable version of "the name rule" across three papers

The same rule, stated three different ways, all with a worked alternative:

| Report | Statement | The examiner's suggested replacement |
|---|---|---|
| June 2022 P2R (Cadbury) | *"Just putting Cadbury in the response does not mean it is in context as Cadbury is in the question."* | *"**chocolate cake designers**"* |
| June 2022 P2 (LEGO) | *"using the word LEGO is the response is not in context as it is in the question."* | *"profit from sales of **Princess Leia toy**"* |
| June 2024 P1R (Café Pinch) | *"**The name of the business does not provide context.**"* | *"replaced the business name with the type of business e.g. **cafe**… or replaced the word products with **filled Ciabattas**"* |

**Three papers, three examiners, one instruction: the business name is worth nothing. A product, competitor, industry or business type is worth the mark.**

## 62. ⭐ TWO DIFFERENT 3-MARK EXPLAIN TECHNIQUES

> *"**Questions that ask to explain an advantage or disadvantage need a different technique to those that ask candidates to explain a method or way.**"* (June 2022 P2, LEGO)

| Question asks for… | The three rungs | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **a method / a way** | name the method → how it works → **the business outcome** | *"job enrichment" → "employees feel trusted and valued" → "the business would be increasing its productivity"* |
| **an advantage / a disadvantage** | name the effect → **why it arises** → **the consequence for the business** | *"customers might not be telling the truth" → "could cause a misunderstanding" → "creating a product people don't want"* |

**Confusing the two is why candidates list methods on advantage questions and describe effects on method questions.**

**And the AO formula as a one-liner, from the same report:** **Analyse = application + analysis. Justify = application + analysis + evaluation.**

## 63. ⭐ CONNECTIVES ARE NOT ANALYSIS

June 2022 P2 (LEGO) Q1(g) — two marked answers on the *benefits of being a multinational*, **5 marks and 4 marks**.

- The **4-mark** answer was **better structured and better informed**: two clean paragraphs, perfect *"This is because… Therefore… As a result… Hence…"* chains, named *"Harry Potter Legos"*, *"plastic"*, *"trade discount"*.
- The **5-mark** answer was one rambling paragraph with almost no connectives — but kept returning to **business outcomes**: *"more profit"*, *"survive in the market for long time"*, *"increased consumer loyalty"*, *"Lego will be benefited"*.

**Examiner on the 4-mark answer:** *"not further developed as to how being a multinational could further enhance the selling of toys"* · *"needed to **develop further the comments made and give interconnected points**."*

> ### 🔴 The lesson
> *"Therefore… as a result… hence"* chained together still count for nothing **if each link ends at a mechanism** (*"a cheaper price"*, *"a competitive edge"*) rather than **a business outcome**.
>
> **Rung check: does each chain end on the BUSINESS — profit, survival, loyalty, market position? If it ends on "cheaper price", keep going.**

## Quick pre-flight checklist

Before you hand anything over, run this:

- [ ] **3-markers:** Is this ONE idea developed in three steps, or three named ideas? (Three ideas = 1 mark.)
- [ ] **2-markers:** Does the second half name *this* business, or could it be about any business?
- [ ] **6-markers:** Have I written any conclusion or judgement? (It earns nothing — that time was better spent on another chain.)
- [ ] **6-markers:** Is the context sustained "throughout", or just in sentence one?
- [ ] **9-markers:** Did I state a clear choice and defend it — or did I describe both options?
- [ ] **12-markers:** Am I in continuous prose, with no bullets?
- [ ] **12-markers:** Do I have a real conclusion, ideally weighing "it depends on…" before committing?
