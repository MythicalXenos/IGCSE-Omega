## How this audit was checked

Source coordinates below are PDF page numbers (the printed folio agrees for these question pages). The relevant official-answer block is also in `By_Paper/4BS1_<session>_<paper>.md`; existing family models are in `10_Chain_Bank.md` at the entry shown.

The MS allocates **AO1 3, AO2 3, AO3 3 and AO4 3** to each of these 12-mark questions. The applicable level bands are **Level 1: 1–4; Level 2: 5–8; Level 3: 9–12**. Level 3 requires accurate knowledge, detailed contextual application, detailed connected reasoning, and a clear justified judgement based on thorough evaluation. Those are rubric descriptors, not a recipe assigning three marks to each paragraph. The bank’s constructed answers remain unscored.

Missing item-specific ER or candidate scripts are explicitly recorded below; they did **not** prevent QP/MS answer review. Candidate awards are attributed only to the official source that prints them.

## Review by reasoning family

### 1. Location — site factors must be specific to the operation

#### May–June 2019 P2 — Tata Motor Company

**Trace:** QP p.14 (factory-location stem; see also the company scale figures on p.13); matching P2 MS p.19, levels p.20. QP/MS files: `Paperlords_Archive/IGCSE/4BS1_Business_Studies/May-June_2019/4BS1_Business_Studies_May-June_2019_P2_{QP,MS}.pdf`. The MS has the four 3-mark AO headings and accepts skilled labour and accessibility; its applied examples are engineers/designers and access to ports, motorways or rail for materials and completed vehicles.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** compare access to skilled engineers/designers with transport access for incoming materials and outgoing cars. Link an accessible skilled workforce to lower operating/location costs, while transport links support continuity of supply and delivery. Weigh higher land/infrastructure costs near skilled labour and the fact that transport infrastructure may matter less than other site factors. Conclude conditionally on workforce availability, total location cost and delivery needs.

**Marking rationale:** merely listing “labour” and “transport” is not AO3. Develop each into a Tata-specific consequence; use the MS’s cost trade-off and ranking point for AO4. The case’s global scale can support context, but does not by itself prove that a particular site is best.

**Official candidate evidence (separate from the model):** the June 2019 P2 exemplar booklet, original PDF pp.24–28, reproduces two Q4(c) scripts awarded **8/12 and 7/12**, with examiner comments. Neither is full marks. The comments emphasize that a long list of factors is not a substitute for development and evaluation. See `Sources/Official_Pearson/June2019_P2_Exemplar_Responses_Selected_Pages.pdf` and `Perfect_Answers/External_Exemplars/2019-06_P2_Official_Exemplar_Booklet.md`.

#### May–June 2024 P2R — Jimmy Choo

**Trace:** QP p.19; matching MS p.21, levels p.22. The QP says most manufacturing suppliers are in Italy, with some in Spain and China, and a new clothing/shoe factory would require skilled workers. The MS accepts a skilled workforce and road/rail/sea accessibility, then asks candidates to consider higher labour cost and whether infrastructure is the most important factor.

The MS names Italy as a potential skilled-labour location, but the answer must treat cost reductions as conditional, not guaranteed. Conclude on the relative importance of skills, transport and cost at the candidate site.

### 2. Competitive advantage — separate how it is gained from how it is sustained

#### May–June 2019 P2R — Yellow

**Trace:** QP p.14 gives four specific features: wide fashionable range, limited advertising, broad demographic, and supplier stock ready for store shelves. Matching MS p.18, levels p.20; the MS content is case-compatible but does not print the stem again.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** connect variety and different price ranges to one-stop family shopping; connect quick response to fashion demand and ready-to-sell supplier stock to timely availability; connect low manufacturing cost to reasonable prices. Balance this against different tastes within families and customers who prefer durable clothing over fashion changes. Judge how far the claimed advantage depends on customer segment and whether the cost/speed offer is maintained.

**Marking rationale:** the question asks how Yellow *may have gained* advantage; the four prompt bullets are not enough by themselves. Each needs an outcome for Yellow and a limit. The ER commentary says many candidates used the bullets but did not develop them or justify their answer. Source: `Perfect_Answers/External_Exemplars/2019-06_P2R_Yellow.md`, Q4(c). No candidate score or script is claimed from that commentary.

#### May–June 2022 P2 — LEGO

**Trace:** QP pp.17–18 provides LEGO’s quality values and “Toy of the Century” history, then connects new toys to films such as Harry Potter. Matching MS p.21, levels p.22.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** longstanding reputation and durable quality support trust; reliable toys and a well-known brand can sustain preference, while film-linked sets introduce products to fans and support demand. Balance the higher price against cheaper alternatives, and the risk that an unsuccessful film tie-in leaves LEGO with development/investment costs and unsold toys. Judge the durability of the brick/brand advantage against the uncertainty of particular licensed products and price-sensitive buyers.

**Marking rationale:** the key verb is *maintained*: connect ongoing quality/reputation and current film-linked products to continuing preference, then test whether those advantages endure. The MS’s counterpoints are price and film success; do not substitute an unrelated “low cost” argument.

### 3. Customer demand, research, targeting and marketing choices

#### November 2021 P2 — Huawei market research

**Trace:** QP p.16 introduces Huawei’s quality, customer-needs and technology aims and a new-product context; Q4(c) is p.18. Matching MS p.20, levels p.22.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** research identifies needs/wants and informs design decisions; applying comments to watch demand or smartphone features/sizes may prevent costly development of a product with little demand, protect Huawei’s finances and support competitiveness. Balance this against the possibility that an original watch with little competition could succeed and that needs change rapidly with technology. Conclude on the usefulness of research as risk reduction, not a guarantee.

**ER evidence:** the item-specific commentary is reproduced, with its transcription caveat, in `Perfect_Answers/External_Exemplars/Advisory_Reports_Nov2023_Nov2024_Nov2021_2019P1R.md`. #### May–June 2022 P2R — Cadbury marketing a new product

**Trace:** QP p.18; matching MS p.23, levels p.24. The MS accepts research to identify a product gap (e.g. chocolate ice cream), customer loyalty, repeat purchases/recommendations, and the risk of bad publicity or discounts/free gifts reducing returns.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** use research to identify a product customers may want; a well-received ice cream can add sales and recommendations, while loyal customers may try later products. Weigh this against bad publicity damaging reputation/sales and the cost of offers needed to sustain loyalty. Decide whether launch importance depends on the quality of research, customer response and the cost of maintaining loyalty.

#### May 2023 P2 — IKEA demographic segmentation

**Trace:** QP p.19 sets IKEA’s customer-taste and home-furnishing context. Matching MS p.23, levels pp.24–25. (Consult the source PDF if a page-layout detail is material.) The MS points are age/family and income segments, age-specific furniture needs and country-level range decisions; counterpoints are imperfect categories and no guarantee of expected buying.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** demographic information can guide product and range choices—reasonably priced, child-suitable furniture for young families; different options for higher-income customers; comfortable, reliable chairs for older customers—and country-specific stocking. Balance against people not fitting one segment and customers not behaving as predicted. Conclude segmentation is a useful starting point, not a guarantee of purchases; combine it with observed customer behaviour.

**Official candidate evidence:** May 2023 P2 ER, printed pp.56–57, reports a **10/12** candidate response. #### November 2023 P2 — Kraft Heinz advertising variety

**Trace:** QP p.20 states Kraft Heinz uses television and posters, sells to young and old, and used Ed Sheeran in a Heinz tomato-sauce advertisement. Matching MS p.25, levels p.27. The MS distinguishes the celebrity’s younger audience from posters’ short, repeated messages; its counterpoints are no guaranteed sales increase from celebrity endorsement and posters being damaged/difficult to evaluate.

Balance against endorsement not guaranteeing sales and poster wear/measurement limits. Judge whether the mix is useful only when each method reaches a relevant target and its results justify the spend.

**Correction/rationale:** this is specifically about *variety*, not generic advertising. The reviewed ER excerpt (`Perfect_Answers/External_Exemplars/2023-11_P2_Kraft_Heinz.md`, Q4(c)) says candidates named valid methods but often failed to develop and justify; it gives cohort-level Level proportions, not an individual score. #### May–June 2025 P2 — Starbucks product trials

**Trace:** QP p.18 says trials occur before products are introduced to all coffee shops. Matching MS p.20, levels p.21. The MS identifies wasted money on an unpopular coffee/cake, the need to learn whether customers may buy, and the uncertainty that a disliked trial product might later become popular or that research guarantees purchases.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** a limited trial can gather customer opinion before full rollout and reduce exposure to wasting money on an unpopular product. Balance against trial findings not guaranteeing broader-market demand; a trial result may not predict later tastes. Decide based on how representative the trial is and the scale of a full rollout, without inventing sample counts or guaranteed cost savings.

**Corrections made:** the previous model said “a few hundred” trial customers, although the QP/MS give no sample count, and implied sample size alone makes results accurate. It now says sample suitability/representativeness matters and treats larger sample size only as the MS’s qualified point.

#### June 2026 P2R — Apple responding to market changes

**Trace:** QP p.19 names new features, applications, folding devices and customers seeking cheaper phones. Matching MS p.20, levels p.21. MS supports responding through design/pricing and a wider range, but notes Apple may already be responding and loyal buyers may accept high prices.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** new foldable features may reach customers and protect competitiveness; considering lower-cost competitors may help retain people with limited income. Balance this against Apple already updating its devices and loyal customers’ willingness to pay; a lower-price model may not improve its position. Judge response by whether the shift is material to Apple’s buyers and whether adapting preserves the value proposition.

#### June 2026 P2 — Nando’s market orientation and future success

**Trace:** QP p.18 asks whether market orientation guarantees future success. Matching MS p.25, levels pp.27–28. MS accepts learning food tastes, research of new dishes, repeat purchase and revising dishes before a wider launch; counterpoints are research delay and a product-oriented approach.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** customer research can guide dishes and allow changes before wider launch, reducing the risk of unpopular menu choices and supporting repeat purchases. Balance against research taking time while trends move and Nando’s choosing to pursue its own quality/innovation. The word *guarantee* requires the conclusion to say that market orientation helps but cannot ensure success; judge by speed of change and Nando’s ability to act on findings.

**Corrections made:** the prior model called a test kitchen “far cheaper” than a failed launch and said first movers are often remembered; neither is established by the case/MS. Those absolute claims have been removed. The answer now confines itself to the MS-supported risk-reduction and time/strategy trade-offs.

### 4. Service quality and public relations

#### May–June 2021 P2 — NEXT maintaining quality

**Trace:** QP p.16 describes NEXT’s aim to improve manufacturing and cut costs while maintaining product quality; Q4(c) is p.18. Matching MS p.20, levels p.21. The MS applies clothing/furniture and the same standard across 70 countries, with poor reputation linked to fewer customers/closed shops; counterpoints are inspection cost and difficulty regaining loyalty after quality failure.

#### May–June 2024 P2 — TUI providing quality service

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** a good holiday experience can encourage return bookings and recommendations to family/friends. Weigh the cost of employing enough staff to meet customer needs against the risk that poor car-hire/service variety damages loyalty, which is difficult to regain. Conclude on service consistency and cost across TUI’s offerings.

#### May–June 2025 P2R — Rabanne public relations

**Trace:** QP p.18 says Rabanne uses refillable glass bottles, sustainable packaging and charity donations. Matching MS p.20, levels p.21. MS links donations/environmental action to customer interest and possible purchasing, while noting customers may care more about the products and sales effects are not guaranteed.

Balance that against customers prioritising fragrance/aftershave and the absence of a guaranteed sales increase. ### 5. Finance and measuring success

#### May 2023 P2R — M&M cash-flow forecasting

**Trace:** QP p.19; matching MS p.24, levels p.26. The MS connects forecasts to future net cash flow and decisions on investment, specialist staff and product features, with a caution that forecasts are predictions and available cash/costs/customer response are uncertain.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** forecasts help M&M judge when cash may be available to add features or employ specialist staff; insufficient forecast cash may justify waiting. A forecast informs timing/affordability but does not guarantee receipts or that customers will like the product. Decide using forecast reliability, cash reserves and the investment’s expected benefit. Do not confuse cash-flow forecasting with an income statement.

**Official candidate evidence:** May 2023 P2R ER, printed pp.44–45, attributes **10/12** to a candidate response and says Level 3 candidates linked their points and concluded with balanced, justified reasoning. The examiner also warns that some candidates confused a cash-flow forecast with a statement of comprehensive income. Source: `Perfect_Answers/External_Exemplars/2023-05_Series_Irsi_AudeRose_IKEA_MM.md`. That 10/12 is a candidate award, not a mark for the constructed spine.

#### November 2024 P2 — Premier Inn profit as the only success measure

**Trace:** QP p.18; matching MS p.24, levels p.26. The MS accepts profit comparison between hotels and use of profit to assess new sites, but says profit depends on outside factors such as travel restrictions and names market share/customer satisfaction as additional measures.

**Answer spine — constructed, unscored:** profit indicates whether revenue exceeds costs over a period; comparing hotels can inform investment and planned openings. Balance against travel restrictions outside the business’s control and the other measures of success in the MS. Judge profit as a useful financial indicator, but test the absolute word *only*: ignoring customer satisfaction or market share can hide important performance information.

**Corrections made:** “only profit tells it whether a new site is paying for itself” overstated profit’s exclusivity; profit alone does not establish cash available or explain performance. The prior “full car park” illustration and unsupported claim that market share/satisfaction necessarily lead profit were removed.

**ER evidence:** item-specific examiner commentary is transcribed, with an explicit text-layer caveat, in `Perfect_Answers/External_Exemplars/Advisory_Reports_Nov2023_Nov2024_Nov2021_2019P1R.md`. It says stronger answers explain the case for profit and counterbalance with why it may not be best/why other measures matter; it cautions that undeveloped counterbalance makes the top level unlikely. No individual script or score is claimed.
