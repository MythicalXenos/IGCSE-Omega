## ⚠️ CRITICAL: TWO DIFFERENT SPECIFICATIONS

Pearson publishes similar-looking materials for **two different qualifications**. They are **not interchangeable**:

| | **IGCSE Business 4BS1** ← *your qualification* | GCSE Business 1BS0 |
|---|---|---|
| Papers | Paper 1 (Investigating Small Businesses) + Paper 2 (Investigating Large Businesses) | Paper 1 + Paper 2 (different structure) |
| Paper total | **80 marks** | 90 marks |
| 12-mark AO split | **AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3** | AO1b = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3a = 3, AO3b = 3 |
| 6/9/12-mark tariffs | ✅ same tariffs | ✅ same tariffs |

**Where a source is GCSE 1BS0, it is labelled as such and must not be presented as 4BS1 material.** The *technique* transfers; the *exemplar scripts* do not.

## The discovery: Principal Examiner Feedback reports

The single most valuable external resource is Pearson's **Principal Examiner Feedback (PEF)** report series. Unlike the mark schemes already in the archive, each PEF report contains:

- **the actual question**
- **a real candidate's written response, reproduced verbatim**
- **the mark it was awarded**
- **an examiner comment explaining exactly why**

This is exactly the `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` standard, and there are more PEF reports than the 5 examiner reports already held in the archive.

> **Note on transcription.** The candidate responses below are reproduced from Pearson's own PEF PDFs, which are text-based (not images). Minor OCR artefacts from the source file are preserved where present, and are marked `[sic]` where they could confuse.

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 3/3 · Q2(c) Explain

> *"One impact of ineffective communication is the workers may do mistakes as the instructions are unclear to them. This means, once mistakes are done, there will be wastage. Revenue cost may increase and profit decreases."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This candidate correctly identifies the impact is 'workers may do mistakes' they have then gone on to explain this well with the links that 'instructions are unclear to them' and 'there will be wastage.'"*

**Examiner tip — important and counter-intuitive:**
> *"No context is required for explain questions but often we see candidates applying context unnecessarily. This candidate has then unnecessarily further explained this one impact but as we can see from the mark scheme candidates can only gain 2 further marks for explaining this reason."*

**Two lessons.** (1) The 3-mark explain chain is identify → develop → develop. (2) **Explain questions do NOT need context** — adding it wastes time. (3) Once you have identify + 2 developments, **stop**; extra explanation earns nothing.

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 9/9 · Q2(f) Justify

**Question:** Justify which option PPS should choose to improve its revenue — trialling a new brand of dog food, or advertising.

> *"[Trialling] a new brand of dog food in the shop and advertising it can make customers interested and loyal because you are introducing a new brand of dog food, and the pet owners might be interested in buying it because they will be curious if their pet likes it and if it is better than their current one. Therefore, because of this profit increases and so does the revenue[,] all customers are kept interested. But, this may also be bad because PPS would have to invest money in advertising, manufacturing and how it looks and that would bring revenue down.*
>
> *In my opinion, given the business situation of PPS the best choice is introducing a new dog food brand because it increased brand image greatly."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows what is expected from candidates in order to gain the full marks for this type of 'justify' question. This candidate has chosen to look at **both** options which is acceptable. **Two critical corrections to technique, straight from the examiner:**

1. **"Candidates can choose to weigh up the advantages and disadvantages of ONE option and then provide a conclusion, OR they could choose to weigh up BOTH options and then provide a conclusion."** And from the June 2022 P1R paper summary: ***"'Justify' questions do not need to consider both options."*** You do not have to cover both. But you **must** conclude.

2. The examiner says *"There are no knowledge marks."* On this 9/9, the candidate never defines anything — every sentence is application, chain or judgement.

## ⚠️ 1 mark vs 2 marks · Q3(c) Calculate closing balance

**Data:** Opening balance €16,000 · Cash inflow €22,000 · Cash outflow €20,000

**1 mark —**
```
cash inflow - outflow = 22000 - 20000 = 2000 → closing balance
16000 - 2000 = 14000
€14000
```
> *"Whilst this candidate has not got the final answer correct, due to their working out, and as per the mark scheme, they have gained access to 1 mark."*

**2 marks —**
```
16000 + 22000 = 38000
38000 - 20000 = 18000
€18000
```
> *"This candidate has good recall of the formula for the closing balance, has identified the correct answer therefore gains access to the full available marks."*

**Rule:** always show working. A wrong answer with correct method still banks a mark. (And note: closing balance = opening + inflow − outflow. The 1-mark candidate subtracted instead of adding.)

# GENERAL EXAMINER RULES — collected across the PEF series

These are stated repeatedly, across sessions and papers, and are worth more than any single exemplar.

### On context (the most repeated rule in the whole series)

> *"'State' and 'Outline' questions require context in order to gain the full available marks."* — June 2022 P1R paper summary

> *"All 'State' questions require candidates to include context to their response – encourage candidates to check if their response is generic and can be applied to any business or have they included phrases that link it directly to the given scenario?"* — June 2022 P1

> *"Just putting IKEA in the response does not mean it is in context as IKEA is in the question."* — June 2023 P2

> *"Candidates must be aware that there are no marks available for knowledge, and that the response must be in context."* — June 2022 P2R

**The rule:** naming the business is **not** context. Giving the business's **actual product, service, competitor, location or figure** is.

### On 'Explain' — context is NOT required

> *"No context is required for explain questions but time and time again we see candidates applying context unnecessarily."* — June 2022 P1R

> *"No context is required for explain questions but often we see candidates applying context unnecessarily."* — June 2024 P1

### On 'Justify' — you do not need both options

> *"'Justify' questions do not need to consider both options."* — June 2022 P1R and June 2023 P1R paper summaries

> *"Candidates do not have to consider both options in their response. Candidates can achieve the full marks only considering the one option."* — June 2022 P2R

### On 'Define' — examples do not earn the mark

> *"For 'Define' questions candidates are required to show they have a clear understanding of the term, where candidates have given examples of primary sector activities they have not gained the mark."* — June 2022 P1

> *"Candidates should try and avoid using the term in their response."* — Nov 2023 P2 and June 2024 P2

### On calculations

> *"Candidates should be encouraged to include their working out. There are occasions where candidates have the incorrect final response but the correct working out so have been able to gain a mark."* — June 2022 P1

> *"Check the question carefully to see if one or two decimal places are required in the answer."* — June 2022 P2R

### On time and wasted words

> *"Not to spend time repeating the questions – this is not necessary and simply wastes time."* — June 2022 P1R paper summary

### On the 12-mark (identical to the archive finding)

> *"There is good context throughout the response as well as a balanced argument with a clear evaluation."* — the standard closing comment on every 12/12

## ⭐ THE DEFINITION OF CONTEXT — stated outright by the examiner

On Q3(b) (outline how social media could promote the business), a candidate wrote *"Cafe Pinch could use social media to advertise its products by which large geographical area will be aware of the business."* — awarded **1/2**.

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This shows a typical response of a candidate who does not understand the requirement from the command word 'outline.' Whilst this candidate has correctly identified that the business could use social media to advertise which gains them access to 1 mark, they cannot gain access to the second mark due to lack of context.*
>
> ***The name of the business does not provide context.***
>
> *Candidates can choose to **name the products/services the business offers**, they could choose to **name competitors in the same industry** or they could **state the industry the business operates in** to provide a contextualised response. In this case, the candidate could have replaced the business name with the type of business e.g **cafe** to provide context or replaced the word **products** with **filled Ciabattas**, as stated in the opening of the paper."*

### 🎯 This is the most actionable paragraph in the entire bank.

**Four — and only four — routes to a context mark:**

| # | Route | Example for Café Pinch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Name the products/services** | "filled ciabattas", "teas", "breads" |
| 2 | **Name a competitor** | "other cafés in Berlin" |
| 3 | **State the industry** | "the café market" |
| 4 | **Name the type of business** | "café" |

**And the one thing that never works:** writing the business's name. *"The name of the business does not provide context."*

This single rule explains almost every lost AO2 mark documented in this bank — the June 2019 P1R "product" vs "food", the June 2019 P1 "the business" and "products", and the June 2024 P1 "product" vs "dog coats and dog leads".

## ⭐ THE 9-MARK RULE — stated outright by the examiner

> **ResultsPlus Examiner Tip (verbatim):**
> *"**Candidates do not need to consider both options.** Candidates need to make a clear choice out of the two options and be able to balance out their response as to why the business should or should not choose that option. Candidates are then expected to provide a clear overall judgement to evaluate their decision."*

**Note what *is* required: balance *within your chosen option*, plus a clear overall judgement.** "Balance" does not mean "cover both options" — it means weigh the chosen option's strengths against its weaknesses. This finally explains why the verified 9/9 in `02_Verified_Exemplars.md` turned *against its own choice* mid-answer.

## ⚠️ Define — 1 mark, and the recurring trap

**Question:** Define variable costs. **Response:** *"Cost that change when level of output change."* → **1 mark**

> *"'This candidate has provided a thorough definition of variable costs."*
>
> **Examiner Tip:** *"where candidates have given **examples** of variable costs that a business may incur they have not gained the mark."*

**The threshold is exact:** *"costs that change"* alone is **not** sufficient. *"Costs that change **when level of output changes**"* is. The definition must name the **driver**.

## ⚠️ Calculation — the decimal-place trap again

**Question:** Calculate, to two decimal places, the percentage decrease in retained profits (2021: €16,260 → 2022: €12,594).

The candidate recalled the formula and worked correctly **but answered to one decimal place** → **1/2**.

> *"the candidate cannot access the second mark as they have not fully read the question stating 'to two decimal places'… without this working out this candidate would not have achieved any marks but because of this working out they have gained a mark even though the final answer is to one decimal place."*

**Two rules in one:** (1) read the decimal requirement and obey it exactly; (2) show working — it converted a total failure into 1 mark. **This exact trap appears in June 2022 P2R, June 2024 P1R and June 2023 P1R. It is the most reliably repeatable mark loss on the paper.**

# GENERAL EXAMINER ADVICE — November 2024 Paper 1

**Source:** `4bs1-01-pef-20250123.pdf` — the paper-summary advice section, **verbatim**:

> *"Any question which refers to a specific business should give answers that are in the context of that business. This should be remembered when answering the **one mark 'state' questions** where answers need to be specifically applied to the business referred to in the exam paper."*

> *"**The biggest reason candidates underachieved on this paper was because candidates failed to answer the specific question asked.** It is imperative that candidates read the questions carefully and re-read them a second time. Students should identify the specific topic being examined and not provide generic responses that are not specifically addressing the question posed. This is specifically pertinent to question 4c where many students failed to show adequate knowledge of TQM."*

> *"This advice is given each series but unfortunately not always adhered to; many students are still failing to provide developed counterbalance in their answers. **For justify and evaluate questions students must always provide a balanced assessment. Failing to provide developed counterbalance means it is extremely unlikely the candidate can reach the top level in the mark scheme.**"*

**Three points worth carrying into every paper:**

1. **The single biggest cause of lost marks is not answering the question asked** — not lack of knowledge. Even on the toughest 12-marker, the problem was candidates *"failed to show adequate knowledge of TQM"*, i.e. they wrote about something adjacent.
2. **Developed counterbalance is non-negotiable at 9 and 12 marks.** Without it, *"extremely unlikely… to reach the top level."*
3. **Even 1-mark state questions require context.** There is no tariff where context is optional.
