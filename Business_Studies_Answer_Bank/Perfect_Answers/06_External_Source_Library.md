## Why this folder exists

The archive in `Paperlords_Archive/` holds **5** 4BS1 examiner reports. Pearson publishes **more**, and its **Principal Examiner Feedback (PEF)** reports are richer than the archive's copies. Each PEF report contains:

- **the actual question**
- **a real candidate's written response, reproduced verbatim**
- **the mark it was awarded**
- **an examiner comment explaining exactly why**

That is the `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` standard, and it is the highest-quality evidence available for this qualification. These are the same sources the archive's examiner-report PDFs come from — the PEF series simply covers far more sessions, and in some cases the *full* report rather than selected pages.

## ⚠️ CRITICAL: TWO DIFFERENT SPECIFICATIONS

Pearson publishes similar-looking materials for **two different qualifications**. They are **not interchangeable**:

| | **IGCSE Business 4BS1** ← *your qualification* | GCSE Business 1BS0 |
|---|---|---|
| Papers | Paper 1 (Investigating Small Businesses) + Paper 2 (Investigating Large Businesses) | Paper 1 + Paper 2 (different structure) |
| Paper total | **80 marks** | 90 marks |
| 12-mark AO split | **AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3** | AO1b = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3a = 3, AO3b = 3 |
| 6/9/12-mark tariffs | ✅ same tariffs | ✅ same tariffs |

**Where a source is GCSE 1BS0, it is labelled as such.** The *technique* transfers; the *exemplar scripts* do not. Never present a GCSE 1BS0 script as a 4BS1 exemplar.

# GENERAL EXAMINER RULES

Collected across the whole PEF series. **These are worth more than any individual exemplar** — they are stated repeatedly, across sessions, and they govern every question on the paper.

## 1. The context rule — the most repeated rule in the series

> *"'State' and 'Outline' questions require context in order to gain the full available marks."* — June 2022 P1R paper summary

> *"All 'State' questions require candidates to include context to their response – encourage candidates to check if their response is generic and can be applied to any business or have they included phrases that link it directly to the given scenario?"* — June 2022 P1

> *"**Just putting IKEA in the response does not mean it is in context as IKEA is in the question.**"* — June 2023 P2

> *"**The name of the business does not provide context.**"* — June 2024 P1R

### The four — and only four — routes to a context mark

Stated outright by the examiner on June 2024 P1R Q3(b):

> *"Candidates can choose to **name the products/services the business offers**, they could choose to **name competitors in the same industry** or they could **state the industry the business operates in** to provide a contextualised response."*

| # | Route | Example (Café Pinch) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name the products / services | "filled ciabattas", "teas", "breads" |
| 2 | Name a competitor | "other cafés in Berlin" |
| 3 | State the industry | "the café market" |
| 4 | Name the type of business | "café" |

> *"**Just one piece of context will suffice.**"* — June 2024 P1

### The evidence — the same lost mark, five years apart

| Paper | 0 / low mark | Full mark | The word that earned it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 2019 P1R | "product" | "food" | **food** |
| Jun 2024 P1 | "products" | "dog coats and dog leads" | **dog coats** |
| Jun 2024 P1R | "products" | "filled Ciabattas" | **ciabattas** |
| Jun 2023 P2 | *(generic)* | *(generic)* + "furniture" | **furniture** |
| Jun 2023 P2 | *(generic)* | "…a sick employee who looks after the sales of **tables and chairs**" | **tables and chairs** |

**Before handing in any paper: scan every answer for the words "product", "products", "the business", "customers" and replace each with the actual noun from the case.** Two minutes; worth a mark on almost every question.

## 2. "No marks for knowledge" — and which tariffs it applies to

> *"**There are no knowledge marks available for this question.**"* — Jun 2019 P1 Q3(e), a **9-mark justify** (in `03_Calibration_Below_Full_Marks.md`)

> *"**Candidates must be reminded that there are no marks available for knowledge**, and that the response must be in context."* — **Jun 2023 P2 Q1(g), a 6-mark analyse**

**This second one is a significant finding.** It was already known that justify (9) and evaluate (12) rank zero for AO1 on some tariffs — but the June 2023 P2 report states it of an **analyse** question, where the AO split is `AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3` and AO1 contributes **nothing**.

**Consequence:** on a 6-marker, a response containing three correct definitions of "multi-national", "limited liability" and "unincorporated business" — all accurate — scored **0/6**.

Jun 2022 P2R (Cadbury) states the same rule for its analyse question, making **three separate reports** confirming it:

> *"Candidates must be aware that there are no marks available for knowledge, and that the response must be in context of Cadbury."* — Jun 2022 P2R Q1(g)

### Where knowledge is worth marks — and where it is worth nothing

| Tariff | AO split | Knowledge (AO1) marks |
|---|---|---|
| **3 — explain** | AO1 = 3 | **All 3** — knowledge *is* the question; context not required |
| **6 — analyse** | AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3 | **Zero** |
| **9 — justify** | AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 | **Zero** |
| **12 — evaluate** | AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 | 3 — the only level-based tariff that pays for it |

**So: the 3-mark explain is the ONLY place where pure knowledge earns marks.** Everywhere else, knowledge must be applied, chained or evaluated, or it scores nothing.

> *"An 'Analyse' question is looking for application and analysis, whereas a 'Justify' question is looking for application, analysis and evaluation."* — Jun 2023 P2 introduction

## 3. 'Justify' — you do NOT need both options

> *"**'Justify' questions do not need to consider both options.**"* — June 2022 P1R **and** June 2023 P1R paper summaries

> *"Candidates do not have to consider both options in their response. Candidates can achieve the full marks only considering the one option."* — June 2022 P2R

> *"Candidates need to make a clear choice out of the two options and be able to **balance out their response as to why the business should or should not choose that option**. Candidates are then expected to provide a clear overall judgement to evaluate their decision."* — June 2023 P1R

**"Balance" means weighing your chosen option — not covering both.** This is why the verified 9/9 argues *against its own choice* mid-answer.

## 4. 'Explain' — context may or may not be required; check the AO

> *"No context is required for explain questions but time and time again we see candidates applying context unnecessarily."* — June 2022 P1R

> *"An explain question which **does not have to be in context** of IKEA. The Assessment Objective being tested is **AO1 (knowledge)**."* — June 2023 P2

**But not always.** Some mark schemes allocate explain to **AO2**, where context *is* required. **Check the AO in the mark scheme:** `AO1` → no context needed; `AO2` → context required.

## 5. 'Define' — three ways to lose a mark you should have had

> *"For 'Define' questions candidates are required to show they have a clear understanding of the term, where candidates have **given examples** of primary sector activities they have not gained the mark."* — June 2022 P1

> *"Candidates should try and **avoid using the term in their response**."* — Nov 2023 P2, June 2024 P2, June 2023 P2

**And the threshold is exact.** On *variable costs*: *"costs that change"* alone scored **0**; *"Cost that change **when level of output change**"* scored **1**.

| Failure | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Giving examples instead of a definition | listing primary sector businesses | **0** |
| Using the word being defined | "quality control is controlling quality" | **0** |
| Naming the driver imprecisely | "costs that change" | **0** |
| Defining a related but different term | *person specification* → defined *shortlisting* | **0** |

## 6. Calculations — two rules, no exceptions

> *"Candidates should be encouraged to include their working out. There are occasions where candidates have the incorrect final response but the correct working out so have been able to gain a mark."* — June 2022 P1, and repeated in every report

> *"Check the question carefully to see if one or two decimal places are required in the answer."* — June 2022 P2R

**The two-decimal-place trap recurs in every session examined:**

| Paper | What went wrong |
|---|---|
| Jun 2022 P2R | working correct, answer missing the second decimal place |
| Jun 2023 P1R | percentage decrease answered to 1 d.p. |
| Jun 2023 P2 | gross profit margin given as **31.4%** instead of **31.38%** |
| Jun 2024 P1R | correct operating profit figure, but divided by gross profit |

**This is the most reliably repeatable mark loss on the paper.** Write to the stated decimal places, and always show working — it converts a wrong answer from 0 marks into 1.

## 6b. When to STOP on a 3-marker

> *"This particular candidate has explained this benefit with **more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time when there are only 3 marks available.**"* — Jun 2022 P1 Q2(c), on a response that scored **3/3**

Every other report tells candidates to write more. This one tells a full-mark candidate to write **less**. Both rules coexist:

| Tariff | Structure | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|
| **3 marks** | identify (1) + **exactly two** developments (2) | then **stop** — further links earn nothing |
| **6 / 9 / 12 marks** | keep chaining | depth is what earns the levels |

## 6c. The third mark on a 3-marker is the business consequence

From Jun 2022 P1 Q2(d) — the most precise mark-by-mark breakdown in the bank:

> *"This candidate correctly identifies **find a new supplier who will sell materials cheaper**… then successfully explained this method by stating **'…means reduced cost of production…' gaining a second mark** and gaining the final mark for **'…more gain from profit, as revenue increases.'** **If the candidate omitted the final three words then this response would've gained just two marks.**"*

**Deleting three words would have cost a mark.** The structure is therefore:

```
IDENTIFY the point  (1)
   → explain HOW it helps  (1)
      → what it does to the business's MONEY / OUTCOME  (1)
```

Confirmed independently by Jun 2023 P2 Q1(f): the 2/3 response stopped at *"helps the business save a bit of money"*; the 3/3 carried on to *"higher value and more profit"*.

## 6d. The 5/6 → 6/6 gap: LINK your factors, don't add more

From Jun 2022 P2R Q1(g) — the most precise statement of what separates the top two bands of an analyse question:

> *"This response shows a clear understanding… **However, the response did not gain full marks because it lacked the logical chains of reasoning. Perhaps the candidate could have linked the two factors together**, by moving near to supplies might be difficult to get employees to work at the factory, or locating near to a city provides workers who have the skills."*

The 5/6 response had **two valid factors, correctly applied** (it used "cocoa seeds" — a perfect Cadbury anchor). It still lost a mark. The examiner's diagnosis is not "add a third point" — it is **link the two you already have**.

**Why:** the Level 3 AO3 descriptor says *"detailed **interconnected** points"*. Two points in sequence are not interconnected. A 6/6 does not have **more** points; it has points that **bear on each other**.

**The move to practise:**

```
INSTEAD OF:  Factor A → consequence.   Factor B → consequence.
DO:          Factor A → consequence → which in turn affects Factor B → consequence
```

## 7. Time, relevance and balance

> *"Not to spend time repeating the questions – this is not necessary and simply wastes time."* — June 2022 P1R paper summary

> *"Candidates should try and avoid using the term in their response, this will help them ensure that they have answered the question asked."* — June 2023 P2

> *"**The biggest reason candidates underachieved on this paper was because candidates failed to answer the specific question asked.** It is imperative that candidates read the questions carefully and re-read them a second time… Students should identify the specific topic being examined and not provide generic responses."* — Nov 2024 P1

> *"**For justify and evaluate questions students must always provide a balanced assessment. Failing to provide developed counterbalance means it is extremely unlikely the candidate can reach the top level in the mark scheme.**"* — Nov 2024 P1

**Note:** the biggest cause of lost marks across the whole series is **not** lack of knowledge — it is answering an adjacent question, or failing to counterbalance.

# SOURCE CATALOGUE

## 8. The LISTING CAP — one mark, however many points you list

Now confirmed **at mark-scheme level**, which removes any doubt. The official mark scheme for the June 2019 Paper 2 exemplar booklet says, in terms, for two different questions:

> *"**Answers that list three impacts with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark.**"* (Q1(f))
> *"**Answers that list three reasons with no explanation will get a maximum of 1 mark.**"* (Q2(d))

**This is not a guideline. It is the mark scheme.** Three undeveloped points score exactly the same as one undeveloped point.

Third independent confirmation, from the Nov 2021 P1 ER — *"Candidates who list more than one way of measuring business success can only gain one mark."*

**The rule:** on an Explain question, **one point developed twice beats three points listed**. Every time.

---

## 9. No marks for a definition on an 'Explain' question

Official mark scheme, June 2019 P2 Q2(d), verbatim:

> *"**NB No marks are awarded for a definition.**"*

A surprising number of candidates open an Explain answer with *"Job production is when…"* or *"Kaizen is the continuous improvement of…"*. It earns **zero**, and it consumes the time you needed.

**Now confirmed twice more in the same booklet from the candidate side.** Kaizen response B opened *"Kaizen is the continuous improvement of goods"* — a **correct** definition — and the examiner recorded it as a defect: *"Unfortunately there were no knowledge marks available for this question."*

> **Rule: on an Explain question, start with the reason. Never with the definition.**

## 10. Correct, relevant knowledge can still earn NOTHING

The sharpest example in the whole bank, from June 2019 P2 Q3(e) (9-mark Justify):

> *"Candidates should be aware that there are no knowledge marks allocated for this type of question and therefore **comments about how the research is collected, or that secondary research could be outdated, cannot be awarded marks**."*

**"Secondary research can be out of date" is true, standard, textbook, and worth zero marks here.** Same for describing how a method works.

**The test is not whether the point is correct. It is whether the point attaches to *this* business's situation.**

| Point | Marks? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "secondary research could be outdated" | ✗ | true of every business — no application |
| "secondary research is collected from other manufacturers' reports, so it may not reflect what Tata's Indian customers want" | ✓ | applied to Tata |

## 11. LENGTH IS NOT MARKS — the 400-word finding

Two candidates answered the same 12-mark evaluate (June 2019 P2 Q4(c)):

| | Response A | Response B |
|---|---|---|
| Length | **~400 words** | **~110 words** |
| Factors raised | 17+ | 4 |
| **Mark** | **8/12** | **7/12** |

**Four times the length bought one mark**, because response B wrote an actual judgement and response A wrote none.

Examiner, on the 400-word answer: *"The response demonstrates numerous knowledge and application points but often these are not developed or evaluated sufficiently to achieve a higher level… **However, the response does not evaluate the comments sufficiently to move it into Level 3.**"* Level 3 begins at 9/12. **300 of those 400 words replaced evaluation the candidate never attempted.**

This is the quantified form of Pearson's own tip: *"Too often, candidates write far too much for AO3 and leave insufficient space for a sound judgement."*

## 12. A conclusion must DECIDE and RANK — introducing new factors is a hedge, not a judgement

The 7/12 response wrote a conclusion containing *"But the success of them depends on…"*. The structure was right. **It still scored 7/12**, because:

> *"In conclusion, the right choice of can factories location could help Tata Motor Company to reduce it costs, save the time on to find new partners. But the success of them depends on **how many available land** for the big factories there are and **how the locals would react** on it."*

Both conditions are factors that appear **nowhere else in the answer**. The examiner did not mention them at all.

**A conclusion that hands the judgement to factors you have not analysed reads as evasion.** Three requirements:

1. **Decide** — *should*, *most important*, *the key factor*
2. **Rank** — say which factor outweighs which, and why
3. **Look backwards** — rest on factors already analysed, never new ones

**Compare the official AO4 content**, which is a ranking of the discussed material: *"Infrastructure is **one of many factors** affecting the choice of location and other factors **may be more important**."*

## 13. Apply to the SUBJECT the question names, not the obvious business

June 2019 P2R Q1(g) asked *"Analyse why **Yellow** might encourage **its clothing manufacturers** to use Kaizen."*

> *"Many of the responses that were seen for this question answered from the perspective of the business that manufactures the clothes and then gave the many benefits of Kaizen. **The question asked why Yellow might encourage its clothing manufacturers to use Kaizen, so therefore the response should be in the context of Yellow and not the manufacturer.**"*

**A whole cohort applied to the wrong business.** The consequences must land on whoever the question makes the subject: Yellow's reputation, Yellow's sales, Yellow's returns.

|---|---|---|

## 15. AO4 is written in the mark scheme as a CONCESSION

Look at every AO4 item in the official June 2019 P2 mark schemes:

> *"**Although** this type of pricing method guarantees a profit on the cost of manufacturer it does not guarantee that sales are going to be made"* (Q2(f))
> *"**Even though** the sales may be higher, the profit per vehicle will be lower."* (Q2(f))
> *"**although** gaining this information is important for Tata Motor Company it is very costly in time and man power."* (Q3(e))
> *"**By** locating near a skilled labour force, the cost of land/infrastructure **may be higher**"* (Q4(c))

**Every single one is "yes, but…".** Pearson writes evaluation as a concession followed by the downside. If your conclusion approves your own choice unconditionally, you are not writing AO4.

## 16. 5/6 on an Analyse is one link short — finish at the business outcome

Every 5/6 in the June 2019 exemplar booklet was 5/6 for the **same** reason: the chain stopped one step before a business outcome.

| Script | Examiner's missing link |
|---|---|
| Q1(g) P2 response A, 5/6 | *"could have **developed** the [second] comment further to move the response into the top of Level 3"* |
| Q1(g) P2R response A, 5/6 | *"could have included something with regard to how a benefit… might be **improved customer satisfaction, which could increase sales**"* |
| Q2R response B, 4/6 | *"could have developed the comments made about customers **returning to Yellow to purchase more products** and telling their friends and family"* |

**The missing final link is almost always sales, profit or reputation.** Finish the sentence there.

## 17. A fluent, chained answer with NO case-study fact is bottom-of-Level-2

June 2019 P2 Q1(g) response B, verbatim:

> *"Good public relations is extremely important for Tata Motor Company as it could increase their amount of customers. This is because customers will feel more comfortable and attracted towards Tata Motor Company, which will increase their chance of shopping there. This will create more revenue for Tata as more customers will create more sales."*

Well written. Three linked steps. Names the business twice. **Scored 3/6 — bottom of Level 2.**

**Not one case-study fact.** The examiner states the fix outright: *"If candidates had referred back to the scenario at the front of the paper, it may have helped them… The details about working in the community, and building schools and houses, are all to do with the public."*

> **Self-check for any 6-mark Analyse: count the case-study facts you have used. If the answer is zero, you are at the bottom of Level 2 no matter how well you write.**

## 18. ⭐⭐ THE OFFICIAL LEVEL DESCRIPTORS — the rubric itself

**Nothing else in this bank has these.** Every other rule describes what successful answers *did*; these are the words the examiner is **looking for**. Full text: `External_Exemplars/2019-06_P1_Official_Exemplar_Booklet.md`.

**6-mark Analyse** — Level 1 = **1–2**, Level 2 = **3–4**, **Level 3 = 5–6**.
**9-mark Justify** — Level 1 = **1–3**, Level 2 = **4–6**, **Level 3 = 7–9**.
**12-mark Evaluate** — Level 1 = **1–4**, Level 2 = **5–8**, **Level 3 = 9–12**.

### The whole strategy in one table — one escalating adjective per AO

| | Level 1 | Level 2 | **Level 3 — target this** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AO2 — context** | *Limited* application | *Sound* application | **Detailed** application **throughout** |
| **AO3 — chains** | *Limited* connections | *Interconnected* points | **Detailed interconnected** points with **logical** chains |
| **AO4 — judgement** | *Simple* justification, *limited* evaluation | *Sound* evaluation | **Clear justification** based on a **thorough** evaluation |

### Three findings that fall out of the rubric itself

**1. AO2 Level 3 requires "throughout".** Level 2 is *"sound application… although there may be some inconsistencies"*; Level 3 is *"detailed application… **throughout**"*. **The single word "throughout" separates the 3–4 band from the 5–6 band.** One good contextual point in the opening line is a Level 2 answer.

**2. AO3 Level 2 explicitly forgives *"logical inconsistencies"*. Level 3 does not.** A chain that does not actually follow is a Level 2 chain, however long it is.

**3. AO4's Level 3 word is "thorough" — the highest bar in the qualification**, and it sits on the objective worth only 11–12%. This is the mechanical explanation of the **7/9 → 9/9 gap**: a 7/9 is a Level 3 answer whose AO4 is not yet "thorough".

## 19. "For example…" is NOT development

June 2019 P1 Q2(d): a candidate named **one** method (social media — so no listing penalty) and then wrote *"For example: using platforms such as facebook and instagram"*. **1/3.**

> *"The candidate has then gone on to give **examples** of social media rather than **develop** on the given method."*

**An example adds an instance. An explanation adds a consequence.**

> **After naming your point, the next word should be "This means…" or "This allows…" — never "For example…".**

## 20. A judgement is NOT a verdict

A 12-mark answer ended: *"Therefore primary research would be quite important to Backyard Shoez."* The examiner called this *"a very basic judgement"*. **4/12 — top of Level 1.**

| | Verdict | Justification | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|
| *"Therefore primary research would be quite important."* | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ |
| A Level 3 judgement | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

The rubric asks for *"a clear justification based on a **thorough** evaluation"* — **a verdict, plus the reasoning that produced it, plus the case against.**

Related: a 9-mark answer that opened *"Bonus payments may be better…"* was still told *"There is no attempt to make any judgement or evaluation."* **Deciding in one line is not judging.**

## 21. The TWO-WORD CONTEXT FIX — the examiner states it outright

Two examiners reduce the context rule to a single lexical substitution:

> *"Had the candidate replaced terms such as **'product'** with **'food'**, it would have helped them move further into level 2 **with the potential of a level 3 response**."* — June 2019 P1R Q1(g)
>
> *"there are loose references to **suppliers**. If these references were in the context of Backyard Shoez, **such as shoe/accessory suppliers**, it would have helped the candidate move out of level 1."* — June 2019 P1 Q4(b)

**"Suppliers" → "shoe suppliers". "Product" → "food".** The examiner says the second is a potential **two-level** jump. The context mark is not earned by a naming a *category*; it is earned by attaching the **case study's product** to it.

> **Self-check:** underline every generic noun in your answer — *product, customers, service, staff, competitors, suppliers, business*. For each, ask whether the case study supplies a more specific word. Wherever it does, use it.

**And the examiners name the phrases that earned the context marks in the two 6/6s and the 10/12:**

| Script | The phrases the examiner credited |
|---|---|
| P1 Q1(g) 6/6 | *"eg shoes"*, *"lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre"* |
| P1R Q1(g) 6/6 | *"small cakes"*, *"fine dining"* |
| P1 Q4(c) 10/12 | *"designs of shoes and clothes"*, *"rivals in shopping centre"* |

**Two reusable templates:** **designs of [the case study's product]** · **rivals in [the case study's location/market]**.

---

## 22. A logical 9-mark answer with no application is worth 3/9

June 2019 P1 Q3(e) response B contains a genuine four-link trade-off between crowdfunding and an overdraft — *time-consuming → may take too long → overdraft is immediate → but creates debt repaid monthly → reduces profit*. **It scored 3/9 — the top of Level 1**, because there is no Backyard Shoez in it anywhere.

> **AO2 is 38–39% of the qualification. A well-reasoned answer with no application hands most of it back.**

---

## 23. Sometimes TWO factors ARE expected

A nuance to rule 6d. This bank holds two verified **6/6** scripts that take **one** factor and run it in both directions. But an examiner also told a candidate they *"needed to grasp **at least two main benefits**"* (June 2019 P1 Q3(d)).

**The reconciliation: depth always works; undeveloped breadth never does.**

| Question shape | What full marks needs |
|---|---|
| *"Analyse the importance/impact of X to Business Y"* | **one** factor developed in both directions can reach 5–6 |
| *"Analyse the benefits of using [a tool] to do [a task]"* | **at least two** distinct benefits, each developed |

> **Depth over breadth — but if you stall, add a second factor and develop it fully. Never add an undeveloped one.**

---

## 24. You can write TOO MUCH on a 6-marker

> *"The final points made in this response are not necessary as the points made prior to the link are well explained and demonstrate logical chains of reasoning."* — June 2019 P1R Q1(g) response A, **6/6**

**Once the chains are complete, extra material adds no marks and costs time.** This is the same principle as the listing cap, arriving from the other direction — and it matters most on a 12-marker, where the time you overspend on AO3 is exactly the time you needed for AO4.

## 25. "JUST USING THE SCENARIO WILL NOT GAIN A MARK" — the sharpest form of the name rule

Nov 2023 P2 Q1(d):

> *"candidates gave a valid reason… but did not put it in context of Kraft Heinz and therefore did not gain the mark. **Also, just using the scenario in the response will not gain a mark as Kraft Heinz is in the question.**"*

**A candidate who writes *"Kraft Heinz should…"* has put the scenario in the response and still scores zero**, because *"Kraft Heinz is in the question"*.

> **The test: is your contextual word already printed on the question paper?** If yes, it is not application. Pick something from the case study that the question itself did not mention.

**Eighth confirmation.** Three State questions on the Nov 2023 P2 paper were failed by **~90%** of candidates for this one reason.

---

## 26. Accurate knowledge earns NOTHING where there is no knowledge mark

Nov 2023 P2 Q3(b), a **2-mark Outline** (AO2 — Application):

> *"A number of candidates described what robotics was, **this is knowledge and therefore did not gain them any marks, even though the description was accurate.**"*

The examiner concedes the description was correct and awards it zero. **Eight independent confirmations** now, spanning 1, 2, 3, 6 and 9-mark questions.

---

## 27. Definitions need PURPOSE, and need the WHOLE concept

Two failures from Nov 2023 P2:

> Q1(c) define **trade bloc**: *"a group of countries, **which is correct**, but does not include **why they join together**."* → less than a quarter of the cohort got the mark.
>
> Q3(a) define **public limited company**: *"candidates must be clear and give a **full** definition of the term… Most mentioned that **public bought shares**."* → nearly three quarters failed.

> **The complete definition formula: [what it is] + [what it does / why it exists].** One feature is not a definition.

---

## 28. The RELEVANCE rule — true is not the same as relevant

Nov 2023 P2 Q2(d): *"A number of candidates referred to **shareholders would not be happy** which is **not relevant to using retained profit**."*

"Shareholders would not be happy" belongs to **share capital**. It is a true business statement attached to the wrong topic.

> **A point earns nothing if it belongs to a different concept than the one asked about — however true it is.**

---

## 29. The DIRECTION-OF-BENEFIT drill

Nov 2023 P2 named the beneficiary misread twice in one paper, in opposite directions:

| Question | What candidates did | What was asked |
|---|---|---|
| Q3(d) reducing waste | *"referred to how **customers** could reduce their waste"* | benefits to **Kraft Heinz** |
| Q4(b) | *"gave benefits to **Kraft Heinz** of being located in a particular country"* | benefits to **the country** |

> **Before writing any "benefits of" answer, write the beneficiary in the margin.** On this paper that single habit was worth a whole Level on two questions.

## 30. Calculations — the answer line, and the asymmetric decimal rule

Nov 2023 P2 Q1(e), complete:

> *"if the question asks for two decimal places and their response has **three** decimal places they will **not gain all the marks** available"* — and *"marks will **not be given** for a response that only gives **one** decimal place."*

> *"**there is a line at the bottom of the box for their response.** This makes it clearer and easier for the examiner… some candidates had their answer within their workings, **which was not always easily identified**."*

**Seventh d.p. confirmation, and the first stating the one-decimal failure outright.**

> **Two decimal places means two — not three, not one.** And write the answer **on the line**, not inside the working.

**Also (Nov 2023 P2, administrative):** MCQ — draw an **X** in the box, not a tick. Write **inside** the lines. If you continue elsewhere, write **"continued" / "contd page ___" / "see separate sheet"** — *"Examiners are unable to guess."*

---

## 31. The 12-marker is 80% Level 2 — and the reason is always the same two things

Nov 2023 P2 Q4(c), the cohort distribution:

> *"A variety of valid comments were made by candidates — different methods attract different types of people. **However, they then failed to develop these points and justify their decisions** to enable them to gain the higher level marks. **Nearly four fifths of the cohort gained a Level 2 mark and just over a tenth of the cohort gaining a Level 3 mark.**"*

**Not a knowledge failure.** An entire cohort had the points and could not convert them — because it did not **develop** them and did not **justify** a decision. Plus: *"A number of candidates only completed a few lines."*

> **Time budget: 1 minute per mark.** The 12-marker is 15% of the paper and needs **~12–14 minutes**. A conclusion written in the last 90 seconds is a Level 2 conclusion.

---

## 32. Answer the SPECIFIC question, not the general topic

Nov 2023 P2 Q1(g) — *analyse the benefits of using social media **to collect market research data***:

> *"a large number of candidates gave very detailed response of the benefits of using social media **but not in the context of collecting market research data**… **Some candidates repeated the information from the stem which is also not worthy of a mark.** Just 15% of the cohort gained a Level 3 mark."*

> **The precision drill:** underline (a) the **business**, (b) the **action**, (c) the **purpose**. Every chain must serve all three. If a sentence would still be true for a business not doing that specific thing, it is off-question.
>
> **And the case study is evidence to build on, not to copy.** Repeating the stem earns nothing.

## Checklist — the seven ways to lose marks that recur in EVERY report

1. Naming the business instead of using a case-study fact (four routes to context)
2. Listing points instead of chaining them (the listing cap)
3. Defining the term on an Explain question (no marks for definitions)
4. Writing correct generic knowledge on an Analyse/Justify question (no AO1 marks)
5. Chains that never reach a business outcome
6. A conclusion that hedges or introduces new factors instead of deciding and ranking
7. On a data question, reasoning about the figure instead of naming a number from it
