## Why this folder exists

The archive in `Paperlords_Archive/` holds **5** 4BS1 examiner reports. Pearson publishes **more**, and its **Principal Examiner Feedback (PEF)** reports are richer than the archive's copies. Each PEF report contains:

- **the actual question**
- **a real candidate's written response, reproduced verbatim**
- **the mark it was awarded**
- **an examiner comment explaining exactly why**

That is the `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` standard, and it is the highest-quality evidence available for this qualification. These are the same sources the archive's examiner-report PDFs come from — the PEF series simply covers far more sessions, and in some cases the *full* report rather than selected pages.

---

## ⚠️ CRITICAL: TWO DIFFERENT SPECIFICATIONS

Pearson publishes similar-looking materials for **two different qualifications**. They are **not interchangeable**:

| | **IGCSE Business 4BS1** ← *your qualification* | GCSE Business 1BS0 |
|---|---|---|
| Papers | Paper 1 (Investigating Small Businesses) + Paper 2 (Investigating Large Businesses) | Paper 1 + Paper 2 (different structure) |
| Paper total | **80 marks** | 90 marks |
| 12-mark AO split | **AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3** | AO1b = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3a = 3, AO3b = 3 |
| 6/9/12-mark tariffs | ✅ same tariffs | ✅ same tariffs |

**Where a source is GCSE 1BS0, it is labelled as such.** The *technique* transfers; the *exemplar scripts* do not. Never present a GCSE 1BS0 script as a 4BS1 exemplar.

## ⚠️ Operational note — a real limitation of this harvest

The fetch route parses **PDFs only up to 30 pages**. Some Pearson PEF reports are longer, and the **12-mark evaluate sits at the end of the paper**, so on those reports the 12/12 exemplar falls beyond the limit and cannot be retrieved this way.

| Report | 12-mark retrievable? |
|---|---|
| Jun 2024 P1, P1R, P2 | ✅ yes |
| Jun 2023 P2 | ✅ yes (held from the archive excerpt) |
| Jun 2023 P1R | ✅ yes |
| **Jun 2022 P1** | ❌ **no** — examiner's summary confirms a 12/12 exists (*"balanced argument with a clear evaluation. 12 marks."*) but the script is past page 30 |

**Workarounds, in order of reliability:** (1) a shorter mirror of the same report; (2) the archive's own excerpt files, where held; (3) accepting the examiner's summary line without the script. Not yet applied — recorded here so it is not mistaken for a complete record.

# GENERAL EXAMINER RULES

Collected across the whole PEF series. **These are worth more than any individual exemplar** — they are stated repeatedly, across sessions, and they govern every question on the paper.

## 1. The context rule — the most repeated rule in the series

> *"'State' and 'Outline' questions require context in order to gain the full available marks."* — June 2022 P1R paper summary

> *"All 'State' questions require candidates to include context to their response – encourage candidates to check if their response is generic and can be applied to any business or have they included phrases that link it directly to the given scenario?"* — June 2022 P1

> *"**Just putting IKEA in the response does not mean it is in context as IKEA is in the question.**"* — June 2023 P2

> *"**The name of the business does not provide context.**"* — June 2024 P1R

### The four — and only four — routes to a context mark

Stated outright by the examiner on June 2024 P1R Q3(b):

> *"Candidates can choose to **name the products/services the business offers**, they could choose to **name competitors in the same industry** or they could **state the industry the business operates in** to provide a contextualised response."*

| # | Route | Example (Café Pinch) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Name the products / services | "filled ciabattas", "teas", "breads" |
| 2 | Name a competitor | "other cafés in Berlin" |
| 3 | State the industry | "the café market" |
| 4 | Name the type of business | "café" |

> *"**Just one piece of context will suffice.**"* — June 2024 P1

### The evidence — the same lost mark, five years apart

| Paper | 0 / low mark | Full mark | The word that earned it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 2019 P1R | "product" | "food" | **food** |
| Jun 2024 P1 | "products" | "dog coats and dog leads" | **dog coats** |
| Jun 2024 P1R | "products" | "filled Ciabattas" | **ciabattas** |
| Jun 2023 P2 | *(generic)* | *(generic)* + "furniture" | **furniture** |
| Jun 2023 P2 | *(generic)* | "…a sick employee who looks after the sales of **tables and chairs**" | **tables and chairs** |

**Before handing in any paper: scan every answer for the words "product", "products", "the business", "customers" and replace each with the actual noun from the case.** Two minutes; worth a mark on almost every question.

## 2. "No marks for knowledge" — and which tariffs it applies to

> *"**There are no knowledge marks available for this question.**"* — Jun 2019 P1 Q3(e), a **9-mark justify** (in `03_Calibration_Below_Full_Marks.md`)

> *"**Candidates must be reminded that there are no marks available for knowledge**, and that the response must be in context."* — **Jun 2023 P2 Q1(g), a 6-mark analyse**

**This second one is a significant finding.** It was already known that justify (9) and evaluate (12) rank zero for AO1 on some tariffs — but the June 2023 P2 report states it of an **analyse** question, where the AO split is `AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3` and AO1 contributes **nothing**.

**Consequence:** on a 6-marker, a response containing three correct definitions of "multi-national", "limited liability" and "unincorporated business" — all accurate — scored **0/6**.

Jun 2022 P2R (Cadbury) states the same rule for its analyse question, making **three separate reports** confirming it:

> *"Candidates must be aware that there are no marks available for knowledge, and that the response must be in context of Cadbury."* — Jun 2022 P2R Q1(g)

### Where knowledge is worth marks — and where it is worth nothing

| Tariff | AO split | Knowledge (AO1) marks |
|---|---|---|
| **3 — explain** | AO1 = 3 | **All 3** — knowledge *is* the question; context not required |
| **6 — analyse** | AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3 | **Zero** |
| **9 — justify** | AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 | **Zero** |
| **12 — evaluate** | AO1 = 3, AO2 = 3, AO3 = 3, AO4 = 3 | 3 — the only level-based tariff that pays for it |

**So: the 3-mark explain is the ONLY place where pure knowledge earns marks.** Everywhere else, knowledge must be applied, chained or evaluated, or it scores nothing.

> *"An 'Analyse' question is looking for application and analysis, whereas a 'Justify' question is looking for application, analysis and evaluation."* — Jun 2023 P2 introduction

## 3. 'Justify' — you do NOT need both options

> *"**'Justify' questions do not need to consider both options.**"* — June 2022 P1R **and** June 2023 P1R paper summaries

> *"Candidates do not have to consider both options in their response. Candidates can achieve the full marks only considering the one option."* — June 2022 P2R

> *"Candidates need to make a clear choice out of the two options and be able to **balance out their response as to why the business should or should not choose that option**. Candidates are then expected to provide a clear overall judgement to evaluate their decision."* — June 2023 P1R

**"Balance" means weighing your chosen option — not covering both.** This is why the verified 9/9 argues *against its own choice* mid-answer.

## 4. 'Explain' — context may or may not be required; check the AO

> *"No context is required for explain questions but time and time again we see candidates applying context unnecessarily."* — June 2022 P1R

> *"An explain question which **does not have to be in context** of IKEA. The Assessment Objective being tested is **AO1 (knowledge)**."* — June 2023 P2

**But not always.** Some mark schemes allocate explain to **AO2**, where context *is* required. **Check the AO in the mark scheme:** `AO1` → no context needed; `AO2` → context required.

## 5. 'Define' — three ways to lose a mark you should have had

> *"For 'Define' questions candidates are required to show they have a clear understanding of the term, where candidates have **given examples** of primary sector activities they have not gained the mark."* — June 2022 P1

> *"Candidates should try and **avoid using the term in their response**."* — Nov 2023 P2, June 2024 P2, June 2023 P2

**And the threshold is exact.** On *variable costs*: *"costs that change"* alone scored **0**; *"Cost that change **when level of output change**"* scored **1**.

| Failure | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Giving examples instead of a definition | listing primary sector businesses | **0** |
| Using the word being defined | "quality control is controlling quality" | **0** |
| Naming the driver imprecisely | "costs that change" | **0** |
| Defining a related but different term | *person specification* → defined *shortlisting* | **0** |

## 6. Calculations — two rules, no exceptions

> *"Candidates should be encouraged to include their working out. There are occasions where candidates have the incorrect final response but the correct working out so have been able to gain a mark."* — June 2022 P1, and repeated in every report

> *"Check the question carefully to see if one or two decimal places are required in the answer."* — June 2022 P2R

**The two-decimal-place trap recurs in every session examined:**

| Paper | What went wrong |
|---|---|
| Jun 2022 P2R | working correct, answer missing the second decimal place |
| Jun 2023 P1R | percentage decrease answered to 1 d.p. |
| Jun 2023 P2 | gross profit margin given as **31.4%** instead of **31.38%** |
| Jun 2024 P1R | correct operating profit figure, but divided by gross profit |

**This is the most reliably repeatable mark loss on the paper.** Write to the stated decimal places, and always show working — it converts a wrong answer from 0 marks into 1.

## 6b. When to STOP on a 3-marker

> *"This particular candidate has explained this benefit with **more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time when there are only 3 marks available.**"* — Jun 2022 P1 Q2(c), on a response that scored **3/3**

Every other report tells candidates to write more. This one tells a full-mark candidate to write **less**. Both rules coexist:

| Tariff | Structure | Stop rule |
|---|---|---|
| **3 marks** | identify (1) + **exactly two** developments (2) | then **stop** — further links earn nothing |
| **6 / 9 / 12 marks** | keep chaining | depth is what earns the levels |

## 6c. The third mark on a 3-marker is the business consequence

From Jun 2022 P1 Q2(d) — the most precise mark-by-mark breakdown in the bank:

> *"This candidate correctly identifies **find a new supplier who will sell materials cheaper**… then successfully explained this method by stating **'…means reduced cost of production…' gaining a second mark** and gaining the final mark for **'…more gain from profit, as revenue increases.'** **If the candidate omitted the final three words then this response would've gained just two marks.**"*

**Deleting three words would have cost a mark.** The structure is therefore:

```
IDENTIFY the point  (1)
   → explain HOW it helps  (1)
      → what it does to the business's MONEY / OUTCOME  (1)
```

Confirmed independently by Jun 2023 P2 Q1(f): the 2/3 response stopped at *"helps the business save a bit of money"*; the 3/3 carried on to *"higher value and more profit"*.

## 6d. The 5/6 → 6/6 gap: LINK your factors, don't add more

From Jun 2022 P2R Q1(g) — the most precise statement of what separates the top two bands of an analyse question:

> *"This response shows a clear understanding… **However, the response did not gain full marks because it lacked the logical chains of reasoning. Perhaps the candidate could have linked the two factors together**, by moving near to supplies might be difficult to get employees to work at the factory, or locating near to a city provides workers who have the skills."*

The 5/6 response had **two valid factors, correctly applied** (it used "cocoa seeds" — a perfect Cadbury anchor). It still lost a mark. The examiner's diagnosis is not "add a third point" — it is **link the two you already have**.

**Why:** the Level 3 AO3 descriptor says *"detailed **interconnected** points"*. Two points in sequence are not interconnected. A 6/6 does not have **more** points; it has points that **bear on each other**.

**The move to practise:**

```
INSTEAD OF:  Factor A → consequence.   Factor B → consequence.
DO:          Factor A → consequence → which in turn affects Factor B → consequence
```

## 7. Time, relevance and balance

> *"Not to spend time repeating the questions – this is not necessary and simply wastes time."* — June 2022 P1R paper summary

> *"Candidates should try and avoid using the term in their response, this will help them ensure that they have answered the question asked."* — June 2023 P2

> *"**The biggest reason candidates underachieved on this paper was because candidates failed to answer the specific question asked.** It is imperative that candidates read the questions carefully and re-read them a second time… Students should identify the specific topic being examined and not provide generic responses."* — Nov 2024 P1

> *"**For justify and evaluate questions students must always provide a balanced assessment. Failing to provide developed counterbalance means it is extremely unlikely the candidate can reach the top level in the mark scheme.**"* — Nov 2024 P1

**Note:** the biggest cause of lost marks across the whole series is **not** lack of knowledge — it is answering an adjacent question, or failing to counterbalance.
