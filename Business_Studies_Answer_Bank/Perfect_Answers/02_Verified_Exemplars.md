# 3 MARKS — EXPLAIN

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 3/3

**June 2019, Paper 1, Question 1(f)** — *Explain one way a business could increase its gross profit margin.* (3)
**Source:** `4BS1_Paper1_Exemplar_Commentaries_June2019.pdf` pp. 5–6 · Scan: `Jun2019_P1_Q1f_explain_3of3_and_1of3.png`

> "A business could reduce it's cost of sales to increase its gross profit margin. It could reduce it's cost by focusing on lean production which is reducing waste to save money by using less raw materials"

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response shows an understanding of how a business could increase gross profit margin by reducing the cost of sales. It continues to explain how this way can help a business to increase its gross profit margin by focusing on lean production leading to the use of fewer raw materials, thus gaining the full available marks."*

### Why this is a perfect 3/3

Trace the chain — it maps exactly onto the mark scheme's `(1) … (1) … (1)` structure:

| Step | Text | Earns |
|---|---|---|
| **Identify** | "reduce it's cost of sales to increase its gross profit margin" | 1 |
| **Because** | "by focusing on lean production which is reducing waste to save money" | 1 |
| **Leading to** | "by using less raw materials" | 1 |

**One idea. Three steps. Full marks.** It is short — roughly 40 words. Length is not the objective.

**Note:** the answer opens with almost no preamble and never defines "gross profit margin". It goes straight into the chain. Compare the 1-mark response below, which spends its words naming three different methods.

# 6 MARKS — ANALYSE

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 6/6

**June 2019, Paper 1, Question 1(g)** — *Analyse the importance of satisfying customer needs to Backyard Shoez.* (6)
**Source:** `4BS1_Paper1_Exemplar_Commentaries_June2019.pdf` pp. 7–9 · Scan: `Jun2019_P1_Q1g_analyse_6of6.png`

> "If Backyard Shoes meets the demand of satisfying customer needs, they would make the customer to believe that Backyard Shoes products, eg shoes, are of very high quality and it builds trust & links with their customers so that they're more loyal. As more customers are satisfied, Backyard Shoes would gain popularity through spreading of the mouth of customers. They would help this business to build a good, well-known brand name. As more and more influenced customers coming in, Backyard Shoes, as a result, would make more revenue and profit. Their cost are also decreased because Backyard Shoes got free advertising through their loyal customers. This again would help their total revenue to increase, although there are lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre."

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 3 – 6 marks.*
> *This response has referred to the scenario given throughout the response, not just the business name, but added context in terms of the business activity.*
> *The response shows detailed application of knowledge and understanding of the importance of satisfying customer needs. This candidate has outlined valid points and linked them well with logical chains of reasoning, as well as explaining the effects these can have on the business."*

### Why this is a perfect 6/6 — the "throughout" test

The examiners' key phrase is **"throughout the response, not just the business name"**. Watch how the chain builds:

```
satisfy customer needs
  → customers believe products are high quality  ("eg shoes" — the business's actual product)
  → builds trust and loyalty
  → satisfied customers spread word of mouth   (free promotion)
  → brand name becomes well known
  → more customers → more revenue and profit
  → costs decreased via free advertising
  → total revenue increases
  → BUT: there are lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre
```

Every single link is anchored to *Backyard Shoez the shoe retailer*, not to "a business". Note the concrete context details: **"eg shoes"** and **"lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre"** — those are lifted from the case, which is precisely what AO2 "detailed application … throughout" pays for.

**Note the counterpoint in the final clause.** Even at 6 marks, where there is no AO4, this candidate still lands a limitation. It is not required, but it demonstrates understanding — and it does not cost anything.

**And note the language quality.** "make the customer to believe", "Their cost are also decreased", "spreading of the mouth". Grammar errors do not stop a 6/6. **Content and context are marked, not SPaG.**

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 6/6

**June 2019, Paper 1, Question 4(b)** — *Analyse the usefulness of profitability ratios to a business.* (6)
**Source:** `4BS1_Paper1_Exemplar_Commentaries_June2019.pdf` pp. 26–29 · Scan: `Jun2019_P1_Q4b_analyse_6of6.png`

> "Profitability ratios help access the performance of Backyard Shoes, respective of its profits. It helps to see how profitable is a business. Backyard Shoes can make use of gross profit for the year/operating profit to check its cost analysis and bring down its expenses under control if it has exceeded the gross profit margin. Profitability ratio get help Book directors/owners at Backyard Shoes know how much they receive in return for the capital employed. If the ROCE is high, owners maybe better motivated to launch new shoes of better quality at Backyard. This aids in investment decisions for Backyard. Moreover, it provides a useful method to increase prices or reduce purchase of stock (shoes to be sold) for Backyard, based on the gross profit and operating profit. They can be used to make comparisons with the previous year, and make improvements and set new targets to achieve in sales for Backyard Shoes."

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 3 – 6 marks.*
> *This response outlines the main profitability ratios and what impact they can have on the business. Throughout this response, there is detailed application of the knowledge demonstrated as well as a clear understanding of business concepts. This candidate is able to analyse how useful these profitability ratios will be to the business and what the business can do with the information gathered on these ratios.*
> *This response gained all available marks for this question."*

### The technique to copy here

This answer solves a hard problem: **profitability ratios are abstract.** The candidate converts each ratio into a *decision the business can take*:

| Ratio | Converted into a decision |
|---|---|
| Gross profit / operating profit | "check its cost analysis and bring down its expenses under control" |
| ROCE | "owners maybe better motivated to launch new shoes of better quality" |
| Gross + operating profit | "increase prices or reduce purchase of stock (shoes to be sold)" |
| Year-on-year comparison | "make comparisons with the previous year… set new targets to achieve in sales" |

**That is the move for any 6-mark "analyse the usefulness/importance of [tool]" question.** Do not describe the tool. Show what the business *does differently* because it has it. That is AO3 — "investigate and analyse".

Also note: the candidate names the business **five times** (Backyard Shoes, Backyard ×3, at Backyard). That is what "detailed application throughout" physically looks like on the page.

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 9/9

**June 2019, Paper 1, Question 3(e)** — *Justify which source of finance Backyard Shoez should use.* (9)
**Source:** `4BS1_Paper1_Exemplar_Commentaries_June2019.pdf` pp. 20–25 · Scan: `Jun2019_P1_Q3e_justify_9of9.png`

**This is the single most valuable item in the bank** — a verified full-mark 9-marker, spanning two pages of handwriting.

> "Backyard Shoez should choose Option 1 since crowdfunding would provide a platform for the retailer to raise a large sum of funds. Therefore, more money could be earned in large quantities enabling the business to develop the packaging of men's shoes or to use other methods of selling like e-tailing which can allow the business to target a much wider market. This ensures that there is sufficient money compared to an overdraft where a set limit is decided by the bank. Moreover, an overdraft can be very expensive as interest rates may be high. This would mean that more monthly cash leakages from the business would limit the amount of money to be used to pay for warehousing or insurance of the new and unique footwear. Thus the business may have to use other external sources of finance like debentures which would affect the stability of the business and increase its debt. However, if the retailer uses crowdfunding then every investor would own a share of the business. This could result in a loss of control as the new investors may have different objectives and may want to continue only selling shoes and handbags for ladies. In addition, the business would have to inform the members regarding the venture of every step and action of selling the men's shoes. Thus if customers do not like the new shoes then it would earn bad publicity for the retailer and the members would refuse to continue investing. In conclusion Option 1 is more suitable since a very large amount of money is needed to add value to the men's shoes to reduce the risk of failure of the new product. That would also mean that the retailer can avoid using the bank so no repayments or interest rate are paid"

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 3 – 9 marks.*
> *This response shows detailed application of the candidate's knowledge throughout the response. There is a great understanding of business concepts and the issues the business might face for when deciding between each option with clear context of the business throughout.*
> *The candidate has shown a well-balanced argument between the two finance options and how this will impact on the business.*
> *This response demonstrates detailed points with logical chains of reasoning.*
> *This candidate has shown they are able to make a judgment with clear justification."*

### The architecture — copy this skeleton

| Move | Text | AO served |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Decide in the first sentence** | "Backyard Shoez **should choose Option 1** since crowdfunding would provide a platform… to raise a large sum of funds." | AO4 opens immediately |
| **2. Chain the benefit** | raise large sum → develop packaging of men's shoes → e-tailing → "target a much wider market" | AO2 + AO3 |
| **3. Contrast with the rejected option** | "compared to an overdraft where a set limit is decided by the bank… interest rates may be high" | AO2 + AO3 |
| **4. Follow the consequence further** | high interest → "more monthly cash leakages" → less money for "warehousing or insurance" → "may have to use other external sources of finance like debentures" → "increase its debt" | **AO3 in its purest form — a four-link chain** |
| **5. Turn against your own choice** | "**However**, if the retailer uses crowdfunding then every investor would own a share… could result in a **loss of control**… may want to continue only selling shoes and handbags for ladies" | **AO4 — this is what makes it 9/9, not 7/9** |
| **6. Push the drawback further** | investors have different objectives → must inform members of every step → if customers dislike the shoes → "bad publicity… members would refuse to continue investing" | AO3 |
| **7. Conclude, still in context** | "**In conclusion** Option 1 is more suitable since a very large amount of money is needed to add value to the men's shoes to reduce the risk of failure of the new product. That would also mean that the retailer can avoid using the bank so no repayments or interest rate are paid" | AO4 — clear justification |

### The three things that separate 9/9 from 7/9

1. **A verdict in sentence one.** Not a description of both options. The choice is made before anything else is written.
2. **A genuine counter-argument (move 5).** The candidate argues *against* their own choice, then resolves it. Compare the 7/9 response (in `03_Calibration_Below_Full_Marks.md`), which the examiner said *"has started to make a judgement"* but *"had this candidate provided a thorough evaluation… they would have gained further marks."* **The difference between 7 and 9 here is the depth of evaluation, not more knowledge.**
3. **Context carried to the very last sentence.** "men's shoes", "the retailer" — every abstract point lands back on the case.

**Zero definitional content.** Nowhere does the candidate explain what crowdfunding or an overdraft is. The examiner confirms: *"there are no knowledge marks available for this question."*

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 12/12

**June 2024, Paper 1, Question 4(c)** — *Evaluate the importance of marketing to PPS so it can keep its market share. You should use the information provided as well as your own knowledge of business.* (12)
**Source:** `June2024_P1_Examiners_Report_Excerpts_12mark_Evaluate.pdf` · Scans: `Jun2024_P1_Q4c_evaluate_12of12_part1.png`, `..._part2.png`

**Context:** PPS is a pet hotel and grooming service. *"Another pet business is about to open a rival pet hotel in the village. This is the first time PPS has faced competition."*

> "Marketing is the use of advertising to influence a customer into making a purchase. Marketing is very important for PPS to keep its market share. This is because PPS can use Strategies such as above-the-line promotion to display pet grooming adverts all over Alhaurin el Grande. This will increase the amount of new pet owner's who decide to use the pet hotel and grooming service. Therefore, PPS can still maintain and even increase their market share in pet enthusiast industry.
>
> However this marketing technique can be disadvantageous for PPS. This because the increased demand from new customers may force Rachel to hire new overnight pet specialists as the current few may be too overwhelmed. This will lead to a surge in monthly expense at PPS. Therefore, PPS may receive a lower profit margin for a high and more suitable market share.
>
> Alternatively, Rachel could implement primary market research on current pet owners of PPS. This could be done with focus groups and questioning after a stay at the pet hotel. This means that PPS can start producing exactly the pet toys and services that their customers exactly need. Therefore, increasing brand loyalty and maintaining pet industry market share.
>
> Overall, marketing is crucial for PPS to keep it's market share as this is the first time they face a competitor. PPS should implement market research to create better suited pet toys while doing television adverts to bring new pet owners. This depends on how much the television time costs and the quality of market research. This may negatively affect PPS in the short term but in the long term PPS will benefit as remaining market leader."

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This example shows that the candidate has clearly understood how marketing impacts upon PPS. They have been able to counter balance their argument well with plenty of context throughout the response. There is well thought out consideration that marketing is important to PPS. Finally, there is a conclusion that adds value to the response and effectively uses the 'it depends rule'."*

And from the report's introduction to this question:
> *"Those candidates who accessed level 3 considered the importance of marketing in the context of PPS and the impact this can have on the business, they have then successfully linked their points together in a logical order and been able to conclude their answer with a well-balanced and justified argument."*

### The architecture

| Paragraph | Function | AO |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Defines marketing, then **immediately answers the question**: "very important for PPS to keep its market share" and gives a reason with a concrete local detail — **"all over Alhaurin el Grande"** | AO1 + AO2 |
| 2 | **"However"** — turns to the downside: more demand → must hire overnight pet specialists → "surge in monthly expense" → **"lower profit margin for a high and more suitable market share"** | AO3 |
| 3 | **"Alternatively"** — a *second* strategy entirely (primary market research, focus groups) and chains it to brand loyalty | AO2 + AO3 |
| 4 | **"Overall"** — verdict, then **"This depends on…"** | **AO4** |

### The "it depends rule" — the single most transferable technique here

Look at how the conclusion is built:

> *"Overall, marketing is **crucial** for PPS… **This depends on** how much the television time costs and the quality of market research. This **may negatively affect PPS in the short term but in the long term** PPS will benefit as remaining market leader."*

This is the formula:

```
VERDICT  (commit to a position)
   ↓
"THIS DEPENDS ON…"  (name the specific conditions that would change your answer)
   ↓
SHORT TERM vs LONG TERM  (the reliable axis for any conclusion)
   ↓
RESTATE THE VERDICT IN CONTEXT  ("as remaining market leader")
```

It commits, **and** it recognises the conditions under which it would be wrong. That combination is what the level 3 descriptor means by *"a well-balanced and justified argument"*.

### Notice what this 12-mark answer actually does about the case

It uses **PPS-specific facts**: Rachel by name, pet grooming, pet toys, overnight pet specialists, Alhaurin el Grande. It never once writes "the business" generically. In a 12-marker, **AO2 alone is worth 3 marks** — and it is the cheapest 3 marks on the paper, because the case study is handed to you.

Also note: **continuous prose, no bullets** — the examiner report states bullets *"will not allow them to gain the higher levels."*
