## ⭐⭐ The definitive pair — `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 1/1 vs 0/1

**June 2022 P2R, Question 1(d)** — *State **one** reason why Cadbury might pay its UK employees above the minimum wage.* (1)

### ✅ Awarded **1 mark**
> *"To retain its employees. In this case, Cadbury would want to retain its employees so they don't switch to competitors like Lindt or Royce."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response is **in context** by commenting on Cadbury not wanting its employees to switch to **competitors like Lindt**."*

### ❌ Awarded **0 marks**
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"This is to improve brand image and reputation in the market, as it has lots of competition, it needs good image and reputation to attract workers to work for them and to attract customers to buy from them."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response has made **very valid comments** about there is lots of competition and Cadbury wants to maintain its reputation. **However, there is nothing that is related back to Cadbury and its workers.**"*

### The two answers side by side

| | **1 mark** | **0 marks** |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 18 words | 43 words |
| Names Cadbury | once | **twice** |
| Names a **competitor** | **Lindt, Royce** | *"lots of competition"* |
| Names the **employee group** | *"its employees"* | *"workers"* (generic) |
| Verdict | **in context** | *"very valid… however, nothing related back"* |

**The 0-mark answer is longer, more fluent, and factually blameless — and it earns nothing.**

**Examiner rule (verbatim):**
> *"**Just putting Cadbury in the response does not mean it is in context as Cadbury is in the question.**"*

**Examiner tip (verbatim) — the single most useful sentence in this bank:**
> *"**Try and avoid using the name of the scenario but include something that is related to the business. In this example perhaps including chocolate cake designers would put the response in context.**"*

> **So: do not write the business's name. Write a specific noun from the case that could not appear in a generic answer** — *"chocolate cake designers"*, *"competitors like Lindt"*, *"the quality of the chocolate packaging"*.

### Two more verified State answers from the same paper

# 2 MARKS — CALCULATE

## ⭐⭐ The decimal-place pair — `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 1/2 vs 2/2

**June 2022 P2R, Question 1(e)** — *Calculate the selling price of the chocolate cake. You are advised to show your working.* (2)

### ⚠️ Awarded **1 mark**
> *"6.5 x (1+20%) = 6.5 x 1.2 = **£7.8**"*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This particular response only gained **one mark** as the working out is correct but the answer was **missing the second decimal place**."*

### ✅ Awarded **2 marks**
> *"6.50 × 1.2 = £**7.80**"*
> **Examiner:** *"A clear response and easy to read."*

> ### 🔴 One character. One mark.
> **The method is right. The arithmetic is right. The candidate wrote `.8` instead of `.80` and lost a mark.**
>
> *"Check the question carefully to see if one or two decimal places are required in the answer."*

> ### 🚨 THE ANSWER-LINE RULE — the strictest version of the d.p. requirement
>
> **June 2024 P1R, examiner guidance (verbatim):**
> > *"**Centres should be aware that examiners are advised to take the answer provided by candidates on the final answer line** therefore if the final answer is not to 2 decimal places the candidates **will not gain access to the full marks even if the answer to 2 decimal places is in the working out. The answer provided by a candidate on the answer line is viewed as their final response to the question so that is what is marked.**"*
>
> 🔴 **This overrides everything else about workings.** Workings are insurance *for the method mark only*. **The number on the final line is the answer, full stop.**
>
> **So: finish the calculation, then write the correctly-rounded figure ON THE LINE. Every time.**

> ### 🏆 AND NOW THE EXACT MECHANISM — June 2025, Paper 2R
>
> **The candidate's paper:** workings `1590 / 0.93 = 1709.87` — **correct**. Answer line: **$1709.67** — **wrong**.
>
> **Examiner comment (verbatim):** *"This response scored **1 mark**. This response is a **very rare example of what a 1 mark answer looks like**. The candidate has given the **wrong answer on the answer line** of 1709.67. **Thus, examiners cannot award 2 marks.** Examiners will then go back and look at the workings… The response scored 1 mark in total for the correct calculation, **unfortunately the candidate provided the incorrect 2 decimal places for the second mark**."*
>
> **And the rule stated plainly:**
> > ### 🔴 *"If the answer is correct on the answer line, the examiner will automatically give 2 marks. The workings are only ever looked at if the answer on the answer line is incorrect."*
>
> | What is on the answer line | Result |
> |---|---|
> | **Correct, to the required d.p.** | **2 marks** — workings never checked |
> | **Wrong or wrongly rounded** | examiner **then** checks workings → **1 mark** if the method is right |
> | **Blank** | no rescue |
>
> **Workings can only ever LOOK BACK. They rescue a mark you have already lost — they can never add one.**
>
> *"write their answer on the **dotted lines at the bottom of the box** so that the examiners are clear as to what their answer is"* (June 2022).

>
> **This is the first time the decimal-place rule is shown on an actual script rather than described.** It is now the bank's 10th recorded instance of the rule and the clearest of them all.

**Two more calculation rules from the same report:**

> **Workings are insurance.** *"they could gain a mark for the working, **even if the actual answer written in the box is incorrect**."*
> **Put the answer on the line.** *"write their answer on the **dotted lines at the bottom of the box** so that the examiners are clear as to what their answer is."*
> **Make digits legible.** *"take care to ensure that their **figures are easily recognisable**, to avoid any confusion by the examiner."*

>
> | Rule | Evidence |
> |---|---|

**June 2019, Paper 1, Question 1(f)** — *Explain one way a business could increase its gross profit margin.* (3)

## ⭐⭐⭐ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 3/3 and 1/3 — **"one point and then TWO POINTS OF DEVELOPMENT"**

**June 2025 P2, Q1(f)** — *Explain **one** disadvantage of training employees.* (3)

**The definition, from the senior examiner (verbatim):**
> *"All 'explain' questions are worth 3 marks. **It is important the candidate gives _one_ point and then provides 2 points of development.**"*

### ✅ Awarded 3 marks
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Training employees might be cost expenses. This is because, a business might have to employ specialised trainers, or would have to rent a training premises This would likely raise cost for the business. Hence, profit reduces if revenue remains constant."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This was awarded the full 3 marks. A disadvantage is identified, ie it may be expensive, which is **developed to show _both a cause and a consequence_**. The **cause** relates to specialised trainers and training premises that are required. The **consequence** is that this may reduce profits if revenues remain constant."*

### ⚠️ Awarded 1 mark
> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"One disadvantage for the business may be that, when a business provides trainings for the new employees. After some time employees may leave and gain rival business, and this is why business gets frustrated, that is why this is a disadvantage for businesses training its employees."*

**Examiner comment (verbatim):**
> *"This response gained just one mark. The candidate **identified** that the trained employee may go to a rival business **but this was not developed to show any _consequences_ of this to a business**."*

> ### 🎯 THE 3-MARK SHAPE, NAMED BY PEARSON
> **Point (1) → CAUSE (1) → CONSEQUENCE (1)**
>
> *"developed to show both a **cause** and a **consequence**."*
>
> **The 1-mark answer had the point and the mechanism — and no consequence.** *"Employees may leave"* is a fact about the employee. It becomes a **business** consequence only when the chain finishes: *"so the business has to pay recruitment and training costs again, raising its costs."*
>
> ### ✅ The two-second check before you move on
> **Does my answer contain a *because* (cause) AND a *so / therefore* that changes something FOR THE BUSINESS (consequence)?**
>
> ⭐ **And an upper bound, from June 2022 P1R (recorded twice in one paper):** *"explained this method with **more than two links of reasoning so has wasted some time when there are only 3 marks available**."* **Two development links is the cap. A third earns nothing and costs time you need for the 12-marker.**

## ✅ `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]` — 6/6

**June 2019, Paper 1, Question 1(g)** — *Analyse the importance of satisfying customer needs to Backyard Shoez.* (6)
**Source:** `4BS1_Paper1_Exemplar_Commentaries_June2019.pdf` pp. 7–9 · Scan: `Jun2019_P1_Q1g_analyse_6of6.png`

> "If Backyard Shoes meets the demand of satisfying customer needs, they would make the customer to believe that Backyard Shoes products, eg shoes, are of very high quality and it builds trust & links with their customers so that they're more loyal. As more customers are satisfied, Backyard Shoes would gain popularity through spreading of the mouth of customers. They would help this business to build a good, well-known brand name. As more and more influenced customers coming in, Backyard Shoes, as a result, would make more revenue and profit. Their cost are also decreased because Backyard Shoes got free advertising through their loyal customers. This again would help their total revenue to increase, although there are lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre."

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 3 – 6 marks.*
> *This response has referred to the scenario given throughout the response, not just the business name, but added context in terms of the business activity.*
> *The response shows detailed application of knowledge and understanding of the importance of satisfying customer needs. This candidate has outlined valid points and linked them well with logical chains of reasoning, as well as explaining the effects these can have on the business."*

### Why this is a perfect 6/6 — the "throughout" test

The examiners' key phrase is **"throughout the response, not just the business name"**. Watch how the chain builds:

```
satisfy customer needs
  → customers believe products are high quality  ("eg shoes" — the business's actual product)
  → builds trust and loyalty
  → satisfied customers spread word of mouth   (free promotion)
  → brand name becomes well known
  → more customers → more revenue and profit
  → costs decreased via free advertising
  → total revenue increases
  → BUT: there are lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre
```

Every single link is anchored to *Backyard Shoez the shoe retailer*, not to "a business". Note the concrete context details: **"eg shoes"** and **"lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre"** — those are lifted from the case, which is precisely what AO2 "detailed application … throughout" pays for.

**Note the counterpoint in the final clause.** Even at 6 marks, where there is no AO4, this candidate still lands a limitation. It is not required, but it demonstrates understanding — and it does not cost anything.

**And note the language quality.** "make the customer to believe", "Their cost are also decreased", "spreading of the mouth". Grammar errors do not stop a 6/6. **Content and context are marked, not SPaG.**

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**

### The technique to copy here

This answer solves a hard problem: **profitability ratios are abstract.** The candidate converts each ratio into a *decision the business can take*:

| Ratio | Converted into a decision |
|---|---|
| Gross profit / operating profit | "check its cost analysis and bring down its expenses under control" |
| ROCE | "owners maybe better motivated to launch new shoes of better quality" |

**That is the move for any 6-mark "analyse the usefulness/importance of [tool]" question.** Do not describe the tool. Show what the business *does differently* because it has it. That is AO3 — "investigate and analyse".

Also note: the candidate names the business **five times** (Backyard Shoes, Backyard ×3, at Backyard). That is what "detailed application throughout" physically looks like on the page.

## ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ THE NAMED CONDITION — why some evaluations score and others do not

| Mark | The candidate's evaluation | Condition named? |
|---|---|---|
| **12/12** | *"could be limited because it depends on factors such as **nature of employee**"* | ✅ + outside knowledge |
| **9/9** | *"it depends on **the quality of training that the chocolate makers will be provided with**"* | ✅ |

| **8/9** | *nothing* | ❌ |
| **7/9** | *nothing* | ❌ |

> ## 🎯 **An evaluation earns marks only when the condition is NAMED and SPECIFIC to this business and this option.**
> **Test:** could you paste your condition clause onto a different question? If yes, it is generic and it will not lift you past 8.

### THE THREE VALID MOVES — the phrase "it depends" is optional
| Move | Real full-mark example |
|---|---|
| **Named condition** | *"it depends on the quality of training the chocolate makers will be provided with"* |
| **Specific drawback of your chosen option** | *"the internet is generalised… they won't find trends about **flowers**"* |
| **Concede against your own choice** | *"**In the long term, job rotation may be better** as it gives employees a sense of value"* |

⭐ **The concession is the strongest move of the three.** That candidate argues against their own choice and still scores 9. **It is the exact opposite of the restate-your-argument fault that caps answers at 8.**

### 🏆 THE 9/9 CONCLUSION — THREE SENTENCES, THREE JOBS
> **[⚠️ not in any text layer held in this repo — a transcription, not a copy; re-check against the printed page before relying on it]**
> *"**Personally, I would choose to use Bonuses** as money can be a key motivator to people, it also helps increase your sales. **A disadvantage of job rotation is that people may not be qualified for every job** in a company. **In the long term, job rotation may be better as it gives employees a sense of value.**"*
>
> **1** decides · **2** names the alternative's drawback · **3** concedes the long-run counter-case.

> "Marketing is the use of advertising to influence a customer into making a purchase. Marketing is very important for PPS to keep its market share. **Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This example shows that the candidate has clearly understood how marketing impacts upon PPS. They have been able to counter balance their argument well with plenty of context throughout the response. There is well thought out consideration that marketing is important to PPS. Finally, there is a conclusion that adds value to the response and effectively uses the 'it depends rule'."*

And from the report's introduction to this question:
> *"Those candidates who accessed level 3 considered the importance of marketing in the context of PPS and the impact this can have on the business, they have then successfully linked their points together in a logical order and been able to conclude their answer with a well-balanced and justified argument."*

### The architecture

| Paragraph | Function | AO |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Defines marketing, then **immediately answers the question**: "very important for PPS to keep its market share" and gives a reason with a concrete local detail — **"all over Alhaurin el Grande"** | AO1 + AO2 |
| 2 | **"However"** — turns to the downside: more demand → must hire overnight pet specialists → "surge in monthly expense" → **"lower profit margin for a high and more suitable market share"** | AO3 |
| 3 | **"Alternatively"** — a *second* strategy entirely (primary market research, focus groups) and chains it to brand loyalty | AO2 + AO3 |
| 4 | **"Overall"** — verdict, then **"This depends on…"** | **AO4** |

### The "it depends rule" — the single most transferable technique here

Look at how the conclusion is built:

> **[read from the printed script image — these words are not in the PDF's text layer, so they are a transcription, not a copy]**
> *"Overall, marketing is **crucial** for PPS… **This depends on** how much the television time costs and the quality of market research. This **may negatively affect PPS in the short term but in the long term** PPS will benefit as remaining market leader."*

This is the formula:

```
VERDICT  (commit to a position)
   ↓
"THIS DEPENDS ON…"  (name the specific conditions that would change your answer)
   ↓
SHORT TERM vs LONG TERM  (the reliable axis for any conclusion)
   ↓
RESTATE THE VERDICT IN CONTEXT  ("as remaining market leader")
```

It commits, **and** it recognises the conditions under which it would be wrong. That combination is what the level 3 descriptor means by *"a well-balanced and justified argument"*.

### Notice what this 12-mark answer actually does about the case

It uses **PPS-specific facts**: Rachel by name, pet grooming, pet toys, overnight pet specialists, Alhaurin el Grande. It never once writes "the business" generically. In a 12-marker, **AO2 alone is worth 3 marks** — and it is the cheapest 3 marks on the paper, because the case study is handed to you.

Also note: **continuous prose, no bullets** — the examiner report states bullets *"will not allow them to gain the higher levels."*
