# Third-party advice, tested against Pearson

**What this file is.** Every candidate is told a set of rules by tutors, revision sites and forums.
Some are true for 4BS1, some are true for **GCSE Business 1BS0** (a different qualification) and are
smuggled across, and some are simply wrong here. **This file states each claim, says where it comes
from, and tests it against the evidence in this bank.**

> ## ⚠️ HOW TO READ IT — the status of these sources
> **Nothing in this file is evidence.** It is a list of *claims found elsewhere*, each checked against
> Pearson's own documents. **Where a third-party claim and Pearson disagree, Pearson wins** — the
> authority order in `README.md` is not negotiable. **None of these sources is a Pearson publication,
> and none is relied on anywhere else in the bank.** They are recorded because a candidate will meet
> them, and because two of them contradict the bank's own evidence so directly that a student holding
> both would freeze in the exam hall.
>
> **Provenance is weak by design and is stated as such:** these were surfaced by **search snippets on
> 13 Nov 2026**, not audited page by page. The claims are reproduced as the snippets gave them; the
> URLs are recorded so a later pass can read the pages in full. **A snippet is not a source.**

## 1. "Both options / both sides are required" — **contradicted**

**Pearson, twice, in its own words:**
> *"Candidates do not have to consider both options in their response. Candidates can achieve the full
> marks only considering the one option."*
> *"Candidates can choose to weigh up the advantages and disadvantages of one option and then provide a
> conclusion, or they could choose to weigh up both options…"*

**And measured, with the limit stated:** the archive's one-option scripts score **7/9** and **8/9**
(Cadbury job share; June 2026 P1R crowdfunding, where Pearson writes *"chooses to look at one option,
which is acceptable"*). **It does not hold a 9/9 that discusses only one option** — so the *rule* is
Pearson's, and the *demonstration* in this archive is 8/9, not 9. **Do not present one-option as a
route to full marks on this bank's evidence; present it as permitted, which is what Pearson says.**

**Why the myth persists:** on a **12-mark Evaluate** the level descriptors *do* require balance. **A
Justify does not.** The two questions sit next to each other on the paper and the advice leaks between
them.

## 2. "You must write a conclusion" — **half true, and the useful half is not the one quoted**

| What Pearson pays | What it does not pay |
|---|---|
| an **AO4 judgement** — a decision, a weighing, or a named condition. On a 9-marker that is 3 of the 9 marks | a paragraph that *begins* "In conclusion" |

**The proof that the paragraph is optional:** the **second 12/12** in this bank **has no conclusion
paragraph at all.** Its last paragraph decides — *"the effectiveness … could be limited because it
depends on factors such as nature of employee"* — and stops.

**The proof that a conclusion is not sufficient:** the **8/9** whose conclusion merely repeats what
was argued (*"a judgement made that repeats the financial implication that was argued previously"*),
and the **8/9** that writes *"it much depends on the objectives set by the owner"* and is still asked
for the *'it depends' rule*.

> **The rule to carry:** **a conclusion earns marks only for what it adds that is not already on the
> page.** If your last paragraph could be deleted without changing the answer's judgement, it was not
> a conclusion.

## 3. "Five linked, developed strands" — **contradicted**

**Length and count are not in the descriptors.** Measured in this bank:

| Script | Length | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| a 12/12 (May 2023 P1 Q4(c)) | **≈330 words**, two and a half paragraphs, no bullets, no "In conclusion" | **12/12** |
| a 6/6 (June 2019-style Analyse) | **43 words — three sentences** | **6/6** |
| a 6/6 (June 2025 P1R Q3(d)) | two short paragraphs, **no conclusion** | **6/6** |

**What the level descriptors actually ask for is a property of each chain, not a count of them:**
*detailed interconnected points* with *logical chains of reasoning*, applied *throughout*. **Three
chains that each end on a business outcome beat six that stop at "this is good for the business".**

---

## 4. "3-mark Explain needs no case-study application" — **supported**

**Pearson:** the 3-mark Explain is **AO1: 3** on the schemes, and a report says it outright — *"No
context is required for explain questions but often we see candidates applying context unnecessarily."*

⚠️ **The trap on the other side of the same coin:** *"NB No marks are awarded for a definition."* **A
definition is not the mechanism.** The 3 marks are one chain: **cause → effect → business outcome.**

---

## 5. "Say 'it depends on…'" — **half true, and the false half is what gets copied**

**Supported:** a **named condition** is one of the three moves that reaches the top band. Every 9/9 in
this bank that uses the phrase names something specific to that business and that option.

**Contradicted:** *"give a factor (cost, location, size, demand, objectives)"* — a list of **generic**
factors is exactly what the 8/9s do. The evidence:

- **8/9:** *"it depends on **how much they have spent in these expenditure sample**"* — vague.
- **8/9:** *"it **much depends** on the objectives set by the owner for CCT"* — present, unnamed, and
  Pearson still asks for *"the 'it depends' rule"*.
- **10/12:** the script Pearson says **uses** the rule is asked to use it *"earlier and throughout"*.
- **11/12:** the script that **does not** use the rule is praised for *"a clear judgement"* and asked
  for *"more sophisticated application"*.
- **12/12:** no phrase, but a condition — *"nature of employee"* — with knowledge from outside the case.

> **The test:** *could your condition clause be pasted onto a different question?* If yes, it is worth
> nothing. **"It depends on cost" fails. "It depends on whether the Shanghai customers return after the
> price rise" passes.**

## 6. "The 12-mark conclusion must weigh the arguments" — **true for GCSE 1BS0, one route for 4BS1**

The revision site that states this is writing about **Edexcel GCSE Business (1BS0)**, a **different
qualification** with different level descriptors and a different AO structure. This bank has a standing
rule against mixing the two (`README.md`: *"its technique may transfer but its scripts must never be
presented as 4BS1 exemplars"*).

**For 4BS1**, three routes reach AO4:

| Route | Real full-mark example |
|---|---|
| **Weigh** | *"Although it causes a huge cost… the increase in sales may over the cost in long term."* |
| **Condition** | *"The success of this depends on how much employees get to voice their own concerns."* |
| **Decide** | *"the effectiveness … could be limited because it depends on factors such as nature of employee"* |

**What is common to all three: the last words make a judgement.** A summary of the argument is not a
judgement in any of them.
