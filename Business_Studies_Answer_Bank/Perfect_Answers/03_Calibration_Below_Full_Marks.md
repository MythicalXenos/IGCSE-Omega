# 03 — CALIBRATION: WHAT LOSES MARKS

These are **`[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]`** scripts that did *not* gain full marks. All quotes are verbatim from official Pearson materials.

> ### ⚠️ WHERE THE REASON COMES FROM — and it is not always the same place
> **Every mark in this file is printed by Pearson.** Where Pearson also states the defect *in the script's own examiner comment*, it is quoted as such — that is the great majority of this file.
>
> **For the June 2026 matched pair, it does not.** Those comments state what each script **did**; they do not state what was **missing**. **The diagnosis for that pair is the bank's**, drawn from the two scripts and from the level descriptors the same report prints — and it is labelled in place so it is never quoted as an examiner sentence. **The marks are Pearson's; the explanation of the gap is mine.**

**Why this file matters more than the perfect answers.** Everyone can see that a good answer is good. What is hard — and what actually moves a grade 7 to a 9 — is recognising the *specific, repeatable* mistakes that Pearson penalises again and again. Every one of these patterns recurs across papers and sessions, so each error you eliminate here is worth marks in five or more future questions.

## The single most expensive error in the paper

### 3-mark "Explain": listing instead of explaining → **1/3**

**June 2019 P1 Q1(f)** — *Explain one way a business could increase its gross profit margin.* (3)

> "A business can increase its gross profit margin by reducing cost of sales. Also, another way is to reduce unnecessary expenses of the business. And rising the price of products of the business. That way gross profit margin would increase."

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given 1 mark.*
> *This particular response gave **three ways** in which a business could increase its gross profit margin therefore limiting the marks awarded to 1. Had the candidate explained any one of these three ways, then they would have gained further marks."*

**Diagnosis:** three valid ideas, **one mark**. The candidate knew three times as much as the 3/3 candidate and scored a third of the marks.

Compare with the 3/3 answer on the same question: *one* idea, pushed through *three* steps.

**Mark scheme confirmation:** *"NB Answers that list three benefits with no explanation will get 1 mark only."* This is not examiner discretion — it is written into the mark scheme.

**The fix:** the moment you catch yourself writing "Also…", "Another way…" or "In addition…" in a 3-mark answer, stop. Take the idea you just wrote and push it one step further instead.

---

### 3-mark "Explain": examples instead of development → **1/3**

**June 2019 P1 Q2(d)** — *Explain one above-the-line promotion method.* (3)

> "Social media can be used to promote its products. For example: using platforms such as facebook and instagram to advertise your products."

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given 1 mark.*
> *This response clearly states one method of above the line promotion a business could use. The candidate has then gone on to give **examples** of social media rather than develop on the given method, thus gaining only one mark for the method stated."*

**Diagnosis:** naming more instances of the same thing is **not** development. "Facebook and Instagram" are both just social media — the candidate has widened the example, not deepened the reasoning.

**The fix:** after you name your method, ask *"so what happens next for the business?"* and write that. The 3/3 answer did exactly this: television → influence people to buy → rise in sales and revenue.

## The context marks you give away for free

### 6-mark "Analyse": no context at all → **3/6**

**June 2019 P1R Q1(g)** — *Analyse the importance of customer satisfaction to Lavender* (a fine-dining and small-cakes business). (6)

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 2 – 3 marks.*
> *The above response represents many of the responses seen. The candidate has demonstrated the effects of customer satisfaction on a business but unfortunately there is **no context throughout this response**. Had the candidate replaced terms such as 'product' with 'food', it would have helped them move further into level 2 with the potential of a level 3 response.*
> *This candidate has outlined two valid points of why customer satisfaction would be important and has been able to show logical chains of reasoning.*
> ***To gain further marks, candidates must write in the context of the business scenario set.*** This response is limited to the bottom of level 2."*

**This is the most fixable mark loss in the entire bank.** The candidate had the reasoning right — *"outlined two valid points… logical chains of reasoning"* — and still sat at the bottom of Level 2 purely because they wrote "product" instead of "food".

**The examiner spells out the exact fix:** *"Had the candidate replaced terms such as 'product' with 'food'…"*

**The mechanism:** AO2 is 3 of the 6 marks. A generic answer forfeits that entire AO. Level 2 "sound application" is marked down from Level 3 "detailed application **throughout**".

**The fix — a mechanical habit:** after drafting any 6-mark answer, scan it for the words **"a business"**, **"the business"**, **"product"**, **"customers"**. Replace each with the case study's actual noun. That is a 30-second edit and it is worth 1–2 marks.

### 6-mark "Analyse": context only in the name → **2/6**

**June 2019 P1 Q1(g)** — *Analyse the importance of satisfying customer needs to Backyard Shoez.* (6)

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 1 – 2 marks.*
> *This response is relevant to satisfying customer needs but **the only reference made to the scenario is the name of the business**. Comments are brief and could have been expanded to gain further marks. There has been some attempt, albeit limited, to connect the points made.*
> *…**The candidate has been too vague with regards to 'the business' and 'products'** – had this response been in context to the given scenario and maybe gone into detail on what the customer needs were, then it would have gained further marks.*
> *This response is limited to the top of level 1."*

**Diagnosis:** writing "Backyard Shoez" once at the start is **not** context. The examiner explicitly separates *"the name of the business"* from what the 6/6 did — *"referred to the scenario given throughout the response, not just the business name, but added context in terms of the business activity."*

**The distinction, precisely:**

| ✗ Name only | ✓ Business activity |
|---|---|
| "Backyard Shoez must satisfy customers to survive." | "Backyard Shoez products, **eg shoes**, are of very high quality" |
| "the business", "products" | "**lots of competitors near the busy shopping centre**" |

**The test:** in a 6-marker, you should be naming the business or its actual products, services, competitors or location in **most sentences**, not just the first one.

### 6-mark "Analyse": valid points never developed → **2/6**

**June 2019 P1 Q4(b)** — *Analyse the usefulness of profitability ratios.* (6)

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 1 – 2 marks.*
> *This response shows **some very valid comments but a pity that these have not been developed**. This candidate has not been able to apply their knowledge to the business clearly… **If these references were in the context of Backyard Shoez, such as shoe/accessory suppliers, it would have helped the candidate move out of level 1.***
> *To move further, this candidate needs to **add context and expand on the points made with logical chains of reasoning**.
> **This response is limited to the top of level 1.**"*

**The examiner's suggested phrase is "shoe/accessory suppliers"** — that is the level of specificity required. Not "suppliers". *Shoe* suppliers.

## The judgement gap — 9 and 12 marks

### 9-mark "Justify": describing instead of deciding → **3/9**

**June 2019 P1 Q3(e)** — *Justify which source of finance Backyard Shoez should use.* (9)

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 1 – 3 marks.*
> *This candidate has spent time outlining how the business might crowdfund. **This is not what the question is asking.** A number of responses seen have demonstrated knowledge of both options; **there are no knowledge marks available for this question.***
> *This response shows limited application of their knowledge of both finance options. Some limited connections are made between the points this candidate has made with little judgement.*
> *To move further, the candidate could have **applied each option directly back to Backyard Shoez** and how the need for finance to launch their new products will affect them. The candidate could have then **provided a judgement for which option they would suggest… with a justification based on the business information.***
> *This response is limited to the top of level 1."*

**This is the definitive statement of the most common serious error in the paper.**

> **"There are no knowledge marks available for this question."**

On a 9-mark *justify*, describing what crowdfunding is earns **nothing**. The question asks you to *choose*. Every word spent defining an option is a word not spent arguing for one.

The examiner then gives the remedy in three steps: (1) apply to the business, (2) give a judgement, (3) justify it from the business information.

---

### 9-mark "Justify": judgement started, not finished → **7/9**

**June 2019 P1 Q2(f)** — *Justify a motivation option.* (9)

**Examiner's comments, on the 7/9 (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 3 – 7 marks.*
> *…This response shows detailed interconnected points with logical chains of reasoning. **There is good justification of why each method should or should not be used by the business.***
> *The candidate has **started to** make a judgement based on both options, which moves the response into level 3. **Had this candidate provided a thorough evaluation of the information presented, then they would have gained further marks.***
> ***This response is limited to the bottom of level 3.***"

**This is the 9-mark ceiling-breaker.** Knowledge is complete. Chains are logical. The *only* thing missing is a **thorough** evaluation. "Started to make a judgement" = 7. "Clear justification based on a thorough evaluation" = 9.

**Practical translation:** the difference between 7 and 9 is roughly *three or four sentences* of genuine evaluation — a real counter-argument and a resolution, exactly like the 9/9's *"However… loss of control…"* passage.

And on the 4/9 response from the same question:

> *"This response shows an understanding of both methods of motivation. The candidate has gone on to explain the impact each of these methods can have on the business as well as the employee being rewarded."*
> *(Level 2 – 4 marks)*

**Understanding both options with explained impacts = 4/9.** That is the mark you get for describing. It is less than half marks.

### 12-mark "Evaluate": knowledge with no application → **4/12**

**June 2019 P1 Q4(c)** — *Evaluate the importance of primary market research.* (12)

**Examiner's comments (verbatim):**
> *"This response was given Level 1 – 4 marks.*
> *This response provides **plenty of knowledge** of primary market research through the use of questionnaires and interviews. There is **very limited analysis** throughout this response with **little reference to the business**, therefore showing limited application of the knowledge shown. There is a **very basic judgement** made at the end of the response.*
> *To gain further marks, this response would need to have **more context** other than the reference to the launch of the new products. **Each point made… needs to be expanded upon with chains of reasoning**, referring back to the point made. It would also be helpful if a **sound evaluation** was provided to summarise how important primary market research would be to a business, like Backyard Shoez.*
> *This response is limited to the top of level 1."*

**Diagnosis:** *"plenty of knowledge"* and still **4 out of 12**. Four defects named in order: limited analysis, little reference to the business, basic judgement, no chains of reasoning.

**Remember the AO weighting:** in a 12-marker, AO1 is only 3 of the 12 marks. Plenty of knowledge alone cannot exceed Level 1 (max 4).

---

### 12-mark "Evaluate": strong analysis, thin evaluation → **10/12**

**May 2023 P2 Q4(c)** — *Evaluate the importance to IKEA of using demographic segmentation.* (12)

> *"A well developed response to the question, showing sound knowledge, application of knowledge and analysis with **limited judgements**."* — **Level 3, 10 marks**

**Diagnosis:** this candidate did nearly everything right and still dropped 2 marks, purely on AO4. Since AO4 is 3 of the 12, a *limited* judgement costs you most of that third.

**Compare the 12/12**, which ends with an explicit *"This depends on how much the television time costs and the quality of market research"* plus a short-term/long-term split. **That conclusion — not more subject knowledge — is what closes the gap from 10 to 12.**

### 🏆🏆 THE MATCHED PAIR — the same question, scored **11/12** and **6/12** in the same session

**June 2026 Paper 1R, Q4(c)** — *"Evaluate the extent to which social objectives may contribute to the success of Spud Bros."* **(12)** — **five marks apart on one stem, in one report.** This is the cleanest calibration evidence in the bank, because everything that could differ between two candidates — the case, the paper, the session, the year, the examiner's mood — is **held constant**, and only the **answer** differs.

Both scripts are `[VERIFIED CANDIDATE EXEMPLAR]`s with their marks printed. Full transcripts: `External_Exemplars/2026-06_P1R_SpudBros.md`; the marked pages are rendered in `Exemplar_Scans/J26P1R_Q4c_11of12.png` and `…_6of12.png`.

| | **11/12** | **6/12** |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Continuous prose, two long paragraphs + a conclusion | Continuous prose, several short paragraphs |
| **Every point** | A benefit **and** a drawback | **A benefit only** |
| **The chain** | **Six links**: charity → posted online → recipients comment → reputation → *"recognise Spud Bros from other potato businesses"* → competitiveness → sales | Clean three-link chains: *"customers want to spend more → increase in sales → increase in profit"* |
| **Context** | In every paragraph, inside the chains | **Also present throughout** |
| **Conclusion** | **Concedes, then outweighs:** *"Although it causes a huge cost… the increase in sales may over the cost in long term."* | **Restates** what was already said |

**Examiner comments — verbatim, both of them:**
> **11/12:** *"This response shows that the candidate understands how social objectives have contributed to the success of Spud Bros. **They offer balance in their argument with appropriate levels of context throughout the response.** Total: **11 marks**"*
>
> **6/12:** *"This example shows that the candidate understands social objectives and the benefits it brings in the context of Spud Bros. Total: **6 marks**"*

> ### ⚠️ WHAT THE REPORT DOES AND DOES NOT SAY — read this before quoting it
> **Pearson states the marks, and for the 11/12 it states that the script offers balance with context throughout. It does NOT state which mark was missing, or why, in either case.**
>
> **The diagnosis below is the bank's, drawn from the scripts themselves plus the level descriptors the same report prints** — it is **not** an examiner sentence, and it must never be quoted as one.
>
> **What makes the diagnosis safe here is that the two scripts are the same question, and the difference between them is not subtle: the 11/12 answers the question twice — for and against — and the 6/12 answers it once.**
>
> **And the report supplies the standard the pair is measured against, verbatim, in its own Q4(c) commentary:**
> > *"Those candidates who accessed **Level 3** considered the importance of social objectives, **provided balance in the context of Spud Bros** and the impact they have on the business. Then they **linked their points together in a logical order** and were able to **conclude their answer with a well-balanced and justified argument**."*
>
> **Three requirements, named in order: balance · logical linking · a well-balanced and justified conclusion. Nothing about length. Nothing about the number of points.**

**What the 6/12 had, and it was not enough:** correct knowledge of social objectives, **context throughout**, and **clean chains that end on a business outcome**. Everything this bank teaches for the 3-, 6- and 9-mark tariffs — and it is worth **half marks**, because **the descriptor for the top levels of a 12-marker is not about the quality of your argument for the business, it is about whether you argued both ways.**

### ⚠️ AND THE ONE THING THE 11/12 IS NOT
**It is not a model of the "it depends" formula — it does not use one.** It outweighs in plain words: *"Although it causes a huge cost… the increase in sales may over the cost in long term."* **Outweighing earns AO4. The phrase does not.** *(The two 12/12s in `02_Verified_Exemplars.md` each take a different route again — one names its condition explicitly, one has no conclusion paragraph at all. Three full-or-near-full 12-markers, three different shapes, one requirement in common: **the argument has two sides and the last word decides.**)*

## The merged diagnosis

Reading all the examiner comments together, the same four failures recur, and none of them is about knowing more content:

| # | Failure | Where it bites | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Listing instead of developing** | 3-mark explain → 1/3 | One idea, three linked steps. Never write "Also…" |
| **2** | **Generic language — "a business", "products"** | 6-mark analyse → 3/6, 2/6 | Replace with the case's nouns. *Food*, not product. *Shoe suppliers*, not suppliers. |
| **3** | **Describing options instead of choosing** | 9-mark justify → 3/9 | No knowledge marks exist. Decide in sentence one. |
| **4** | **Shallow or absent evaluation** | 9-mark → 7/9 · 12-mark → 10/12 | Counter-argue against your own choice, then resolve it with "this depends on…" and short vs long term. |
| **5** | **Arguing ONE side only** | 12-mark → **6/12** *(Spud Bros, Jun 2026 — the matched pair)* | **The top-level descriptor is not about how good your argument for the business is. It is about whether you argued both ways.** A benefits-only answer with perfect chains and full context is a Level 2 answer, and no amount of polish moves it. |

**Note what is absent from that list: subject content.** Every single one of these candidates demonstrated the relevant business knowledge. They lost marks on **how they deployed it**.

## Two more examiner rules worth memorising

**Do not use bullet points in 12-markers.** From the May 2023 P2 examiner report:
> *"Some candidates have used bullet points or numbered items, this will not allow them to gain the higher levels."*

**Do not repeat the question as your opening line.** From the June 2019 P1 Q2(d) commentary, on a response that still scored 3/3:
> *"The first sentence is not needed in this response. The candidate has simply repeated the question, therefore wasting time and space."*

Those seconds matter — the same examiner report repeatedly notes candidates who *"only completed a few lines"* on the final 12-marker because of poor time management.
