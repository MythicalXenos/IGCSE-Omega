# The chain sheet — every topic Paper 1 has asked, in chains

**How to use this.** Each row is **two chains**, not two sentences. Turn a chain into prose by adding
the case's own nouns and the word *"because"*:

> `bonus → output ↑ → revenue ↑` becomes
> *"A bonus per box sold would push the two chocolatiers to make more boxes **because** they are paid for
> output, **so** revenue rises without hiring another employee."*

**Never copy a chain without a noun from the case in it.** A chain with no case noun is a description,
and descriptions score Level 2.

**Reading order if you are short of time:** Theme 3 and Theme 4 first (they own the 2(f) Justify and
most 6-markers), then Theme 2 (it owns 3(e)), then Themes 1 and 5.

## Theme 3 — Finance *(the most examined theme on Paper 1)*

| Topic | Chain 1 | Chain 2 | The "however" / condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cash vs profit** | cash pays wages, rent, suppliers **on the day** → a profitable business with no cash cannot pay them → insolvency | cash buys stock before it is sold → running short stops production → lost sales | profit is an accounting figure and cannot be spent; cash is timing, not success |
| **Cash flow forecast** | predicts the month cash runs low → arrange an overdraft **in advance** → no missed wages | shows whether a big purchase is affordable → avoids over-committing | it is a forecast, not a fact — a late payer or a quiet month makes it wrong |
| **Break-even** | shows the output needed to cover costs → set a sales target → price or cost decisions | margin of safety shows how far sales can fall before a loss | **assumes every unit is sold at the same price and costs are a straight line** |
| **Break-even chart limits** | cannot show reputation, brand, loyal customers | one product at one price — ignores a changed mix | it is a model: useful for the decision, blind to the market |
| **Retained profit** | no interest and no lender → cheaper than a loan | keeps control with the owners | limited to what has been earned; may be needed for day-to-day cash |
| **Selling unused assets** | raises cash without debt → no repayments | removes an idle cost (storage, insurance) | sell the wrong asset and capacity is lost |
| **Bank loan** | known fixed repayments → budget with certainty | large sum up front → buy the machine now | interest and collateral; repayments continue if sales fall |
| **Overdraft** | flexible, short-term → covers a seasonal gap | no formal long application | expensive per day; repayable on demand |
| **Venture capital** | money **plus expertise** → grows faster than a loan allows | no repayments → cash stays in the business | investors take **a share and a say**; original owners may lose control |
| **Crowdfunding** | many small backers → money without interest | the campaign itself is publicity → early customers | a public failure; rewards cost money; may not reach the target |
| **Share capital** | sells shares → large sums for expansion | limited liability attracts investors | existing owners' control is diluted; dividends expected |
| **Statement of comprehensive income** | revenue − cost of sales − expenses → profit → shows whether the year worked | compare years → spot a falling margin before it is a crisis | it is history: it says what happened, not what will |
| **Statement of financial position** | assets vs liabilities → shows what the business owns and owes | shows liquidity → can it pay debts as they fall due | book values may not reflect what assets would fetch |
| **Gross profit margin** | GP ÷ revenue × 100 → shows pricing and cost control | falling margin → raise price or cut supplier cost | a high margin on low sales is still a small profit |
| **ROCE** | operating profit ÷ capital employed → return on the money invested | compare with a bank's interest → is the business worth it? | one year is not a trend |
| **Current / acid test ratio** | current assets ÷ current liabilities → can it pay this year's bills | acid test strips stock → harsher, truer test | stock-heavy businesses look worse on acid test by design |
| **Budgets** | set a spending limit → compare actual → fix the overspend | delegate spending → managers own their costs | a budget can be spent *because* it exists |
| **Financial documents to a bank** | several years' accounts → a trend → the bank can price the risk | shows existing debts → affordability | documents show the past; the loan is about the future |
| **Interest rate rise** | repayments ↑ → costs ↑ → price ↑ or margin ↓ | customers' disposable income ↓ → demand ↓ | fixed-rate loans delay the effect; a strong brand can pass it on |
| **Interest rate fall** | borrowing cheaper → invest now | mortgages cheaper → customers spend more | only helps if the business or its customers actually borrow |

## Theme 4 — Marketing

| Topic | Chain 1 | Chain 2 | The "however" / condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Purpose of market research** | find out what customers want **before** spending → less risk | identifies the right price and promotion → fewer wasted launches | costs money and time; the sample can be wrong |
| **Primary research** | collected for this question → specific and current | you own the data → competitors do not have it | expensive and slow; small samples mislead |
| **Secondary research** | cheap and fast → a starting point | industry data → context on rivals and the market | may be outdated or collected for another purpose |
| **Questionnaires** | many responses → measurable percentages | closed questions → easy to compare | leading questions bias it; low response rate |
| **Focus groups** | discussion → **why** people think it, not just what | reactions to a real product/taste → immediate feedback | small group; one loud voice dominates; expensive per head |
| **Product trial / test marketing** | real customers, real setting → proof before a full launch | a limited area → failure stays small | costs time; a rival can copy what works |
| **Segmentation** | target one group's needs → better product fit → loyalty | a clear segment → cheaper, sharper advertising | ignores other groups; tastes move between segments |
| **Mass marketing** | everyone → biggest possible volume → economies of scale | one campaign, one message → cheaper per customer | no one feels spoken to; rivals can niche around you |
| **Boston Matrix** | **dogs** → stop making them → cost removed | **stars** → invest → revenue growth in a growing market | labels are a snapshot; a "dog" can be a loss-leader |
| **Product life cycle** | extension (new packaging, new market, new promotion) → sales revive | a planned replacement → continuity of revenue | extension only delays decline; cost of repeated relaunches |
| **Branding** | recognised logo → trust → charge a premium | brand → new products sell faster under the same name | building a brand is expensive and slow; one scandal damages it |
| **Packaging** | protects the product → fewer breakages | shelf presence → noticed → bought | costs per unit and can be seen as waste |
| **Price skimming** | high launch price → early adopters pay → margin funds the next launch | signals quality | invites rivals in fast; late majority waits for the price to fall |
| **Penetration pricing** | low price → rapid market share → habit formed | volume → unit costs fall | low margin; customers may expect that price forever |
| **Cost-plus pricing** | cost + mark-up → every sale is profitable | simple to apply | ignores what customers will pay and what rivals charge |
| **Competitive pricing** | matches the market → customers do not switch on price | safe where products are similar | margin is set by rivals; a price war hurts everyone |
| **Promotional pricing / discount** | short-term volume → clears stock | attracts new customers to try | cheapens the brand; buyers wait for the next discount |
| **Above-the-line promotion** | mass media → large audience quickly → awareness | builds a brand image | expensive and hard to measure |
| **Below-the-line promotion** | targeted offer → measurable response | coupons/events → trial | time-intensive; reaches fewer people |
| **Sponsorship** | brand beside a known event/club → association → awareness | hospitality and tickets → relationships with clients | expensive; **bad results attach to you too** |
| **Public relations** | press coverage → credibility money cannot buy | manages a crisis → limits damage | you do not control the story |
| **Viral / social media** | free to post → reach far beyond local customers | shares → brand awareness at almost no cost | you cannot control negative comments; needs constant content |
| **E-newsletters** | direct to people who opted in → high relevance → repeat purchase | cheap per customer | ignored or filtered as spam |
| **Place — physical store** | customers see and try → impulse purchase | location → footfall | rent and rates; limited to those who travel |
| **Place — online / e-commerce** | sells to anyone → larger market | no shop rent → lower overheads | delivery and returns costs; no physical experience |
| **Customer satisfaction** | satisfied customer → repeat purchase → revenue without acquisition cost | recommends → word of mouth → free customers | satisfaction is not the same as sales; a rival can undercut |
| **Customer needs** | meet the need → chosen over rivals | unmet need → lost sale and a review | needs change; meeting one may raise cost |

## Theme 2 — People

| Topic | Chain 1 | Chain 2 | The "however" / condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wide span of control** | few managers → lower wage cost | fewer layers → faster decisions | less supervision → errors and uneven quality |
| **Narrow span** | close supervision → consistent quality | clear support for new staff | more managers → higher cost |
| **Delayering** | fewer layers → lower cost and quicker communication | more responsibility per manager | overload and losing promotion routes |
| **Centralised** | one policy → consistency and control | faster because one person decides | slow to react locally; demotivates branch managers |
| **Decentralised** | local managers decide → faster, better fit to local customers | responsibility → motivation | inconsistency; less control of cost |
| **Internal communication** | clear brief → staff know targets → fewer errors | feedback channels → problems surface early | too many channels → confusion; too few → rumour |
| **External communication** | with customers/suppliers → trust and repeat orders | with investors → access to finance | one bad public message is hard to retract |
| **Written channel** | a record → disputes settled | everyone gets the same words | no tone or feedback; can be ignored |
| **Face-to-face** | immediate feedback → misunderstanding fixed | builds relationships → motivation | no record; expensive in time |
| **Internal recruitment** | already knows the business → shorter induction | promotion → motivates others to stay | no new ideas; leaves a gap to fill |
| **External recruitment** | new skills and fresh ideas | a wider pool → better candidate | expensive and slower; risky fit |
| **CV / shortlisting** | evidence of experience → shortlist the plausible | cheaper than interviewing everyone | a CV can exaggerate; gaps need explaining |
| **Interview** | tests manner under pressure → how they behave with people | both sides ask questions → realistic expectations | nerves hide good candidates; bias |
| **On-the-job training** | real equipment, real customers → productive while learning | cheaper; no travel | bad habits passed on; mistakes cost money |
| **Off-the-job training** | expert teaching → new ideas and best practice | no disruption to production | expensive; no output while away |
| **Induction training** | safety and rules → fewer early accidents | expectations and culture → settles faster → less early leaving | costs supervisor time |
| **Full-time staff** | available all week → continuity for customers | committed to the business | fixed cost even in a quiet week |
| **Part-time staff** | hours match demand → cost falls when trade is quiet | flexible cover for peaks | less continuity; harder to train |
| **Temporary / casual** | called in when needed → no pay when not | covers seasonal peaks | no loyalty; training cost repeats; reliability |
| **Financial motivation — bonus / piece rate** | paid for output → more produced → productivity ↑ → unit cost ↓ | earnings ↑ → retention | **quality can fall**; rivalry between staff |
| **Commission** | paid on sales → more selling effort → revenue ↑ | self-motivating, no fixed cost | pressure selling damages reputation |
| **Fringe benefits** | extra reward → feels valued → retention | cheaper than a wage rise in some cases | does not motivate someone who needs cash |
| **Above-minimum wage** | attracts applicants → better choice | retention → lower recruitment cost | wage bill ↑ permanently |
| **Job rotation** | variety → less boredom → fewer leave | multi-skilled staff → cover absence | training time; not everyone wants change |
| **Job enrichment** | more responsibility → feels trusted → motivation | decisions made by the person closest to the work | not everyone wants more work; pay may matter more |
| **Retention / labour turnover** | low turnover → less recruitment and training cost | experience → better service | keeping the wrong person is a cost too |
| **Measuring employee satisfaction** | survey → early warning of problems → fix before staff leave | shows whether motivation spending worked | staff may not answer honestly |
| **Productivity** | training or technology → output per worker ↑ → unit cost ↓ | more output without more wages → margin ↑ | workload ↑ can demotivate; quality may drop |

## Theme 1 — Business activity, forms, growth, location, external factors

| Topic | Chain 1 | Chain 2 | The "however" / condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sole trader** | keeps all profit → full motivation | cheap and quick to start | **unlimited liability**; limited capital; alone |
| **Partnership** | shared capital → more to start with | shared skills and cover for illness | shared profit; unlimited liability; disagreements |
| **Private limited company** | sell shares to family/friends → capital without losing control | limited liability → owners' homes safe | accounts are public; shares not sold to the public |
| **Franchise — franchisee** | known brand → customers from day one | training and support → lower failure risk | fees and royalties → lower margin; less control |
| **Franchise — franchisor** | fees → income without running outlets | others' money → faster growth | a poor franchisee damages your brand |
| **Multinational** | lower labour/production costs abroad → lower unit cost | larger market → more sales | exchange rates, local laws, reputation risk |
| **Social enterprise** | grants and support → funding a profit-seeking rival cannot get | mission → motivated staff and community goodwill | profit is not the aim → investors are harder to attract |
| **Growth** | economies of scale → unit cost ↓ | market share → pricing power | diseconomies: communication and coordination costs rise |
| **Economies of scale** | bulk buying → cheaper materials per unit | fixed costs spread over more units → lower unit cost | only if the extra output can be sold |
| **Aims changing** | competition → focus on retention instead of growth | new owner or regulation → new objectives | objectives that are not written down cannot be measured |
| **Location** | near customers → footfall and repeat visits | near suppliers/labour → lower transport and wage costs | rent and competition for the site; online reduces its weight |
| **Business failure** | poor cash flow → cannot pay debts → closure | not adapting to the market → sales fall | failure can be a cash problem in a profitable business |
| **Interest rates up** | repayments ↑ → costs ↑ → price ↑ or margin ↓ | customers' disposable income ↓ → demand ↓ | fixed-rate debt delays it; a strong brand can absorb it |
| **Interest rates down** | borrowing cheaper → invest in capacity | customers spend more → demand ↑ | only helps if the business or its customers borrow |
| **Exchange rate — depreciation** | exports cheaper abroad → demand ↑ | imports dearer → costs ↑ | depends on whether it buys imported materials |
| **Tariff** | import cost ↑ → price ↑ → less competitive | may switch to domestic suppliers | a domestic supplier may be more expensive |
| **Legislation** | compliance → avoids fines and closure | safer/greener → reputation | cost of equipment and training; time |
| **Globalisation** | larger market → more customers | cheaper inputs from abroad | more competition at home |
| **Stakeholders** | employees → motivation → service | customers → repeat purchase | shareholders may want profit before either |

## Theme 5 — Operations

| Topic | Chain 1 | Chain 2 | The "however" / condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Job production** | unique to each customer → premium price | skilled staff → quality and pride | slow; high labour cost per unit |
| **Batch production** | batches → some scale, some variety | change the batch → respond to demand | stock waits between batches; machinery set-up time |
| **Flow production** | continuous line → very low unit cost | high volume → meets mass demand | expensive machinery; **inflexible if designs change** |
| **Technology / robotics** | consistent quality, 24 hours → unit cost ↓ | does dangerous/repetitive work → fewer accidents | large capital cost; job losses → motivation |
| **JIT stock control** | little stock held → storage cost ↓, less waste | cash not tied up in stock | **no buffer** → one late delivery stops production |
| **Quality control** | inspection at the end → faults stopped before sale | protects reputation | you pay for the fault before you find it |
| **TQM** | everyone responsible for quality → faults prevented → less waste | customer focus → repeat purchase | training and a culture change; slower at first |
| **Quality service** | good service → repeat booking and recommendation | staff training → consistency | expensive to staff at every level |
| **Suppliers** | reliable supplier → no production delays | cheaper supplier → lower unit cost | cheap can mean late or poor quality |
| **Resource efficiency / waste** | less waste → lower material cost | greener → reputation and legal compliance | equipment investment needed |
| **Health and safety** | compliance → no fines or closure | safe workplace → fewer accidents → less lost time | cost of equipment and training |
| **Productivity** | better method or machine → more output per worker | unit cost ↓ → competitive price | redundancies may demotivate those who remain |

## The eight sentences that fit almost anything

When you are stuck, these six shapes and two clauses work on any case. **Add the case's noun.**

1. *"This means **the business** [verb] more **its product** for the same cost, so unit cost falls and the margin rises."*
2. *"Because **its customers** [want/need X], doing this means they are more likely to **buy again**, which raises revenue without extra advertising."*
3. *"This would affect **the finance department**, which must [pay/fund/track] it, so cash is tied up for longer."*
4. *"A second reason is that **its staff** [would/would not] have to be trained, which costs time and money before any benefit arrives."*
5. *"However, this also means **the business** cannot [adapt / afford / supervise], so the benefit may be lost if **demand changes**."*
6. *"Overall, **it depends on whether [the case's own customer or number]** — because if it does not, the cost is certain and the benefit is not."*

## The condition bank — for the 9- and 12-markers

A condition must name **a fact that could only be written about this case.** Pick from:

- **a number in the case** — *"provided the £7 500 is genuinely available"*, *"if the 40 extra covers fill"*
- **a customer type** — *"if the wedding customers will pay a premium for a named baker"*
- **a product line** — *"if the vegan range sells as well as the existing menu"*
- **a staff fact** — *"with only three employees, one person cannot both train and produce"*
- **a competitor** — *"unless the rival on the same street cuts its price first"*
- **a time horizon** — *"in the short term the cost is certain; in the long term the reputation compounds"*

**Never:** *"it depends on the objectives"*, *"on how much it costs"*, *"on the economy"* — all
pasteable, all worth nothing.
