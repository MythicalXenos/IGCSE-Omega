## SKILL-CALC — quantitative skills and calculation method

**Practice — constructed, unscored.** Northstar Appliances is a large manufacturer. Use these figures: annual revenue £12,000,000; cost of sales £8,400,000; operating expenses £2,700,000; fixed costs for one product £15,000; selling price £50/unit; variable cost £30/unit. It makes 540 acceptable units in 24 labour-hours. It expects £28,000 cash in and £35,500 cash out next month; opening cash is £12,000. The exchange rate is £1=US$1.25. Calculate (a) gross profit and gross profit margin, (b) operating profit and margin, (c) break-even output, (d) productivity per labour-hour, (e) next-month net cash flow and closing balance, and (f) convert a US$2,500 import bill into pounds.

**Answer / recalculation.** (a) Gross profit = £12.0m−£8.4m=**£3.6m**; GPM=£3.6m/£12m×100=**30%**. (b) Operating profit=£3.6m−£2.7m=**£0.9m**; OPM=£0.9m/£12m×100=**7.5%**. (c) Contribution=£50−£30=£20/unit; BE=£15,000/£20=**750 units**. (d) 540/24=**22.5 acceptable units per labour-hour**. (e) Net cash flow=£28,000−£35,500=**−£7,500**; closing=£12,000−£7,500=**£4,500**. (f) US$2,500÷(US$1.25/£)=**£2,000**. Method checks: distinguish margin (profit/revenue) from markup (profit/cost); show units; retain negative sign for net outflow; closing balance = opening + net flow; divide, not multiply, when converting USD back to pounds. No rounded values are required here.

**Checked record:** all six results independently recalculated; formula/units and conversion direction checked. These constructed numbers are not Pearson figures.

## SKILL-INTERPRET — read data/documents and make evidence-based implications

**Practice — constructed, unscored.** Northstar’s year-on-year results: revenue £10m→£12m; cost of sales £6.4m→£8.4m; operating expenses £2.4m→£2.7m; customer complaints 400→620; employee turnover 18%→12%. Explain two changes and make a cautious overall judgement about performance.

**Answer / rationale.** Revenue rose 20% (£2m/£10m), but gross profit stayed at £3.6m (10−6.4; 12−8.4); GPM fell from 36% to 30%. Operating profit fell from £1.2m to £0.9m, a 25% decrease, and OPM fell from 12% to 7.5%. Complaints rose 55% (220/400), while turnover fell 6 percentage points. This is mixed: sales and staff retention indicators improved, but margins/profit and complaints worsened. The data do not prove why; investigate price/cost mix, product quality and complaint rate per sale, and consider the period/market before deciding. Rationale: compare like with like, calculate from the correct base, distinguish percentage-point changes from relative percentages, and avoid claiming causation from correlation.

## SKILL-AO1 — recall, select and communicate business knowledge

**Practice — constructed, unscored (short-answer rehearsal).** (a) Define market share. (b) State one non-financial method of motivation. (c) Name one internal economy of scale.

**Answer / rationale.** (a) The firm’s sales as a proportion of total market sales over the same period/measure (a formula may be given if asked). (b) Job enrichment, job rotation or autonomy. (c) Purchasing, technical, managerial, financial or risk-bearing economies arising within a growing business (one valid example). Use the relevant term accurately; do not substitute sales revenue for profit or “weak competition” for the specification’s reason “the business is not competitive.” These are constructed recall checks, not official items.

## SKILL-AO2 — apply knowledge/calculations to a case

**Practice — constructed, unscored (application rehearsal).** A 300-employee regional distributor has a 90-day customer-credit period, pays suppliers in 30 days and is considering an overdraft. Explain why the timing is relevant to this business and name one risk of relying on an overdraft.

**Answer / rationale.** The distributor may need to pay suppliers about 60 days before collecting the related customer cash, creating a working-capital timing gap despite sales/profit. A short-term overdraft may bridge it, but it can be costly, variable-rate or repayable on demand and is not a safe permanent substitute for long-term finance. Application comes from using the 300-employee context only where relevant and tying the 90/30-day terms to the business’s cash timing; merely repeating a case name is not reasoning.

## SKILL-AO3 — analyse with developed causal links

**Practice — constructed 6-mark-style analysis, unscored.** Northstar’s factory is experiencing rising packaging defects. Analyse two ways a TQM programme could affect the business.

**Answer / rationale.** First, training and worker-led root-cause checks could identify a poorly calibrated sealing machine; correcting it may reduce defective packs, which lowers rework/returns and protects Northstar’s operating margin. Second, involving suppliers in material standards could reduce inconsistent packaging inputs; fewer line stoppages may raise output per labour-hour and improve delivery reliability. These outcomes depend on training/time/cost and management follow-through; a TQM label alone does not guarantee improvement. The answer builds cause → operational effect → business outcome in Northstar’s case. This is not a Pearson-marked response; the tariff-style prompt only rehearses extended analysis.

## SKILL-AO4 — evaluate, justify and reach a supported judgement

**9-mark-style practice — constructed, unscored.** Northstar must reduce complaints. It can either (A) train customer-service staff to resolve issues or (B) introduce a discount voucher for every complaint. Justify which option it should choose, using a condition that could change the decision.

**Answer / rationale.** Prefer training first because it addresses the handling process and may solve problems at the first contact, rebuilding trust without giving every complainant a price reduction. A voucher could provide immediate goodwill, but repeated compensation raises cost and does not fix the product/service cause; it may also reward avoidable complaints. The decision depends on complaint analysis: if a verified product defect is widespread, fix production/quality before either service-only action; if complaints concern one-off delays, a limited voucher may help. A supported choice compares alternatives and makes the condition explicit. Constructed; no score/mark prediction.

**12-mark-style practice — constructed, unscored.** Northstar is deciding whether to launch a premium energy-efficient appliance nationally now or test it in two regions first. Evaluate which approach is better, using the case evidence: market research finds 62% stated interest among 500 surveyed customers; the existing model has a 30% gross profit margin; the new product requires £2m development and has no actual sales data. Reach a justified conclusion and state what evidence could change it.

**Answer / rationale.** A national launch could capture demand quickly and exploit the stated interest, and a 30% margin on the existing model suggests the business has experience with profitable appliances. However, stated interest is not purchases; the sample’s representativeness and willingness to pay are unknown, and £2m development plus national stock/marketing creates exposure before actual demand is proven. A two-region test costs time and may allow competitors to move, but gives sales, price, reliability and service evidence and limits initial inventory risk. On these facts, test first with clear success measures (conversion, contribution after launch costs, returns and repeat demand), then scale if thresholds are met. A rapid full launch becomes more persuasive if reliable pre-orders, representative willingness-to-pay data and production capacity demonstrate demand and cash can cover the £2m. This judgement weighs short-term opportunity against uncertainty and links a condition to the decision; it is constructed, not an official indicative answer or candidate score.

**Checked record.** The 9-mark rehearsal compares options and gives a contextual condition; the 12-mark rehearsal develops both sides, interprets evidence limits and concludes with decision criteria. Neither imposes a universal paragraph count/AO quota. Official calibration remains bounded to the actual May 2023 scripts/items described in the source review (P2 Q3(e): 5/9; Q4(c): 10/12), not these constructed answers.

## SKILL-EXAM — command words, calculator, context and mixed demands

**Practice — constructed, unscored.** For each command, say what response is being requested: (a) state one advantage; (b) define “delegation”; (c) explain one effect of a price rise on an importer; (d) calculate a percentage change; (e) analyse an option; (f) justify a choice; (g) evaluate a strategy. Then answer (c) for a retailer importing $150 components when $1=£0.80 and the rate changes to $1=£0.90.

**Answer / rationale.** State = give a concise relevant fact; define = give the meaning; explain = link a reason/effect; calculate = select correct data/formula, show method and result with units/rounding as asked; analyse = develop linked consequences; justify = recommend and support a choice; evaluate = weigh evidence/alternatives and reach a supported conclusion. Import cost initially = $150×£0.80/$=£120; later = $150×£0.90/$=£135; increase £15, or £15/£120×100=**12.5%**. The pound weakened in this quote, raising the retailer’s import cost if other terms remain unchanged. Check calculator entry, denominator (original value for percentage change), units and command word; adapt depth to marks and the item-specific MS rather than memorising a universal answer shape.

**Checked record.** This practice spans command-word families, calculator use, case application, interpretation and extended response. The course papers allow calculators; use the question’s supplied data/formulae and the matching mark scheme. Constructed, unscored.
