# Session plan — Business, 17:00

**Purpose of this file:** so that the teaching hour is spent teaching, not deciding what to teach.
It is written for me (the agent), not for the student. Everything in it is already banked; every
claim it relies on is cited to a file that has been checked.

---

## 0. The three constraints the session is built around

1. **Target is 9, not "better".** `12_Grade_9_Marks_Budget.md`: 133/160, **27 marks may be lost**, and
   **AO4 is only 18 of 160**. The target is therefore *discipline across the whole paper*, not deeper
   evaluation.
2. **The biggest measured loss in the archive is a reading failure, not a knowledge failure.** The
   **0/9** on June 2025 P1R Q2(f) contains context, chains, balance and a decision — and scores zero
   because it answers *"should CCT pursue satisfaction?"* to a question about *"measuring success"*.
   **Nine marks.** Everything else in the bank costs one or two.
3. **He writes; I do not write for him.** No finished sentences before he has produced his own; no
   micro-drills; no unexplained vocabulary; no shorthand. He is willing to write, so the session is
   **four short writes and one long write**, not a lecture.

## 2. The five blocks, in order

### Block 1 — THE FRAME (20 min) · the nine-mark lesson

**The idea:** *the stem's noun tells you what the answer is about; its verb tells you what to do.*
A measure is a thing you look at; an option is a thing you choose.

**Sequence:**
1. Read him the Q2(f) stem only: *"The owner of CCT wants to measure the success of the business.
   The owner is considering two options: Option 1: customer satisfaction · Option 2: profit. Justify
   which **one** of these two options the owner of CCT should choose."*
2. **He writes** two opening sentences: one for a candidate who thinks the question is *"which option
   should CCT pursue"*, one for a candidate who knows it is *"which option measures success"*.
3. Then read the real **0/9** opening — *"By satisfying customer's, CCT will increase customer
   loyalty"* — and Pearson's verdict: *"it doesn't link them to a measure of success… 0 marks."*
4. Then the **8/9**'s two measuring sentences: *"customer satisfaction is difficult to measure as all
   tourists will have varying levels of satisfaction"*; *"profit doesn't indicate if CCT is growing"*.

**Exit criterion (his words, not mine):** he can state the difference between *"is this a good thing
to do"* and *"is this a good yardstick"* — **and** point at which sentence in his own opening does the
yardstick work. **If he cannot, block 1 repeats; nothing after it is worth doing.**

⚠️ **Do not say "read the question carefully".** That is advice he already has and it has not worked.
The transferable move is mechanical: **underline the noun after "measure / evaluate / assess" before
writing anything.**

---

### Block 2 — THE COMMAND WORD → THE SHAPE (15 min) · five shapes, no new content

**The idea:** the tariff fixes the shape; the topic does not. He needs five shapes, not 38 papers.

| Tariff | Shape | What is NOT in it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Define | what it is, in the scheme's three elements | examples, the word itself |
| 1 State | one fact **with a CCT noun in the sentence** | a definition |
| 3 Explain | one chain: cause → effect → **business outcome** | a definition (*"no marks are awarded for a definition"*) |
| 6 Analyse | two or more chains, context throughout | **any judgement — there is no AO4** |
| 9 Justify | **choose**, chains for the choice, the drawback of the choice, decision + condition | knowledge of both options (*"no knowledge marks"*) |
| 12 Evaluate | 3/3/3/3 — both sides **and** a weighing decision | a summary of what was already said |

**Verified against all 988 items** (`15_Tariff_Fingerprint.md`): 3-mark is AO1 only, **6-mark has no AO1 and no AO4**, 9-mark has no AO1, 12-mark is the only one that pays for knowledge. **If a student asks "why no conclusion on a 6-marker?" — that table is the answer, not an opinion.**

**He writes** one sentence per shape, on CCT topics from the same paper — 3(e) (selling assets v
retained profit), 1(g) (the app), 4(b) (technology), 4(c) (interest rates), 1(d) (secondary research),
2(b) (satisfying customer needs). Six sentences, six shapes.

**Exit criterion:** each sentence has the right *shape*, not the right *content*. I check shape only.

---

### Block 3 — CONTEXT, AND THE NAME TRAP (10 min)

**The idea:** context is **the business's own nouns** — its people, products, places, numbers — never
its name. Pearson: *"The name of the business does not provide context."*

**He writes:** fix three supplied sentences by replacing the business name with a real noun.
- *"CCT should use customer satisfaction because it is a well-known business."* → what noun belongs?
- *"This will increase CCT's profit."* → which customer, which tour, which cost?
- *"CCT could use secondary research."* → which source, about what?

**Exit criterion:** he produces *"reviews on TripAdvisor for the Shanghai bicycle tours"*, not *"CCT's
reviews"*.

---

### Block 4 — THE LONG WRITE (30 min) · one 12-marker, timed

**Task:** June 2025 P1R **Q4(c)** — *"Evaluate the impact on CCT of an increase in the Chinese interest
rate. You should use the information provided as well as your own knowledge of business."* (12)

- **13½ minutes** on the clock (1.125 min per mark — `13_Paper_Craft_and_Timing.md`).
- He writes it **without seeing the 11/12 or the 10/12**.
- Then we read the two real scripts and Pearson's two comments side by side: the **10** is told it
  *"uses the 'it depends rule'"* and asked to use it *"earlier and throughout"*; the **11** is praised
  for a *"clear judgement"* and asked for *"more sophisticated application"*. **The 10 scores lower
  than the 11 while being the one that used the phrase.** The phrase is not the mark.
- Then **he re-writes only his last paragraph**, to add the condition — not the whole answer.

**Exit criterion:** his conclusion **names the circumstance** in which the opposite verdict would be
right, and it is a circumstance from CCT's own case, not *"it depends on the objectives"*.

#### Block 4a — IF HE ASKS "WHAT DOES A 12 LOOK LIKE?" (5 min, only if asked)

**All four published 12/12s are now in the bank** — `16_Four_Verified_Twelve_Markers.md`. Two of them
**open with a definition** (June 2024 PPS; June 2022 P1R Can Culleretes) and **two do not** (May 2023
Irsi; June 2022 P1 TBTS). **One of them never writes "it depends" anywhere** — the June 2022 P1 TBTS
script carries its judgement on *"In the short term… However, in the long term…"*. **So do not hand him
"it depends on X" as a formula.** The four share exactly one thing:

> **two sides, and a last word that decides — with the circumstance named.**

**Two sentences he can use, both from real 12/12s, and they are different shapes on purpose:**
- *"This depends on how much the television time costs and the quality of market research."* (PPS)
- *"In the short term, breakages in reliable communication may not significantly impact TBTS… However,
  in the long term, [it] may cause irreparable damage to TBTS reputation and be the reason for its
  failure."* (TBTS)

**Ask him which one he would actually write tomorrow.** Either is worth 12. What is not is a summary
paragraph that repeats the body — that is the 10/12.

### Block 5 — THE CLOCK, AND THE TWO CHEAP MARKS (10 min)

1. **The clock:** 90 min / 80 marks = 1.125 min per mark; **22½ minutes a question**; the four 1-mark
   items in each question take a minute each. `13_Paper_Craft_and_Timing.md`.
2. **The two cheapest marks in the paper** (`12_` §4): a **calculation at the wrong d.p. loses the
   mark** even with correct working; a **"state" answer with no context scores 0**.
3. **He writes** the two calculations from the paper — 1(e) current ratio (2 d.p.) and 3(c) gross
   profit margin (2 d.p.) — and we check the **answer line only**.

**Exit criterion:** both answers to two decimal places, written as *x.xx* and not *x.x*.

## 3. What I must not do

- **Do not teach content.** He is not short of content; the archive's zeroes are all shape failures.
- **Do not give a model answer before he writes.** Model answers are for comparison, after.
- **Do not use the word "analysis" without an example.** Every term gets a sentence he can point at.
- **Do not claim a mark for his answer.** Only Pearson marks. I can say *"this fails the condition
  test"*, never *"this is an 8"*.
- **Do not present a constructed answer as a script.** Labels in `README.md` apply.
