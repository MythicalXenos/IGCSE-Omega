# Business activity and influences — checked teaching units

**Scope of this batch:** specification clauses 1.1.1–1.1.2 only (objectives). These two units do not complete Section 1 or the qualification. Official item references below are used as bounded practice/source checks; constructed tasks are labelled and have no invented score.

## 1.1.1 — Financial and non-financial objectives

**Requirement (Pearson specification §1.1.1, printed p.9):** financial aims/objectives — survival, profit, sales, market share and financial security; non-financial aims/objectives — social objectives, personal satisfaction, challenge, independence and control.

**Teach it.** An objective is an outcome a business or owner is trying to achieve. Financial objectives concern financial performance or position: survival means continuing to operate; profit is revenue less total costs over a period; sales concerns the value or volume sold; market share is the business’s proportion of total market sales; financial security means a sufficiently secure financial position, such as reliable cash/reserves or manageable commitments. Do not confuse profit with cash flow: a profitable business can lack cash when bills fall due.

Non-financial objectives concern other desired outcomes: contributing to society, personal satisfaction, taking on a challenge, independence, or retaining control. The categories describe the aim, not a guarantee that aims are mutually exclusive. A social objective may also improve sales or reputation; an owner seeking control may accept lower growth or a different source of finance. Which aim takes priority depends on the organisation, owner, stage and circumstances. Do not assume every business has only one objective or that profit maximisation is always its sole goal.

**Official practice cross-check (identity matched, bounded):** May 2023 4BS1/02 Q1(a)(i), QP printed p.3, asks which option is a financial objective. The matching P2 MS PDF p.4 identifies C, survival; the distractors challenge, social objectives and personal satisfaction are non-financial. May 2023 4BS1/02R Q2(a), QP printed p.9, asks for a non-financial objective in the M&M context. The matching P2R MS PDF p.10 accepts contextualised environmental or school-support aims and rejects an answer not contextualised to M&M. These are one-mark recognition/application checks, not complete practice of every listed objective.

**Practice — constructed, unscored.** A small clothing manufacturer has made losses for two years. Its founder wants the business to keep operating, build a cash reserve, increase sales and market share, and eventually earn profit. The founder also wants to remain independent and retain control of major decisions, take pride in taking on the challenge of expanding a durable-garment range, and donate unsold usable clothing locally. (a) Classify each stated aim as financial or non-financial. (b) Explain one possible tension between two aims. (c) Recommend which aim should be the immediate priority and justify it from the case.

**Answer / marking rationale.** (a) Financial: survival, financial security (cash reserve), sales, market share and profit. Non-financial: independence and control (the founder wants to retain decision-making authority), personal satisfaction/pride, challenge (taking on range expansion), and the social objective (donation). (b) For example, donating usable garments may create collection/handling costs or reduce stock available for sale, potentially slowing the reserve/profit target; conversely, a credible social contribution could support reputation and later sales, so the outcome is not automatic. (c) Survival is a defensible immediate priority because two years of losses threaten continued operation; the founder should monitor cash availability as well as accounting profit. A different recommendation can be justified if it addresses the case evidence and explains a plausible consequence. Credit correct classification, causal explanation and contextual judgement; no numerical score is claimed.

**Checked record.** All ten specification examples are explicitly taught and tested: five financial and five non-financial. The constructed task’s potential tension is framed as conditional, not certain. The official samples were checked against the printed QP item and matching MS extracts after confirming paper identity from the covers; this is not a page-image audit of either complete paper.

## 1.1.2 — Why objectives change as a business evolves

**Requirement (Pearson specification §1.1.2, printed p.9):** objectives may change in response to market conditions, technology, performance, legislation and internal reasons.

**Teach it.** Objectives are not fixed promises. As the business and its circumstances evolve, managers or owners may revise priorities, targets or timescales. Explain the link: identify the change, show how it affects the business or its constraints/opportunities, then connect that effect to a plausible revised objective. The direction depends on the facts: new technology could enable growth but require investment; weaker performance could make survival more urgent; a law can raise compliance costs or create demand for compliant products. A changed objective is not inevitable merely because a factor exists.

- **Market conditions:** changing demand, competitors, customer preferences or input prices can make a previous sales or market-share target unrealistic or make survival/profit/security more urgent.
- **Technology:** a new production or sales technology can reduce costs, improve quality or open a channel; alternatively, implementation costs or disruption can alter priorities.
- **Performance:** actual results versus targets matter. Strong growth/profit may allow expansion or social aims; losses, weak cash generation or falling sales can shift attention to survival and financial security.
- **Legislation:** new or changed legal requirements may require compliance investment, alter products/processes or create opportunities; objectives may be revised to meet obligations while remaining viable.
- **Internal reasons:** owners’ values or personal circumstances, a change of ownership/management, revised strategy, resources or business stage may change what the organisation wants or can pursue.

These factors can overlap. Separate a reason from its consequence and avoid claiming that every legal, technological or market change must produce the same objective.

**Practice — constructed, unscored.** Meridian Foods is a fictional medium-sized producer. Its original objective was to grow market share by expanding low-cost packaging. Three years later: (1) a new competitor has taken customers; (2) automated packing equipment is now available; (3) recent profit is below forecast and cash is tight; (4) a new rule requires recyclable packaging; and (5) the founder has sold a majority stake to investors who want a stable return. For each numbered change, state one plausible way the objective could change and explain the link. Then recommend the first priority for the next year, using at least two case facts.

**Answer / marking rationale.** (1) Market conditions: lost customers may prompt a realistic customer-retention or recovery target rather than an unchanged expansion target. (2) Technology: automation could support a productivity/cost or quality objective, but only if investment and implementation are feasible. (3) Performance: below-target profit and tight cash make survival/financial security or restoring profit more urgent than rapid expansion. (4) Legislation: make legal compliance and recyclable packaging a required operating objective; the firm may also pursue a compliant-product sales target, but compliance cannot be treated as optional. (5) Internal reason: majority ownership/management priorities changed, so a stable-return objective may replace the founder’s independent growth preference. A defensible next-year recommendation prioritises compliance and cash security before aggressive market-share growth: non-compliance risks legal consequences, while tight cash limits capacity to fund expansion. Other rankings can earn credit if the response weighs relevant evidence and explains consequences. This is a model of reasoning, not official indicative content or a candidate response; unscored.

**Checked record.** Each of the five listed reasons is separately practised. The response treats the law as a constraint, not a discretionary aim, and treats investment effects as conditional. No sampled official question is presented as testing this whole list; this constructed case was checked against the specification’s five categories and the causal links in the answer were reviewed for plausibility.
