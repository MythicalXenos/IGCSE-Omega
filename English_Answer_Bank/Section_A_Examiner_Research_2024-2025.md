## Cross-report findings for Q3 and Q6 (10-mark analysis)

### What earns the analysis marks

- Answer **how** the writer presents the exact focus in the question. Do not substitute a summary of what happens or what the writer believes.
- Select a few apt, short references; explain the wording, pattern or structural choice; then connect its effect to the question’s focus and the writer’s perspective.
- Analyse the particular effect, not a stock response. “This engages the reader”, “makes the reader want to read on”, or “makes the text interesting” is not an explanation unless the candidate specifies *how* and *why*.
- Treat structure as evidence: shifts in tone, focus, pacing or narrative viewpoint can matter, but name only choices actually present in the text.
- Avoid “feature spotting”: a correctly named device earns little by itself. Do not call every “like/as” a simile or invent alliteration where the wording does not support it.
- Do not analyse the paper’s photograph or glossary as if it were a deliberate language/structural choice by the writer.
- Avoid long copied quotations and paragraph-by-paragraph retelling. Brief, embedded evidence leaves room for the candidate’s own analysis.

## Cross-report findings for Q7 (15-mark comparison)

- Compare **throughout**, with both texts in each paragraph. Two separate summaries plus a final “they are different” sentence is not equivalent.
- Compare the writers’ **ideas and perspectives**, not just the names of techniques. Explain what a similarity or difference reveals about their viewpoints, purpose, tone or presentation.
- Keep references balanced across the texts; brief, discriminating evidence is better than long quotation or an unsupported list.
- Useful comparison axes vary by paper: overall attitude; purpose and audience; tone; choice of examples; opening/ending; structural progression; or how each writer presents a shared theme.
- Avoid comparisons of punctuation for its own sake, the pictures, or superficial facts such as both texts using first person unless that feature is tied to a meaningful difference in perspective.
- Do not state which text you prefer. Do not leave Q7 blank: the June 2024 P1 report notes that some candidates did not answer it, pointing to time management as a likely issue. Regardless of the report wording, reserve enough time to attempt the 15-mark comparison.

## Retrieval questions: Q1, Q2, Q4 and Q5

Pearson repeatedly warns candidates to use the question’s stated line references and the correct text. Give the exact kind of information requested, with enough detail to make the point identifiable, but do not write several alternatives for a one-mark item or copy a large passage. In two-mark retrieval, give two distinct, relevant points.

## Working rule for the learner

For Q3/Q6, use: **choice → short evidence → precise implication/effect → link to the question**. For Q7, use: **Text One’s choice/view + Text Two’s choice/view → explain the contrast or similarity → explain what it reveals about both perspectives**. If the sentence would still fit almost any text, make the analysis more specific.
