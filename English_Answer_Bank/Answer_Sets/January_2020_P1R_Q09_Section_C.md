## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed discursion

> **Which kind of need?**
>
> The sentence is true in three different ways and false in a fourth, so the honest answer is to sort them out before agreeing with any of it.
>
> Take the plainest kind of need first, the practical one. Nobody fills in a hospital form, appeals a school decision or gets a passport without somebody explaining it, and the person who explains it is usually not paid to. We build organisations for the same reason at a larger scale: a cooperative exists because forty farmers can afford a cold store and one cannot. That is not sentiment. It is arithmetic, and it is why the statement is so hard to argue with: strip away other people and most of us cannot do the paperwork of being alive.
>
> Then there is the second kind, the one the statement is really about. Family units offer support, friends are essential to our well-being, and I do not think that is a soft claim; I think it is a medical one. A person with three people who would notice if they stopped answering their phone is a different animal from a person with none, and no amount of self-reliance changes it.
>
> But now the fourth case, where I start to disagree. Some people on their own are more resourceful. I know a man who lives alone by choice, runs his own workshop, and is not lonely in the slightest; the solitude is not a wound he is hiding, it is a workshop he built. The statement, read strictly, tells him he is deficient. And there is the other side the MS quietly admits: friends can take up too much time, friendships can be problematical. Anyone who has spent a year managing somebody else's crisis knows that company has a cost, and pretending otherwise is how people end up exhausted and polite.
>
> So where does that leave me? Agreeing, but about something narrower than the sentence says. People need *some* people — not all people, and not all the time. The need is real; the supply is choosable. We are herd animals who are also able to walk to the edge of the herd and back.
>
> And that distinction is not academic. It is the difference between a person who is alone for a season and a person who is abandoned, between choosing your company and having none. The statement collapses those into one, and the collapse is comforting, and it is wrong.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Positions that score:** agree fully on practical and emotional grounds; agree with a distinction between need and preference (as here); disagree by defending self-reliance; agree but concede the cost of company.
- **MS material to use:** humans as herd animals forming groups; organisations built to help each other; cooperation needed for success; family support; friends essential to well-being; some people alone are more resourceful; friends can take too much time; friendships can be problematical.
- **Support that works:** cooperation at scale (cooperatives, unions, crews); the health effects of isolation; the cost of being everybody's support; chosen solitude.
- **What to avoid:** a story instead of an argument; lifting ideas from the friendship texts; a list of “we need family, we need friends, we need love”; an unfocused ending.
