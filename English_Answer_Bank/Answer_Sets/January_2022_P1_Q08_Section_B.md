## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed website contribution

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from the QP bullets, the MS indicative content (printed p. 12) and this paper's report. **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **Six kinds of traveller, and the hotel that will ruin or rescue your week**
>
> Here's the thing nobody tells you when you book: there is no such thing as a good hotel. There is only a good hotel *for you*. I've slept in twenty-three of them in the last four years — some by choice, most by accident — and the pattern is always the same. The place that ruins one person's holiday is the exact place that saves another's.
>
> **First, work out which traveller you are.** Hotels are full of distinct species. There are the ones who want silence — usually older, usually well-off, usually booked in for a fortnight — and for them a lobby with piped music is an insult. There are the ones who want noise: students, backpackers, the twenty-somethings who will genuinely brag online about surviving a terrible hotel. There are people escaping something, and people who have problems at home and need a week where nobody asks. There are reviewers, who are a species of their own and should be treated as armed.
>
> **Now the good news.** Cleanliness beats grandeur every time; a spotless room in a plain building is worth more than a marble lobby with a stained bath. Good staff are the whole game — not fawning, just efficient and quiet, the kind who fix a thing before you've finished complaining about it. Security matters more than the brochure admits, especially if you're alone. And a view you didn't pay extra for is the best feeling in travel.
>
> **And the bad, which is easier to write.** Thin walls. Air conditioning that sounds like a small aircraft. A bar so dark and cramped that nobody stays in it, which the hotel then calls “intimate”. Breakfast that is a basket of sad bread. Locks that don't lock. Charges for wifi, charges for the safe, a “city view” that is a car park with a bin in it. And the deepest cut of all: a hotel that is simply wrong for you, so that you spend four nights feeling like an intruder in someone else's idea of a holiday.
>
> My rule, after twenty-three attempts: read the reviews for *who* loved it, not *whether* they did. If the people who loved it sound like you, book it. If they sound like your parents, don't.
>
> *Tell us the worst place you've stayed — and who it would have been perfect for. We'll publish the best replies next month.*
