## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed letter

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from the QP bullets, the MS indicative content (printed p. 13) and this paper's report. **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **Dear Maya,**
>
> First — congratulations on finally booking it. I know how long you've been talking about this trip, and I also know that the reason you keep texting me at midnight is that you cannot decide how to get there. So here is everything I know, arranged honestly, because you are my friend and I would rather you were annoyed with me now than stranded in a bus station in three weeks.
>
> **Trains first, because I think they win.** The great advantage is that you do nothing. You book a seat, you turn up, you get on, and four hours later you are somewhere else having read ninety pages of a book. No route planning, no tolls, no argument with a satnav. You get the views that cars can't reach — the good bridges, the long valleys — and you can look at them instead of at the back of a lorry. It is also, let's be honest, the least damaging way to do this.
>
> **Buses are the cheap option**, and cheaper than you think if you book early, which you should do tonight rather than in the manner you usually do things. Modern ones have wifi and charging points, so you can work. The cost is time: they are slower, they stop everywhere, and the person behind you may have brought a chicken. In exchange you will see small towns you'd never have detoured to, and some of them are lovely.
>
> **Cycling is the one I would choose**, and the one you should only choose if you are honest about the training. It takes you off the road entirely — footpaths, tracks, forest — and it is the only way I know of arriving somewhere feeling that you earned it. It is also tiring, it rains, and you cannot bring the bag you have already packed.
>
> **The negatives, in one paragraph.** Journeys take longer than the timetable claims. Roads and bridges are not always in good repair, and some routes are genuinely not safe after dark — the advice I was given on a trip like yours was simple: do not travel at night, and do not argue with that advice. Some services are run down and uncomfortable. Several need booking weeks ahead. Cycling needs a level of fitness you have, but not yet.
>
> **What you will actually remember** is not the transport. It is the villages every ten miles, the traders on the verge, the noise of a town arriving at you all at once, the neon, the traffic, the sunset you will photograph badly. That is the trip. The seat is just how you get to it.
>
> Write back and tell me which one you've picked, and let me know the dates — I may come with you.
>
> **All my love,**
> **Jess**

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Transport types:** train, bus, bicycle, car, plane, ferry, coach, motorbike, or a combination with a change in the middle.
- **Structures:** options reviewed one by one with a recommendation (as here); a costed comparison table described in prose; advice organised by the three bullets.
