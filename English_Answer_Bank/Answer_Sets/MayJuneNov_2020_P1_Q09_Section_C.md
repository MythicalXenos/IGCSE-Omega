> **A plan is a lantern, not a map**
>
> The sentence sounds like advice from a parent, and like most advice from parents it is roughly right and badly worded. The problem is the word *future*. You cannot plan a future. You can only plan a next step, and then another one, and the argument about whether that counts as planning is where this question gets interesting.
>
> Begin with the case for. A plan is how an intention becomes a Thursday. Students have to decide what to study, and the ones who decide early get the qualifications that open the doors they later want; a plan helps you focus on what matters and, more importantly, helps you notice when you have stopped doing it. There is a reason every serious organisation writes things down. The plan is not a prophecy; it is a device for keeping your attention in one place long enough for something to happen.
>
> But the case against is not laziness, and it deserves better than it usually gets. A definite plan can stop you taking alternative opportunities, and this is the cost that the statement never mentions. I have watched a student refuse a placement she would have loved because it did not fit the five-year diagram she had drawn at fifteen. The diagram was not wrong. It was just older than she was. There is also the plain fact that we do not know what the future will bring; the last few years taught that lesson to everybody at once, and a pandemic does not consult the timetable.
>
> And then there are the people who go with the flow and are still successful. I do not think they are unplanned; I think they are planned differently — they keep the destination loose and the standards tight, which is a more sophisticated skill than filling in a form.
>
> So where do I come out? In favour, but of a smaller thing than the statement claims. Everyone should have a next step and a reason for it. Nobody should have a map they are afraid to redraw. The word I would keep from the sentence is *important*, and the word I would argue with is *the future*, because the future is the one thing you cannot be early for.
>
> Plan the term. Plan the money. Plan what you would do if the plan fails — that is the one most people skip, and it is the one that separates planning from wishing.
