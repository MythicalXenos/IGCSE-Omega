## Matching MS criteria

**Indicative content (MS p. 17):** No audience is specified, so the examiner is assumed to be the audience. Candidates may agree or disagree and may use a variety of arguments; the indicative points are not exhaustive and examiners should be open to a wide range of interpretations. Examples include:

- exercise is important because many people do not get enough;
- outdoor exercise, including walking, is available to nearly everyone and may be relaxing;
- insufficient exercise can contribute to obesity and unhappiness;
- indoor exercise may be just as beneficial;
- some people cannot get outside, and not everywhere has fresh air;
- there are many other ways people can be happy.

**AO4 — 20 marks, Questions 9, 10 and 11 (MS p. 18):** *Communicate effectively and imaginatively, adapting form, tone and register of writing for specific purposes and audiences.*

- **Level 3 (9–12):** communicates clearly; clear sense of purpose and understanding of the expectations/requirements of the intended reader; appropriate use of form, tone and register.
- **Level 4 (13–16):** communicates successfully; a secure realisation of purpose and the expectations/requirements of the intended reader; effective use of form, tone and register.
- **Level 5 (17–20):** communication is perceptive and subtle; task is sharply focused on purpose and the expectations/requirements of the intended reader; sophisticated use of form, tone and register.

**AO5 — 10 marks, Questions 9, 10 and 11 (MS p. 19):** *Write clearly, using a range of vocabulary and sentence structures, with appropriate paragraphing and accurate spelling, grammar and punctuation.*

- **Level 3 (5–6):** develops and connects appropriate information and ideas; structural and grammatical features and paragraphing make the meaning clear; uses a varied vocabulary and spells words containing irregular patterns correctly; uses accurate and varied punctuation, adapting sentence structures as appropriate.
- **Level 4 (7–8):** manages information and ideas, with structural and grammatical features used cohesively and deliberately across the text; uses a wide, selective vocabulary with only occasional spelling errors; positions a range of punctuation for clarity, managing sentence structures for deliberate effect.
- **Level 5 (9–10):** manipulates complex ideas, utilising a range of structural and grammatical features to support coherence and cohesion; uses extensive vocabulary strategically with rare spelling errors that do not detract from overall meaning; punctuates writing with accuracy to aid emphasis and precision, using a range of sentence structures accurately and selectively to achieve particular effects.

The candidate's 11/20 and 6/10 therefore sit one band below Level 4 in both objectives; the published levels and marks are Pearson's, not an inference from this grid.
