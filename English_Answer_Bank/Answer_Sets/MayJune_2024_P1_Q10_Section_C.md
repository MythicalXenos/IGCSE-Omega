## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 17), the AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 18–19) and this paper's own report (pp. 50–53). **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The graft**
>
> The knife is small and very sharp and my grandfather holds it the way other people hold a pen. He is showing me how to take a scion — a stick, basically, cut from last year's growth — and marry it to a tree that has already decided what it wants to be.
>
> ‘You have to line up the green bits,’ he says. ‘The cambium. If they don't touch, it won't take.’
>
> I am fifteen and I have come here because my mother said he shouldn't be lifting things. He lifts things anyway. The allotment is eleven metres by thirty and he has been on the waiting list for it since before I was born, which is a fact I like more than I should.
>
> He makes the cut. He does it in one movement, and the wood gives a small clean sound, like a page turning.
>
> ‘There,’ he says. ‘That's next September's apple.’
>
> I look at it. It is two sticks held together with tape.
>
> * * *
>
> I am twenty-six and the tape has been gone for years. The join has healed into a lumpy seam, the colour of a knuckle, and the tree is leaning the way old trees lean. Every September it produces a fruit that is not quite either variety: too sharp to eat off the branch, perfect in a pie.
>
> My grandfather died in the March after the graft. He never ate one.
>
> I come here on Sundays because the council keeps threatening to turn the site into flats and somebody has to be seen. I cut the grass badly. I fix the shed door. I sit on the bench with a flask and do nothing for an hour, which is what he did, and which I used to think was the most boring thing a person could choose to do.
>
> * * *
>
> Last week a boy from the plot next door asked me what I was doing with the knife. He is nine or ten, and he had the same look I must have had — the polite face you wear when an adult is being slow about something.
>
> So I showed him. A scion, a slit, the green bits touching. I told him he would not eat from it and that he would have to remember where it was.
>
> He said, ‘Why bother, then?’
>
> Because it is the only thing I have ever made that keeps going without me, and because the whole of the future is just that: a cut, a join, and somebody younger standing in the wet with a knife, being told where to put the green bits.
>

*Word count: 436 words of body text (438 including the title line).*

## Craft map — how the story meets the descriptors

| Element | Where | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|
| Flashback as the whole structure | Present-day graft, then a bracketed time-shift to twenty-six, then back | Report: successful candidates used “flashbacks”; examiner note: “the candidate uses flashbacks and variety of sentence and paragraph lengths for effect” |
| Deliberate short sentences | “He makes the cut.” / “I cut the grass badly.” / “He never ate one.” | The 27/30 script is praised specifically for “very short sentences for effect” |
| Wide sentence-length range | Paragraphs of one line next to long accumulating sentences in ¶8 and the close | AO5 9/10 was lost for want of “range”; this is the targeted fix |
| Few characters, well developed | Grandfather, narrator, the neighbouring boy | Report: “fewer characters which were well-developed and a well-thought-out plot” |
| Refuses the sci-fi default | No flying cars, no collapse, no apocalypse | Report: those plots “sometimes struggled to maintain focus on the title” |
| Title withheld, then earned | Last paragraph names the future without quoting the title as a first line | MS: explicit reference may be deferred to the end; report warns against an “afterthought” |
| Time is the subject, not the setting | A graft that fruits after the grafter's death | “The Future” answered by structure rather than by an invented year |
| Figurative language used sparingly and precisely | “like a page turning”; the seam “the colour of a knuckle” | Report: “figurative techniques… deliberately to achieve particular effects” |

| Punctuation variety | Bracketed section breaks, colon list in the close, semicolons, single inverted commas throughout | AO5 L5: punctuation and structure “for emphasis and precision” — and the range the marker wanted |

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Readings of the title (report's own list):** waking in the future; time travel; fantasy or sci-fi; environmental dystopia; future sporting success; taking control of one's own future; a life turned around by an event; crime or relationships with future consequences.
- **Time structures:** straight chronology with a forward-looking close; a flashback frame (as here); a single day that implies decades; a letter to the future self.
- **What to avoid:** gore for its own sake; a plot borrowed from a film or game; more than three characters; a long build to an unrelated twist; the title as the first line and then explained; a “learned” story bent to fit.
- **Length:** approximately 400 words. The 27/30 exemplar runs longer; control, not volume, is what is rewarded.
