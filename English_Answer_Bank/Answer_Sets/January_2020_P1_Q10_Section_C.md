## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

> **The Opportunity**
>
> The night porter found me in the corridor at eleven with a mop and a bucket, and he said, *You speak Somali?*
>
> I said, *Enough.*
>
> He said, *Bed four. His daughter is on the phone from Nairobi and nobody on this ward can talk to her.*
>
> I am not a nurse. I clean the wards at night because it pays better than the market and because the hours let me study in the day, and in two years nobody had ever asked me for anything except more bin bags. I put the mop against the wall and followed him, and I remember being annoyed, and I remember the corridor floor was still wet behind me and shining.
>
> Bed four was a man of about sixty with a fractured hip and a language nobody on the ward spoke. The phone was held to his ear by a nurse who had been standing there for four minutes, saying, *Sir? Sir?* in English, louder each time, as though volume were a language.
>
> I took the phone. The daughter was frightened and talking fast: was he eating, had they operated, was anyone with him, had anyone told him about the funeral on Saturday. And I translated, and then something happened that I have never been able to explain properly. I did not translate word for word. I could hear what she actually needed to know, and I could see what he could actually bear, and I put the two things together in the space of one breath — so she was told that he was eating, and he was told that his brother had been buried properly, and neither of them was told anything that would keep them awake.
>
> It took nine minutes. At the end the man held my wrist, not my hand, and said something that in Somali is what you say to somebody who has carried something for you.
>
> Then I went back to the corridor and finished the floor.
>
> The following Thursday the ward sister stopped me by the linen cupboard with a form in her hand — a training post, hospital interpreter, part-time, funded, closing on Friday, and she had already filled in the top of it in her own handwriting. She said, *You did nine minutes of that job last week. Do you want to do it for a living?*
>
> I have been doing it for six years. I do not think of that night as the night I was discovered, because nobody discovered me; a porter asked a question in a corridor and I happened to be standing on a wet floor with a mop, and I said *enough*, which turned out to be the exact right amount. But it was the first time anyone had asked me for something other than more bin bags, and I have come to think that is all an opportunity really is: a question, asked at the moment when you are standing close enough to answer it.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Interpretations from the report:** sporting trials, adoption, career change, study abroad, performing success, missed opportunities — all legitimate; the report notes that missed opportunities produced some of the *better* narratives because they allowed original treatment.
- **Structures that keep momentum:** opportunity arrives mid-story and is acted on (as here); the opportunity is refused and the refusal costs something; two characters and one location; a single evening.
- **Ways to make the title deliberate:** have the protagonist do the thing before knowing it is the opportunity; let a second character name it; end on a sentence that reframes the earlier action.
- **What to avoid:** the opportunity appearing only in the last paragraph; a training montage; a kidnap escape; a talent-show plot; a borrowed film plot; a story spanning years; “then everything changed”.
