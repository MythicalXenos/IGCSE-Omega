## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed description

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 17), the AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 18–19) and this paper's own report (pp. 55–57). **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **Shanti, who runs the launderette**
>
> Shanti is seventy-one and does not look it, partly because she refuses to sit down. She wears the same two cardigans in rotation, both of them a green that has given up, and a watch her husband left her which she has never had repaired and which therefore tells a time that has not existed since 2008.
>
> The launderette is four machines and a folding table and a smell of hot cotton and old coins. She knows every customer's wash cycle, and she has opinions about all of them. She will tell you that the man in number two uses too much powder. She is right.
>
> Her kindness is not soft. She has a laminated list pinned next to the change machine — not a rota, a list of names and hospital appointments, in her own square handwriting. Mrs Adeyemi, Tuesday, 9.40, clinic three. The man from the flat above the chip shop, Thursday, whatever the van can do. When she takes them she does not make a performance of it; she locks up, puts the sign on the door, and comes back two hours later smelling faintly of antiseptic, and starts the machines again.
>
> Once I watched her deal with a boy who had put his school shirt in the wrong dryer and shrunk it. He was eleven, maybe, and terrified, and his mother was going to be home in an hour. Shanti took the shirt out, looked at it, said nothing, and then went into the back and came out with one of the spares she keeps in a plastic bag for exactly this. She had three. She had washed and folded them herself and put them in the bag, and she did not tell him that, and she did not tell his mother either.
>
> The effect of her is cumulative and hard to describe. People stay longer in that launderette than in any launderette in the city. They come in for a wash and end up sitting on the plastic chair for half an hour, telling her things. She listens the way other people rest — completely, without wanting to be somewhere else.
>
> What it has meant to me is smaller and more embarrassing: I have started noticing the people at the edges of rooms, because she always does, and because being noticed by her once, at the age of nine, with a lost glove, is the sort of thing a person spends the rest of their life trying to deserve.
>

| Physical description and “lifestyle or career” | ¶1–¶2 the launderette as her economy and her stage | Report: successful answers gave “physical description of the appearance and lifestyle or career of the kind person” |

| The **effect on others**, as the MS demands | ¶5 people staying longer, telling her things | MS: “the effect on others”; not merely the narrator's feelings |
| A final effect on the narrator, kept short | ¶6 one sentence of consequence | Keeps description dominant while the “meant to me” beat is present |

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **People (MS):** real (alive or dead) or imaginary — teachers, friends, family members, neighbours, colleagues, someone known only in a professional role (a bus driver, a pharmacist, a coach).
- **Kindness shown through:** strangers rather than intimates (the report's preference); money and time given; patience with someone difficult; work done anonymously.
- **Structures:** three scenes and an effect (as here); one continuous encounter; a person seen through the eyes of several others; the person described at their job.
- **What to avoid:** writing about kindness as an idea; a eulogy with no specifics; a narrative of one rescue with a beginning, middle and end; stacking adjectives; presenting the person as flawless — fallibility makes the kindness credible.

## Why this model targets the top bands — and what it does not claim

- **AO4:** a credible, particular portrait with a voice of its own (the narrator is mildly wry), three specific instances of kindness to strangers, and an effect that is shown in other people's behaviour before it is stated.
- **AO5:** adjective choice over adjective quantity, varied sentence lengths, and punctuation used for structure rather than decoration.
- **Not claimed:** not the 22/30 script; no wording comparison possible. The 22/30 record is used for its lesson that a credible portrait with good adjectives is Level 4 until something lands.
