> **The ten minutes before the door**
>
> The corridor outside the music room had a floor of green tiles and a ceiling of the kind that carries sound too well. I sat on a plastic chair with my case on my knees and listened to a girl I did not know play the second movement of something, badly, and beautifully, and stop.
>
> Applause. The scrape of a chair. Then my name, said twice, the second time with a question in it.
>
> I want to describe those ten minutes properly, because everything that was frightening happened in them and nothing at all happened in them.
>
> It began in the hands. Not shaking — that came later — but cold, and slightly slow, as though they belonged to someone who had been out in weather. My fingers left the handle of the case and went to the hem of my skirt and folded it, and unfolded it, and folded it again. I noticed I was doing it and stopped, and then noticed that I had started again.
>
> The mouth goes first, actually. Dry, in a way that water does not fix, and a taste like the inside of a coin. I swallowed twice and my ears clicked. Somewhere behind me, a radiator knocked, once, with the self-importance of a thing that has knocked before and knows it will be listened to.
>
> Then the hearing sharpened, which is the strange gift of it. I could hear the adjudicator's pen on the paper, a page turned, the small wet sound of someone in the row behind me breathing through their nose. I could hear my own pulse in the collar of my shirt. The room had become a place where every ordinary noise was being amplified for my benefit and against me.
>
> The worst part was the waiting itself. Not the fear of playing, but the not-yet-playing: the ten minutes in which I was still somebody who might fail, and could not get it over with.
>
> And then the door, and the light, and the long walk to the middle of a floor that was much bigger than it had any business being, and a piano that looked, from where I stood, like a piece of furniture that had been left in a field.
>
> I put the violin under my chin. The strings were cold. I lifted the bow, and it hovered, and the room inhaled.
>
> I have been nervous many times since — before interviews, before hospitals, before a telephone call I did not want to make — and it has never been quite the same. Those ten minutes taught me the shape of it: that nerves are almost entirely made of waiting, and that the waiting ends.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Occasions (report's list):** exam nerves; a new school; public performance; a sports fixture; a job interview; a first date.
- **Structures:** the minutes before an event (as here); the event itself described in slow motion; the aftermath and the realisation of growth.
- **Keeping it descriptive:** a fixed span of time, present or immediate past, and no plot beyond the threshold; the effect stated once at the end.
- **What to avoid:** a physiology essay with no occasion attached (“the pupils dilate, adrenaline is released”); a full narrative of the day; sentimentality about how brave the candidate was; the word “nervous” repeated as a substitute for showing it.

## Why this model targets the top bands — and what it does not claim

- **AO4:** the description is confined to a fixed ten minutes, which is what makes the tension structural; the effect is delivered in the last three lines without a moral, and the register is honest rather than brave.
