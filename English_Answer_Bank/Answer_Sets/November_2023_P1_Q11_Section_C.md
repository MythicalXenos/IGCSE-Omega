## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed description

> **The reading room on the second floor**
>
> The reading room is on the second floor of the central library, at the back, past the lift that has been out of order since the building was refurbished and therefore past everybody who has given up.
>
> It is a long room with a high ceiling and eleven tables. The tables are the kind that were made once and never again: pale wood, thick legs, a brass stamp in the corner from a workshop that closed in 1961. The chairs do not match. This is because, at some point in the last sixty years, eleven chairs were replaced one at a time by whoever happened to be in charge, so the room is a small museum of institutional decision-making, and the one nearest the window is a fold-out metal thing that creaks if you breathe on it.
>
> Light comes in from the south, in three windows, and by four o'clock it has crossed the room and landed on a specific shelf of bound newspapers, and the dust in that light is the entire point of the room. You can see it turning.
>
> Sound is what people notice first and remember longest. Not silence — silence is what the room is made of, but underneath it there is a particular orchestra: the enormous soft cough of a leather chair, a page turning in three stages, the radiator ticking like a cooling engine, and, from the floor below, the muffled, underwater noise of a librarian's trolley. Somebody at the seventh table is breathing heavily, in the way that people do when they are concentrating. Nobody is annoyed. Nobody looks up.
>
> There is a man who is always there. He is not my friend and I do not know his name. He wears a brown jacket in every weather and he reads with a pencil in his mouth, and in four years I have never once seen him look at his phone, because I do not think he has one.
>
> This is the place that inspires me, and it is not because of what is in the books. It is because I have watched, in that room, what a person's face looks like when they are thinking about something hard and nobody is watching them do it. The shelves go up eight feet and do not care who you are. There is nothing to be, in that room, except a person reading, and that is the whole of the freedom I want: eight feet of shelves, three windows, eleven tables, and an hour in which nobody needs an answer from you yet.
>

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Places (report's list):** a region, a city, a relative's house, a bedroom, a beach, a university, a school, a bookshop, a mosque, a garden, a gym or sports centre. Real or imaginary per the MS — though this report notes the *real* ones read as more authentic.
- **Structures:** a fixed survey of the room (as here); a place at one hour of the day; the place described through each sense in turn; the place as it looks empty and then full.
- **Where the inspiration goes:** inside the description, as the reason for what is noticed (safest); a single short closing movement (also acceptable); a final sentence that simply says “this place inspires me” (the failure mode this report names).
- **What to avoid:** the inspiring teacher/friend; the inspiring match or performance; a generic “beautiful place”; a short factual account; the appended inspiration paragraph.
