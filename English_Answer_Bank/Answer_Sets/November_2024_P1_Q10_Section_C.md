## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

> **Platform nine**
>
> The train is eleven minutes late, which gives me time to regret the whole thing. I have been regretting it since Birmingham. I have a coffee I do not want and a sign I decided not to make, and I am standing under the departures board watching the numbers rearrange themselves.
>
> Twelve years. That is the number I keep turning over. Twelve years is long enough that I do not know what his voice sounds like any more, only that it is lower than mine and that it used to go quiet when he was angry, and I have never met another person who does that.
>
> I spot him before he spots me, which is the one advantage of being early. He is fatter. That is the first thought, and it is an unkind one, and it is also relief: he is forty-one and his coat does not fit and he is looking at the platform number rather than at the people, the way he always did. He has the same bad posture. He stands like a question mark.
>
> Then he sees me, and does the thing he always did — half a wave, stopping halfway, as though he had remembered in time that enthusiasm is embarrassing.
>
> We do not hug. We say each other's names, which is strange, and then we both talk at once about the parking.
>
> The pub is four minutes away and we are there for two hours. He has two children. I knew this, from a card, from a mutual friend. It is different to be told it by him. He tells me about the youngest one's ears and the operation, and I find that I am angry, briefly and stupidly, that I was not told at the time — that he went through a hospital and I was in another city being right about something that no longer matters.
>
> That is the thing nobody warns you about. The old argument is still there, in the corner of the room, thinner than it was and much less interesting, and we both step around it politely and then, at the second drink, he says, ‘I was a nightmare about that,’ and I say, ‘You were,’ and he laughs, and it is finished. Twelve years, and it takes four words.
>
> He gets the 18.42. I wait with him because that is what you do. When the train comes in he says, ‘Same time next year,’ which is a joke, and then neither of us makes it a joke, and I write the date in my phone while he is getting on.
>
> And that was the reunion: not the argument repaired, or the twelve years got back, but two men on a platform agreeing to be people who might meet again.
>

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Reunion types the report lists:** school, friends, family gatherings, revisiting places, lost-and-found pets, and “unusual and individual topics”.
- **Emotional register:** warm; awkward (as here); disappointed; comic; quietly sad. The report allows happy endings and warns against gore and bodies.
- **Structures:** a train platform and a pub; a school hall; a WhatsApp group meeting for the first time; a reunion with a place rather than a person.
- **What to avoid:** a film or game plot; more than three characters; a long build with the title dropped in as a last word; melodrama (death, illness, violence); a learned story bent to fit.
