> **The boots**
>
> My favourite possession is a pair of football boots that are two sizes too big for the boy who now wears them, and they are not mine any more, which is part of why I keep them.
>
> They are black, or they were. What is left is a black that has been bleached to charcoal across the toes by two seasons of sun and washed to a pale grey at the heels where the leather has cracked into a fine net of lines, like the mud of a dried river. The right boot has a scuff the size of a thumbprint on the instep — from a tackle, in the rain, in a match I still think about — and the leather there has gone soft and slightly shiny, the way a hand does at the place it always holds a bag.
>
> The studs are the old kind, metal, six to a sole, and three of them are replacements, so they do not match: two are blunt and grey and original, one is bright and clearly newer than the rest. The laces have been changed so many times that the eyelets have begun to ream out oval. The tongue on the left boot is stitched with a thick white thread in a zigzag that is not a manufacturer's stitch — my mother did that, badly, on the night before a final, with the boot on her knee and the needle held the way you hold a needle when you have never been taught.
>
> And inside, in the heel of each boot, my brother's name is written in permanent marker, then mine underneath in a different hand, then, faintly, a third, which is my cousin's, and which has mostly worn away. Three names, one pair of boots, and a family that has never been able to afford to throw anything away that still held a foot.
>
> I have not played in them for two years. They live on the shelf above my desk, and they are the only object in this room that is not about the person I am now. They are about a wet Saturday in November, a final that we lost, a needle and white thread, and the fact that a thing can be mended and mended and keep its shape. I look at them when I think I have failed at something, and the scuff says: that happened, and you got up, and the studs still fit.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Objects:** a phone, a bike, a musical instrument, a watch, a camera, a piece of jewellery, a book, a tool, a games console, a football shirt. The MS also permits an **imaginary** possession.
- **Structures:** parts of the object in order (as here); the object in three conditions; the object measured against the body that uses it; the object at rest and the object in use.
- **How to carry significance:** wear, repair, inherited handwriting, a replacement part, a mark with a story attached — shown rather than explained.
- **What to avoid:** the shopping trip or birthday morning; the giver as the main subject; a page about feelings with the object offstage; “my phone, because it has everything on it” with no detail; interchangeable “special to me” paragraphs.
