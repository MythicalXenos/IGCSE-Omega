## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed discursion

> **Adventure, and the thing people mistake for it**
>
> “Be adventurous” is two words doing the work of an argument, so let me start by saying what I take it to mean. It cannot mean “take risks”. A person who drives too fast on a wet road is not adventurous; they are a hazard with a personality. Adventure, in the sense worth defending, is the willingness to accept uncertainty in exchange for experience — to go somewhere you cannot fully predict, and to be changed by the going.
>
> On that definition I agree with the statement, and I agree quite strongly — but only because I have excluded the version of it that gets people hurt.
>
> Consider what adventurousness actually buys. The unexpected is where most of the good material in a life comes from; you cannot plan to meet the person who changes your mind, or to find the thing you did not know you were looking for. Spontaneity creates opportunities that careful people never see, simply because careful people are not in the room where the opportunity happens. And there is a developmental case, which I take more seriously than the “only young once” line that everyone reaches for. Doing something difficult while uncertain is how a person finds out what they are like when they are not in control. That knowledge is not available any other way.
>
> But the case for caution is stronger than the adventurous usually admit. Planning is not the enemy of enjoyment; it is often the condition of it. A traveller with a booked bed enjoys the afternoon more than one who is watching the light go and calculating distances. Foresight exists because the world is genuinely dangerous in ordinary ways — bad roads, bad weather, people who mean harm — and because a decision made in three seconds is usually a decision made badly. Nobody who has sat in a hospital corridor wishes they had been more spontaneous on the motorway.
>
> So the interesting question is not whether to be adventurous but where to spend it. I would say: be adventurous about experience and conservative about consequences. Try the food, take the detour, say yes to the invitation, learn the thing you will probably be bad at. Do not be adventurous with your brakes, your savings, or another person's safety, because those are the areas where the downside does not teach you anything — it simply removes options.
>
> That is my qualified agreement, then. The statement is right about how to live and wrong if it is read as advice about how to drive. Be adventurous, by all means. Be adventurous in the direction of more life, not less, and know the difference before you set off.
