## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed description

> **The footbridge at the level crossing**
>
> The best place I know is a footbridge, and it is not beautiful in any way that would survive a photograph. It is galvanised steel, painted a green that has gone chalky, and it carries about forty people a day over two railway tracks between a scrapyard and a row of shops that sell tyres and mobile phone covers.
>
> You reach it by a ramp rather than stairs, which is why the cyclists use it, and the ramp has a handrail that is worn smooth in two places — one where hands go, and one lower down, where children's hands go. At the top there is a platform barely wider than a corridor, with a waist-high parapet, and if you stand at the eastern end you can see the whole length of the tracks: the signal box, the hump of the bridge, and, on a clear day, the cooling towers, which look like something out of a different country.
>
> The trains are the reason I go. There is a light on a pole that turns amber before a train comes, and a bell starts, and then the barriers come down with a sound like a slow, deliberate argument — clunk, clunk, clunk — and the traffic on both sides stops and the whole town holds its breath for forty seconds. I have never once got tired of standing above that. The train comes through underneath you with a violence you can feel in the handrail, and for about two seconds the bridge is the only place in the world where the noise is coming from.
>
> And then it is over. The barriers go up, the cars start, somebody's engine coughs, and the town resumes as though nothing had happened. That resumption is the part I love: the ordinary noise coming back on, the queue at the tyre place shifting forward, a man on a bicycle pushing off from the ramp.
>
> I go there when a day has gone badly, and I go there when it has gone well, and the place does the same thing either way. It puts me above something that is much bigger and much older than my week, and it does it without asking anything of me. Nobody has ever spoken to me on that bridge. Nothing there is anyone's, and nothing there is mine, and I have never had to be anything while standing on it — which is, I think, the closest description I have of feeling happy.
