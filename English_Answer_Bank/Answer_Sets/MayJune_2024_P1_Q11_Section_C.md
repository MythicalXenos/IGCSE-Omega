## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed description

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 17), the AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 18–19) and this paper's own report (pp. 57–59). **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The back room of Rafiq's Cycles**
>
> The untidiest place I know is the back room of Rafiq's Cycles, which is not a shop so much as a rumour of one, and which can only be entered sideways because of the wheels.
>
> There are perhaps ninety wheels. They hang from the ceiling on hooks, they lean in stacks against the wall like a pack of cards that has given up, and there is one — the front wheel of something old, with a wooden rim — that has been promoted to the shelf above the kettle and is dusted weekly. Everything else is a wheel. Grubby boxes of inner tubes are wedged under the bench in a way that suggests they are load-bearing. There is a coffee cup with a spanner in it and a spanner with a coffee ring on it.
>
> The smell is the part I would recognise blindfolded: rubber, chain oil, the sharp cold smell of a puncture repair, and underneath it, faintly, the toast that Rafiq's mother makes upstairs and brings down on a plate he then uses for ball bearings.
>
> The light is one bulb on a flex, and it does something generous to the mess: the chrome on the handlebars turns to a gold line, the spokes make a cage of shadows on the whitewashed wall, and the dust in the air becomes visible and almost festive, hanging in the bulb's heat.
>
> Sound: a radio that is always tuned slightly to the left of a station. The tick of a freewheel being spun and left to run down. Rafiq saying 'Bismillah' under his breath before he hits something with a mallet.
>
> The floor is the least tidy part and the most informative. There is a tide line of dark grit along the skirting where the wet bikes have dripped, a square of lino worn through to the boards by the same three footsteps, and, near the door, a patch of paint the exact shade of the frame that was being sprayed the week everything else stopped. Nothing has been swept since. The broom is there; it is simply also holding up a shelf.
>
> It is not a beautiful mess, like a teenager's bedroom or a beach with plastic on it. It is an ordered mess, which is worse and better. Rafiq can put a hand into a box he has not opened in a year and come out with the exact size of cable. The chaos is the filing system. To tidy this room would be to destroy it, and everybody who works here knows it — which is why the untidiest place I know is also the calmest, and why I go there when I cannot think.
>

*Word count: 444 words of body text (450 including the title line).*

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Places (MS):** houses, bedrooms, sheds, garages; schools, shopping malls, markets, parks, beaches, streets, “even rubbish tips”; real or imaginary. The report's own examples add a slum, a ruined building, a fish market, and (unusually) “the candidate's mind”.
- **Approaches:** a fixed viewpoint described in detail; a walk through the space that stops at three or four stations; the room through one sense at a time; the mess explained as a system (as here).
- **How much feeling:** the MS asks for place and atmosphere, so mood is welcome — but the report's successful answers kept the place dominant. One short reflective movement, as here, is enough.
- **What to avoid:** a sibling's bedroom cliché; a list of objects; a story about visiting the place; “disgusting!”, “gross!” and other unearned judgements; comic exaggeration that abandons description.

## Why this model targets the top bands — and what it does not claim

- **AO4:** a single, specific, sensory place with genuine atmosphere and a light comic register; the superlative in the question is answered with an argument about *kinds* of untidiness rather than with more mess.
- **AO5:** punctuation doing structural work in each paragraph, sentence lengths varied from four words to thirty, and vocabulary that is concrete rather than inflated.
- **Not claimed:** it is not the 17/30 script; that script is image-only and no wording comparison exists. The 17/30 record is used for its two lessons — Level 3 is where competent-but-un-crafted description stops, and errors alone can cost an AO5 mark.
