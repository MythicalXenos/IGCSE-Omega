## What the script does — and where the four missing marks went

These observations are mine, keyed to the published criteria and the question-level commentary; they are **not** Pearson commentary and carry no marks.

**Strengths the criteria recognise:**

- **It stays descriptive, not narrative.** Pearson's first complaint about the cohort is that “some candidates wrote narrative responses”; this script describes a person, his work and his character, and uses incident only as illustration (“causing them to roll their eyes”). That is the task kept in focus.
- **It answers “why this person”.** The MS asks for “descriptions of the person, how they have been inspirational or why they are important to them”. The script covers all three: physical description (black hair, “a funny moustache”, “neither too skinny nor fat”), professional significance (“iconic cartoons that modern day animators still look back to and admire”), and a personal link (watching the cartoons with his mother “in the precious days of my childhood”).
- **The opening is cinematic and the payoff is specific.** “Blazing, confident and inspired, a lone artist crafted his masterpiece in the confines of his studio” delays the name until the second paragraph, which creates the “perceptive and subtle” moment Pearson mentions. It works because the second paragraph then names him and the third drops to bathos (“Physically, he wasn’t a very notable man”).
- **Vocabulary is precise rather than merely ambitious.** “Confines”, “charismatic”, “medium”, “fluid”, “vitality”, “taboo”, “phased out”, “niche”, “unparalleled”, “reimagined” — the AO5 judgement (“extensive, accurate vocabulary”) is defensible on this evidence.
- **Two embedded quotations carry other voices** — the mother's “It was as if they were alive,” and a former colleague's “It was like the king was also the court's jester” — which is why Pearson calls the form and register “on occasion … sophisticated”. Quoting secondary witnesses is a genuine descriptive technique, not decoration, because each quotation adds a different angle on the man.
- **Punctuation is doing real work:** semicolons joining the character summary to the evidence (“charismatic and comedic; his cartoons had…”), the dash-free appositive in “A far-cry from the cuteness of ‘Mickey’”, the colon introducing the quotation, and the parenthesis-free aside “In the reality of his studio, however”.
- **A flaw is admitted rather than smoothed.** The “dark sense of humour” paragraph — references to “substances and other topics of taboo”, crude jokes — deliberately undercuts the saintly role model. That balance is what keeps the piece from reading as an advertisement, and it is the most likely source of “at times this is perceptive and subtle”.

**Weaknesses consistent with 17/20 and 9/10 rather than 20/20 and 10/10:**

- **Agreement and word-class slips.** “a unique flavour of ‘slap-stick’ and charming” (adjective in a noun slot); “was known of his life’s work” (for “known for”); “he was terribly genius in … terrible humour”; “other fans of him … seems to share” (plural subject, singular verb). These are exactly the “small number of errors” Pearson refers to, and at 9/10 they cost a mark without impeding meaning.
- **Tense inconsistency in reported speech.** “one of his former colleagues said, as they recount the time he performed…” — past report, present reporting verb, and singular/plural mismatched across “one … they”.
- **The personal connection is thin.** The childhood-memory paragraph is the strongest moment, but it is the only place the writer is actually present. The MS invites “why they are important to them”; most of the script is third-person appraisal, so the “role model” claim rests on asserted admiration (“I respect Walt Disney for his unparalleled creativity”) rather than demonstrated experience.
- **The ending overreaches.** “in ways that cannot be expressed in a reasonable amount of words, without creating something that will rival the Bible in terms of content, at least” is an inflated, tangled construction; the self-aware “at least” does not rescue it. Compare the neat, controlled final image of the P1 Q10 story — here the conclusion loses the precision the rest of the script earns.
- **Drafting is visible.** A whole clause is struck through on p. 54 and several words are replaced mid-sentence. This is not penalised, but it does mean the reproduced script is not a fair copy, and readers should not treat it as a polished model to imitate line by line.

## What 30/30 would have required

My reading of the grids, offered as a checklist rather than a rewrite.

- **AO4 (+3).** Make the personal link the spine rather than a paragraph: the strongest Q11 answers “detail their connection with the individual concerned” across the piece, not just once. Tighten the closing paragraph so the last sentence lands on the role model rather than on the writer's own cleverness.
- **AO5 (+1).** Proofread agreement and reported-speech tense, and remove the two or three constructions that are grammatically tangled even though their meaning is clear. The vocabulary is already at Level 5.
- **Nothing here needs more content.** The script runs to roughly 450 words against “approximately 400”, so the marks lost are control and focus, not coverage.
