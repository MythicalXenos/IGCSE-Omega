## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 17), the AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 18–19) and this paper's own report (pp. 49–51). **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **Two hours on a Sunday**
>
> The shop opens at seven and closes at seven, and my father has never once in nineteen years written a different number on the board. Khata, rice, cooking oil, phone credit, the kerosene that nobody buys any more but that he keeps on the third shelf because somebody might. I know the prices better than I know the periodic table.
>
> On Sundays it is mine. He sleeps in, and I take the keys, and by half past nine the whole street has been in: Mrs Fernandes for her milk and the news about her son, the boy from the garage for two samosas and nothing else, an old man who counts his coins twice and apologises for counting them twice.
>
> The fair was on the rec. I could hear it from behind the counter — the generator, the same four bars of a song on a loop, somebody's delighted screaming.
>
> At eleven I did something I had not planned. I turned the sign to CLOSED, put the money in the tin under the counter, and walked out with the keys still in my hand.
>
> The fair was smaller than it sounded. Two rides, a hook-a-duck, a man selling bright pink candyfloss out of a plastic bag. I bought nothing. I stood by the railing for a while and watched a girl in a yellow jacket win a small stuffed frog and then give it, immediately and without ceremony, to the child behind her, who had lost.
>
> That was all. I was back by half past twelve. My father was in the chair by the window with his tea, and he looked at the sign, and then at me, and said nothing at all.
>
> On Monday there were two ice creams in the freezer, the kind with the wooden spoon, which we do not stock. He had walked down to the shop on the corner to buy them, which he has not done since my mother died, because he does not like leaving the till.
>
> We ate them behind the counter at four o'clock with the door open. He said, ‘You should go out more.’ I said, ‘I know.’ We watched the street do nothing.
>
> He had not shouted. That was the thing I had been afraid of all morning, and it did not happen, and in the end that is what it was: not the fair, not the pink candyfloss, but a father deciding to hand back a set of keys he could have kept.
>

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Readings of the title (report's list):** escaping parental control; imprisonment; war; choosing a future parents reject; freedom after exams; even a tragic quest.
- **Structures:** a small domestic liberty (as here); a single journey with no arrival; a character's one decision, shown in retrospect; a place that changes hands.
- **What to avoid:** gore and violence; a film plot; more than three characters; heavy dialogue; a long build with a rushed resolution; “it was all a dream”; the title as the first line and then explained.
