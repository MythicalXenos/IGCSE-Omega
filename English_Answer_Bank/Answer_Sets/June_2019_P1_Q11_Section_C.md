## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed description

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 16) and AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 17–18), with method from this paper's own report and the 23/30 examiner notes (printed p. 48). **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The morning the dough came alive**
>
> For thirty-four years I could not make bread, and the strangest part of that sentence is that for most of those years I did not know it. I could cook. I could read a recipe and produce the thing the recipe described. What I could not do — what I had never once done — was make something that responded to me.
>
> The kitchen I learned in smelled of nothing. That was the first surprise: a room where bread is made does not smell of bread. It smells of flour, which is dust with ambitions, and of yeast, which smells like a warm cellar, and of the faint metallic sourness of the tap. On the counter, before I began, the ingredients were three separate facts. Flour in a bag. Water in a jug. Salt in a small dish, grey and coarse and crystalline, like the inside of a shell.
>
> What it is like not to have the skill is mainly a matter of hands. Mine were the wrong instrument. For the first ten minutes the dough was a wet grey shag, clinging to my fingers, climbing the heel of my palm, and every instinct I had said add flour, add flour, until I had added so much that the whole mass stiffened and went quiet under my hands like something embarrassed. That is what ignorance feels like at close range: a constant urge to correct a thing you do not yet understand.
>
> Then, in the second week — the fourth loaf, if anyone is counting — the change. The dough stopped being a lump and started being a body. It pushed back. It came away from the counter cleanly and made a sound when I turned it, a small tacky tearing sound, and under my palm I could feel an elastic resistance, warm, and not quite even, stiffer at one corner than the rest. I remember standing still with my hands on it, stunned, the way you are stunned when a piano note is finally in tune.
>
> It rose. That is the other thing nobody tells you: it happens in the dark, slowly, and there is nothing to watch. I kept lifting the tea towel as if the bowl might have news for me.
>
> My first three loaves were bricks. I am not being modest. They had the density of wet sand and the profile of a flattened ball, and when I tapped their bases they gave a dull, tight, honest thud, like a book being closed.
>
> What I learnt, in that plain kitchen over that grey February, was that the skill is not in the recipe. It is in noticing — reading a dough by hand, hearing the difference between a hollow knock and a dead one — and that noticing is slow, and quiet, and quite unlike knowing.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Skills (report's list):** any sport, driving, cycling, an instrument, a language or academic subject; a “life lesson”; and “a time when something had gone very wrong”.
- **Structures:** the before/process/after shape used here; the same skill described at three stages (clumsy, competent, fluent); the moment of failure described in detail and the modest recovery after it.
- **Keeping it descriptive:** present-tense or sensory past; the body and the senses as the main material; no sequence of events; the learning stated as an insight rather than a moral.
- **What to avoid:** a story of the day you learnt to swim with a plot; a general essay on “the importance of learning”; “and then I tried again and eventually I could do it”; a skill with no texture in it; a moral tacked on in the last line.

## Why this model targets the top bands — and what it does not claim

- **AO4:** all three moves the report names are present and separated, the register stays descriptive throughout, and the answer closes on “what it meant” — the MS's own third requirement — rather than on a final event.
