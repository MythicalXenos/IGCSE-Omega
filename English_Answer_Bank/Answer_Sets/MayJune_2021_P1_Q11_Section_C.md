> **The green three-wheeler**
>
> My favourite form of transport is a three-wheeled auto-rickshaw, and it is green in the way that a pond is green, which is to say not by design but by weather.
>
> The body is a shell of sheet metal over a frame, and it has been repainted so many times that the panels have a soft, slightly swollen look, like a bruise. The canopy is canvas, black, patched in two places with a lighter thread, and it sags in the middle where the rain collects and then releases all at once when the driver takes a corner. The windscreen is a single sheet of glass with a crack across the bottom corner that has been there long enough to have acquired a nickname, and the wipers are operated by a hand, not a motor: a lever beside the driver's knee, worked back and forth with the same rhythm as a pump.
>
> Inside there is a bench, thinly padded, that holds three people who do not know each other. There is a mirror the size of a postcard with a photograph of somebody's child wedged behind it. There are flowers — plastic, marigold-orange — wound around the grab handle, and a small brass bell that the driver rings at corners instead of using the horn, which is either superstition or courtesy and is probably both.
>
> But the reason it is my favourite is not the fittings. It is the sound and the shake. A three-wheeler does not travel so much as vibrate forward: the engine sits under the seat and sends its two-stroke rattle up through the bench and into your teeth, and at a standstill it idles like a small aeroplane waiting for permission. The smell is petrol and dust and, faintly, coconut oil from somebody's hair. When it turns, the whole shell leans, and everyone on the bench leans with it without discussing it, which is a small, unadvertised piece of cooperation that happens forty times a journey.
>
> A car would be quieter and a bus would be cheaper, and both would keep you at a distance from the street. The three-wheeler does not. It puts you at the height of a standing pedestrian, in the middle of the noise, with the hot air coming in sideways and the driver's elbow close enough to touch, and it makes the ordinary business of getting across a city feel like a shared event rather than a delay.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Forms of transport:** a bicycle, a bus, a train, a ferry, a plane, a motorcycle, a rickshaw, a lift, a boat — the MS also permits an **imaginary** form.
- **Structures:** the vehicle part by part and then its sensation (as here); the vehicle empty and then full; three journeys in three weathers; the vehicle compared to a car or bus.
- **Where the “positive effect” can sit:** the rhythm of the ride; the view; the company of strangers; the fact that it makes a familiar route strange.
- **What to avoid:** the holiday journey narrative; a page of timetables and routes; describing the destination; “my car because it gives me freedom” with no detail; interchangeable descriptions that could be of any vehicle.
