## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 18) and AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 19–20), with method from this paper's own report. **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The gap in the wall**
>
> The gap in the wall behind the mosque is not really a gap. It is where a gate used to be, and the gate has been gone for longer than either of us has been alive, and the bricks have worn into a shallow V that a person can get through if they turn sideways and do not mind their coat.
>
> My sister is eleven and has decided that we are late, although we are not late, and that therefore we must go through the gap.
>
> I say the road way is three minutes longer. She says three minutes is a lot. She is at the age when a minute is a piece of property.
>
> So we go through. The passage behind the wall is the width of one person and smells of wet cement and, underneath that, of the gutter it was built over. There is a puddle of unknown depth at the halfway point and my sister steps in it without breaking stride, which is either courage or the fact that she is wearing her brother's old trainers and does not care what happens to them.
>
> Halfway along, the light goes. Not dark — the top of the passage is open to the sky, and the sky is doing that thing it does at half past four, going orange and useless. My sister stops. This is the moment I have been expecting since we turned off the road, and I have prepared a sentence about how I told her so.
>
> Then she says, ‘There's a cat.’
>
> There is a cat. Four kittens, actually, and the mother, in a cardboard box that somebody has put on a ledge and lined with a towel, and a saucer of milk that somebody has filled. We are not the first people through the gap. Somebody has been coming through it every day, in the dark, to feed a cat.
>
> We are four minutes late. Our mother is at the gate with her keys out and her mouth already open to say something.
>
> My sister says, ‘We found kittens,’ and the mouth closes, and we go in.
>
> The cat is still there in March. My sister has named all five, and she has worked out, by asking questions at the shop on the corner, whose towel it was. An old man comes at six every evening and my sister has begun to go with him, along the road, the long way, because there is no need to hurry now that she knows what is at the end of the gap in the wall.
>

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Interpretations (report's list):** a risky route home; cheating in exams; sporting competitions; a short cut to riches; a haircut; a cake baked too quickly; ordering a takeaway. Note that the report calls the flimsiest ones “tenuous”, so a metaphor needs to be earned.
- **Tone:** comic, tense, or gentle (as here). Both positive and negative outcomes are permitted — but the report says positive ones were rare, which is an opportunity rather than a rule.
- **What to avoid:** murder, kidnapping, disappearance; a long build with a rushed ending; more than three characters; a learned story; a title dropped in as a final word with nothing preceding it.
