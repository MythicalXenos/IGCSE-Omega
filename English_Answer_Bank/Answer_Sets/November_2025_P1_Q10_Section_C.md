## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 17) and AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 18–19). Method drawn from **labelled** verified reports for the same form (November 2021 P1; May/June 2022 P1 and P1R; November 2023 P1). **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The Missed Message**
>
> My phone died at 11:40 on the morning of the trials, and I did not charge it until nearly four, and in between those two times the coach selected the team without me.
>
> I know the exact minutes because I have looked at the screen since. The message arrived at 11:52: *Squad announced at 2. Come to the ground even if you are not on the list — I want to talk to you about the captaincy.* I did not read it. At 11:52 I was on the number 14 bus with a dead phone in my pocket, telling my sister that I had probably not been picked, and she was telling me, in the calm and infuriating way she has, that I could not possibly know that.
>
> I had convinced myself by one o'clock. I had a whole story ready: that the coach had never rated me, that the boy who bowls faster than me had taken my place, that the whole season had been a long polite no. I lay on my bed and listened to the ceiling fan and did the thing that people do, which is to build a case out of nothing and then feel it settle like fact.
>
> My sister came in at half past three and said, *Charge your phone.*
>
> I said, *There's no point.*
>
> She said, *Charge your phone, Idiot*, and plugged it in herself, and stood there with her arms folded while it woke up, and I watched the screen fill: eleven notifications, two missed calls, and one message from a number I had not saved.
>
> I have never moved so fast in my life. I ran the whole way — one and a half kilometres in the heat, with my kit bag banging against my back — and got to the ground at 4:12, and the team was already on the field, and the coach was standing by the pavilion with a clipboard and a face like a door.
>
> He looked at me for a long moment. Then he said, *You are forty minutes late and you are still the only person who turned up when he thought he had been dropped.* He tapped the clipboard. *Captain. Second slip. Do not be late again.*
>
> My sister says I should be grateful to her for the last four words. I am not. I am grateful for the twenty minutes she stood in my doorway refusing to accept the case I had built, and for the fact that a message can sit unread in a dead phone while a person quietly rehearses his own disappointment, and that neither of those things — not the battery, not the story I told myself — was the truth.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Interpretations fitting “missed”:** a message read too late; a message never sent; a message sent to the wrong person; a message misread; a message deliberately ignored and then regretted; a message that arrives after the event.
- **Vehicles:** text, voicemail, letter, note, email, sign, announcement.
- **Structures:** one afternoon (as here); two scenes with the message as hinge; the message quoted before its meaning is understood.
- **What to avoid:** the deathbed message (crowded and sentimental); a stolen phone plot; an entirely negative outcome without purpose; the message first appearing in the final paragraph; a plot lifted from a film; “it was all a dream”.
