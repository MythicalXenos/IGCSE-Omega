## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

> **The Crossroads**
>
> The petrol station sits on the junction where the coast road meets the old turnpike, which means that between eleven at night and six in the morning I am the only lit thing for nine miles. I work the night desk. I have done it three years, the pumps have been robbed twice, and I have been frightened exactly once — not on either of those nights.
>
> It was a Tuesday in February, and the rain was coming in sideways off the sea. At twenty past two a hatchback pulled in on the wrong side of the pumps, which nobody does unless they are tired or in a hurry, and a woman got out and stood in the rain without putting her hood up.
>
> She came to the window. She had a boy asleep in the back seat, still in his school jumper, and she had the flat, careful voice of somebody who has been crying for a while and has stopped.
>
> “I've got eight pounds,” she said. “I need to get to Tavistock. Is that enough?”
>
> Tavistock is forty-one miles. Eight pounds is not enough, and she nodded as though she had known that before she asked.
>
> The rules are very clear, and they exist because somebody once took advantage. No credit. No filling cans. No letting people wait inside after midnight. The rules are printed and laminated and they are, in the narrow sense, correct.
>
> She did not beg. That was what undid me. She said thank you, and she went back out into the rain, and she sat in the driver's seat with her hands on the wheel and did not start the engine, and I stood behind the till with the laminated rules six inches from my elbow and watched a woman decide how to tell her son that they were going to have to turn around.
>
> I could see the indicator going on the car, and off, and on again, as if the car itself were thinking.
>
> I put twenty pounds of my own money in pump four and turned the key.
>
> She came back to the window. I said it was a promotion, that we did it on Tuesdays, and not to argue because it was already in the book. She looked at me for a long moment, and then she laughed, once, which was somehow worse than the crying.
>
> “What's your name?” she said.
>
> I told her, and she wrote it on the back of a receipt, and she said, “I'll pay it back,” and I said, “I know,” and neither of us believed that.
>
> The car went left at the junction and the rain took the tail lights.
>
> I was told afterwards — by my manager, who counted the till at six and is not a sentimental man — that what I had done was technically theft. He said it while making two cups of tea and putting one in front of me.
>
> I have thought about that night more than any other, and not because of the money, which never came back. I think about it because that was the first time I understood that a crossroads is not where two roads meet. It is the moment before you choose, when both roads are still open and you are still the person who has not decided.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Readings of the title:** the literal junction (as here); a life decision at a turning point; a reunion at a crossroad in a town; a character who takes the wrong road and finds something.
- **Structures:** one night, one decision, one consequence (as here); two travellers who meet at the same junction and part; the same junction across two decades.
- **What to avoid:** a crossroads bolted onto the final paragraph; a long, convoluted plot in which the title is incidental; very heavy dialogue that carries the plot; a violent ending that the story has not earned; a moral stated outright.

## Why this model targets the top bands — and what it does not claim

- **AO4:** the junction is the story's engine, not its decoration — the choice is made at the pumps and the title is earned in the last two sentences. The narrator has a voice, a job and a limitation, which is what “characters as well as plot” means in practice.
- **AO5:** plain, exact nouns do the work (the laminated rules, the receipt, pump four); the pacing tightens through the woman's three sentences and releases in the manager's kettle; dialogue is four exchanges, each moving the story.
