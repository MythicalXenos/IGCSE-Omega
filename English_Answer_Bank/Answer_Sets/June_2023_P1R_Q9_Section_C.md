## Matching MS criteria

**Indicative content (MS p. 17, quoted in full for Q9):** “As no audience is specified, the examiner is assumed to be the audience. Candidates are free to agree or disagree with the statement and may present a variety of arguments. **AO4 — 20 marks, Questions 9, 10 and 11 (MS p. 18):** *Communicate effectively and imaginatively, adapting form, tone and register of writing for specific purposes and audiences.*

- **Level 2 (5–8):** communicates in a broadly appropriate way; some grasp of purpose and of the expectations/requirements of the intended reader; straightforward use of form, tone and register.
- **Level 3 (9–12):** communicates clearly; clear sense of purpose and understanding of the expectations/requirements of the intended reader; appropriate use of form, tone and register.
- **Level 4 (13–16):** communicates successfully; a secure realisation of purpose and the expectations/requirements of the intended reader; effective use of form, tone and register.
- **Level 5 (17–20):** communication is perceptive and subtle; task sharply focused on purpose and the expectations/requirements of the intended reader; sophisticated use of form, tone and register.

**AO5 — 10 marks, Questions 9, 10 and 11 (MS p. 19):** *Write clearly, using a range of vocabulary and sentence structures, with appropriate paragraphing and accurate spelling, grammar and punctuation.*

- **Level 3 (5–6):** develops and connects appropriate information and ideas; structural and grammatical features and paragraphing make the meaning clear; varied vocabulary and correct spelling of irregular patterns; accurate and varied punctuation, adapting sentence structures as appropriate.
- **Level 4 (7–8):** manages information and ideas with structural and grammatical features used cohesively and deliberately across the text; wide, selective vocabulary with only occasional spelling errors; positions a range of punctuation for clarity, managing sentence structures for deliberate effect.
- **Level 5 (9–10):** manipulates complex ideas using a range of structural and grammatical features; extensive vocabulary used strategically; punctuation accurate to aid emphasis and precision, with sentence structures chosen selectively for effect.

The distance between 17/30 and a Level 4 total is small and worth stating plainly: at these grids, 20/30 would be 13/20 (Level 4) + 7/10 (Level 4). This script is two marks off AO4 Level 4 and one mark off AO5 Level 4 — **three marks in total** separate it from 20/30. It is not a weak script; it is a script that stops at “clear” and “varied” instead of reaching “successful” and “selective.”

## What the script does — and where it falls short

These observations are mine, keyed to the published criteria and to the question-level commentary; they are **not** Pearson commentary and carry no marks.

**Strengths the criteria recognise:**

- **It answers the actual question in the actual form.** The script is discursive from the first sentence (“I partly do agree with this statement…”), takes a partially-agreeing stance, and returns to that stance at the end. It does not drift into narrative — the failure mode Pearson says “a small number of candidates” fell into.
- **It uses evidence of a sort, and Pearson credits that.** Two named studies and one quotation (“a study conducted by university of Ohio in 2014 shows that 76% of successful people had started with less money”; “a survey conducted by Forbes in 2012”; the Mansa Musa line). The AO4 comment names this technique explicitly. The value being rewarded is rhetorical support, not verified fact — and the transcript flags that the figures are unverified.
- **It handles both sides.** The “Although money is a major factor…” pivot and the whole “As having more money can open much more doors to success” paragraph address the counter-case, which is what “discuss” demands.
- **Length is appropriate.** The transcribed text runs to roughly 370 words against the paper's “approximately 400 words”, so nothing here is lost to brevity; the marks lost are to development and control, not to length.
- **Paragraphing is visibly used** and Pearson credits it directly (“Paragraphs are used correctly”).

**Weaknesses consistent with Level 3 rather than Level 4:**

- **Assertion instead of development.** Each piece of “research” is dropped in and moved past: no explanation of why starting with less makes success more likely, no examination of what the 6.9% figure would even mean, no reckoning with the obvious objection that a survey of “successful people” cannot tell you about the unsuccessful. Level 4 asks for a “secure realisation of purpose”; here the purpose is met, the argument is not built.
- **The two paragraphs of the middle section overlap.** The “less money makes you careful/desperate/focused” material and the “money opens doors” material both make the same underlying move — money is one factor among several — so the response circles rather than escalates. Pearson's tip (“more development of ideas”) points at exactly this.
- **Sentence-level errors are visible in the facsimile, not just in the summary.** Pearson’s “comma splicing” is visible where a comma stands between two complete sentences (“…this statement, As I once Mansa Musa exclaimed…”; “…planning out the idea, Due to the reason that we don’t have alot to work with…”), and missing words occur where the script telescopes a phrase (“…an ave person inbecoming successful”). The transcript preserves “would n’t,” “isnt,” “alot,” “inorder,” “inbecoming,” “poull” and “paniced” precisely because irregular spellings are part of the AO5 judgement.
- **Register wobbles.** “Alot of money why???” is a rhetorical question aimed at nobody in particular; “[struck] without it you are nothing” is an absolute claim the rest of the script does not support. “Quite lively” captures this: the voice is present, but it is not yet controlled.
- **The quotation is used as decoration.** “Starting from scratch makes it more worthy” is attributed to Mansa Musa on his death bed without any framing of who he was or why the line matters, and it is not returned to. The strongest scripts, in Pearson's words, make “the pertinent point that success must be judged by a range of factors, money being one of them” — that point is present here, but only in the last two sentences of the middle section, not as the spine of the answer.

## What 20/30 would have required — grounded in the published descriptors

This is my reading of the grids, offered as a checklist rather than a rewrite of the candidate's answer.

- **AO4 (+2 to reach 13, Level 4).** Turn each example into an argument: state the claim, give the evidence, then say what follows from it. Make the conclusion do work — the final paragraph (“hard work beats anything”) currently introduces a new idea rather than closing the one the essay opened.
- **AO5 (+1 to reach 7, Level 4).** Proofread for the comma splices and missing words Pearson names, and vary the sentence openings — five sentences in this short script begin “And …” or “As …”. The vocabulary is already at the level required; the sentence management is not.
- **Neither of these requires more content.** They require the same content, developed and checked — which is precisely the examiner tip printed beside the script.
