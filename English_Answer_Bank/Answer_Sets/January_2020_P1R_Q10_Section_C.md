## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed story

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 17) and AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 18–19), with method from this paper's own report. **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The Promise**
>
> The play started at seven and my shift ended at seven, and I had promised.
>
> I had promised in the kitchen on Sunday, with my hands in the sink, in the careless way you promise things when the day is three days away. *I will be there. I will be in the second row. I will clap so loudly that Mr Farouk will ask me to leave.* My brother is nine and he had a paper crown to wear and one line to say, and he had practised the line on me forty times, at the bus stop, at the table, through the bathroom door.
>
> On Thursday the supervisor went home sick at four and by half past six there were two of us on the floor and a queue at the till that went past the magazines. I am not going to pretend that this was a moral crisis. It was arithmetic: eleven people waiting, one of me, and a clock over the door that had never in its life been on my side.
>
> I rang the school at ten past seven from the stockroom, and the phone rang in an empty corridor, and I rang again at twenty past and a caretaker answered and said the hall was full and the infants were on.
>
> Then I did something I am not proud of and would do again. I took off my tabard, folded it, put it under the counter with a note that said BACK AT EIGHT, and walked out through the queue with my head down while a woman in a red coat said, quite loudly, *Are you serious?*
>
> I got there at twenty-five to eight. The hall doors were open at the back and the lights were up for the last song, and I stood in the doorway in my work shoes, and I could not see him, and then I could: second row, paper crown slightly crushed, mouth moving a half-second behind everybody else.
>
> He saw me during the applause. He did not wave. He did something much worse — he sat up straighter, as if he had been waiting to be allowed to, and then he looked at the teacher to check that she had seen me come in.
>
> Afterwards he said, *You were late*, and I said, *I know*, and he said, *But you came*, and then he wanted an ice cream, and the queue at the shop was only four deep and I had eight minutes to get back.
>
> The supervisor gave me a written warning on Friday. I took it without argument, because she was right about the till and wrong about the evening, and I have kept the warning in a drawer with the paper crown, which he left in the car and which I have never thrown away. It was the only promise I made that year that cost me anything, and it is the only one I can still remember making.

- **Structures:** one evening and one deadline (as here); the promise made in scene one and tested in scene two; a promise narrated by the person waiting.
