## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed discursive answer

> **Never too old — but not at every age the same**
>
> The statement is kindly meant and slightly false, because it treats “old” as one condition. My grandmother is eighty-four and learning to use a tablet so that she can see her sister in Toronto. My neighbour is fifty-six and has just taken up the cello, badly and with enormous pleasure. Neither of them is doing anything remarkable. Neither of them is doing anything an eighty-year-old body, or a fifty-six-year-old life, would find easy.
>
> Start with the part I agree with, which is nearly all of it. The reasons people give for not starting are usually not about the thing itself. They are about the audience: the fear of being the slowest in a room of twenty-year-olds. That fear is real and it is worth dismantling, because in most adult classes nobody is watching and the teacher is grateful to have someone who turns up. Age also brings things that youth does not: time, money for the training, the habit of finishing what you begin. My neighbour practises at six in the morning. A sixteen-year-old would be asleep.
>
> But the statement has a factual problem, and pretending otherwise is not kindness. Bodies do decline. Concentration is harder to hold. Sleep gets worse, and everything is harder when you are tired. There are things that a person who starts at sixty will not master, and telling them otherwise sets them up for a disappointment that will feel like proof. The honest position is not “you can do anything” but “you can do more than you think, and not everything”.
>
> There is also a category the statement ignores: the things that are not new skills but new situations. Learning to use a bank's app when you are eighty is not a hobby; it is a demand. Being “never too old to try something new” is a pleasant sentence for a person choosing a cello, and a cruel one for a person being told their branch has closed.
>
> So I agree, with a condition. Nobody is too old to try. But “trying” has to mean trying the thing at the age you actually are, not at the age the sentence imagines — with the slower pace, the shorter session, the smaller ambition. My grandmother will never be fast on that tablet. She can see her sister, which is the whole point, and it took her four months to get there, and that is what never being too old looks like in practice: slower than a slogan, and real.
>
