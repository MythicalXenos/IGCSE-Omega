## Exact QP context — Q11 (printed p. 21)

> **11** Describe somewhere that you have been that was very busy.
> (Total for Question 11 = 30 marks)

Same rubric: one question; 1 hour; no re-telling of the source texts; approximately 400 words.

*Form note:* two constraints in one line. “Somewhere that **you have been**” asks for the first person and a real, specific place — not a generic busy scene. “**Very** busy” asks for a superlative intensity, which means the piece must convey **crowding, noise and pressure**, not merely mention a lot of people. And the verb is still *describe* — the report says most candidates forgot that.

## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed description

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's **own mark scheme** (printed p. 18 content, pp. 19–20 grids) and **own examiner report**. **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The bus stand at half past five**
>
> The bus stand at half past five in the morning is a machine made of people, and it runs on noise.
>
> Long before anything is visible there is the sound: the tinny call of conductors shouting destinations into the dark, the diesel idle of eleven buses, the flat clang of a spanner dropped on concrete and the answering shout of the man who dropped it. Somewhere a radio is playing a song at the wrong speed. Somewhere a goat is complaining, and no one is listening to the goat.
>
> Light comes up over the roof of the terminal in bands. First grey, then the yellow of the sodium lamps losing, then a hot white that finds the dust and hangs in it. The dust is the real population here. It gets into the tea, into the folds of the sari of the woman selling it, into the wet of the driver's eye.
>
> Then the crowd, which is not a crowd but a hundred small negotiations. A man holds a suitcase above his head like a tray. Two boys push a trolley of melons through a gap that is exactly the width of a trolley of melons. A woman in a green shawl sleeps sitting up, entirely upright, entirely gone, with her ticket held loosely between two fingers so that anyone could read it and nobody does.
>
> The smells arrive in order of strength: diesel, then jaggery, then the sharp green smell of the fish going into the 6.10, then, from the tea stall, the burnt-sugar smell of the kettle that has been on since four.
>
> Noise again, but different now — the day shifting gear. Horns. The scrape of a shutter. A baby, briefly, and then not. The announcement system crackles on and says something that is not quite a language, and every single person on the platform adjusts their position by half a step, all at once, like a field of grass under wind.
>
> By six it is unbearable and I love it. There is nowhere in the world less lonely than a bus stand at half past five: nobody knows me, everybody is in my way, and the whole roaring, sweating, pushing, patient thing is moving — has been moving since four, will go on moving until midnight — and for one hour I am inside it, standing still, being carried.
>

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Places (report's list):** a tube or train journey; busy streets; a shopping centre; a market; a bus or train station; an airport. Also acceptable: a school corridor at break, a stadium concourse, an emergency department, a religious festival, a border crossing.
- **Structures:** an hour of one place (as here); a walk across the space, stopping at five stations; the same place through each sense; arriving and staying rather than arriving and leaving.
- **How busy to make it:** the “very” should be answered with compression — too many things in too small a space — not with the word “busy” repeatedly.
- **What to avoid:** a journey with a destination; characters with names and stories; a short factual account (“the station was crowded and there were many shops”); lists of people without rendering; the over-ambitious vocabulary this paper's AO5 comment warns about.

## Why this model targets the top bands — and what it does not claim

- **AO4:** description sustained across a whole piece; atmosphere built from sound, dust and crowd physics rather than adjectives; one closing sentence that turns observation into meaning without becoming narrative.
- **AO5:** deliberate alliteration (“the flat clang… the answering shout”; “the burnt-sugar smell of the kettle”), sentence lengths from five words to forty, and punctuation used to pace rather than decorate.
