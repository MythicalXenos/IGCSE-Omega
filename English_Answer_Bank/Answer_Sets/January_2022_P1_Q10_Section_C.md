> **The Invitation**
>
> The card arrived in a brown envelope with a second-class stamp, and it was addressed to my brother, who had been dead for two years and three months.
>
> *The Tuesday Club requests the pleasure of Daniel’s company at its twenty-fifth anniversary. Friday the fourteenth, seven thirty. The bowling club, Mill Lane. Buffet.*
>
> I read it four times at the door with my coat still on. Then I put it on the hall table, went into the kitchen, and made a cup of tea I did not drink.
>
> On the Friday I went. I told myself it was to tell them, politely, that they had the wrong address, that whoever kept the membership list had not been informed. I took the card with me, which was my excuse for going.
>
> The bowling club smelled of chips and floor polish and, underneath, of the drains. There were paper tablecloths, a raffle, and a laminated sign that said TEA & COFFEE 50p. An old man in a blazer was arranging sausage rolls on a plate with the concentration of a man defusing something.
>
> “Are you Daniel’s sister?” he said, without looking up. “You’ve got his face.”
>
> I said that Daniel had died, in the September, two years ago.
>
> He said, “I know,” and put down the plate. “I wrote to you in the February and you didn’t answer, so I thought I’d try again. We do the card every year. It’s on the list.”
>
> It turned out that my brother had spent six years of Tuesday evenings in this room, in a league of eleven men and one woman, and that he had never once mentioned it at home. Not because it was a secret. Because it was too ordinary to report, in the way that nobody tells you they had a sandwich.
>
> They showed me the scorebook. His handwriting, small and slanted, the totals underlined twice. He was, by all accounts, a poor bowler with an excellent attitude and a dangerous habit of keeping score honestly, including his own failures, which the club found funny and I found so completely recognisable that I had to look at the raffle for a while.
>
> At the end they gave me a carrier bag. In it were his bowling shoes and a trophy the size of a teacup, awarded, the plaque said, for Most Improved, 2018.
>
> I have been to the club every Tuesday since. I am not a good bowler either.
>
> The invitation was for my brother. I went in his place, and I came home with him.
