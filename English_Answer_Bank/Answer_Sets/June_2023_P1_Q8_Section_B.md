## Exact QP prompt — Q8 (printed p. 15)

> Use ideas from BOTH Text One and Text Two in the Source Booklet to answer this question.
>
> You have been asked to give a talk to your peers about the importance of walking and getting outside.
>
> You should include:
> - advice about different types of walks
> - the benefits of walking outside
> - the problems you might experience.
>
> Write the text of your talk.
>
> Think carefully about the purpose of your talk and the audience for whom it is intended.
>
> (30)

## Matching MS criteria

**Indicative content (MS p. 13):** Use a suitable register for a talk to peers and address all three areas. Pearson gives examples, not a closed list, and says to reward all valid points.

- **Advice about different types of walks:** walking in a park, hiking in hills, climbing a mountain, a lunchtime stroll, an evening walk, walking by the seaside, a trail, woods/forests or rough tracks.
- **Benefits of walking outside:** wellbeing, fitness, overall health, overcoming stress, increased brain capacity, improved sleep, happiness, beautiful scenery or a sense of achievement.
- **Problems candidates might experience:** making the effort or time, ignoring attractive distractions, needing to be fit/prepared, becoming discouraged, finding it too much or exhausting, or finding the route too far.

**Assessment objectives:**

- **AO1, 10 marks (MS p. 14):** Level 5 is 9–10. It requires apt, persuasive selection/interpretation, a wide range of relevant points and well-focused comments with perceptive references to information and ideas.
- **AO4, 12 marks (MS p. 15):** Level 5 is 11–12. It requires perceptive, subtle communication, sharp focus on purpose and intended audience, and sophisticated form, tone and register.
- **AO5, 8 marks (MS p. 16):** the highest band printed in this Q8 grid is **Level 4, 7–8 marks**, for manipulating complex ideas with structural/grammatical control, strategic vocabulary, accurate punctuation and selective sentence choices.

**Report/mark-scheme AO5 level-label discrepancy — now explained, both printings preserved:** Pearson's examiner judgement on printed p. 40 reads “AO5: Whilst there are a small number of errors, this does manipulate complex ideas with a range of features and an extensive vocabulary. **Mark 8, Level 5**.” The matching Q8 AO5 grid on printed p. 16 has **four** levels only, with 7–8 as **Level 4** and no Level 5 anywhere. Both readings are quoted as printed and neither is altered. Three checks settle the interpretation:

1. **The grid makes a Level 5 impossible for this AO.** Section B AO5 is capped at 8 marks across four levels, so there is no fifth level to be at; a Level 5 label cannot describe a mark of 8/8.
2. **The report's own sentence is the Level 4 descriptor.** The grid's Level 4 bullet reads “Manipulates complex ideas, utilising a range of structural and grammatical features…” and “Uses extensive vocabulary strategically”; p. 40 paraphrases exactly that and then labels it Level 5.
3. **The same report gets the four-level scale right elsewhere.** Its other Section B AO5 judgements are printed as “Mark = 6, Level 3” (p. 48) and “Mark = 4, Level 2” (p. 42), both correct against a 1–2 / 3–4 / 5–6 / 7–8 grid. p. 40 is therefore an isolated slip, most plausibly carried down from the AO4 line directly above it, where 11–12 *is* Level 5.

**What follows for use:** the awarded mark, **8/8**, is the maximum and is unaffected; when recording this candidate, quote the report's printed label **and** the grid level, with this explanation. The discrepancy in the published documents is not hidden — it is a printing error in the report, not a marking judgement in doubt.
