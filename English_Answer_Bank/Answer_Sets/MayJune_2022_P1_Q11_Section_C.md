## What this paper's own report says about Q11

- **The central failure, named twice:** “a significant number of candidates **did not focus on the descriptive nature of the task and wrote narrative responses focusing on re-telling the events rather than describing the gift itself**.” And: “many responses were narrative-based, with pockets of description. They told the story of how they received the gift with little focus on describing. **The description tended to focus on how they got the gift, where they were and the people around them rather than the gift itself.**”
- **The condition for success:** “if too little time was given to describing the gift and exploring why it meant so much to the candidate, the response became less focused.”
- **Gifts chosen:** “pets, smart phones, jewellery, cars and family heirlooms as well as more abstract gifts such as religion, life, love and friendship.”
- **Stronger candidates:** “demonstrated a focused and sustained attempt at crafting a piece of description… able to **stay sharply focused on the gift itself, describing it in detail and looking at the reasons why it was significant**. They were often impressive with their use of **imagery, symbolism and effective opening paragraphs**.”
- **Weaker:** “pedestrian, undeveloped or unclear… told the story of the gift and how they got it… described the joys of a birthday or Christmas Day and **the gift only appeared at the end and was not described in detail**.”
- **AO5:** stronger candidates used “metaphors, similes and alliteration”, “a range of structural devices” and “developed descriptive and imaginative vocabulary”.

**Consequence:** the model opens *inside* the description — the object is in the first clause, and the room, the giver and the occasion are deliberately kept out. Symbolism and a simile do the work the report credits.

## [CONSTRUCTED, GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed description

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from this paper's MS (printed p. 18) and AO4/AO5 descriptors (pp. 19–20), with method from this paper's own report. **Not a Pearson script; not examiner-marked; no score claimed.**

> **The black bicycle**
>
> It is a bicycle the colour of a wet slate roof, and it is older than I am by about thirty years.
>
> The frame is heavy in a way that bicycles are not made heavy any more: steel tubing you can feel the thickness of through the paint, a top tube that slopes almost flat, a chain guard like the case of a musical instrument. The paint has been touched up in three places with a brush, badly, so that the black there is a shade too glossy and sits on the surface like a scab. There is a brass bell with a worn thumb-lever that does not ring so much as cough. The saddle is leather, cracked in a map of small rivers, and it has been re-riveted at the back where the original rivets gave up.
>
> On the crossbar, near the seat, there is a strip of dark green tape wound around the frame, and under the tape — you can see one letter of it — someone has painted a name in white enamel. It is not my name. I have never taken the tape off.
>
> The wheels are the part I love. They are not alloy; they are chrome, and chrome holds light the way a mirror holds a face, so that on a bright morning the two wheels carry two moving pieces of sky and the bicycle appears to be lit from inside itself. The rear hub has a three-speed gear with a trigger on the handlebar, and the trigger clicks like a switch in an old house: first, second, third, each one a small mechanical certainty I have come to trust more than most things.
>
> The brakes are rod brakes rather than cables, thin metal arms that squeeze the rim from above, and they do not stop the bicycle so much as negotiate with it. In the rain they are a rumour of stopping. I have learned to plan.
>
> What this bicycle has meant to me is a matter of distance. Before it, my city was a set of places I was driven to. After it, the city became continuous: the hill I could not once get up without standing on the pedals, the long fall towards the river where the air changes temperature halfway down, the particular corner where the smell of frying bread begins. It gave me a body's knowledge of where I live, which no map has ever given anybody.
>
> It is chained to the railings now, going quietly rusty, and I still ride it badly and often. It is the only gift I have ever been given that I use every week and have never once finished using.

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Objects (report's list):** pet, phone, jewellery, car, family heirloom — plus instrument, tool, book, item of clothing, photograph.
- **Abstract gifts the MS allows:** friendship, family, support, a talent, faith. If chosen, the MS still asks for a “sense of the gift”, so the abstraction needs concrete embodiment (what the support actually looked like).
- **Structures:** parts of the object in sequence (as here); one object in three lights (new, broken, still used); the object measured against the body that uses it.
- **What to avoid:** the birthday-morning narrative; the gift appearing only at the end; a page about the person who gave it; a plot; describing the room instead of the object.
