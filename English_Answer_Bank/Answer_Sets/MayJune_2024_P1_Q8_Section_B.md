- **The damage being caused to the environment and our climate:** damage to land; reduced natural resources; fewer trees/deforestation; less fresh water; fewer fossil fuel resources; damage to wildlife (from plastics); release of methane from farming; waste (caused by the throwaway society); problems caused by industrial emissions; carbon emissions; melting ice caps/glaciers; extreme weather events.

## [CONSTRUCTED GRADE 9 TARGET] Constructed letter

**Label:** original writing for this exact task, built from the MS indicative content and the June 2024 report's introduction, using verified ideas from both texts but no quoted wording. **Not** a Pearson script, **not** examiner-marked, **no score claimed.**

> Dear Amara,
>
> How are you? I hope the exams went better than you expected. I'm writing because of something that happened on Tuesday, and I think you'll know what I mean.
>
> We were walking back from the bus stop and there was a coffee cup in the hedge — one of the plastic-lined ones that never breaks down — and you said, “What's the point? Me putting that in a bin changes nothing.” I didn't argue with you at the time because I was too tired, so I'm arguing now, in writing, where you can't interrupt me.
>
> Here's the damage, quickly, because I know you know it. The ice is melting faster than anyone planned for, and the glaciers that would raise sea levels are past the point where we can pretend it is someone else's problem. Weather is getting more extreme — more heat, more flooding, more of everything at once. Forests are being cut down and the land underneath them stops holding water. Plastic is in the sea and in animals and now, apparently, in us. None of this is news to you. That's part of why the cup annoyed me: you already know.
>
> Now the part I actually want to say. You are right that one cup is nothing. But the argument only works if you stop there. It is not one cup — it is your cup, plus whether you get the bus instead of asking your brother for a lift, plus the charger you leave plugged in all night, plus the second-hand shop you walked past because new was easier. Those are all small. They are also all decisions, and decisions are the only thing anyone actually controls.
>
> And some of them are not small at all. If you and I and eight other people asked the school to keep the heating on a timer and stop selling bottled water in the canteen, that is a real change to a real building. That is what campaigning looks like at our level — not a placard, just a request that is hard to refuse because it is reasonable and there are ten of us. When I said individuals can make a difference, I did not mean one person recycling; I meant enough people asking the same question at the same time.
>
> Which brings me to why you said it. I think you are not lazy and you are not stupid. I think you feel powerless — you are seventeen, you cannot vote, and the people making the actual decisions are in rooms you will never be in. I feel that too. And there is a version of it where you decide it is pointless and carry on, and then in five years you are still carrying on and nothing has changed. The other version is smaller and less dramatic, and it is the one I am asking for: do the things that are in front of you, and say the things out loud.
>
> I'm not asking you to be perfect. I ate a mango in April that had flown about six thousand miles, so I am hardly in a position to lecture anyone. I'm asking you to stop saying “what's the point”, because you said it in front of your little sister, and she believed you.
>
> Write back — even if it's just to tell me I'm annoying. Come round on Saturday and we can plan the canteen thing properly. Bring the list of the drinks they sell.
>
> Lots of love,
> Yusuf

*Word count: 586 words including the greeting and sign-off.*

## Accepted alternatives recorded

- **Register:** a letter to a friend may be warm, jokey, exasperated or earnest. The MS asks only for “a suitable register for a letter to a friend”; the report's own emphasis is that the writing must be the candidate's own.
- **Second bullet alternatives:** planting trees, school recycling schemes, a repair café, second-hand uniform sales, meat-free canteen days, a community clean-up, or a petition — all are within “form groups to tackle specific issues” and “campaign”.
- **Third bullet alternatives:** the MS's own list gives eight distinct reasons; a candidate may choose two and develop them properly rather than gesturing at all eight. “Climate change does not necessarily seem a bad thing” and “it will not happen for ages” are especially usable because both appear in Text Two's opening.
- **Unused Text Two material:** the argument that the phrase “climate change” itself sounds harmless (p. 5, lines 1–10), the bath-tub metaphor (p. 7, lines 46–50) and “greenwashed” (p. 7, lines 52–53) are all strong, and all deliberately unquoted here.
- **Unused Text One material:** the water statistic (“only 0.03% of the 70% of water available to us is fresh water”, p. 3, lines 33–35), the methane-and-livestock argument (p. 3, lines 52–58) and the “buy local” point (p. 3, lines 50–52) would each support a paragraph.

## Why this model targets the top bands — and what it does not claim

- **AO1:** all three bullets are developed with points drawn from both texts and then extended into the writer's own examples; no sentence is copied, which is the specific failure the June 2024 report says “can never be a successful way to respond”.
- **AO4:** the form is a letter to a friend — greeting, shared memory, direct second person, mild teasing, a request to write back, a named sign-off. The purpose is clear (persuade one specific person to act) and the audience is unmistakably a peer, not an examiner.
- **AO5:** varied punctuation for a warm, spoken rhythm (dashes, semicolons, colon, parentheses, a short one-line paragraph), correct apostrophes, no comma splices, no missing articles.
- **Not claimed:** no examiner mark, level or score. Pearson's published 30/30 belongs to a script this route cannot read; the model has not been marked.
