# Question 9 — what the evidence actually says

Built from **11 examiner reports (2020–2025)** and **5 marked Q9 scripts**. Every claim below is
traceable to a document in this repo. Where the evidence is thin, it says so.

---

## 1. The mark scheme is not about arguing

AO4 is 20 of the 30 marks. Verbatim, May 2025 mark scheme, page 22:

| Level | Marks | The three bullets |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–4 | Communication is at a basic level, and limited in clarity · Little awareness of purpose and intended reader · Little awareness of form, tone and register |
| 2 | 5–8 | Communicates in a broadly appropriate way · Some grasp of purpose · Straightforward use of form, tone and register |
| 3 | 9–12 | Communicates clearly · Clear sense of purpose · Appropriate use of form, tone and register |
| 4 | 13–16 | Communicates successfully · A secure realisation of purpose · Effective use of form, tone and register |
| 5 | 17–20 | **Communication is perceptive and subtle** · **Task is sharply focused on purpose** · **Sophisticated use of form, tone and register** |

Not one of those fifteen bullets mentions balance, counter-argument, conclusion or evidence.

And page 21, on the question itself:

> "As no audience is specified, the examiner is assumed to be the audience. Candidates are free to
> agree or disagree with the statement and may present a variety of arguments… Examiners should be
> open to a wide range of interpretation."

AO5 is the other 10. Level 5 needs: *"Punctuates writing with accuracy to aid emphasis and precision,
using a range of sentence structures accurately and selectively to achieve particular effects."*

| Script | AO4 | AO5 | Total | Level | Structure it used |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jun 2022 | **19**/20 | **10**/10 | **29**/30 | 5 | One imagined comparison, sustained for the whole answer: an 18th-century writer vs a 21st-century one |
| Jun 2025 | 17/20 | 9/10 | 26/30 | 5 | Four examples from four areas of life, each turning from cost to gain |
| Jun 2024 | 16/20 | 8/10 | 24/30 | 4 | Two-sided running argument with a practical recommendation and a qualified verdict |
| Jun 2023 (A) | 11/20 | 6/10 | 17/30 | 3 | Assertion, little detail |
| Jun 2023 (B) | 11/20 | 6/10 | 17/30 | 3 | Assertion plus three invented statistics |

**The 26/30 is not the ceiling.** Its own judgement says it "meets all of the Level 4 criteria and the
strong sense of purpose moves it into Level 5" — 17 is the *bottom* mark of Level 5. The 29/30 sits
two marks higher on the same scale and is a completely different kind of answer.

## 3. What is constant across all eleven reports

These phrases recur, near-identically, from 2020 to 2025. They are the stable description of the top
band, whatever the question was.

**The winning description — appears in 8 of 11 reports:**

> "More successful candidates **adopted a persuasive and argumentative tone** and had clearly been
> **prepared to write this kind of response**."

**Structure:** "a clear structure with a strong introduction and conclusion" (Jun 2023, Jun 2024,
Jun 2025); "well organised responses with a range of well-developed ideas" (Jan 2022).

**Techniques, named:** "the use of questions, expert opinion, anecdotes and listing" (Jun 2022,
Jun 2023, Jun 2025); "rhetorical questions, counterarguments, and analogies"; "well-integrated
anecdotes or hypothetical examples" (Jun 2025).

**Detail:** "detailed examples" (Jun 2025); "detailed description"; "a good range of ideas".

**Tone:** "engaging and lively manner" (2020, 2022); "an engaging, confident voice, drawing the reader
in with flair and rhetorical sophistication" (Jun 2025); "their arguments showed maturity" (Nov 2020).

**"Sustained"** — the word appears in every single report as the thing weak candidates cannot do:
"struggled to develop and **sustain** a response", "develop and **sustain** their ideas effectively."

## 4. What is punished, in the report's own words

**Pre-taught phrases.** Named and quoted across four series:

> "I am going to be making strong and robust points on my stance"
> "these points fall short of the mark because…"
> "as the dust settles, where do we find ourselves?"
> "indubitably exigent" *(named in 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 — four separate series)*
> "Clearly a topic like this calls for deep thinking"
> "It is important to tell people what side they are on"
> "I am going to present some robust points"
> "I will continue to convince you to switch to my side"
> "I do not pretend to know everything, I only speak on matters I know I will win"
> "Once we have embraced a stand point we must look at the whole picture"

And the verdict on all of them: *"more engaged in remembering the scaffolding than they were in
constructing a considered argument."*

Also named: *"quite a few opened with a philosophical quotation or with their 'most crucial reason.'"*

**Writing a story instead of an argument:** "sometimes these were too personal and not discursive,
sounding more like a narrative" (Jan 2023). "Some wrote narrative responses" (Jun 2025).

**Copying the Section A texts:** "they often relied heavily on the texts for their ideas" (2022,
2023); "used ideas and material from the extracts which limited their responses" (Jan 2020).

**Length without a point:** "their answers were often quite long but tended to repeat points and
struggled to advance a clear point of view" (Nov 2020). "Struggled to find enough ideas and these
responses became repetitive or were brief" (every series).

**Invented statistics:** the Jun 2023 17/30 script B used three of them and gained nothing.

## 5. Claims this evidence kills

| Claim I made earlier | What the records show |
|---|---|
| "Open by saying what people think, where you stand, and what you'll show." | The 29/30 opens on a scene and never states a position. The 24/30 opens with "I think most people would agree… But I think there are positives and negatives". Both are fine. **There is no sanctioned opening.** |
| "You need a real other side / a concession paragraph." | The 29/30 gives the other side **two sentences** and kills it. The 24/30 gives it a whole essay and scores **lower**. |
| "End by restating your position." | The 29/30 ends on a question. The 24/30 ends on a qualification ("I disagree to a point… as long as…"). The 26/30 restates. All three pass. |
| "Use four examples from four areas of life." | That is one working shape (26/30). The 29/30 develops **one** comparison. |
| "Balance is what examiners want." | Balance is what most good candidates happen to produce. It is worth **nothing on its own**: the balanced answer scored 24, the unbalanced one scored 29. |
| "Don't use rhetorical questions as openers." | Not supported and never was. Questions are explicitly **credited** as a technique in four reports. |
| "AO5 9/10 needs flawless punctuation." | The 29/30 has a comma splice ("Climate change, among others is one of these worries") and a wrong word ("literacy") and still takes **10/10**. |

## 6. What the three top scripts have in common

Strip away the shapes and only four things are left:

1. **Every paragraph names the question's own terms or points straight back to them.**
   29/30: "This is what English has become", "English has become more valued".
   26/30: "a worthy example of this", "the challenge of raising their children".
   24/30: "working in a group", "working alone" — in every single paragraph.

2. **Something you can see.** A quill scratching parchment. A red squiggly line. A frozen MacBook.
   Chores done alone in a hall of residence. A football match. Not "it is very difficult" — a thing.

3. **A turn.** Each example or section moves from cost to something gained, or from an assumption to
   its reversal ("or so you think"). Nothing sits still.

4. **A voice that knows it is talking to someone.** "You". A question to the reader. A verdict.

## 7. What to actually do in the exam

**Before you write (2 minutes):** pick the angle, and pick three things you can describe
concretely. Not "reasons" — things.

**The opening (2 minutes):** two sentences. Either a scene, or where you stand. Both are proven.
What is *not* proven is any sentence you have used before.

**Each paragraph:** one thing, described in detail, with the question's terms in it, ending somewhere
other than where it started.

**The ending:** whatever you like, as long as it is short and it is about the question. A question, a
qualification, a restatement — all three have scored.

**The last two minutes:** the AO5 list below. Every one of these has been named in every report since
2020:

> missing basic sentence punctuation · comma splicing · missing or misused apostrophes ·
> homophones · misspelling of basic vocabulary · not capitalising 'I' · missing capital letters at
> the start of sentences · random capitals mid-sentence · subject–verb agreement · verb tenses ·
> missing words · weak paragraphing

That list is worth up to 10 marks and it is the cheapest thing on the paper.
