## Plain-language notes on the reading

- **"literacy"** (p.41) is confirmed at 800 dpi — the candidate wrote *literacy*, not *literary*.
  Given the context ("true, passionate literacy") it is a word-choice slip. Note that on p.39 the
  candidate writes **"literary masters"** correctly.
- **"to ^take inspiration"** (p.40) is confirmed: the candidate wrote "to", struck a word, and
  inserted "take" with a caret above the line.
- **"above it"** (p.40) — the pronoun is *it*, carrying over from "industrial estates"; it is not "us".
- **"neccessary"-style slips** do not appear here; this script's AO5 is 10/10.
- The script **stops mid-argument** — "Some, although few, would argue the opposite. They would" runs
  onto the next page, and there is no closing paragraph. It ends on a question.

## What it actually does — the shape

Five blocks, not a conventional introduction-body-conclusion:

| # | Page | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 39 | An imagined **18th-century scene**: desk, countryside, quill, parchment, "literary masters" |
| 2 | 39 | "**But in reality, it's the 21st century**" — the same scene, ruined: grey, traffic, frozen MacBook, notifications |
| 3 | 40 | The **verdict**, in short flat sentences: "This is what English has become… No inspiration, no room for imagination… You no longer need to think for yourself anymore." |
| 4 | 40 | A **rhetorical question** that states the stakes: "How is one possibly supposed to produce anything worthy of publication, at times like this?" |
| 5 | 40–41 | **One sentence of the other side**, then it is killed in the next sentence: "Some, although few, would argue the opposite… But the reason why writing has become more valued is because it's so rare." |

It then keeps going for another twelve sentences and ends on: "Who knows how much time we have left?"

### The devices it uses, all of them visible on the page
- **Second person throughout** — "you", so the reader is inside the scene
- **Two time settings** as the whole architecture
- **Photographable objects** — quill, parchment, red squiggly line, white screen, hand-written letter
- **Personification** — "the grey clouds frown in sadness"
- **A rule of three** — "tree-less, paper-less, joy-less world"
- **Three rhetorical questions**, the last one closing the answer
- **A paradox as its best line** — writing is "more valued" *because* "it's so rare"

### What it does NOT do
- No stated position in the opening. It opens on a scene.
- No four-example structure. It develops **one** comparison for the whole answer.
- No separate counter-argument paragraph. The other side gets two sentences, then is answered.
- No conclusion paragraph. It ends on a question.
- No pre-taught phrases of any kind.

## Why this matters more than the 26/30 script

This script and the June 2025 26/30 script share **nothing** structurally. One is a single sustained
image; the other is four unrelated examples from four areas of life. Both scored Level 5 for AO4.

That means the marks are **not** in a template. They are in sustained attention to the statement plus
detail you can actually see. The examiner says as much: "almost always sharply focused on the task
even though they take an original, unusual approach."

It also shows how far the writing can roam. This answer drifts into deforestation and climate change —
well away from "the internet" — and still takes 19/20 for focus. The generosity is real, but it is
bought with the quality of the writing, not with the relevance of the content.
