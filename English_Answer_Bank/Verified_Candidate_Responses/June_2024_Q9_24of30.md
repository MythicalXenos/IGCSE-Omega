## What it does — the shape

**A running two-sided argument, paragraph by paragraph, with a recommendation in the middle and a
qualified verdict at the end.**

| # | Move | Evidence in the script |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Write the question back, then state a position that is neither yes nor no | "But I think there are positives and negatives of both working in a group, or alone." |
| 2 | Take a benefit, then immediately undercut it | "This can be good for getting a wide range of ideas… But on the other hand, opinions might clash" |
| 3 | Switch to a second setting to widen the range | "But what about working together on something less academic? … win a football match" |
| 4 | Name the limits of the question itself | "it could vary greatly from person to person and activity to activity" |
| 5 | Take the counter-intuitive line | "Working in a group can help eliminate competition… or so you think." |
| 6 | **Offer a practical fix** | "a way to make it more enjoyable… is to let people pick their groups" |
| 7 | Admit the cost of your own preferred side | "Working alone all the time isn't good for anyone either, though." |
| 8 | End with a **qualified** verdict, not a restatement | "I disagree to a point… as long as you have a healthy social life as well." |

Move 6 is the one the other high scripts do not have: it **proposes something**. That is what makes
the answer feel like it is going somewhere rather than just weighing.
