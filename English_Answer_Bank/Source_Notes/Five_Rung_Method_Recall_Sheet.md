# The Five-Rung Method — one-page recall sheet (4EB1)

Print this. It is the whole method on one page; the teaching that explains it lives in
`Mark_Ladder_4EB1.md` and in the chat lesson of 29 Oct 2026.

---

## The core idea

Marks are not given for *noticing* a feature. They are given for showing **how far your sentence
travels from the quotation to the reader**. The examiner cannot see your thinking; they can only see
that distance on the page. Every level descriptor on this paper is measuring how far you went.

## The five rungs (zoom out each time)

1. **Word** — name it precisely. Two or three words of quotation, not a sentence.
2. **Picture** — what does it make the reader see or hear?
3. **Feeling** — what does it do to the reader's response?
4. **Purpose** — why does the writer want that *at this point* in the text?
5. **Choice** — why that word/structure and not the obvious alternative?

Level 2 stops at rung 2. Level 3 reaches rung 3. Level 4 reaches rung 4. Level 5 reaches rung 5.
*(This is a teaching model, not the mark scheme's own wording — the descriptors say basic / some /
clear / thorough / perceptive, which is the same shape.)*

**Rung 5 is the sentence almost nobody writes:** "He chose 'slashed' rather than 'cut' or 'carved'
because…" That single move is what "discriminating references" means.

## The six structural moves (the neglected half of AO2)

Sequence · contrast · pacing (sentence length) · repetition · position (first and last lines) ·
address (pronouns, direct address) · withholding. **Use at least one per Q3/Q6 answer.** Every
descriptor from Level 3 up demands structure as well as language.

## Q3 / Q6 — 12 minutes

Plan 2 min (pick 3 quotations, one of them structural) → write 8 min (3 short paragraphs, one rung
each deeper) → 2 min check. **Never retell the text.** The named examiner complaint: answers that
"did not focus on the writers' techniques… instead describing or re-telling the content".

## Q7 — 15 marks

- **Both texts in every paragraph.** One-text answers are capped at 6/15.
- Compare **stance, not topic**: where is the writer standing, what do they want, how close are they
  to the subject (participant/observer, alone/accompanied, expert/novice)?
- 4–5 paired points; end each paragraph with one synthesising sentence.
- Never: three paragraphs on Text One, then three on Text Two.

## Q8 — three grids, three examiners

| Grid | Marks | What that examiner reads only |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | 10 | Did you use **every bullet**, from **both** texts, with enough points? |
| AO4 | 12 | Is the **form/tone/register** right for the named audience and purpose? |
| AO5 | 8 | Spelling, punctuation, sentence variety, paragraphing |

Before writing: underline the **audience noun** and the **form noun** in the question. The first two
sentences must signal both. One paragraph per bullet.

## Section C — 30 marks (AO4 20, AO5 10)

- **Q9 discursive:** a line of argument. Thesis → counter → rebuttal → conclusion. Anecdote is not
  argument.
- **Q10 narrative:** one scene, entered mid-action, controlled time, sensory specifics. Title may
  appear only at the end.
- **Q11 descriptive:** a place, not a plot. Organise spatially or by sense; strong verbs.

First two lines establish mode and voice. Every paragraph does one job. The ending lands, it does not
trail off.

## The 90-second proofread (AO5 = 18 marks across the paper)

1. Every sentence has a main verb and ends with punctuation.
2. No comma splices — two complete sentences joined by a comma only.
3. `its / it's`, `their / there / they're`, `your / you're`.
4. Capital `I`; capitals at every sentence start; none randomly mid-sentence.
5. Articles: `a / an / the` present before singular nouns.
6. Tense consistent — especially in narratives that start in the past.
7. Subject–verb agreement: "the crowd **was**", "the boys **were**".

Read the last paragraph backwards, sentence by sentence — it breaks the flow and the errors surface.

## Daily 8 minutes

Take one sentence from any past paper in `Paperlords_Archive`. Write rungs 1–5 on it. That is the
whole exercise. Do not reread notes to revise — produce from memory first, then check.
