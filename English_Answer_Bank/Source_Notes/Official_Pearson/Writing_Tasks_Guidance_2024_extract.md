# Pearson: "Additional guidance on the writing tasks for International GCSE English Language B"
# Published April 2024. 3 pages. Supplied by the candidate, 2 Nov 2026. Verbatim extracts.

## Format and structuring of texts (page 2)
"For each text type, students should think about the audience, form and purpose where named in the
question (especially for question 8) and plan a response which addresses those."

"The response should be written in **continuous prose**, and students should **not**, for example,
for question 8 attempt to reproduce graphically the appearance of a newspaper article using a
larger font headline, images or columns."

"Students should use linguistic features (for example, paragraphs, different sentence types,
discourse markers and content) to structure their writing in effectively sequenced paragraphs.
There should be clear organisation and structure with an **introduction, development of points and
a conclusion, whichever text type is being written.**"

"For some text types, predominantly an article or a contribution to a website, it may be
appropriate to make **sparing** use of bullet points in addition to continuous prose. However, for
most tasks, structuring writing in sentences and paragraphs is the norm."

"The mark scheme for each question paper gives an outline of the expected form of responses...
Examiners emphasise in each mark scheme that students may use **'a range of approaches'**...
indicating that they will accept any reasonable response to the question set."

## Question 8 text types set (sample assessment materials to Nov 2023)
- Article for a school or college magazine, website
- Speech/talk to peers
- Contribution to a website
- Letter

"The questions set may refer to a digital location for the texts, such as a website. This does not
impact on the expected format of the writing."

## LETTER — what students may include
- "An appropriate initial greeting and letter ending, according to the formality of the context
  named in the question ('Dear Sir/Madam' with 'Yours faithfully'; 'Dear Ms Prendergast' with
  'Yours sincerely') or for informal/peer audiences ('Dear Jamal' with 'Best wishes, Sam')."
- "Other typical formulations such as 'I look forward to hearing from you' for letters of
  application or requests for action from a headteacher."
- "A date as this is a usual feature of a handwritten letter."
- **Not required:** "Addresses of sender or recipient"; "Any special layout with addresses."

## SPEECH / TALK — what students may include
- "A suitable opening which makes clear that an audience is being addressed."
- "A suitable closing formulation to flag to the audience that the speech/talk is ending."
- "No other special features are expected. Students should **not format their writing as a script.**"

## CONTRIBUTION TO A WEBSITE — what students may include
- "Headline/title, **strapline**, sub-headings if appropriate."
- "An **introductory (overview) paragraph containing the whole article in a nutshell.**"
- "Effectively/fluently sequenced paragraphs explaining each aspect of the article in more detail."
- **Not required:** "Columns, images, or by-line."

## ARTICLE — what students may include
- "Present some information using bullet points."
- "Make use of sub-headings."
- "No other special features are expected."

## What this confirms, corrects and adds
CONFIRMS:
- "strapline" is Pearson's own word for what this repo has called a standfirst. Same thing.
- Letter: matched salutation and sign-off. Formal = "Yours faithfully". Named person = "Yours
  sincerely". Friend = "Best wishes".
- Speech: opening that addresses the audience, closing that flags the end.
- Contribution: title, strapline, overview paragraph, then sequenced paragraphs.

CORRECTS (this repo previously overstated):
- A by-line is **not required** on an article or contribution. It was described here as earning
  AO4. It is optional; Pearson lists it under "not required".
- Column layout, images and graphic headlines are **not** merely optional - faking them is
  discouraged. "Continuous prose" is the expectation.

ADDS:
- **Every text type needs an introduction, development and conclusion.** This is stated for all
  forms, not just the Q9 essay. A talk that starts mid-point loses structure marks.
- An **overview paragraph** at the start of an article/contribution, "containing the whole piece
  in a nutshell", is the expected feature.
- Bullet points are permitted but should be "sparing".
- A date on a letter is acceptable and normal. Addresses are not needed.
