# November 2024 P1 (4EB1/01) mark scheme — facsimile transcription, printed pp. 14–20

**Why this file exists.** `4EB1_01_2411_MS.pdf` has a damaged text layer: on printed pp. 4–20 every body-text character is drawn as a **2×2-pixel image object** (pp. 5–20 carry between 68 and 260 of them per page on top of a handful of real text spans). A text-extraction route therefore returns almost nothing, which is how the earlier "empty mark scheme" verdict was reached and how this defect came to be described in the bank as a defect in the **published content**.

**That description was wrong, and this file corrects it.** The printed pages are complete and legible. Rendering them as images at 120–150 dpi with PyMuPDF recovers every word. The defect is in the **file's text layer**, not in Pearson's marking content, and it affects any automated reader — including Pearson's own hosted copy, which has the same damaged layer. Nothing was ever missing from the paper.

**Method.** Each page below was rendered to PNG at 140–150 dpi and read as an image. Only what is visibly printed is transcribed; layout is flattened into lists. Transcription check: the rendered AO4 and AO5 descriptors match, word for word, the same grids printed in the mark schemes for May/June 2019, January 2020, May/June 2020, January 2022 and the other 4EB1 papers, which is an independent consistency check on the reading.

**Correction of record (29 October 2026):** statements in the bank that this mark scheme is "published defective … every grid page carries only question numbers, AO labels, level labels and bullet glyphs" are accurate **only as descriptions of the text layer**. Where the bank said the defect was "verified in Pearson's hosted copy via a text-extraction route", that sentence proved the wrong thing — the text route fails on both copies precisely because the layer is broken. The answer sets for Q8, Q9, Q10 and Q11 have been re-pointed at this paper's own grids using the transcriptions below, and the borrowed descriptors are retired.

## Printed p. 14 — Q8 indicative content

**SECTION B: Reading and Writing**

> **8** A suitable register for an article for a school or college website should be adopted. Candidates should address all areas. The following are some points that candidates may make but there are other possibilities. Some candidates may interpret the task more widely and use other examples than those mentioned in the passages.
>
> **Why we should celebrate**
> - marking special moments in our lives
> - to praise someone for an achievement
> - to join with others in our community
> - to feel better
> - to be grateful
> - to cheer us up
> - it is life-affirming
> - it brings order to chaotic times
> - to look back over the past year
> - to think about possible beneficial changes
> - to become happier
>
> **What to celebrate**
> - birthdays
> - weddings
> - anniversaries
> - national holidays
> - religious festivals
> - community celebrations
> - personal or group successes
>
> **Ways to celebrate**
> - by buying presents
> - by having feasts
> - by having parties
> - by going out
> - with other people
> - by making positive changes
> - by making resolutions.
>
> Reward all valid points.

## Printed p. 18 — Section C indicative content

**SECTION C: Writing**

> **9** As no audience is specified, the examiner is assumed to be the audience.
>
> Candidates are free to agree or disagree with the statement and may present a variety of arguments.
>
> Content may include references to: you do not, necessarily, need money to be happy; enjoyment of the natural environment is free, e.g. walking, swimming, appreciating wildlife; company of friends and family is free; valuing the simple things of life; money is required for basic essentials, e.g. food and drink, and transport (not just luxury items); some things require money, e.g. entertainment venues, theme parks, sporting events or holidays and travel; technology costs a lot of money and is often considered essential.
>
> Examiners should be open to a wide range of interpretation.

> **10** No indicative content can be specified, since candidates may choose to interpret the title as they wish.
>
> Candidates should be rewarded for such qualities as a sense of drama, vivid description, excitement or suspense.
>
> NB: Explicit reference to the title may not be mentioned until the end of the story.
>
> Examiners should be open to a wide range of interpretation.

> **11** Candidates may choose a wide range of places (which may be real or imaginary) such as airports, railway stations, markets, shops, sporting events, celebrations, festivals, cities. Answers may include descriptions of the place and the effects it had on them.
>
> Candidates should be rewarded for their powers to evoke a sense of place and atmosphere, using effective vocabulary.
>
> Examiners should be open to a wide range of interpretation.
