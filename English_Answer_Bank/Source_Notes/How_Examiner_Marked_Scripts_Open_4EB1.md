# How examiner-marked 4EB1 scripts actually open

Every quotation below is verbatim from `Verified_Candidate_Responses/June_2023_Section_A.md`, whose marks are Pearson's published judgements, not mine.

## The top scripts open flat, then go straight to evidence

**Q6 — 10/10, Level 5.** First sentence:
> "The writer presents his experience as overwhelming."

Eight words. No hook, no question, no stance announcement. It names the thing, then the next sentence quotes "it was hell".

**Q7 — 15/15, Level 5.** Two sentences:
> "Both texts present their respective experiences of being outdoors. However, while Text One is informative and inspiring, Text 2 is descriptive and the negative personal experience on a trek outdoors."

Sentence 1 names the common ground flatly. Sentence 2 names the difference. Nothing else.

**Q3 — 9/10, Level 5.**
> "The writer uses colloquial language and a chatty tone; this is to engage and relate to target audience, 'young people'."

**Q6 (P1R) — 10/10.** Opened with a plain assertion about the writer's language, then quoted.

**Q11 — 23/30, Level 4.** Three short declaratives, no thesis:
> "Ravens caw in the pine wood ahead. The bitter wind bites my face. The patchwork country spreads out before me."

**Q11 — 26/30, Level 5.** One image-sentence, then content:
> "Blazing, confident and inspired, a lone artist crafted his masterpiece in the confines of his studio."

**Q10 — 30/30 — the highest Section C mark in the archive.** No thesis at all:
> "My stomach fluttered. My hands, slick with sweat, grasped at one another in my lap."

**Q8 — 30/30.** Register first, content second:
> "Greetings my fellow peers. I am Abigail Pennington and I will be talking to you about the importance of walking and going into the great outdoors."

## The two lowest Section C scripts open by announcing a stance before any content

**Q9 — 17/30, Level 3.**
> "Some people would agree that fresh air and exercise are the keys to happiness but I understand why some wouldn't."

**Q9 — 17/30, Level 3.**
> "I partly do agree with this statement, As I once Mansa Musa exclaimed on his death bed, 'Starting from scratch makes it more worthy.'"

Both spend the first sentence on *where the writer stands* rather than on *what the two things in the statement actually are*. Both follow it with a list of separate topics rather than a development.

## The evidence gap, stated honestly

There are **only two authentic Q9 scripts in the archive and both are 17/30.** There is no examiner-marked Q9 above 17 available here, so no claim can be made from evidence about what a 25+/30 discussion opening looks like. The scripts above are Q3/Q6/Q7/Q8/Q10/Q11.

The transfer is legitimate only because Q9, Q10 and Q11 share **one AO4 grid and one AO5 grid** (MS p.22–23). What the grid rewards — "sharply focused on purpose", "perceptive and subtle", "extensive vocabulary strategically" — does not change between the three. But the *shape* of a strong discussion opening is inference from that grid, not observation of a marked script. It should be labelled as such.

## What can be said from the grid alone

AO4 Levels 3 vs 4, MS p.22, verbatim:

- Level 3 (9–12/20): "Communicates clearly. Clear sense of purpose and understanding of the expectations/requirements of the intended reader. Appropriate use of form, tone and register."
- Level 4 (13–16/20): "Communicates successfully. A secure realisation of purpose and of the expectations/requirements of the intended reader. Effective use of form, tone and register."
- Level 5 (17–20/20): "Communication is perceptive and subtle. Task is sharply focused on purpose… Sophisticated use of form, tone and register."

Nothing in that grid mentions taking a side, hedging, or the number of arguments. The whole scale is about **purpose being realised and the reader being addressed deliberately**. That is why a well-run discussion can reach Level 5 whether it commits or qualifies — and why a list that stops cannot.
