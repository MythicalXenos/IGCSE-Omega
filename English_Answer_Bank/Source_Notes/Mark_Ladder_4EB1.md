# The 4EB1 mark ladder — verified grids, and what separates each level

**Compiled 29 October 2026 from the official mark schemes in `Paperlords_Archive` (June 2025 01,
November 2024 01, June 2024 01 all agree on the allocations below).** This is a working reference:
where the marks sit, what each level literally says, and the single change that moves a script up a
level. Nothing here is invented — the descriptor wording is quoted, the rest is interpretation and is
labelled as such.

---

## 1. Where the 100 marks actually are

| Q | Marks | AO | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | AO1 | retrieval |
| 2 | 1 | AO1 | retrieval |
| 3 | 10 | AO2 | levels-based analysis |
| 4 | 1 | AO1 | retrieval |
| 5 | 2 | AO1 | retrieval |
| 6 | 10 | AO2 | levels-based analysis |
| 7 | 15 | AO3 | levels-based comparison |
| **Section A total** | **40** | AO1 5 / AO2 20 / AO3 15 | |
| 8 | 30 | AO1 10 / AO4 12 / AO5 8 | three separate grids, added |
| **Section B** | **30** | | |
| 9 / 10 / 11 | 30 | AO4 20 / AO5 10 | two grids, added |
| **Section C** | **30** | | |
| **Paper** | **100** | AO1 15 / AO2 20 / AO3 15 / AO4 32 / AO5 18 | |

**Three consequences, and they are the whole strategy.**

1. **Only 5 of the 40 Section A marks are finding things in the text.** The other 35 are Q3 + Q6 + Q7,
   which are all levels-based. "I'm weak at Section A" almost always means "I'm stuck at Level 2–3 on
   levels-based answers", not "I can't read".
2. **AO4 + AO5 = 50 of 100 marks**, and all of it is writing craft. Half the qualification is judged on
   how you write, not what you know about the texts.
3. **Q7 alone is 15 marks** — more than half of Section B's content, and the biggest single jump
   available to a 50-something candidate.

## 2. The mechanism that sets your level: "best fit"

From the mark schemes, near-verbatim:

> "Examiners should first decide which descriptor most closely matches the answer and place it in that
> level. The mark awarded within the level will be decided based on the quality of the answer and will
> be modified according to **how securely all bullet points are displayed at that level**."

Read that twice. **Every level has exactly the same number of bullets — usually two or three — and you
are judged on the secure display of *all* of them.** So a script that nails "analysis" but has thin
evidence does not get a Level 5 mark; it gets Level 3. The levels are not a ladder of effort, they are
a checklist of *dimensions*, and the examiner takes the highest level where **every** column is filled.

The recurring vocabulary, which is the same idea expressed five ways:

| Level | Word the descriptors use | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | basic / limited / little | identifying, barely |
| 2 | some / valid but not developed | naming a device, valid but floating quote |
| 3 | clear / appropriate / relevant | explaining what the device does |
| 4 | thorough / secure / effective / detailed | exploring *how*, with precise evidence |
| 5 | perceptive / sophisticated / discriminating | choosing the *best* evidence and explaining the writer's choice |

Your 50-something scripts are almost certainly sitting at **Level 2–3**, because that is exactly the
band where most candidates plateau. The reason is nearly always the same: **the answer says what a
device is and roughly what it does, and then stops.** Level 4 needs the *how*, Level 5 needs the
*choice*.

## 3. Q3 and Q6 — AO2, 10 marks each

| Level | Marks | Descriptor (abridged, verbatim) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | No rewardable material |
| 1 | 1–2 | Basic identification and little understanding…; references limited |
| 2 | 3–4 | **Some** understanding of and comment on language and structure…; selection valid but **not developed** |
| 3 | 5–6 | **Clear** understanding and **explanation** of language *and* structure… including use of vocabulary *and* sentence structure; references appropriate and relevant |
| 4 | 7–8 | **Thorough** understanding and **exploration**… vocabulary, sentence structure **and other language features**; references **detailed**, fully support the points |
| 5 | 9–10 | **Perceptive** understanding and **analysis**…; references **discriminating** and clarify the points |

**The one change that moves you from 5 to 8:** stop writing "the writer uses X to show Y" and start
writing "the writer chose X rather than Z, which makes the reader feel…, so that by the end of the
extract they…". The Level 4/5 wording is about **exploration and choice**, not about naming more
devices. Three devices explored deeply beat nine devices named.

**The second change:** the descriptors keep saying *language **and** structure*. Most candidates write
about words only. Structure is the cheapest route into Level 4 because hardly anyone does it — and it
is explicitly required at Levels 3, 4 and 5.

Structural moves worth naming every time: opening and closing lines, a shift from panoramic
description to close detail (or the reverse), short sentence after long, contrast, tricolon, paragraph
breaks at a turn, direct address, pronoun shift, repetition, a withheld detail.

**A paragraph template that reaches Level 4** (adapt, do not recite):

1. Point, with the quotation cut to the exact two or three words that matter.
2. What that word/structure makes the reader picture or feel.
3. Why the writer wants that *at this point* in the text.
4. (Level 5) why not the obvious alternative word — what is gained by that choice.

## 4. Q7 — AO3, 15 marks

| Level | Marks | Descriptor (abridged) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–3 | **Does not compare**; description of ideas/perspectives; references limited |
| 2 | 4–6 | Considers **obvious** comparisons; **comment**; references valid but not developed |
| 3 | 7–9 | Considers a **range** of comparisons; **explanation**; references appropriate and relevant |
| 4 | 10–12 | **Wide range** of comparisons; **exploration**… how theme/language/structure are used **across** the texts; references **balanced across both texts** |
| 5 | 13–15 | **Varied and comprehensive** range; **analysis**; references balanced, **discriminating** |

**The hard cap, quoted exactly:** "NB: candidates who have considered only ONE text may only achieve a
mark up to the top of Level 2" — i.e. **6/15 maximum**, even if that one text is analysed
brilliantly. Two texts or nothing.

**What separates Level 3 from Level 4 here** is not the number of points but the *kind* of comparison.
Level 2–3 compares content: "both describe new places", "Text One is a desert, Text Two is
countryside". Level 4–5 compares **how the perspective is constructed and why the writers differ**:
"both are first-person, but Text One's narrator is a visitor being shown around and reports wonder,
while Text Two's writer is alone and reports physical struggle — so the same subject produces
admiration in one and hard-won affection in the other."

**Structure that works:** four or five paired points. In **each** paragraph, both texts, one sentence
of synthesis at the end. Never "Text One…" for three paragraphs then "Text Two…" for three. Examiner
reports have complained about exactly that pattern for years.

Useful comparison axes: genre and purpose; who narrates and how much they know; attitude to the place;
what is selected for description; structure of the extract; register; the ending.

## 5. Q8 — Section B, 30 marks across three grids

Marks are **added from three independent grids**, so a strong piece of writing can still score 18/30 if
the content grid is weak. This is where most candidates silently lose 5 marks.

| Grid | Marks | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | 10 | selection and interpretation of the **given bullet points**, range of relevant points from **both texts** |
| AO4 | 12 | form, tone and register suited to the **specified audience and purpose** |
| AO5 | 8 | accuracy, vocabulary, sentence variety, paragraphing |

**AO1, the forgotten 10.** The bullet points in the question *are* the AO1 grid. Covering all of them,
with material drawn from both texts, is worth roughly 8–9/10 almost by itself. Skipping one bullet, or
writing entirely from one text, drags this grid to Level 2 (3–4) no matter how elegant the prose is.

**AO4 requires the form to actually be the form.** The recurring examiner complaint is a task that
asked for one thing and got another: a letter that reads like a leaflet, a speech with no audience in
the room, an article with no headline. If the task names an audience, address them; if it names a
form, use its conventions — salutation and sign-off for a letter, direct address and rhetorical
questions for a speech, headline and subheading for an article.

**AO5 is out of 8 and is the most controllable marks on the paper.** Level 4 (7–8) needs "rare
spelling errors", "extensive vocabulary used strategically", punctuation "to aid emphasis and
precision". That is a proofreading target, not a talent target.

## 6. Section C — 30 marks, AO4 20 + AO5 10

| Level | AO4 marks | AO5 marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–4 | 1–2 |
| 2 | 5–8 | 3–4 |
| 3 | 9–12 | 5–6 |
| 4 | 13–16 | 7–8 |
| 5 | 17–20 | 9–10 |

**The arithmetic of 25/30.** You need AO4 17 + AO5 8, or AO4 16 + AO5 9. So the honest target is:
**top of Level 4 on AO4, Level 5 on AO5.** Chasing a Level 5 AO4 (17–20) is a stretch; securing AO5
9–10 and pushing AO4 to 16 is the realistic and much cheaper route to 25.

**What Level 4 AO4 asks for**, quoted: "Communicates successfully… A **secure realisation** of purpose
and the expectations/requirements of the intended reader… **Effective** use of form, tone and register."
Level 5: "perceptive and subtle… **sharply focused**… **sophisticated**".

Note what is *not* in the descriptor: length, complexity of plot, or impressive vocabulary. "Secure
realisation of purpose" means the reader can tell from paragraph one what kind of text this is and who
it is for. Most 16/30 responses fail that test in the opening two lines.

**The three options have different success conditions** — this is from the mark schemes' own indicative
content:

- **Q9, discursive:** the examiner is the audience. You may agree or disagree, but you must *sustain an
  argument*. The named failure is "wrote long sections about themselves and their futures, without the
  discursive element" — personal anecdote is not argument.
- **Q10, narrative:** no indicative content is specified, so the marking is entirely on craft: "a sense
  of drama, vivid description, excitement or suspense". Note the permitted licence: "Explicit reference
  to the title may not be mentioned until the end of the story."
- **Q11, descriptive:** reward is for "powers to evoke a sense of place and atmosphere, using effective
  vocabulary". A description that turns into a story drifts away from the task.

## 7. Timing, 3 hours

| Block | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Read both texts properly, annotate | 12 |
| Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5 (5 marks total — do not spend 10 minutes here) | 5 |
| Q3 | 12 |
| Q6 | 12 |
| Q7 | 20 |
| Q8: plan 5 / write 40 / check 5 | 50 |
| Section C: plan 5 / write 40 / check 5 | 50 |
| Final proofread of Sections B and C | 9 |

Section A is instructed as one hour on the paper, and that leaves two hours for the two 30-mark
pieces. Do not overrun on Section A; the writing marks are worth more per minute.

---

## 8. The four things to fix first, in order of marks returned

1. **Q7: always both texts, comparisons woven through every paragraph.** One-text answers are capped at
   6/15. This single habit is worth up to 9 marks.
2. **Q3/Q6: add structure, and say why the writer chose that word.** Moves Level 2–3 to 4. Up to 4
   marks each.
3. **Q8: hit every bullet point, from both texts, and make the form obvious in the first two lines.**
   Protects 10 AO1 marks and 12 AO4 marks from leaking away.
4. **Proofread Sections B and C properly.** AO5 is 18 marks across the paper and responds to discipline
   faster than anything else.
