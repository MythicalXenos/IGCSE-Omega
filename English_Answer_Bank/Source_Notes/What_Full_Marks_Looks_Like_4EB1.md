# What full marks actually looks like — measured from the 14 authentic scripts

**Compiled 29 October 2026** from `Verified_Candidate_Responses/June_2023_Section_A.md`, which holds
fourteen facsimile-checked transcriptions of scripts Pearson itself reproduced, with the scores it
awarded. Nothing here is constructed or estimated: the counts below were measured from those
transcripts with `[editorial notes]` stripped out. Crossed-out wording is excluded; the figures are
therefore slight undercounts.

This file exists because the most useful thing a candidate can be shown is not advice but arithmetic.

## 2. Four findings, in order of usefulness

### (a) Section A carries no marks for accuracy — at all

Q3 and Q6 are assessed on **AO2 only**; Q7 on **AO3 only**. There is no AO5 in Section A. The
full-mark and near-full-mark scripts prove this is not a technicality: the 9/10 Q3 script contains
"*She does this.*" as a sentence, writes "helps Dunbar illustrate **his** points" about a female
writer, and produces "Both of these engages the reader". It scores 9/10.

**Consequence:** in Section A you are never being marked on your English. Do not spend thinking time
on elegance, and do not let a shaky sentence stop you writing the next idea. (The same candidate's
Section C, where AO5 *does* apply, dropped to 17/30.)

### (b) Full marks uses many short quotations, not long ones

The 15/15 Q7 uses **40 quotations averaging three words**. The 8/15 Q7 uses **5 quotations averaging
ten words**. That is the clearest single difference in the whole dataset.

Long quotation is not neutral — it displaces analysis. Every word copied is a word not spent
explaining. The mark scheme asks for references that are "detailed" and "discriminating"; discriminating
means *chosen*, and a chosen quotation is short.

### (c) The 15/15 comparison is 730 words over 8 paragraphs

The 8/15 comparison is 269 words over 4. Roughly **90 words per paragraph is the working unit** in
Section A. Not 200. Not 30.

### (d) Full-mark Section C is ~500 words in 11–12 paragraphs

Both 30/30 Section C scripts sit at 481 and 531 words with 11 and 12 paragraphs — that is about
**45 words per paragraph**. The 17/30 scripts are shorter (293, 372) *and* have fewer paragraphs (6, 7).

The lesson: in Section C, paragraph *frequently*. A new paragraph for each beat of the story or each
shift of focus. Wall-of-text writing is a Level 3 habit; it looks uncontrolled even when the sentences
are fine.

## 3. The anatomy of one full-mark analysis paragraph

From the 9/10 Q3 script (June 2023 P1, on the importance of spending time outdoors), first paragraph,
verbatim:

> "The writer uses colloquial language and a chatty tone; this is to engage and relate to target
> audience, 'young people'. She does this. This helps the reader — younger people — to realise that
> they are the ones being addressed ('you'), and helps present the importance of spending more time
> outdoors."

Four moves, in order, in 55 words:

1. **Name the choice** — "colloquial language and a chatty tone".
2. **Name the audience it serves** — "young people".
3. **Say what it does to the reader** — "helps the reader to realise that they are the ones being
   addressed".
4. **Tie back to the exact question focus** — "the importance of spending more time outdoors".

Move 4 is the one candidates skip, and it is free marks: the question's own phrase, repeated at the
end of the paragraph, proves relevance. The examiner does not have to infer that you answered the
question.

Note what is absent: no device-name parade, no "this engages the reader", no literary terminology
beyond "colloquial" and "tone". Note also that move 3 is not a feeling — it is what the reader comes
to *understand*.

## 4. What this changes about practice

- **Stop rewriting sentences for elegance in Section A.** Correctness is worth zero there. Spend the
  time on a fourth paragraph instead.
- **Cut every quotation to three or four words**, embedded inside your own sentence.
- **Target ~90 words and 6–8 paragraphs** for a Q3/Q6, and 8 paragraphs for a Q7.
- **End every paragraph with the question's own words.** Cheap relevance, every time.
- **In Section C, paragraph at every beat** — aim for 10+ paragraphs, not 6.
- **Do not model your writing on the *appearance* of these scripts.** They are handwritten, crossed
  out, and full of tense slips. They scored full marks because of the density of the analysis, not
  the polish. Copying the polish of nobody is the mistake; copying the *density* is the method.

## 5. The evidence contract for this file

Every score and level quoted above is Pearson's, as printed in its examiner reports, and every
transcript was checked against the report facsimile before being stored (see the flags and
incompleteness notes in `Verified_Candidate_Responses/June_2023_Section_A.md`). Two of the fourteen
reproductions are incomplete excerpts and are marked as such there — P1R Q6 ends mid-phrase and P1R Q7

complete figure. Do not treat any of these as models written for this bank; they are candidates'
own scripts, including their errors.
