# The phrase bank, upgraded — with the one clause that makes it score

**Source:** a "Writer's Effect — Phrase Bank" supplied by the learner (31 Oct 2026). Its ten effect
phrases are kept in full and used as **Blank 3** of the Q3/Q6 sentence machine. One column has been
added, and it is the difference between 4/10 and 7/10.

---

## Why the column matters

4EB1's examiner reports say the same thing every series. From the bank's own research file
(`Section_A_Examiner_Research_2024-2025.md`):

> "Analyse the particular effect, not a stock response. 'This engages the reader', 'makes the reader
> want to read on', or 'makes the text interesting' is not an explanation unless the candidate
> specifies *how* and *why*."

A phrase bank supplies the "how". It cannot supply the "why", because the "why" depends on the words
in front of you. **A page of these phrases used alone sits at Level 2** — valid, not developed. The
grid gives 3–4/10 for exactly that.

The fix costs one clause and no ideas: **name the word, then say what it does that a plainer word
would not have done.** That is the whole of column 3 below.

---

## The bank, with the upgrade clause attached

| If the writer wants to… | You could write… | **Add this (the upgrade)** | Use only if true of the passage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Show fear | Conveys a sense of terror | **because the verb "______" makes the threat physical rather than described** | the text has a physical/visceral danger word |
| Show sadness | Evokes sympathy | **because "______" is stated flatly, with no self-pity, so the reader supplies the feeling themselves** | the tone is understated, not wailing |
| Create danger | Establishes a threatening atmosphere | **because "______" withholds what the danger actually is, leaving the reader to fill the gap** | the text delays or hints |
| Show happiness | Conveys excitement and joy | **because the sentence suddenly shortens, so the writing sounds like someone speaking quickly** | there is a short sentence at the happy moment |
| Show loneliness | Emphasises isolation | **because "______" is the only sentence with no other person in it** | the isolation is shown by absence |
| Show confusion | Creates confusion / disorientation | **because the clauses pile up without full stops, so the reader loses the sentence the way the writer loses the way** | long, comma-heavy syntax |
| Show power | Establishes dominance | **because "______" is an imperative — the reader is told, not asked** | commands/hortatives present |
| Show weakness | Highlights vulnerability | **because "______" is a passive construction, so the writer is the thing being acted upon** | passive voice present |
| Create mystery | Builds suspense | **because the detail is named but not explained, so the reader is given a question instead of an answer** | something withheld |
| Show urgency | Creates a sense of immediacy | **because the present tense arrives without warning, so the reader is inside the moment rather than looking back at it** | tense shift to present |

## The assembled sentence

> The writer uses **______** — "**______**". This **conveys a sense of terror**, **because the verb
> "______" makes the threat physical rather than described**, so the reader **realises** that
> **______** [the question's own words].

Four blanks, three of which are supplied above. Only one requires reading a single word.

---

## Two warnings, both from the mark scheme

1. **Do not use a phrase that isn't true of the text.** "Conveys a sense of terror" is wrong for the
   Yemen passage: it is admiring, not frightening, even where it mentions danger. A mismatched stock
   phrase reads as a response written before the text was read, and costs more than it earns.
2. **Do not use the same phrase twice in one answer.** Six paragraphs of "conveys a sense of…" reads
   as one idea repeated. Vary across the ten.
