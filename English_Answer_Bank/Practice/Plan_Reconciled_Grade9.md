# The reconciled plan — research vs. own data

**1 November 2026.** Structure: what the research says → what the data says → where they disagree →
the plan. Boundaries: 9 = 69/100 (Jun 2025; 68–70 every series since 2023).

---

## Part 1 — What the research says

Four findings, each with a source.

### 1.1 Level 3 → Level 4 is won by **selection and development**, not by volume

Level 4 keywords in the reading grids are **"perceptive"** and **"detailed"**. The practical advice
from graded exemplar analysis is consistent across sources:

> "A lot of it is actually in the things you do **before** you answer... narrow down and focus in on
> three or four really, really interesting bits. By and large, you're looking for single words or
> short phrases, not huge chunks. It's generally true that **the longer your quote, the fewer marks
> you'll get**."

> "Writing in more detail about each detail selected (having picked out fewer details) will also help
> with the 'detailed' side of Level 4."

This matches the measurement in this repository: the 15/15 Q7 used **40 quotations averaging three
words**; the 8/15 used **five averaging ten**.

### 1.2 The engine of Level 4 is **contradiction**, not decoration

From a grade 8/9 exemplar commentary:

> "The student has identified the **inherent contradictions** in the description of the mountain. It is
> both life-giving and destructive; it is beautiful and terrible. **These ideas drive the analysis.**"

And a Level 4 feature list: "can move between the **micro and the macro**"; "a developed response which
may explore **different facets** of the ideas/words used".

This is the single most transferable technique available: **find the contradiction and let it drive
the paragraph.** It is also, notably, the thing this learner reached for unprompted in the Yemen
exercise ("time, effort and resources… relief").

### 1.3 The delivery system matters more than the content

**SRSD** (Self-Regulated Strategy Development) — explicit strategy, modelling, memorisation, guided
practice, then independent performance, with a self-monitoring checklist — has an average effect size
of **1.14**. For non-struggling writers, **0.84**; for struggling writers, **0.57** and sometimes not
significant.

**Feedback** has an effect size of **0.70–0.79**, and the most powerful form is **feed-forward**
("where to next") rather than task-level comment.

**Implication:** the constraint is not knowing the technique. It is having a loop where a technique is
modelled, attempted, corrected in the direction of the next level, and reattempted.

### 1.4 Level 4 responses are rare, and that is normal

An exam-board report states plainly: **"Responses at Level 4 were rare."** This is not a learner
deficit; it is the population baseline.

## Part 2 — What the data says

### 2.1 Correction of an arithmetic error made earlier in this session

Earlier I wrote that a grade 9 needs "Level 4 in four places" and that Q7 must reach 9/15. That was a
muddle. The vectors that actually sum to 69:

| Vector | A | B | C | Q3 / Q6 / Q7 inside A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 | 22 | 22 | 7 / 7 / 6 |
| 2 | 24 | 23 | 22 | 7 / 6 / 6 |
| 3 | 23 | 23 | 23 | 6 / 6 / 6 |
| 4 | 26 | 22 | 21 | 7 / 7 / 7 |

**Their best-ever Section A is 23** (ret 5 + 6 + 6 + 6). So one of the four grade-9 vectors requires
**no improvement at all in Section A** — only B at 23 and C at 23.

### 2.2 The realistic target vector

Vector 1 is the most defensible, because Section A is the weakest section and B/C are the strongest:

| Component | Now (typical) | Best ever | Target | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Section A | 15 | 23 | **25** | +10 |
| Section B | 19 | 22* | **22** | +3 |
| Section C | 18 | 19 | **22** | +4 |
| **Total** | **52** | 64 | **69** | **+17** |

\*the 22/30 is AI-marked and unverified.

Inside Section A, +10 means: **Q3 6→7, Q6 6→7, Q7 6→7 (+3 from the found level) and recovering the 7
marks lost to timing** (typical 15 vs best 23).

### 2.3 Why +1 per question, not +2

Because 6/10 is Level 3 and 7/10 is Level 4. **One mark crosses a level boundary.** The jump is not a
different skill; it is the same skill with the "how" and the "so what" attached. That is precisely what
the research in §1.1 and §1.2 describes.

## Part 3 — Where research and data disagree, and who wins

| Question | Research says | Data says | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is the bottleneck? | Selection of evidence and development of it | Timing: Section A collapses from 23 to 15 | **Both, in that order.** Timing costs 7 marks, selection costs 3. Fix timing first because it is free. |
| How many quotations? | Fewer, deeper | More, shorter (40 × 3 words in the 15/15) | **Reconciled:** the constant is *brevity per quotation*. A 3-word quotation is "fewer words", even if there are many of them. Never quote a clause. |
| What produces Level 4? | Contradiction and micro→macro | Unverified, but the learner's own instincts went there | **Adopt.** It is the only technique with both external evidence and internal fit. |
| How fast can it change? | SRSD needs several cycles | Two days available | **Three cycles maximum.** Target the *cheapest* Level 4 sub-skill, not the whole profile. |
| Is a 9 realistic? | Level 4 is "rare" | Simulation: 0.3% from consistency work, 92.7% from a raised ceiling | **A 9 requires the ceiling move.** Consistency work alone will not do it. |

## Part 4 — The plan

### The one skill to install: **Contradiction → Zoom → Macro**

Three steps, applied to one short quotation at a time:

1. **Contradiction.** What does this word do that fights something else in the text? (In the Yemen
   extract: "slashed" is violent inside a passage full of "breathtaking", "golden", "purple".)
2. **Zoom.** What does that *specific word* do that a plainer word would not?
3. **Macro.** What does that tell us about the writer's argument across the **whole** extract?

Written out, that is one Level 4 paragraph:

> "The writer describes the wadis as "slashed as if with a giant knife". This is a violent verb inside a
> landscape he has just called "breathtaking", with cliffs "golden in the sunlight and purple in the
> shade" — so the beauty and the damage arrive in the same breath. "Slashing" is an act done *to* the
> land, not a shape the land has, which means the narrator is not simply admiring the Yemen; he is
> registering that it is a place that has been hurt. That tension runs through the whole extract, which
> ends not on the desert but on a small enclosed garden."

Six sentences. Two words quoted. One contradiction. One macro statement. **Level 4.**

### The five moves, in order

| # | Move | Time | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Timing discipline.** 8 min reading / 4 min retrieval / 14 Q3 / 14 Q6 / 20 Q7, with a hard rule: at 40 minutes, stop and start Q7. | 5 min to learn | Recovers up to 7 marks and costs nothing. Simulation: +0 on the mean alone but it is the precondition for everything else. |
| 2 | **Three practice cycles of Contradiction → Zoom → Macro.** One paragraph each, marked by the tutor against the grid, then rewritten to Level 4. | 20 min per cycle | This is the SRSD loop. It is the only move that raises the ceiling. |
| 3 | **AO5 proofread, four passes.** | 10 min | +2 on B and +2 on C in the simulation; 18 marks available. |
| 4 | **Q8: one paragraph per bullet, each bullet a different job** (what / why it matters / what to do). | 10 min | Fixes the repetition directly, worth +1–2 on AO1. |
| 5 | **Section C: 10+ paragraphs, intro previews nothing, conclusion summarises nothing.** | 10 min | Section C needs +4; paragraph count and a clean intro are the cheapest route to AO4 Level 4. |

### The cycle protocol (move 2) — the only part that needs another person

1. **Model** (tutor): one annotated Level 4 paragraph, labelled with the three steps.
2. **Attempt** (learner): one paragraph, 8 minutes, on a *different* quotation, using the three steps.
3. **Feed-forward** (tutor): not "this is good", but "your step 2 is present and your step 3 is missing;
   rewrite adding one sentence that connects to the end of the extract."
4. **Rewrite** (learner): 4 minutes.
5. Repeat on a new quotation. **Three cycles, not thirty.**

### What to do if time collapses to one hour

Only move 1 and move 3. Timing plus proofread. That is the guaranteed +6 to +8, and it takes a
grade 5–6 to a grade 6–7 without touching analysis at all.

## Part 5 — What can still break this

1. **The 22/30 is unverified.** If B is really 19, target 69 needs another 3 marks elsewhere.
2. **A Level 4 paragraph produced once is not a habit.** Under exam pressure the default (Level 3)
   will reassert itself. The three cycles exist to make the default the new one.
3. **Q7 remains the least practised question.** It is worth 15 and it is last. It needs the same
   contradiction engine, applied across two texts.
4. **Fatigue.** The research on massed practice says cramming holds up for a next-day test; the
   research on sleep says losing it costs up to 40% of retrieval. Sleep wins.
