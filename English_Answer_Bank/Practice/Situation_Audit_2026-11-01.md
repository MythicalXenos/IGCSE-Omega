3. **Everything else is Level 3.** Level 3 is "clear, appropriate, relevant" — the answer is not wrong,
   it is undeveloped. Level 4 is one clause away, not one year away.

## 3. The evidence about this learner's ceiling

The only marked production from this learner is two paragraphs on the June 2025 Yemen text. Both were
**Level 3** by the AO2 grid. That is *above* the level implied by their mock Q3 score.

So the self-report ("I can't think of this by myself") understates demonstrated performance. The
constraint is not ceiling. It is **production rate, consistency and structure** — three things that
respond to routine, not to insight.

## 4. Realistic outcome distribution

Judged against the boundary stability above, and assuming the remaining study time is used on
structure rather than on new skills:

| Outcome | Mark | Probability | What produces it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grade 3 or below | <37 | ~5% | something goes wrong (illness, blank Q7, Section C collapse) |
| Grade 4 | 48–50 | ~25% | current habits + exam conditions only |
| Grade 5–6 | 51–56 | ~40% | AO5 proofread + retrieval + one more Q7 paragraph |
| Grade 7 | 58–59 | ~20% | all of the above + Q3/Q6 at 6/10 + full Q7 structure |
| Grade 8 | 63–64 | ~8% | the above plus Section C AO4 reaching 15+ |
| Grade 9 | 68+ | **~2%** | requires Section C AO4 at 17–20 (Level 5) — a writing-quality jump, not a rule |

**The honest reading: grade 9 in two days from a grade 3 base is a 1-in-50 outcome.** The realistic
target is **grade 6, with grade 7 as the stretch**. Chasing the 9 does not lower these probabilities —
the work that maximises P(9) is the same work that maximises expected marks — but *believing* a 9 is
required, and treating anything less as failure, does damage: it produces panic, resource-collecting
and avoidance.

## 5. Behavioural risks observed in this session

1. **Resource collection substituting for production.** Three toolkits arrived in 48 hours (the bank,
   the phrase bank, the zoom table). No timed answer has been written yet.
2. **Comparison against finished models**, produced with the whole text, unlimited time and the mark
   scheme open. This reliably produces despair and is not a valid benchmark.
3. **Acceptance criteria that cannot be met**: "consistent 9s and 8s in practice before the exam" is
   impossible by definition — consistency requires multiple attempts over days.
4. **Volume intolerance.** Long explanations are being rejected, correctly. Short, concrete, one task
   at a time is the only format that works here.

## 6. The five highest-value actions, ranked by marks per minute

| Rank | Action | Minutes | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AO5 proofread drill** — four passes on a written paragraph | 20 | +4 to +6 |
| 2 | **Q7: both texts in every paragraph, six fixed axes** | 25 | +4 to +5 |
| 3 | **Q3/Q6: five paragraphs, one choice each, question words at the end** | 30 | +4 |
| 4 | **Q8: one paragraph per bullet, each bullet a different job** | 15 | +2 |
| 5 | **Retrieval discipline: exact number of items, text's own words** | 5 | +1 |

Total: **95 minutes for roughly 15–18 marks** — i.e. 45 → 60–63, a grade 6–7.

Anything beyond that requires AO4 in Section C to reach Level 5, which is writing quality, and cannot
be installed in a day.

## 7. Sleep

Massed study beats spaced study for a test **within 24 hours** (Kornell 2009: 34% vs 54% only when
spacing is available; for next-day tests cramming holds up). Sleep deprivation, by contrast, impairs
consolidation and retrieval by up to 40% (Stickgold 2005). For a single next-day paper the ordering is:
**one hour of retrieval practice > one hour of rereading > one extra hour at the cost of sleep.**

Six to eight hours tonight is worth more than any further study block.
