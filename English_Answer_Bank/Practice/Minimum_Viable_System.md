# The minimum viable system — no insight required

**For a candidate scoring 50–55, targeting 70+ without needing to be clever.**
Every item below is a rule you follow, not a thought you have. Use with any paper in
`Paperlords_Archive`. Nothing here needs the "insight" that made the models feel unreachable.

---

# Part A — Section A floor (bolted on)

## A1. Retrieval: 5 guaranteed marks

Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5 are worth **5 marks total** and cost **4 minutes**. Rules:

- Use **only** the line numbers named in the question.
- Give **exactly** the number of things asked for. One for a 1-mark, two for a 2-mark.
- Copy the text's own words. Do not paraphrase a one-mark item.
- Never write three alternatives hoping one lands — pick one.
- Move on. These are not worth five minutes.

## A2. The Q3 / Q6 sentence machine (10 marks)

Six paragraphs, each built from this skeleton. Fill the blanks; do not compose.

> The writer uses **______** — "**______**". This makes the reader **______**, so the reader **______** that **______**.

**Blank 1** — pick from this list, and only this list:

| Category | Ready-made phrases |
|---|---|
| Word choice | the verb "…", the adjective "…", the noun "…" |
| Contrast | the contrast between "…" and "…" |
| List | the list "…, …, …" |
| Repetition | the repetition of "…" |
| Sentence length | the short sentence "…" / the long sentence beginning "…" |
| Pronouns | the pronoun "we" / "you" / "I" |
| Direct address | the direct address "you" |
| Opening/closing | the opening sentence / the final sentence |
| Tone | the informal tone / the admiring tone |

**Blank 2** — two to four words copied exactly from the text.

**Blank 3** — "picture the scene more sharply" / "understand the writer's attitude" / "notice the contrast" / "feel included in the address" / "feel the effort involved".

**Blank 4** — "realises" / "accepts" / "sees why" / "understands".

**Blank 5** — **the question's own words, copied.** Always. Every paragraph.

That is 5–6/10 mechanically, without a single original idea. Add one paragraph that names a **sentence length** or **opening/closing** choice (structural) and you push toward 7.

## A3. Q7 (15 marks)

Six paragraphs. **Both texts in every paragraph.** Skeleton:

> Text One presents **______** through "**______**", whereas Text Two presents **______** through "**______**". The difference shows that **______**.

Repeat six times with different focus. Full stop. Six of those is 10+/15. One-text answers are capped at 6/15 — never write about one text alone.

# Part B — the mark engine

## B1. Section B: Q8 (30 marks = AO1 10 + AO4 12 + AO5 8)

**Before writing, two pen marks:**

1. Underline the **audience noun** ("your peers", "the editor", "readers of the website").
2. Underline the **form noun** ("talk", "article", "letter", "contribution to a website").

**The four permitted forms from November 2025 onward** — article for a school/college magazine or website · speech or talk to peers · contribution to a website · letter. Anything else is not available in a current paper.

**Opening lines by form** (adapt the nouns; do not decorate them):

- **Talk to peers:** "Right — before you switch off, one question: when did you last actually go outside?" Then address them as "you" throughout.
- **Article:** a headline, then a one-sentence standfirst, then a first line that states the angle.
- **Letter:** "Dear Editor," then name the reason for writing in sentence one.
- **Website contribution:** a short punchy first line, then a claim the piece will support.

**The body: one paragraph per bullet point in the question.** The bullets *are* the AO1 grid. Three bullets = three paragraphs, material drawn from **both** texts in each. Skipping a bullet costs 3–5 marks on its own.

**Close by turning back to the audience** — one sentence that tells them what to do or think.

**Length:** 500–600 words. More does not help.

## B2. Section C (30 marks = AO4 20 + AO5 10)

**Choose the option you can paragraph most easily.** For most candidates that is **Q10, narrative**, because events give you paragraph breaks for free.

**Non-negotiables:**

- **10+ paragraphs.** The full-mark scripts in the archive have 11 and 12. Six paragraphs reads as Level 3 regardless of quality.
- **First two lines establish voice and mode.** No weather, no "In this essay I will", no dictionary definition.
- **480–550 words.**

**Q9 discursive — the four-part shape:** claim → counter-argument → rebuttal → conclusion. One paragraph each per cycle, twice.

**Q10 narrative — enter mid-scene, slow time at the crisis.** One paragraph for ten seconds of action. Title may appear in the final line only.

**Q11 descriptive — a place, not a plot.** Organise by space (foreground → distance) or by sense (sound → smell → sight). Strong verbs, not adjectives.

## B3. The proofread (AO5 is 18 marks across the paper)

Four passes, in this order, two minutes each:

1. Every sentence has a main verb and ends with a full stop. No comma splices.
2. `its / it's`, `their / there / they're`, `your / you're`.
3. Capital `I`; capitals at sentence starts; none random mid-sentence.
4. An article (a / an / the) before every singular noun; verb tenses consistent.

Then read the **last paragraph backwards**, sentence by sentence.
