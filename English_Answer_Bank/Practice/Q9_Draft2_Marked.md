# Draft 2 — "We only regret the chances we do not take" — marked, 2 Nov 2026

Candidate text as submitted (3 paragraphs, ~230 words).

## Honest mark as it stands
AO4 12-13 / 20 (Level 3) · AO5 6 / 10 (Level 3). Total ~18-19 / 30.
Reason: thesis is clear and stated, purpose is clear, ideas are relevant and the
decisive distinction is present and original. What is missing is a close (no ending),
one more developed example, and sentence variety.

## What is genuinely good (not encouragement - evidence)
- "this is regret you can measure. It is finite." - the decisive distinction, in the
  candidate's own words, better than the model version. This is the essay.
- "A price. That price is your security." - real short landing, earned.
- "You could regret it forever." - correct instinct for a close.
- "regrettable" used correctly after correction.
- Thesis in paragraph 1 contains the prompt's terms inside a claim of his own.

## Faults
1. "Not taking chances leave you" -> "leaves you" (subject-verb agreement).
2. "When we get the chance to do something. An opportunity, it often comes at a cost."
   -> fragment; merge or cut.
3. "If you were offered to take on" -> "If you were offered the chance to take on".
4. Tense mixing: "If you had the opportunity... you may end up" -> "If you took... you may end up".
5. "in search for" -> "in search of".
6. No ending. The draft stops on its strongest line instead of landing.
7. Every sentence is 15-22 words. No short sentence anywhere except the two landings.

## What imitation was costing
The candidate's plain register is an asset. The examiner reports punish the opposite of
plain: "pompous and overly formal", "over-ambitious vocabulary", "heavily scaffolded".
Writing "like the model" would have been a downgrade. Write plainly, on purpose.

## Why switching prompts is not the fix
On 3 Nov the candidate cannot choose the prompt. Whatever Q9 statement appears is the one
to be written. Practising only on loved prompts trains a skill that will not be available
in the exam. The transferable skill is writing an unloved prompt competently.
