## Full transcript

Uncertain readings are bracketed. Nothing is silently repaired. There are no struck-out words in this script.

### Intro — p. 43

> Some people may endorse the idea that new opportunities present new challenges. I, as a matter of fact, am among those who agree. My aim here will be to convince you that, indeed, new opportunities do present new challenges.

### ¶1 — job offers — p. 43 → p. 44

> First and foremost, job offers are a worthy example of this. This is true because no matter how / much you prepare, you'll never be fully equipped for a full-time job. It takes time to adjust, even when you've had a part-time job in the past. You'll have studied for the job, knowing what you'll need to do; you'll have practiced for it, with an aforementioned part-time job; but will you really be prepared for the stress that a job requires you to handle? It's a challenge you will have to face.

### ¶2 — scholarships — p. 44

> Secondly, the fact of the matter and inescapable truth is that getting a scholarship to a world renowned school is just as bad. I've seen toppers go to universities like Rochester [or/at] Harvard and barely manage to keep up there. The pressure is unlike any other. You even have to keep up your social life whilst also doing all of the chores at home yourself. There's barely any time left to study. But, of course, being able to overcome this leaves you with an impeccable ability of discipline and time management.

### ¶3 — parenting — p. 44 → p. 45

> Then we have parenting, an experience some dream of, and some dread. New parents, of course, face the challenge of raising their children. Some say it's utterly painful, and others / say they enjoy every second of it. Both are right in their own regards. Having a child is like having a bundle of joy at all times. It learns to walk, talk and eventually starts having friends before you know it. Of course, it is a hassle to change their diapers when they spoil themselves every so often. It really does get annoying when they start wailing at the slightest inconvenience. But at the end of the day, it still is a bundle of joy.

### ¶4 — the candidate's own experience — p. 45

> Lastly, I'd like to present my own experience. A good while back, I had the opportunity to participate in the national math olympiad. The first time I participated, I really wasn't that good at it. But it gave me a glimpse of what these olympiads were like. Eventually I started to join the other olympiads, I had to study for them, challenge myself. Challenges that I wouldn't find elsewhere.

### Conclusion — p. 45 → p. 46

> In ending, it must be reiterated that, new opportunities do indeed present new challenges. There may still be some who oppose this view, but opposing arguments fall flat when up against / points like the challenge of parenting.

*(The script ends there. The last line sits alone at the top of printed p. 46, above the ResultsPlus box. It stops mid-argument — "when up against points like the challenge of parenting" is not a complete resolution, and there is no sentence after it.)*

## What it actually does — the anatomy

| Move | The script's version |
|---|---|
| **Announces its purpose explicitly** | "My aim here will be to convince you that, indeed, new opportunities do present new challenges." |
| **Lists examples under ordinal signposts** | "First and foremost" · "Secondly" · "Then we have" · "Lastly" |
| **Covers four unrelated domains** | work · education · family · personal |
| **Develops each with concrete detail** | shift patterns and part-time jobs; toppers at Harvard and chores at home; nappies and wailing; the first olympiad where "I really wasn't that good at it" |
| **Gives every example a deficit → gain turn** | stress → "discipline and time management"; pressure → "impeccable ability"; nappies → "still is a bundle of joy"; not good at it → "challenges that I wouldn't find elsewhere" |
| **Uses one simile** | "like having a bundle of joy at all times" |
| **Uses one genuine rhetorical question** | "but will you really be prepared for the stress that a job requires you to handle?" |
| **Restates the thesis in the conclusion** | "it must be reiterated that, new opportunities do indeed present new challenges" |
| **Dismisses the opposition in one clause** | "opposing arguments fall flat when up against points like the challenge of parenting" |

## What it does **not** do

- **No concession paragraph.** The other side gets eleven words, at the very end, and is not argued with.
- **No paragraph connects to the last one.** Four separate topics in a row, ordered by "firstly/secondly/then/lastly".
- **No resolution.** The conclusion repeats the introduction. Nothing the reader learns in ¶1–¶4 changes the final position.
- **No sophisticated vocabulary.** "the fact of the matter and inescapable truth is", "Both are right in their own regards", "an impeccable ability of discipline".
- **No developed counterargument**, no analogy beyond the bundle-of-joy simile, no definition pressure, no concession.
- **It is not even error-free.** "In ending" for "In conclusion"; "math" for "maths"; a comma splice in "Eventually I started to join the other olympiads, I had to study for them"; and the script stops mid-thought.

**And it still scored 26/30.**

## Why it scored 26 — read the examiner's words again

> "sharply focused on purpose"
> "maintains the focus on discussing the statement"
> "provides a range of ideas"

Three things, and all three are present in the anatomy table above:

1. **Every paragraph is visibly about the statement.** Job offers, scholarships, parenting, the olympiad — each one is explicitly tied back: "job offers are a worthy example of this", "is just as bad", "face the challenge of", "I had the opportunity to… Challenges that I wouldn't find elsewhere". No paragraph drifts.
2. **There is a range of ideas** — four different areas of life, each with its own detail. Range, not depth.
3. **The purpose is stated and never lost.** The candidate says what they will do in sentence three and then does exactly that for 500 words.

## Retractions — where this contradicts what I taught you yesterday

I built the previous lesson from the examiner reports' *general* language about "more successful candidates" — balanced judgement, counterarguments, strong conclusions. This script is a **real 26/30** and it does several of the things I told you were failures. I was wrong on these points, and here is the correction:

| What I told you | What the 26/30 script does | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Intro announces a stance instead of the issue" is a failure | Opens *"Some people may endorse the idea… I, as a matter of fact, am among those who agree"* — pure stance announcement — then *"My aim here will be to convince you"* | **Retracted.** A clear stance plus a stated aim scored 17/20 AO4. |
| "I am going to…" style framing is penalised | "My aim here will be to convince you that…" is the same move in different words | **Retracted.** What is penalised is *pre-taught phrasing* ("as the dust settles"), not stating your purpose. |
| Paragraphs must answer the previous paragraph | They do not. "First and foremost / Secondly / Then / Lastly" over four unrelated topics | **Retracted.** Ordinal signposting over a range of examples scored bottom-Level-5. |
| The other side needs its own paragraph | Eleven words at the very end | **Retracted.** but see §"the 4 withheld marks" below |
| The conclusion must resolve, not restate | It restates: "it must be reiterated that…" | **Retracted.** |
| No rhetorical questions of the hook kind | "will you really be prepared for the stress…?" — a real question, doing work | Consistent with what I said, worth noting |
| AO5 9–10 needs near-flawless punctuation | "In ending", "math", a comma splice — 9/10 anyway | **Overstated.** Accuracy matters; perfection does not. |

**What survives from the previous lesson:** the banned pre-taught phrases (still named in the report), the value of concrete detail over vague assertion, the danger of invented statistics (the 17/30 script), and the four AO5 error categories — those are real and repeatedly named.

## The recipe this script supports

Achievable, evidence-backed, and it fits how you already write.

**Shape — 5 paragraphs, ~450 words**

| ¶ | Content | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stance + aim**, in your own words: what you think, and what you are going to show. Then a one-line preview of the ground you'll cover. | 50 |
| 2 | **Example 1** — one domain, developed with two or three concrete details, then the turn: what the difficulty costs and what it gives. | 110 |
| 3 | **Example 2** — a different domain, same shape. Different domain is the point: it produces "a range of ideas". | 110 |
| 4 | **Example 3, personal** — something that happened to you or someone you can name. The examiner singles out range; a first-person example is cheap range. | 110 |
| 5 | **Conclusion** — restate the thesis with one addition the middle paragraphs earned, and give the opposition one clause. | 60 |

**The five rules that come straight out of this script**

1. **Every paragraph must name the statement's terms.** "…is a worthy example of this" / "…is just as bad" / "…face the challenge of…". That is what "sharply focused on purpose" means in practice.
2. **Two or three real details per example.** Not "it is hard" but "you have to keep up your social life whilst also doing all of the chores at home yourself". Specificity is what stops an example being a topic label.
3. **Every example gets a turn.** Cost, then gain. The script does this four times. It is the single most repeatable move in it.
4. **Use ordinal signposts.** "First and foremost", "Secondly", "Then we have", "Lastly". They are not sophisticated, and they are not penalised — they make the structure visible in a way an examiner can see in thirty seconds.
5. **End in your own words with the position slightly moved.** The script's ending is its weakest point and probably where some of the withheld marks went.

## The 4 withheld marks

It got 17/20 and 9/10 — the bottom of both top bands. The likely shortfall, judged against the reports' own descriptions of *more* successful answers, is precisely what I described in the previous lesson: **no real concession, no resolution, and an argument that stops mid-sentence.** So the honest position is:

- **Do what this script does → about 26/30.**
- **Add one concession paragraph and a conclusion that resolves rather than repeats → the path past 26**, on inference only, because no 28–30 Q9 script is readable anywhere I can reach.

Given you have one exam and limited time: **learn this script's shape first.** It is real, it is marked, and it is within reach.
