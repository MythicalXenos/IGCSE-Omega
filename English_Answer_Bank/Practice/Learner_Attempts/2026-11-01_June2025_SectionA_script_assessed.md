## 1. The finding: the script contains two kinds of paragraph, and only one of them scores

### Type S — the swap paragraph (load-bearing)
States what the writer chose **instead of** the obvious alternative, and why.

Written twice, explicitly, and they are the two best moments in the paper:

> "The writer uses **'magical'** here instead of **'nice'** because it emphasizes the beauty of the place and reflects that it is otherworldly rather than merely pleasant." (Q3 ¶3)

> "The word **'magnificent'** is used because it reflects grandeur and vastness rather than **beautiful**." (Q6 ¶4)

That second one is the strongest sentence in the script. It is a genuine Level 4 comparison: he names the word the writer *didn't* use and measures the distance. Nobody told him to write it that way; it is already his instinct.

Two further paragraphs are the same move with the alternative left implicit — "spellbound" → *otherworldly, magical*; "nerve and foolishness" → *not courage*.

**Type S count: 2 explicit + 2 near = 4.** These carry nearly all the marks.

### Type P — provenance used as analysis (worse than zero)
"This is because Text one is adapted from a novel, Text two is adapted from a travel book" (Q7 ¶7) reaches for the text's *source* to explain a difference in *perspective*. Form is never the answer; the answer is what the writer wanted the reader to think or feel. In Q7 this sentence arrives exactly where the mark-bearing conclusion should go, so it doesn't just fail to score — it displaces the sentence that would have scored.

## 3. The error to fix immediately

> "Neither writers ever use direct address."

Text Two uses second person twice: "it really is extraordinary how often **you** encounter some little kindly gesture" (l.23), "if **you** are stupid **you** do" (l.34). The sentence is false as well as empty. Also "Neither writers" → "Neither writer". A false claim costs more than a missing one, because it tells the examiner the candidate isn't checking.

Same category, smaller: `"on nerves" I guess`, `I dk`, `(Or if this counts)`. Written doubt inside an answer reads as a candidate who doesn't know. Say it or don't.

## 4. What actually to change — one question

Every scoring paragraph in this script answers one question, and every non-scoring paragraph fails to:

> ### "What did the writer **not** say?"
>
> Name the ordinary word or the ordinary choice. Then say why they went the other way.

It is already there, four times, unforced: *magical, not nice. Magnificent, not beautiful. Nerve and foolishness, not courage. Spellbound, not impressed.*

It is not a generative prompt — it is a **look-up**. For any quotation you have already chosen, the alternative is one of a small set: the plain adjective, the obvious simile, the sentence you expected, the ending you expected. So:

1. You have already underlined a quotation. Good.
2. Write the plain version of it. *He says it was lovely. He says it was magical.*
3. The distance between the two is the point. One sentence.

That is the whole paragraph. It costs what a Type V paragraph costs and it scores, where Type V scores nothing.

**The six phrases to strike on sight** (any of these appearing means the paragraph is not yet a paragraph):

1. This helps the reader visualize / picture…
2. This makes it feel more personal…
3. …uses descriptive and vivid language / heavy imagery…
4. This attracts the reader / hooks the reader. *(bare — no mechanism)*
5. This text is adapted from a novel / a travel book…
6. The writer uses imagery to show the beauty of…

Note what this is *not*: it is not an instruction to write more, faster, or in longer paragraphs. It is an instruction to **delete about 12 sentences and spend the saved space on the question in the box.** Net word count can fall and the mark can rise.

## 5. Concession: the plan's Level 4 lever was not used, and that is fine

Plan v2 §4 prescribed "exactly one contradiction or macro sentence per paragraph." This script contains **zero** sentences of that shape — no "This sits against ___ earlier, where the writer said ___", no "which is why the extract doesn't end on ___ but on ___."
