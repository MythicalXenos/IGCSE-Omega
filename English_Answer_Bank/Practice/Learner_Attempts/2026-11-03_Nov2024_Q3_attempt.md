## Mark: AO2 5/10 (Level 3)

## What is right
Three real points exist in there:
1. Word choice: "centuries" conveys scale.
2. Rhetorical question, answered in the next paragraph — and he notices the STRUCTURAL
   relationship ("in the paragraph thereafter"), which is the thing most candidates miss.
3. Expert opinion used for credibility.

Point 2 is genuinely a Level 3/4 idea. Noticing that the question is answered in the following
paragraph is a structural observation, not a content one.

## What costs the marks
1. REPETITION. The credibility idea appears in P2 ("credible and trustworthy"), P4 ("credible...
   likely to trust") and P5 ("persuasive... act as evidence"). Three paragraphs for one point.
   The result is that ~235 words contain three distinct points, not five or six.
2. EFFECTS ARE GENERIC. "engages the reader", "makes them think" — the examiner cannot tell what
   the reader was engaged BY. Effects need a feeling and a cause.
3. NO SENTENCE-LEVEL COMMENT. The L3 descriptor names "vocabulary AND sentence structure". There is
   no sentence structure anywhere in the answer.
4. THE BEST INSTINCT WAS ABANDONED. "The writer uses the rule of 3 / cumulative structural" was
   struck out. That was the only point that would have reached Level 4, and it was cut.
5. MISQUOTATION. The text reads "rejoice collectively"; the answer writes "revive". The text reads
   "Birthdays first became 'A Thing' in Ancient Egypt"; the answer writes "a thing in ancient egypt"
   without capitals or quotation marks. Q3 explicitly requires "brief quotations".
6. COMPOSING AND REVISING AT THE SAME TIME. Nine struck-out starts in 235 words. Every one is time
   spent twice.

## The speed arithmetic
3 points in 27 minutes = 9 minutes per point. The exam allows 15 minutes for 6-8 points =
2 minutes per point. The gap is not thinking speed; it is that each paragraph is being rewritten
in place instead of written once.
