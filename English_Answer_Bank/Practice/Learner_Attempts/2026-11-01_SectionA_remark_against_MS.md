## 1. Three retractions

**(a) "This text is adapted from a novel / a travel book" is a rewardable point. I called it zero. Wrong.**
May 2025 MS p.15, *Points of comparison*, first bullet, verbatim: **"Text One is a fictional narrative/Text Two is a personal account from a travel book"**. It is also a MS-listed general point on p.14 and p.15. In Q7 the sentence earns. It was only wrong in Q3 and Q6, where the question asks how the writer *presents* something and the source of the text is not on the list.

**(b) "First person" is a rewardable point. I called it zero. Wrong.**
MS p.12, final bullet of the Q6 indicative content, verbatim: **"it is a first-person account."** Also p.15 for Q7 ("is from a first-person perspective", listed under both texts). It is worth little *as stated* — "makes it feel more personal" is the thinnest possible form — but it is a listed point, not a non-point.

**(c) "I guess / I dk / if this counts" — withdrawn entirely.**
That was spoken to me, not written. Nothing to correct in the answer. The underlying issue is real and different: he was unsure how to use "nerve" because he had only met it in idioms (*get on someone's nerves*, *the nerve of him*, *not have the nerve to*). MS p.11 rewards exactly this quotation: *"the use of 'nerve' and 'foolishness' shows how dangerous it is, which is highlighted by the verb 'creep' showing how cautious one must be."* The MS reading is *danger*, not self-awareness. His reading — that the writer is mocking himself — is a different and defensible effect, but **the MS also points at 'creep', which he ignored**. So the paragraph could have had two effects for the same quote.

## 3. The real question: would this survive an unseen text?

He says he would fail on different texts. Test it against the two authentic comparators rather than against opinion.

**The 8/15 Q7 script (June 2023 P1R)** knows both texts perfectly. It summarises: Ben's work experience, Nicky's *Tomorrow's World*, "In contrast to Nicky's experience that directed her towards her career, Ben's experience was not as pleasant." Published mark: **8/15, Level 3**, Pearson noting it "needs a wider range and further development." It had full text knowledge and still scored below what this script would score, because it compares **content** rather than **writers' perspectives**. Text knowledge is not what moves 8 → 13.

**The 8/10 Q3 script (June 2023 P1R)** reels off device after device — positive adjectives, statistics, dialogue, imagery, exclamation mark, the title — with several effects as generic as "creates an image that depicts Gymshark as a…". It scores **8/10 Level 4**. What it has is *coverage*: five developed paragraphs, ~430 words, a named device in every one, and a closing statement about overall presentation.

So the binding variable in both anchors is **number of points covered × named device in each**, not prior familiarity with the passage.

**But the fear is half-right, and here is the exact mechanism:** the MS rewards *specific* points, and on an unseen text he cannot know which ones are listed. That is testable against the documents. Comparing the Q3 indicative content of three papers:

| Category in the indicative content | Jun 2023 | Jun 2024 | May 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening move (first sentence/word) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Closing move / final sentence | ✓ | — | ✓ |
| Contrast between two parts of the text | — | ✓ | ✓ |
| Listing / tricolon | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Named simile or metaphor | ✓ | — | ✓ |
| Positive vs negative language | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Direct address / inclusive pronouns | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Informal / colloquial tone | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Repetition or alliteration | ✓ | — | ✓ |
| Numbers / statistics | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Structural feature (sub-headings, numbering, ordering) | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| First person / personal experience | ✓ | — | ✓ (Q6) |
| Imperative / rhetorical question | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Reader-effect verb attached (shock, reassure, curiosity, alarm) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |

**No paper uses all fourteen. Every paper's list is a subset of these fourteen.** Which means a fixed fourteen-item sweep over any unseen text cannot miss a listed point — it can only waste one. That converts "I won't know what to write" into "run the list and delete what doesn't apply", which is the closed, non-generative procedure that was being asked for.

The five categories present in **all three** papers — **contrast, listing/tricolon, positive vs negative language, opening move, reader-effect verb** — are the ones to sweep first when time is short.

## 4. Hand fatigue — the argument for writing less on Section A

His stated constraint is not thinking speed, it is the hand. That changes the arithmetic, and in his favour:

- Section A total ≈ 820 words. Removing the paragraphs that earn nothing — Q3 ¶4–5, Q6 ¶1, Q7 ¶2 is thin but the two true no-hopers are Q3 ¶5 and the false "direct address" line — plus tightening the vague sentences, cuts roughly **120–150 words**.
- Those words are hand capacity. Banked, they are worth more in **Section C**, which is 500–600 words of continuous writing and where the hand is most likely to fail. Section C AO4 and AO5 are worth **30 marks** of the 100 (MS p.22–23); Section A is 30. Equal value, and Section C is the half that degrades under fatigue.
- Net: **the same or better Section A mark for fewer words, and a fresher hand for Section C.** Nothing here requires writing faster.

## 5. Section B and C — what the MS actually requires

Both are marked on grids, not on indicative content alone, and in both the top band is a *tone* judgement, not a quantity judgement.

**Q8 (Section B, 30 marks): AO1 10 + AO4 12 + AO5 8** (MS p.18–20).
- **AO1 top band 9–10:** "apt and **persuasive in clarifying** the points being made", "a **wide range** of relevant points", "**perceptive** references". AO1 7–8 is "appropriate, detailed", "a **good** number". So the 9–10 band is bought with a *wide range* of points, not with more words.
- **AO4 top band 11–12:** "perceptive and subtle", "**sharply focused** on purpose", "**sophisticated** use of form, tone and register." Note the bottom of this grid: 3–4 is "broadly appropriate", 5–7 "communicates clearly". A talk that reads like an essay sits in 5–7 regardless of its content.
- **AO5 top band 7–8:** "manipulates complex ideas", "extensive vocabulary **strategically**", "punctuates with accuracy **to aid emphasis**".

**Q9–Q11 (Section C, 30 marks): AO4 20 + AO5 10** (MS p.22–23). Same AO4 wording as Q8 but wider bands: 17–20 for the top. AO5 9–10 is the same grid as Q8's 7–8.

**The comparator to work from is already in the bank:** `June_2023_Section_A.md` holds Q8 at **30/30** (AO1 10, AO4 12, AO5 8) and Q8 at **26/30** (AO1 9, AO4 10, AO5 7), plus Q9 at **17/30** twice and Q11 at **23/30** and **26/30**. Those are real examiner-marked scripts at the top, middle and bottom of the range — the right calibration material for B and C, and none of it has been used yet.
