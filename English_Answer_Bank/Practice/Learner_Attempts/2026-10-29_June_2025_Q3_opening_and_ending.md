# Learner attempt — June 2025 P1, Q3: opening and ending (29 Oct 2026)

**Task given:** Look 1 of the four looks — the first and last sentence of Text One, with a "because"
for each. Question: *Explain how the narrator presents his experiences of travelling in the Yemen.*

This file records the learner's own wording verbatim, the assessment against the Q3 grid, and one
model for the same material. The learner's text is not marked with a score — it is an exercise, not a
timed answer — but the level it corresponds to is stated so progress is visible.

---

## 1. What the learner wrote (verbatim)

> The text starts off with "We are here in Yemen at last". The writer uses the words "at last" because
> this helps the reader understand how they took time, effort, and resources to reach there. It
> reflects a strong desire to get to Yemen and also conveys a hint of relief upon reaching it. This
> tells the reader of the setting and the premise immediately.
>
> The writer ends with "It is a magical place". The writer uses the word "magical" instead of "nice"
> because it makes the place feel out of this world whereas, "nice" would have made it feel merely
> pleasant. As a result, the reader leaves with the conclusion that Yemen is a special and
> extraordinary place, reflecting upon the writer's beautiful experiences in Yemen.

---

## 2. Assessment

**Working at:** solid Level 3 on AO2, with one Level 4 habit already present.

Why: the method was executed correctly. The learner took a word out of the quotation ("at last",
"magical"), put the ordinary alternative against it ("nice"), and explained the gap. That is the
single most transferable move on the paper, and it is now demonstrably in place. The "hint of relief"
observation is genuine analysis, not device-spotting.

Four things to fix, in order of marks:

**(a) The referent of "magical" is wrong.** "It is a magical place" refers to the **garden** behind
the walls — "Inside the whitewashed walls was a garden of paradise... It is a magical place" — not to
Yemen. The learner writes "Yemen is a special and extraordinary place". This is a precision error
with consequences: it makes the ending look like a general verdict on the country, when the text has
just spent six paragraphs describing "dangerous places", "unimaginable smells of drains", and
"crumbling tracks". An examiner reading "Yemen is magical" against a passage that calls parts of it
dangerous will mark the response down for misreading, not for style.

**(b) Over-claiming.** "time, effort, and resources" — "at last" supports a long or difficult journey;
it does not support "resources". Restrict claims to what the words carry. Safe version: "a journey
long enough to be worth counting".

**(c) The two sentences were treated separately, not as a frame.** This is the missed level. The
extract opens in eight words of arrival and closes in five words of verdict, with roughly 400 words
between them. The *distance* between them is the structure, and structure is what lifts a Q3 from
Level 3 to Level 4.

**(d) The ending of paragraph two drifts.** "reflecting upon the writer's beautiful experiences in
Yemen" is vague and repeats the question rather than answering it.

---

## 3. The upgrade: what the frame actually does

The text moves from **a quarter of a million square miles** to **one walled garden**. That is the
structural shape of the whole extract, and it is measurable rather than impressionistic:

| Position | Scale | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | a country | "We are here in the Yemen at last" |
| Middle | plains, cliffs, a quarter of a million square miles, Manhattan-sized towns | "an endless expanse of dull brown", "a sea of sand" |
| Closing | one garden behind a gate | "Inside the whitewashed walls... It is a magical place" |

So the narrator's experience is not simply "Yemen is wonderful". It is: the vastness is impressive but
overwhelming — dangerous, loud, smelly — and what finally convinces him is something **small, hidden
and enclosed**. The scale shrinks as the admiration grows.

---

## 4. Model for the same two sentences

> The extract opens and closes on deliberately short sentences — "We are here in the Yemen at last"
> and "It is a magical place" — and everything between them is long, piled-up description. "At last"
> tells the reader the journey has been long enough to be worth counting, so arrival is presented as
> relief before it is presented as anything else; the eight-word sentence lands like someone putting
> down a bag. The final five words are shorter still, and they are not description at all but a
> verdict. Crucially the verdict is not about the Yemen — it is about the garden "inside the
> whitewashed walls". The extract travels from "a quarter of a million square miles of uninhabited
> desert" to a single enclosed garden, and the narrator is only sure he is happy once the scale has
> shrunk. That is what earns "magical" rather than "nice": he has spent the passage being impressed
> and only becomes content when he is hidden.

**Against the grid:** this reaches Level 4 (references "detailed, appropriate and fully support the
points"), because the word-level analysis of "at last" and "magical" is carried by a structural
observation about scale that is true of the whole extract. It is not Level 5 — a Level 5 response
would be doing this across three or four further choices.
