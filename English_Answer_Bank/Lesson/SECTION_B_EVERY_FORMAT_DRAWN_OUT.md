# Every Section B format, drawn out in full — no cross-references

3 Nov 2026. Each format below is written out completely and independently. Nothing says "same as
above". If you only read one, read the one you are least sure of.

Contents
1. Talk
2. Article — school or college website
3. Article — magazine for young adults
4. Website contribution
5. Letter to a friend
6. Letter to a newspaper / editor
7. (Section C) The Q9 essay
8. One page: all seven side by side

---

# 1. TALK

## The layout, in full

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Good afternoon, everyone. Thanks for staying behind. │  GREETING
│ I'll keep this short, which is part of the argument.  │  (audience named out loud)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ I want to talk about ...                              │  ANNOUNCE THE SUBJECT
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ First, ...                                            │  BULLET 1
│ [3 to 6 sentences. One point from Text One, one from  │  (spoken signpost,
│ Text Two, then your own comment or example.]          │   no subheading)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Second, ...                                           │  BULLET 2
│ [3 to 6 sentences, same shape]                        │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Third, the honest part, ...                           │  BULLET 3
│ [3 to 6 sentences, same shape]                        │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ So here is my advice. ...                             │  ADVICE / CALL TO ACTION
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ That is it. Thank you.                                │  THANKS
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Signals on the page: no headline, no subheadings, no "Dear", no sign-off. Just paragraphs.
Contractions: yes.

## Opening, written out
> Good afternoon, everyone. Thanks for staying behind. I will keep this short, which is itself
> part of the argument.
>
> I want to talk about the cheapest thing you can do for yourself, and the thing all of us are
> giving up. Walking.

## Bullet paragraph, written out
> Second, the benefits, and I will not pretend they are complicated. You breathe better. You sleep
> better — genuinely, that is measurable. Things that seemed enormous at four in the afternoon look
> survivable by six. And there is the small one nobody mentions: you start noticing the street you
> have lived on for eleven years. There is a tree outside the shop. I found it in March.

## Closing, written out
> So here is my advice, and it is only one thing. Do not make it a habit. Make it an appointment.
> Tuesday, after school, forty minutes, with someone who will knock on your door.
>
> That is it. Thank you.

# 2. ARTICLE — SCHOOL OR COLLEGE WEBSITE

## The layout, in full

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Your handwriting is getting worse. Why that matters.  │  HEADLINE (big, own line)
│ Nobody is asking for calligraphy. Just for readable.  │  STANDFIRST (1 line, italics)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [2 or 3 sentences setting up the problem. Uses "we"   │  INTRO
│  or "you". No subheading above this one.]             │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Why it still matters                                  │  SUBHEADING 1 (own line)
│ [4 to 6 sentences. Point from Text One, point from    │
│  Text Two, then your own view.]                       │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Why we stopped                                        │  SUBHEADING 2
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ What it costs                                         │  SUBHEADING 3
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [One final line — the shortest sentence in the piece] │  CLOSING
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The three subheadings are the three bullets, renamed in your own words. Voice: "we" for the
school, "you" for the reader. Contractions: yes.

## Opening, written out
> **Your handwriting is getting worse. Here is why that matters.**
> *Nobody is asking you to be calligraphic. We are asking you to be readable.*
>
> There is a moment every teacher has had: a good answer, in an exam, that cannot be marked. Not
> because it is wrong. Because nobody can read it.

## Bullet section, written out
> **Why we stopped**
>
> Obvious answer: keyboards. The less obvious one is that we never practise. You write by hand in
> an exam and nowhere else, so handwriting is the one skill you are assessed on and never trained
> for. Add the notes you take in forty minutes, where speed beats legibility every time, and the
> result is predictable.

## Closing, written out
> Your future examiner has never met you. Give them a fair chance.

# 3. ARTICLE — MAGAZINE FOR YOUNG ADULTS

## The layout, in full

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Time to learn something new?                          │  HEADLINE (the given title)
│ You are not too old, too busy, or too far behind.     │  STANDFIRST (1 line, italics)
│ You are just doing it in the wrong order.             │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ By someone who has tried four hobbies and quit three  │  BYLINE (1 line, optional)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [OPINION FIRST. Not a scene, not a situation — a       │  INTRO
│  claim you are willing to defend. 2-3 sentences.]     │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ What counts                                           │  SUBHEADING 1
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ How to actually do it                                 │  SUBHEADING 2
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Why it is hard                                        │  SUBHEADING 3
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [TWIST: one short sentence that turns the piece]      │  CLOSING
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Voice: one named individual with opinions and a bit of attitude. "I" and "you", mild exaggeration
allowed. Contractions: yes.

## Opening, written out
> **Time to learn something new?**
> *You are not too old, too busy, or too far behind. You are just doing it in the wrong order.*
>
> By the time you finish school, you will have been taught roughly forty subjects and chosen about
> none of them. That is the argument for learning one thing badly, on purpose, as an adult.

## Bullet section, written out
> **Why it is hard**
>
> Three reasons, all real. Time, obviously. Embarrassment, less obviously — being bad at something
> in front of other people is a skill almost nobody practises after the age of sixteen. And the
> plateau: week one feels like flying, week five feels like nothing is happening, and week five is
> when most people stop.

## Closing, written out
> Then be patient with yourself for about six weeks. That is roughly how long a thing takes to stop
> being a decision and start being a Tuesday.

## School article vs magazine article — the whole difference, stated once
| | School or college website | Magazine for young adults |
|---|---|---|
| Who is speaking | a member of the community: "we at this school" | one columnist: "I tried this and failed" |
| First line | a shared situation ("we all do this") | an opinion ("you are doing it wrong") |
| Relationship | equal, friendly, encouraging | writer advising reader, mildly superior |
| Humour | gentle | sharper, self-deprecating or wry |
| Ending | an invitation to act | a twist, a turn of phrase |
| Safest risk | sounding institutional | sounding like an advertisement |

# 4. WEBSITE CONTRIBUTION

## The layout, in full

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ The Room Was Fine. Everything Else Was the Problem.   │  TITLE (own line, big)
│ A contribution from a traveller who has now slept in  │  WHO I AM  ← this line is the form
│ forty-one beds and regretted nine of them.            │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [MY OPINION OR MY THEORY, in first person. 2 or 3     │  INTRO
│  sentences. "I have a theory about ..."]              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Who stays where                                       │  SUBHEADING 1
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ What is good                                          │  SUBHEADING 2
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ What is bad                                           │  SUBHEADING 3
│ [4 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ My own rule                                           │  SUBHEADING 4 (optional)
│ [2 or 3 sentences giving a personal recommendation]   │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Thanks to the site for the space.                     │  CLOSING ADDRESSED TO THE SITE
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Three things make this a contribution and not an article:**
1. The line under the title says **who I am** and why I am worth reading.
2. Everything is in **"I"** — one reader's opinion, including a personal rule at the end.
3. It **ends by speaking to the website or to the other readers.** An article never does this.

Contractions: yes.

## Opening, written out
> **The Room Was Fine. Everything Else Was the Problem.**
> *A contribution from a traveller who has now slept in forty-one beds and regretted nine of them.*
>
> I have a theory about accommodation, and it is this: the room matters far less than the person
> who hands you the key.

## Bullet section, written out
> **What is bad**
>
> Photographs. Every photograph is taken at four in the afternoon in June. The other classic is the
> fee nobody mentions until you are signing — the tourist tax, the city tax, the card fee, the
> charge for the safe you did not use. And the walls: I have listened to an entire argument in a
> language I do not speak, at two in the morning, through eight inches of plaster.

## Closing, written out
> I would rather pay less and leave more. The best place I have ever stayed cost eleven pounds a
> night and had no lock on the door, and I would go back tomorrow.
>
> — with thanks to the site for the space.

# 5. LETTER TO A FRIEND

## The layout, in full

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Dear Hana,                                            │  SALUTATION (comma, not full stop)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ I am writing this on the bus, which tells you how     │  OPENING
│ keen I am about this ...                              │  (personal, not about the topic yet)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ You said last week that ...                           │  SHARED CONTEXT
│                                                       │  (proves you know this person)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ First, ...                                            │  BULLET 1
│ [3 to 6 sentences, speaking to them]                  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Second, ...                                           │  BULLET 2
│ [3 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Third, and this is the part I actually care about ... │  BULLET 3
│ [3 to 6 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ You do not have to agree with me. Just come on        │  SMALL ASK
│ Friday, for one hour ...                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Take care, and say hello to your mum for me.          │  CLOSING LINE
│ Yours,                                                │  SIGN-OFF
│ Zain                                                  │  YOUR NAME
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Contractions: everywhere. That is what proves it is a friend and not an essay.

## Opening, written out
> Dear Hana,
>
> I am writing this on the bus, which tells you how keen I am about this, because I never write
> anything on the bus except messages telling people I am nearly there.
>
> You said last week that art was "not really your thing", and I have been thinking about it ever
> since, mostly because I think you have been sold the wrong version of it.

## Bullet paragraph, written out
> Third, and this is the part I actually care about. Art is the only thing that lets you feel
> something from inside another person's head without any of the awkwardness of asking them. You
> spend forty minutes with somebody's anger, or their grief, or their very bad sense of humour, and
> you come out slightly less alone in the world. That is not a small thing. That is most of the
> reason anybody makes anything.

## Closing, written out
> You do not have to agree with me. Just come on Friday, for one hour, and if you hate it I will
> buy you a coffee and never mention it again.
>
> Take care, and say hello to your mum for me.
>
> Yours,
> Zain

# 6. LETTER TO A NEWSPAPER / EDITOR

## The layout, in full

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Dear Editor,                                          │  SALUTATION (comma)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ I am writing about the lack of after-study activities │  OPENING
│ in our community.                                     │  (the issue, in sentence one.
│                                                       │   No "how are you". No small talk.)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Firstly, the reason there are so few is ...           │  BULLET 1
│ [3 to 5 sentences. Local facts, a named place, a      │
│  number if you have one. No contractions.]            │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Furthermore, the activities that could be offered ... │  BULLET 2
│ [3 to 5 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Finally, the benefits are not only for the students.  │  BULLET 3
│ [3 to 5 sentences]                                    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ I would ask the council to reconsider ...             │  THE ASK
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Yours faithfully,                                     │  SIGN-OFF ("faithfully" — you do not
│ Zain Rahman                                           │  know them. "sincerely" = you do.)
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**No contractions anywhere.** Write "do not", "cannot", "I am", "it is", "we will".
**Use "we" and "our", not "you and I."** You are speaking for a community, to the public.
**No jokes.** Serious, controlled, one long sentence allowed for effect.

## Opening, written out
> Dear Editor,
>
> I am writing about the absence of anything for young people to do in this town after six o'clock.
> Our library closes at five. The youth club closed in 2019 and has not reopened. The nearest
> sports hall is a forty-minute bus ride away, which for a fourteen-year-old is not near at all.

## Bullet paragraph, written out
> Firstly, the reason there are so few activities is not laziness and it is not money. It is
> staffing. Every scheme I have looked into requires two adults present at all times, and the
> volunteers simply are not there. A town of eight thousand people cannot run an evening club on
> goodwill alone; it needs one paid coordinator, and nobody has been willing to fund that post.

## Closing, written out
> I would ask the council to reconsider the funding for a single coordinator, and to publish what
> it would actually cost. If the answer is that it cannot be afforded, we deserve to hear that said
> out loud, in print, by the people who decided it.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Zain Rahman

# 7. SECTION C — THE Q9 ESSAY

## The layout, in full

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [3 MINUTES, NO WRITING: identify the sweep word; fix  │  PLANNING
│  the proportion; fill four places — work, school,     │  (in the margin, not on a
│  family, the exception]                               │   separate sheet)
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PARAGRAPH 1 — OPENING (40-70 words)                   │  POSITION
│ Your claim, in your own words, containing the          │
│ statement's key words. No quotation. No announcement.  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PARAGRAPH 2 — REASON ONE (90-110 words)               │  ARGUMENT
│ Claim → something you can see → one sentence of six   │
│ words or fewer → what it adds up to.                  │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PARAGRAPH 3 — REASON TWO (90-110 words)               │  ARGUMENT
│ A different kind of reason. Same four parts.          │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PARAGRAPH 4 — THE OTHER SIDE, ANSWERED (90-110 w)     │  THE TURN
│ Its best case, seriously → then answer it → then use  │
│ it to sharpen your own claim.                         │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ PARAGRAPH 5 — LANDING (20-40 words)                   │  CONCLUSION
│ The proportion + what follows from it. 1 to 3         │
│ sentences. No "In conclusion". No new idea.           │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [3 MINUTES — READ BACKWARDS, sentence by sentence]    │  ACCURACY
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

No headline, no subheadings, no greeting, no sign-off. The examiner is the audience.

## Opening, written out
> Ask anyone what made their week good and nobody names the thing they bought. They name the walk,
> the phone call, the hour they spent doing nothing at all. **The best things in life are free**,
> and the strange part is that most of us spend our lives trying to pay for them.

## Body paragraph, written out
> Take the clearest example: a Sunday at your grandmother's. The food costs something, but the food
> is not the point. The point is that four people are in one room and nobody is checking their
> phone. **You cannot buy that atmosphere.** You can buy a better dinner, in a better restaurant,
> with worse conversation. Money buys the setting; it does not buy what happens in it.

## The turn, written out
> None of that is the whole truth. Freedom to enjoy free things is not evenly shared: a nurse on a
> double shift has no free evening to walk in, and "the best things are free" can sound like advice
> from someone who has never been poor. That is a fair objection, and it does not defeat the claim.
> **It sharpens it.** The walk is still free. What costs money is the time to take it.

## Landing, written out
> Nobody remembers the receipt. They remember who was at the table.

# 8. ALL SEVEN, SIDE BY SIDE

| | Q9 essay | Talk | School article | Magazine article | Contribution | Letter to friend | Letter to editor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First line | a claim or a scene | "Good afternoon, everyone" | headline + standfirst | headline + standfirst | title + "who I am" | "Dear Hana," | "Dear Editor," |
| Divisions | none | First/Second/Third | subheadings | subheadings | subheadings | paragraphs | Firstly/Furthermore/Finally |
| Voice | "I" or "we", to the examiner | I to you (the room) | we and you | I and you | I only | I to you | we, to the public |
| Contractions | no | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | **no** |
| Last line | proportion + so-what | thanks / call to action | a line that lands | a twist | thanks to the site | "Take care, Zain" | "Yours faithfully, Zain Rahman" |
| What breaks it | no turn; a summary ending | no greeting | no headline or subheadings | no opinion, no personality | no "who I am", no thanks to the site | forming the wrong register (sounding like an essay) | contractions or jokes |
