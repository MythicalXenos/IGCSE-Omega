# Section B — the five formats, drawn out

3 Nov 2026. Plain terms. Each form is laid out as a skeleton with labels, so you can see the shape
on the page before you write a word.

---

## GLOSSARY — the five words you did not know

**HEADLINE** — the big title at the top. In a magazine or on a website it is bigger than everything
else. It is not a summary; it is a hook. Usually 4 to 8 words, often ending in a full stop for
punch. If the question gives you a title, that title IS the headline.

**STANDFIRST** — one line of small text directly under the headline, usually in italics. It is the
sentence that makes someone start reading. Magazines also call it a "dek". One line. Not a
paragraph. Think of it as the sentence a friend would say while handing you the magazine.

**"WE"** — literally the word *we*. In a school website article you are writing as a member of the
school, so you say "we": "we are very good at marking the big things", "we have all done this". It
signals that the writer belongs to the same community as the reader. The alternative, "you", is
used for the reader alone. The best articles use both: "**we** all do this, and **you** know it".

**TWIST AT THE END** — the last line does not just repeat what was said. It says a small unexpected
thing that reframes it. Example ending: "The photographs get lost." (after a piece about travel) or
"Then do it again next month." (after arguing for celebrating small things). It is one short
sentence. It should be the shortest sentence in the piece.

**CONTRACTIONS** — two words joined by an apostrophe where a letter has been removed:

| Long form | Contraction |
|---|---|
| I am | I'm |
| do not | don't |
| cannot | can't |
| it is | it's |
| you are | you're |
| we will | we'll |
| I have | I've |
| would not | wouldn't |
| does not | doesn't |
| that is | that's |

**Use them in:** talks, letters to friends, contributions, magazine articles.
**Do not use them in:** letters to a newspaper or editor (formal). Write "do not", "cannot", "I am".

**CONTRIBUTION** — a piece one reader sends in to a website that publishes writing by its readers.
You are not the website. You are one person in a comment section that has been given space. That is
why it opens by naming who you are and ends by thanking the site.

## FORMAT 1 — TALK

```
[GREETING — say hello to the room out loud]
Good afternoon, everyone. Thanks for staying behind.

[SAY WHAT YOU ARE TALKING ABOUT — "I want to talk about..."]

[BODY — three signposts, one per bullet]
First, ...
Second, ...
Third, ...

[CLOSING — thanks them, or asks them to do something]
That is it. Thank you.
```

No headline. No subheadings. No "Dear". The only things on the page are paragraphs.
**Contractions: yes.** "I'm", "don't", "you'll".

## FORMAT 2 — ARTICLE FOR A SCHOOL OR COLLEGE WEBSITE

```
Your handwriting is getting worse. Here is why that matters.     <- HEADLINE
Nobody is asking you to be calligraphic. We are asking you
to be readable.                                                 <- STANDFIRST (one line, italics)

                                                                <- blank line

[INTRO PARAGRAPH — 2 or 3 sentences, sets the scene]

Why it still matters                                            <- SUBHEADING 1
[paragraph]

Why we stopped                                                  <- SUBHEADING 2
[paragraph]

What it costs                                                   <- SUBHEADING 3
[paragraph]

[CLOSING — 1 or 2 sentences, the twist]
```

A **subheading** is a short line on its own, above a paragraph, that tells the reader what is coming.
Usually 2 to 5 words. It replaces the "First / Second / Third" of a talk.
**Contractions: yes.**

---

## FORMAT 3 — ARTICLE FOR A MAGAZINE (young adults)

Same skeleton as Format 2, with three differences:

```
Time to learn something new?                                    <- HEADLINE (the given title)
You are not too old, too busy, or too far behind. You are
just doing it in the wrong order.                               <- STANDFIRST

By someone who has tried four hobbies and quit three            <- BYLINE (optional, one line)

[INTRO — start with an OPINION, not a scene]
```

1. **It opens with an opinion.** "By the time you finish school, you will have been taught roughly
   forty subjects and chosen about none of them." A school article opens with a shared situation; a
   magazine article opens with a view.
2. **The voice is one person's,** with attitude: "I", "you", mild exaggeration.
3. **The twist at the end is sharper** — a line that turns the piece slightly.

**Contractions: yes.**

## FORMAT 4 — WEBSITE CONTRIBUTION

The one people get wrong. You are a reader who has sent something in. So you must show that.

```
The Room Was Fine. Everything Else Was the Problem.             <- TITLE
A contribution from a traveller who has now slept in
forty-one beds and regretted nine of them.                      <- LINE SAYING WHO YOU ARE
                                                                   (this is what makes it a contribution)

[INTRO — your personal opinion or theory, first person]

Who stays where                                                 <- SUBHEADING 1
[paragraph]

What is good                                                    <- SUBHEADING 2
[paragraph]

What is bad                                                     <- SUBHEADING 3
[paragraph]

My own rule                                                     <- SUBHEADING 4 (optional)

[CLOSING — say something TO the website or TO other readers]
Thanks to the site for the space. I read the comments here more than I should.
```

**The three things that make it a contribution and not an article:**
1. The line under the title says **who you are** and why you are qualified to write it.
2. It is written in **"I"** — one person's view, with a personal rule at the end.
3. It **ends by addressing the website or the other readers.** An article does not do this.

**Contractions: yes.**

## FORMAT 5a — LETTER TO A FRIEND

```
Dear Hana,                                                      <- SALUTATION, comma after the name

[OPENING — personal, casual, not about the topic yet]
I am writing this on the bus, which tells you how keen I am...

[BODY — three paragraphs, one per bullet, each speaking TO them]
First, ...
Second, ...
Third, ...

[ASK THEM SOMETHING / GIVE THEM A SMALL ACTION]
Just come on Friday, for one hour, and if you hate it I will
buy you a coffee and never mention it again.

Take care, and say hello to your mum for me.                    <- CLOSING LINE
Yours,                                                          <- SIGN-OFF
Zain                                                            <- YOUR NAME
```

**Contractions: yes, everywhere.** "I'm", "you're", "don't", "can't". Using them is what proves it
is a friend and not an essay.

---

## FORMAT 5b — LETTER TO A NEWSPAPER (formal)

```
Dear Editor,                                                    <- SALUTATION, comma

[OPENING — state the issue in the first sentence, no small talk]
I am writing about the lack of after-study activities in our community.

[BODY — three paragraphs, one per bullet, arguing in public]
Firstly, ... Furthermore, ... Finally, ...

[ASK FOR THE CHANGE]
I would ask the council to reconsider...

Yours faithfully,                                               <- SIGN-OFF (faithfully, not sincerely)
Zain Rahman                                                     <- FULL NAME
```

**No contractions.** Write "do not", "cannot", "I am", "it is", "we will".
**"We" and "our", not "you and I".** You are speaking for a community, to the public.
**No jokes.** Measured, controlled, slightly serious.

## THE WHOLE THING, AS ONE TABLE

| Form | Opens with | Divisions | Ends with | Contractions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Talk | "Good afternoon, everyone" | First / Second / Third | thanks them | yes |
| School article | headline + standfirst | subheadings | a line that lands | yes |
| Magazine article | headline + standfirst + opinion | subheadings | a twist | yes |
| Contribution | title + who I am | subheadings | thanks to the site | yes |
| Letter to friend | "Dear Hana," | paragraphs | "Take care, Zain" | yes |
| Letter to editor | "Dear Editor," | paragraphs | "Yours faithfully, Zain Rahman" | **no** |

---

## THE 60-SECOND DRILL

Before writing, say out loud: **"This is a ___ to ___."**
Then write only the first two lines and look at them.
- If it starts "Good afternoon, everyone" — talk.
- If it starts with a big line and a small line under it — article or contribution.
- If it starts "Dear" — letter.
- If the second line says who you are and the last line thanks the site — contribution.

If you cannot tell which form your first two lines belong to, the examiner cannot either.
