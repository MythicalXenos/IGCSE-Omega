# SECTION A - FILL-IN TEMPLATES
3 Nov 2026. Companion to SECTION_A_HOW_TO_COMMENT.md and SECTION_A_COMMENTARY_BANK_TWO.md.
Every template is a sentence with blanks. Put your own words in the blanks. Do not memorise these as
essays; memorise the SHAPE, because the blanks change with every paper.

How to use: write the four words QUOTE / MOVE / EFFECT / SO WHAT at the top of your answer space,
then fill each paragraph through that order. Each paragraph below is one of those four steps.

===========================================================================
A. THE UNIVERSAL FOUR-LINE PARAGRAPH - works for ANY feature, any text
===========================================================================

LINE 1 - THE MOVE (what the writer does)
    The writer ______________________ [presents / describes / argues / asks / repeats / admits]
    ______________________ [the idea] as ______________________ [what she makes it seem like].

LINE 2 - THE EVIDENCE (the quotation, always in quotation marks)
    She does this when she writes "______________________________" .

LINE 3 - THE EFFECT (what happens to the reader)
    This makes the reader feel ______________________ , because ______________________ .

LINE 4 - THE SO WHAT (why she wanted that)
    She does this so that the reader will ______________________ [believe / trust / act / feel
    included / stop doubting] ______________________ .

If you only have time for three of the four, drop line 1. Never drop line 4.

===========================================================================
B. REGISTER - the picker
===========================================================================

STEP 1 - TEST IT. Tick what you find in the text:
    Contractions?            none / some / many
    "I" or "you"?            absent / some / constant
    Long words?              formal ("innate", "commemorated") / mixed / plain ("don't force it")
    Sentences?               long and joined / varied / short with dashes
    Headings?                none / subheadings / many

STEP 2 - NAME IT.
    Mostly left-hand ticks  -> FORMAL
    Mostly middle           -> SEMI-FORMAL
    Mostly right-hand       -> INFORMAL

STEP 3 - WRITE IT.
    The writer's register is ______________ , shown by ______________________________
    [name two things you ticked] .

    For example, she writes "______________________________" .

    This makes the reader feel ______________________ , because ______________________ .

    It suits her purpose of ______________________ [informing / persuading / advising / reassuring] ,
    because ______________________ .

STEP 4 - THE EFFECT, ONE LINE EACH, PICK ONE:
    INFORMAL   -> "…makes the reader feel advised by someone like them rather than lectured by
                   someone above them, so the advice is easier to accept."
    FORMAL     -> "…makes the reader feel the piece has been researched and edited, so the claims
                   carry weight before they are even considered."
    SEMI-FORMAL-> "…makes the reader feel informed without being instructed, which suits a piece
                   written for the general public."

===========================================================================
C. SUBHEADINGS
===========================================================================

LEVEL 1 - what they physically do (weaker, use only as an opener):
    The writer breaks the text into sections with subheadings such as "______________" and
    "______________" .

LEVEL 2 - what they do to the reader (use this):
    These let the reader ______________________ [scan the whole piece before reading / skip to the
    part that applies to them / see how many points are coming] , which means
    ______________________ .

LEVEL 3 - what they do to the argument (use this in Q7):
    They also tell the reader what each section is for before they read it, so nothing arrives as a
    surprise, and each point feels ______________________ [separate / complete / settled] .

Q7 CONTRAST VERSION - when only one text has subheadings:
    Text ______ uses subheadings, which let the reader ______________________ ; Text ______ runs
    continuously, so the reader must follow her order. This suits each writer's purpose because
    ______________________ .

===========================================================================
D. PARAGRAPH ORDER AND STRUCTURE
===========================================================================

WHAT TO LIST FIRST - in the margin, write the first two words of each paragraph. You will
immediately see the shape.

COMMON SHAPES, AND WHAT THEY DO:
    history -> science -> advice        = makes the advice feel proven
    general -> specific                 = narrows the reader towards one action
    problem -> explanation -> solution  = the reader never objects, because the objection is
                                          answered before it is raised
    claim -> reasons                    = the reader is given quantity as evidence
    personal -> expert                  = the reader is persuaded by feeling, then by authority

TEMPLATE:
    Her paragraphs move from ______________ to ______________ and then to ______________ .

    That order matters because by the time she says "______________________________" ,
    the reader has already been given ______________________ [the evidence for it / a reason to
    agree / a picture they recognise] .

    The piece also ends on ______________________ [a judgement / a question / an instruction]
    rather than a summary, which leaves the reader with ______________________ .

ONE EXTRA SENTENCE, USEFUL IN ANY STRUCTURE PARAGRAPH:
    Each paragraph answers the question the one before it raised, which makes the piece feel
    inevitable rather than argued.

===========================================================================
E. QUOTATION AND EXPERT OPINION
===========================================================================

LEVEL 1 - the label (earns almost nothing):
    The writer uses expert opinion to support her point.

LEVEL 2 - label plus what the expert is:
    She quotes ______________________ [name] , who is described as a ______________________
    [job] at ______________________ [place] . Giving the expert a job title and a workplace makes
    the claim sound ______________________ [tested / professional] rather than
    ______________________ [felt / personal] .

LEVEL 3 - how many, and what that does:
    She quotes ______ different specialists in a row - a ______________________ , a
    ______________________ and a ______________________ . Because no single voice carries the
    argument, the reader is left feeling the conclusion is shared by ______________________
    [a profession / a field / everyone who studies this] rather than invented by one writer.

LEVEL 4 - when the text quotes direct speech at length (like the Roman invitation):
    The writer lets the source speak in its own words - "______________________________" - instead
    of summarising it, so the reader hears ______________________ [the voice / the person]
    rather than the writer's account of them. This reads as ______________________
    [testimony / evidence] rather than opinion.

ALSO IN THIS FAMILY, AND TREAT THEM THE SAME WAY:
    dates ("around 3,000 BCE")  ->  move a claim out of opinion and into history
    numbers ("4,000-year-old")  ->  make a claim sound measured
    named places or tablets     ->  make a claim sound found rather than made

===========================================================================
F. LISTING
===========================================================================

TEMPLATE:
    She lists "______________________________" .

    Because the list is long and ordinary, celebration feels ______________________
    [constant / normal / unavoidable] rather than occasional, and the reader recognises
    ______________________ [their own calendar / their own week] in it.

VARIANT - when the list is deliberately dull (Text Two's "wake-up, work, eat, sleep, repeat"):
    The list is flat and repetitive on purpose: the rhythm of the sentence
    ______________________ [copies / enacts] the routine it describes, so the reader feels the
    boredom being described rather than being told about it.

VARIANT - rule of three:
    Three items in a row sound complete, so the reader accepts the list as
    ______________________ without counting whether it is.

===========================================================================
G. SHORT SENTENCE
===========================================================================

TEMPLATE:
    After a long sentence, she writes "______________________________" .

    The change of length makes it stand out, because length is the only thing in prose the eye can
    actually see. It also changes the tone: after ______________________ , the sentence sounds
    ______________________ [final / blunt / like her own voice / like a joke] .

===========================================================================
H. DIRECT ADDRESS AND "WE"
===========================================================================

DIRECT ADDRESS ("you"):
    She speaks to the reader as "you" when she writes "______________________________" .
    That turns a general piece of advice into an instruction aimed at one person, so the reader
    cannot stay a spectator - the sentence is now about them.

"WE":
    She writes "we" rather than "you", as in "______________________________" , which places the
    writer inside the group she is describing. The reader therefore feels part of
    ______________________ [a majority / a shared habit] , and a majority is hard to disagree with.

THE SWITCH - if the text moves from "we" to "you", the switch is the interesting moment:
    She begins with "we", then turns to "you" at "______________________________" . That shift moves
    the reader from ______________________ [sharing a problem] to
    ______________________ [being asked to fix it] .

===========================================================================
I. RHETORICAL QUESTION
===========================================================================

DEFINITION TO KEEP IN YOUR HEAD: a question asked to make a point, not to get an answer.

TEMPLATE:
    She asks "______________________________" and ______________________ [answers it in the next
    paragraph / leaves it hanging] .

    The question does two jobs: it makes the reader feel this was something they already wanted to
    know, and it ______________________ [carries them into the next section / makes the answer feel
    earned] .

===========================================================================
J. ANECDOTE (the move your own Q7 answer missed)
===========================================================================

DEFINITION: an anecdote is a short personal story. First person means the writer says "I".

TEMPLATE:
    She stops describing other people and describes herself: "______________________________" .

    This is where the writer becomes a person rather than a voice, which makes the rest of her
    advice sound ______________________ [tested / lived / earned] rather than
    ______________________ [researched / borrowed] .

    The reader trusts her more because ______________________ [she admits she got it wrong first /
    she has actually done it] .

===========================================================================
K. METAPHOR
===========================================================================

METHOD, THREE STEPS: name the two things -> say what the comparison suggests -> say what it makes
the reader feel possible.

TEMPLATE:
    She describes ______________ [thing one] as "______________" [thing two] .

    Calling it that makes it sound ______________________ [manageable / familiar / physical /
    small] rather than ______________________ .

    The reader therefore feels that ______________________ is something they could actually do.

===========================================================================
L. MODAL VERBS (can / should / must)
===========================================================================

TEMPLATE:
    She repeats "can" rather than "must" - "______________________________" .

    That offers the reader ______________________ [possibility / permission] instead of
    ______________________ [obligation / blame] , so her advice never accuses anyone of having
    already failed.

===========================================================================
M. Q7 ONLY - THE COMPARISON PARAGRAPH
===========================================================================

BLANK VERSION, USE THIS SHAPE FIVE TIMES:
    Both writers ______________________ [do what] .

    Text ______ does this by ______________________ , writing "______________________________" .

    Text ______ , however, ______________________ , writing "______________________________" .

    The difference matters because ______________________ [what each writer wants the reader to
    think or do] .

CHECK BEFORE YOU MOVE ON - every comparison paragraph must contain the words "both", "however" (or
"whereas" / "in contrast"), and one quotation from EACH text. If a paragraph has only one quotation,
it is not a comparison and it will not be marked as one.

===========================================================================
N. THE SO-WHAT SPINNER - if you are stuck for line 4
===========================================================================

Pick ONE and finish the sentence:
    …so that the reader will TRUST her when she ______________________ .
    …so that the reader will BELIEVE ______________________ .
    …so that the reader will FEEL ______________________ about themselves.
    …so that the reader will DO ______________________ .
    …so that the reader will STOP ______________________ [doubting / feeling alone / blaming
      themselves] .
    …so that the reader will SEE ______________________ as normal.

===========================================================================
O. THE 90-SECOND VERSION - if you are running out of time on Q7
===========================================================================

Write one paragraph only, with all four texts quoted:

    Both writers treat celebration as ______________________ , but they want different things from
    the reader. Text ______ argues it is ______________________ - "______________" - so her reader
    is ______________________ . Text ______ argues it is ______________________ - "______________"
    - so her reader is ______________________ . One wants the reader to feel
    ______________________ ; the other wants the reader to ______________________ .

That single paragraph, written properly, is worth more than five paragraphs of feature-spotting.

===========================================================================
P. THE FOUR WORDS TO WRITE AT THE TOP OF YOUR ANSWER
===========================================================================

    QUOTE      - the exact words, in speech marks
    MOVE       - what the writer is doing with them
    EFFECT     - what it does to the reader
    SO WHAT    - why the writer wanted that

If a paragraph has no MOVE, it is a list. If it has no EFFECT, it is a label. If it has no SO WHAT,
it is a guess.
