# Every format, with multiple examples per slot

3 Nov 2026. Each form is shown as: the shape, then two or three DIFFERENT examples of each slot,
drawn from different real prompts. The point is that the slot is fixed and the content moves.

---

# FORM 1 — TALK

## Shape
Greeting -> announce -> First -> Second -> Third -> advice -> thanks.
No headline, no subheadings, no sign-off. Contractions yes.

## OPENINGS (different prompts)

Jun 2021, travelling:
"Good afternoon, everyone. I want to talk about the thing most of us say we will do and then don't:
get on a plane. I have three things to tell you, and the third one is the useful one."

2020, losing important items:
"Morning, everyone. Put your hand up if you have lost a phone this year. [pause] Right. So this is
for you."

Jun 2023, walking:
"Thanks for staying behind. I will keep this short, which is part of the argument. I want to talk
about the cheapest thing you can do for yourself."

Treasure hunting (Jun 2022 P1R):
"Good morning. I want to talk about treasure hunting — not the film version, the actual thing. You
might think it's all luck. It isn't."

## MIDDLE — the three spoken signposts (two full examples)

Jun 2023, walking:
"First, the types. There are three worth knowing: the walk to somewhere, which doesn't count; the
loop — one hour, no destination, out of your door and back; and the long one, once a month, which
takes an afternoon and makes you eat like an animal afterwards.
Second, the benefits, and they are not complicated. You breathe better. You sleep better. Things
that seemed enormous at four in the afternoon look survivable by six.
Third, the honest part. It will rain. Your shoes will be wrong. Around week two it will feel like a
chore you invented for yourself."

Nov 2021, outlook on life:
"First, why anyone would want to change. Usually it isn't philosophy — it's a bad year, or a person,
or a job that has stopped fitting.
Second, how to be positive, which is less mysterious than it sounds. Notice one good thing a day and
say it out loud to somebody. That's it. That's the method.
Third, and this is the part nobody says: some negativity is useful. The person who is never worried
is usually the person who hasn't checked anything."

## CLOSINGS

Jun 2021: "So: go somewhere. Not next year. This year, badly, with a friend. Thank you for listening."
2020: "So don't panic, don't blame anybody, and retrace your steps. That is all. Thank you."
Jun 2023: "Do not make it a habit. Make it an appointment. Tuesday, after school, forty minutes. That's it. Thank you."

# FORM 2 — ARTICLE, SCHOOL OR COLLEGE WEBSITE

## Shape
Headline -> standfirst (one italic line) -> intro -> three subheadings -> closing line.
Contractions yes. Voice: "we" (the school) and "you" (the reader).

## OPENINGS (three different prompts)

Nov 2024, "Let's celebrate!":
"Let's celebrate!
Why the smallest excuse for a party might be the best thing your year has going for it.
We are very good at marking the big things. Exams, results, the last day of term. We are much worse
at marking everything else, and everything else is most of a life."

Jun 2026, handwriting:
"Your handwriting is getting worse. Here is why that matters.
Nobody is asking you to be calligraphic. We are asking you to be readable.
There is a moment every teacher has had: a good answer, in an exam, that cannot be marked. Not
because it is wrong. Because nobody can read it."

Jan 2020 P1R, "Friendship":
"Friendship
The people you sit next to are not all the same, and that is the whole point.
We use one word for about six different things. This article separates them."

## SUBHEADINGS (three prompts)

Nov 2024: Why we should celebrate / What is worth celebrating / How to do it properly
Jun 2026: Why it still matters / Why we stopped / What it costs
Jan 2020 P1R: The six kinds of friend / Where they come from / What they are actually for

## MIDDLE PARAGRAPH (two examples)

Nov 2024, under "What is worth celebrating":
"Not only the obvious. A good week. Somebody's first driving lesson. A grandparent's eightieth. A
Tuesday in November when nothing went wrong. If you wait for the official occasions you will
celebrate about four times in your life, and two of those will be somebody else's idea."

Jan 2020 P1R, under "The six kinds of friend":
"There is the friend you have had since you were six and no longer have anything in common with.
There is the friend you only ever see in a group. There is the one you ring at eleven at night. All
of them count. None of them is a substitute for the others."

## CLOSINGS (one line, the shortest on the page)
Nov 2024: "Then do it again next month."
Jun 2026: "Your future examiner has never met you. Give them a fair chance."
Jan 2020 P1R: "You do not need more friends. You need to know which ones you already have."

# FORM 3 — ARTICLE, MAGAZINE FOR YOUNG ADULTS

## Shape
Same as Form 2, but: opinion first, one person's voice with attitude, twist at the end.
Contractions yes.

## OPENING
Nov 2023, "Time to learn something new?":
"Time to learn something new?
You are not too old, too busy, or too far behind. You are just doing it in the wrong order.
By the time you finish school you will have been taught roughly forty subjects and chosen about none
of them."

## The difference in the intro, side by side
School website: shared situation — "We are very good at marking the big things."
Magazine: an opinion — "You are doing it in the wrong order."

## SUBHEADINGS
What counts / How to actually do it / Why it is hard

## MIDDLE PARAGRAPH
Under "How to actually do it":
"Pick something you can do for twenty minutes. Twenty minutes beats two hours, because twenty minutes
happens. Find the cheapest version of the equipment, so quitting costs you nothing. Then find one
person slightly better than you and copy them shamelessly."

## THE TWIST
"Then be patient with yourself for about six weeks. That is roughly how long a thing takes to stop
being a decision and start being a Tuesday."

A second twist, same form, different topic:
"Nobody is good at this on day one. That is not a warning. That is the entire point."

# FORM 4 — WEBSITE CONTRIBUTION

## Shape
Title -> "who I am" line -> my opinion -> three subheadings -> my rule -> thanks to the site.
Everything in "I". Contractions yes.

## OPENINGS (three different prompts)

Jan 2022, places to stay:
"The Room Was Fine. Everything Else Was the Problem.
A contribution from a traveller who has now slept in forty-one beds and regretted nine of them.
I have a theory about accommodation: the room matters far less than the person who hands you the key."

Jun 2022, clear communication:
"The message you meant is not the message they read.
A contribution from someone who has been misunderstood by email four times this month.
Here is what I have learned: nobody has ever been offended by a message that was too clear."

2020 P1R, extreme weather:
"The sky does not negotiate.
A contribution from someone who has now been evacuated twice.
We talk about extreme weather as though it were a photograph. It is not. It is a diary entry."

## SUBHEADINGS
Jan 2022: Who stays where / What is good / What is bad / My own rule
Jun 2022: When it goes wrong / What technology fixed / Why grammar is not snobbery
2020 P1R: The weather itself / What it does to the land / What it does to people

## MIDDLE PARAGRAPH
2020 P1R, under "What it does to people":
"That is the one nobody photographs. Whole villages do not flood; individual houses flood, and the
people in them have nowhere to put the thing they cannot carry. Round the corner, the bus runs on
time. That is what the weather does to a person: it makes the ordinary world carry on without them."

## CLOSINGS (spoken to the site)
Jan 2022: "— with thanks to the site for the space."
Jun 2022: "Thanks to whoever runs this page. I read the comments here more than I should."
2020 P1R: "If anyone reading this has been through it, I would genuinely like to hear your version."

# FORM 5 — LETTER TO A FRIEND

## Shape
"Dear X," -> line like a text -> shared memory -> First/Second/Third -> a small ask -> "Take care," -> name.
Contractions yes.

## OPENINGS (three different prompts)

Jan 2020, art:
"Dear Hana,
I am writing this on the bus, which tells you how keen I am, because I never write anything on the bus
except messages telling people I am nearly there."

Jun 2024, climate:
"Dear Tom,
You're going to laugh at me for writing a letter, but I've been reading too much and I need to say it
to somebody who won't immediately post it."

Nov 2025, manners:
"Dear Ayesha,
I am writing this by hand on purpose, which is either a nice touch or extremely annoying. You can tell
me which when you reply, probably in four words, at midnight."

## THE SHARED MEMORY LINE — the line that proves it is a friend
Jan 2020: "You said last week that art was 'not really your thing', and I have been thinking about it ever since."
Jun 2024: "You told me at the beach that nothing one person does makes any difference. I want to argue with that."
Nov 2025: "You asked me last week whether I thought people were ruder than they used to be. I think the answer is no."

## MIDDLE PARAGRAPHS
Jan 2022 P1R, transport:
"Second, the trains. I know the bus is cheaper. I also know that the bus takes nine hours and the train
takes four, and you will spend the difference sleeping in a hotel you didn't need."

Jun 2024, climate:
"Second, what actually works. Not the big stuff — the small, boring, repeated stuff. A bottle you keep
in your bag. A shop you stop using. Twelve people doing that is worth more than one person doing
everything perfectly for a month and then stopping."

## A SMALL ASK (the line that closes the argument)
Jan 2020: "Just come on Friday, for one hour, and if you hate it I will buy you a coffee and never mention it again."
Jun 2024: "Do one thing. Not five. Text me the one thing."
Nov 2025: "Put the phone in another room for one meal a day and see how strange the first ten minutes feel."

## SIGN-OFFS
"Take care, and say hello to your mum for me. / Yours, Zain"
"Write back, badly. / Take care, / Zain"
"That's enough from me. Eat something. / Take care, / Zain"

# FORM 6 — LETTER TO A NEWSPAPER

## Shape
"Dear Editor," -> issue in sentence one -> Firstly/Furthermore/Finally -> ask -> "Yours faithfully," + full name.
NO contractions. "we" and "our". No jokes.

## OPENING
Jun 2019, after-study activities:
"Dear Editor,
I am writing about the absence of anything for young people to do in this town after six o'clock."

## MIDDLE (three paragraphs, public facts)
"Firstly, the reason there are so few activities is not laziness and it is not money. It is staffing.
Every scheme I have looked into requires two adults present at all times, and the volunteers are not there."

## THE ASK
"I would ask the council to reconsider the funding for a single coordinator, and to publish what it
would actually cost. If the answer is that it cannot be afforded, we deserve to hear that said out
loud, in print, by the people who decided it."

## CLOSING
"Yours faithfully,
Zain Rahman"

## How the same sentence changes between the two letters
Friend: "Don't do it, you'll hate it."
Editor: "I would urge the council not to proceed."
Friend: "I've been thinking about it."
Editor: "This matter has been overlooked for too long."

# FORM 7 — SECTION C, Q9

## Shape
Opening (position, 3 sentences) -> Reason one -> Reason two -> the other side answered -> landing.
No headline. No subheadings. Examiner is the audience. No contractions needed but they are allowed.

## OPENING — three different prompts

"The best things in life are free." Discuss. (Nov 2024):
"Ask anyone what made their week good and nobody names the thing they bought. They name the walk, the
phone call, the hour they spent doing nothing at all."

"New opportunities present new challenges." Discuss. (May 2025):
"A new opportunity is usually described as a door opening. It is more accurate to say the door opens
and the floor disappears."

"We are never too old to try something new." To what extent? (Nov 2023):
"The word to notice here is never. One word, and it either rules out every exception or it doesn't."

## REASON PARAGRAPH — the four parts, two examples

Nov 2024:
"Take a Sunday at your grandmother's. The food costs something, but the food is not the point. The
point is that four people are in one room and nobody is checking their phone. You cannot buy that
atmosphere. Money buys the setting; it does not buy what happens in it."
[claim][picture][short sentence][what it adds up to]

May 2025:
"Think about the scholarship. On paper it is the best thing that could happen to a seventeen-year-old.
In practice you are nineteen hundred miles from everyone who knows you, in a room you cannot afford
to heat. The pressure is unlike any other. Which is exactly why the people who get through it come
out different."

## THE OTHER SIDE, ANSWERED
"None of that is the whole truth. A nurse on a double shift has no free evening to walk in, and 'the
best things are free' can sound like advice from someone who has never been poor. That is a fair
objection, and it does not defeat the claim. It sharpens it. The walk is still free. What costs money
is the time to take it."

## LANDINGS — three prompts
Nov 2024: "Nobody remembers the receipt. They remember who was at the table."
May 2025: "The challenge is not the price of the opportunity. It is the opportunity."
Nov 2023: "You are not too old. You are just later than you wanted to be."

# THE ONLY THINGS THAT CHANGE
1. The first line.
2. Whether the middle uses subheadings, spoken signposts, or neither.
3. The last line.
Everything between them is three blocks, each with a point from Text One, a point from Text Two, and
your own comment.
