## 3. Section A - three engines

### Engine 1: Retrieval (Q1, Q2, Q4, Q5) - 5 marks, should be 5/5
The mark scheme is a list. Any one item earns the mark. Rules:
- Answer in the smallest possible unit. No preamble, no "The narrator sees that..."
- If it asks for TWO, number them 1. and 2.
- Copy the words of the text where possible. Retrieval is not paraphrase.
- Lines are given. Do not stray outside them; anything outside is not marked.

### Engine 2: Analysis (Q3 and Q6) - 20 marks combined
MS grid: L3 = "clear understanding and explanation... references appropriate"
L4 = "thorough understanding and exploration... detailed references"
L5 = "perceptive understanding and analysis... references discriminating"

The formula that reaches L4 every time:
**QUOTE -> NAME THE MOVE -> EFFECT ON THE READER -> SO WHAT**
e.g. "The simile 'slashed as if with a giant knife' makes the valley sound violent and
man-made, which suggests the landscape is powerful enough to seem deliberate. It makes the
reader feel small."
- 6 to 8 of these. Each 3-4 lines.
- Vary the moves: adjective, verb, simile, metaphor, personification, contrast, listing/tricolon,
  short sentence, repetition, direct address, punctuation.
- Comment on STRUCTURE too, not just words: short sentence at the end of a paragraph; the order
  in which information is released; a shift from wide view to close detail.
- Never write "the writer uses a simile to make it more interesting". Name the effect precisely:
  fear, awe, discomfort, intimacy, speed, claustrophobia.
- Q6 explicitly requires "brief quotations". No quotation = capped low.

### Engine 3: Comparison (Q7) - 15 marks, the single biggest question on the paper
MS note, verbatim: **"NB: candidates who have considered only ONE text may only achieve a mark
up to the top of Level 2"** - i.e. 6/15 maximum. Writing only about one text, however well, is
capped at 40%.

Structure that reaches L4/L5:
- Every paragraph compares. Never a paragraph on Text One then a paragraph on Text Two.
- The sentence shape: "**Both** writers present X as Y. **Text One** does this by [method,
  quotation]. **Text Two**, **however**, [method, quotation], which makes the reader feel Z."
- Cover 4-5 axes, one paragraph each: attitude to the place; what they notice (people vs
  landscape); tone (wonder vs dry humour); how the writing is built (long sentences vs short);
  what the reader is left feeling.
- Balance references: at least two quotations from each text in every paragraph.
- Use the comparison vocabulary explicitly: both, whereas, in contrast, similarly, unlike,
  while Text One..., Text Two instead...

## 4. Section B (Q8) - 30 marks, three separate tens

| AO | 10 marks | What earns it |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | 10 | Use ideas from BOTH texts. Every bullet gets a paragraph. Wide range of points. |
| AO4 | 10 | The NAMED audience and FORM. A talk has an opening address, direct address, and a close. A letter has a greeting and sign-off. Getting the form wrong caps AO4 at L3. |
| AO5 | 10 | Accuracy. Same nine-item list as Section C. |

Method, 60 minutes:
1. Read the bullets. They ARE the structure. Three bullets = three body paragraphs.
2. Decide the form and open with it: "Good morning, everyone. Thank you for coming." That single
   line earns AO4 credit that a candidate who starts with content never gets.
3. Each bullet paragraph: a point from Text One, a point from Text Two, then your own gloss.
   That is AO1 range and it is also how "both texts" is proved.
4. Register throughout: second person, contractions, rhetorical questions to the audience.
5. Close by returning to the audience and the purpose.
6. Leave four minutes for the accuracy pass.
