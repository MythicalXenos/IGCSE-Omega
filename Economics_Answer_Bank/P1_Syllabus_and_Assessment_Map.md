# 4EC1 Paper 1 — official specification and assessment map

**Purpose:** establish the authoritative scope and exam architecture before writing or teaching full answers. This is a preparation/reference map, not a lesson and not a prediction of what will appear.

**Checked:** 2026-11-10. Primary source is Pearson’s *International GCSE Economics (4EC1) Specification, Issue 3*. Pearson’s current assessment-support page is used for current explanatory guidance and notional grade boundaries. Where legacy page wording conflicts with the current publication policy, preserve and resolve the conflict explicitly (see §5).

## 1. Qualification identity and aims

- Qualification: Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (9–1), code **4EC1**; current retrieved specification is **Issue 3**.
- First teaching: September 2017; first examination: June 2019.
- Issue 3’s stated changes include onscreen assessment availability and removal of January / addition of November availability. Paper 1 is currently scheduled in June and November; the course is linear and the two papers are taken at the end of the course.
- The official aims include understanding economic concepts and applying them to real situations; calculating, interpreting and evaluating economic data to make reasoned arguments and informed judgements; understanding economic change and its effects on developed/developing economies; understanding issues and possible solutions in mixed economies; and participating as citizens, producers and consumers.
- Pearson says all content bullets are compulsory. “Including” introduces required detail. Examples in the specification are not an exhaustive list of possible assessment contexts.

## 3. Compulsory Paper 1 syllabus — crosswalk frame

This is a condensed paraphrase of the official P1 content headings and bullets. It is a checklist for the detailed item-level crosswalk; it does not replace the specification.

### 1.1 The market system

1. **The economic problem:** scarcity, choice and opportunity cost for consumers, producers and government; PPC diagrams showing productive potential, full/unemployed resources, opportunity cost, growth, and possible/unobtainable output; possible causes of positive/negative growth.
2. **Economic assumptions:** consumer benefit maximisation and business profit maximisation; why consumers may not maximise benefit (calculation difficulties, habits, copying others); why producers may not maximise profit (revenue/sales-maximising managers, customer care, charitable work).
3. **Demand, supply and market equilibrium:** definitions; movements along vs shifts; causes of shifts (advertising, income, fashion/tastes, substitute/complement prices, demographics; production costs, technology, indirect taxes, subsidies, natural factors); equilibrium price/quantity; diagrams for shifts/excess demand/excess supply; definitions/calculations/diagrams for excess demand and supply; market forces removing disequilibrium.
4. **Elasticity:** PED, PES and income elasticity of demand definitions/formulae/calculations and numerical interpretation; elastic/inelastic diagrams; determinants of PED/PES; manufactured vs primary product PES; total-revenue calculations for price elasticity; elasticity significance to businesses/government, taxes/subsidies and income changes.
5. **The mixed economy:** mixed economy and public/private sector definitions; ownership/control/aim differences; what/how/for whom; market failure and inefficient allocation; government intervention; public goods (non-excludable/non-rivalrous/free-rider problem); relative sector roles across economies; privatisation and effects on consumers, workers, businesses and government.
6. **Externalities:** external costs of production and examples; external benefits of consumption and examples; social cost and social benefit definitions/formulae.

### 1.2 Business economics

1. **Production:** land, labour, capital, enterprise; primary/secondary/tertiary sectors; changes in sector importance by employment/output over time and across developing/developed economies.
2. **Productivity and division of labour:** productivity definition and land/labour/capital determinants (including education, training, migration, technology); division of labour definition and advantages/disadvantages to workers and businesses.
3. **Business costs, revenues and profit:** formulae/calculations for total revenue, total costs, fixed/variable costs, average total cost and profit; internal/external economies of scale and types; diseconomies and types; LRAC diagram with efficient scale indicated.
4. **Business competition:** competition’s advantages/disadvantages to firms, consumers and economy (efficiency, choice, quality, innovation, price); large/small firms; growth and staying-small determinants; monopoly definition/features/barriers/advantages/disadvantages; oligopoly definition/features and advantages/disadvantages, including competition, collusion/cartels and price wars.
5. **The labour market:** determinants of labour demand and supply; labour’s quantity/quality importance; education/training and human capital; labour supply/demand equilibrium and shift diagrams; trade union activity and its effects on conditions/wages.
6. **Government intervention:** externality policies (tax, subsidy, fine, regulation, pollution permit) and advantages/disadvantages; regulation of competition, monopoly power, consumer interests and mergers/takeovers; minimum wage reasons, advantages/disadvantages and diagrams for introduction/increase.

## 4. Assessment objectives and question demands

Pearson’s assessment-support page describes the AOs as:

- **AO1:** recall, select and communicate knowledge of economic terms, concepts and issues.
- **AO2:** understand and apply economic knowledge using appropriate terms, concepts, theories and calculations effectively in specific contexts.
- **AO3:** select, organise and interpret information from sources to investigate and analyse economic issues.
- **AO4:** evaluate economic information to make reasoned judgements and draw conclusions.

The verified 42-paper archive census establishes the stable long-answer allocation in the available series: 6 marks = AO2 3 + AO3 3; 9 marks = AO2 3 + AO3 3 + AO4 3; 12 marks = AO2 4 + AO3 4 + AO4 4. No AO1 is awarded on these long responses in the audited papers. Three-mark point-marked explain/draw items are AO2 in the archive census. Exact-paper MS remains controlling.

Pearson’s official command-word page says:

- **Analyse (6):** one-sided analysis; no evaluation or conclusion is required. Develop applied reasoning rather than list points.
- **Assess (9):** balanced, two-sided and applied; no separate conclusion/judgement is required. AO4 involves weighing/comparing factors in economic context.
- **Evaluate (12):** balanced, two-sided and applied, plus a supported judgement/conclusion. Evaluate the concept/action in the question before introducing alternatives.
- Levels-based answers are marked holistically: quality, selectivity, application and developed reasoning matter more than counting bullet points.
- Extract information must be used to support an argument; merely copying/repeating it does not earn application credit.

**Challenge note:** Pearson’s current support page also cautions that examiners do not mechanically cap marks for generic or unbalanced answers. A strong answer must still be judged against the exact descriptors and command word; this does not cancel the need for balance/context at top-level AO4. Do not oversimplify the page into a rigid template.

## 5. Quantitative skills and grade-boundary interpretation

Pearson states quantitative skills appear across both papers and total **10% of the qualification’s marks**; the specification says all skills will be sampled across the lifetime of the qualification and that quantitative-skill questions will be in a business context. Appendix 3’s official skill list is:

- **Calculations:** percentages and percentage changes (including interest on savings); averages (including cost); totals (including revenue, costs and profit); income (including gross and net pay).
- **Graph construction:** constructing graphs from data, including supply and demand curves.
- **Interpretation/use of quantitative data to support and justify economic decisions:** interpreting/using graphs and charts; interpreting/using economic data such as unemployment figures, exports and imports.
- Pearson states the mathematics level is equivalent to upper-secondary mathematics.

This list was independently surfaced in Pearson’s indexed specification extract, but the specification PDF extraction in this audit still did not expose Appendix 3’s actual page. Record the content as verified against Pearson’s published text, while leaving direct appendix-page visual verification outstanding.

Pearson’s assessment-support page says:

- the qualification grade is based on the combined 160 marks;
- the paper-specific 80-mark “notional grade boundaries” are guidance for mock analysis, not separate awarded grades or a guarantee of grade 9;
- therefore Paper 1 practice should target the maximum **80/80**, while the final qualification result also depends on Paper 2, which is out of current scope.
