# Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1)
## Official candidate exemplars and constructed model answers

**Scope:** the searchable archive’s **42 4EC1 question papers** (Papers 1 and 2, including R papers), May/June 2019 to June 2026. This guide combines Pearson’s 2019 exemplar booklets, examiner comments in the available 2023 reports, and clearly labelled models based on the exact question and mark scheme. It is for **4EC1 International GCSE Economics**.

## Storage and evidence policy

The full QPs, mark schemes and three 2023 examiner reports stay in the existing `Paperlords_Archive` ZIP and text index. To avoid duplicating PDFs, this folder stores the inventory workbook and answer guide, not extra source-PDF copies. The workbook records the internal PDF paths and official links; corresponding searchable text files use the same relative path with `.txt` instead of `.pdf`.

Labels used here:

- **OFFICIAL — full marks:** Pearson published a real candidate response and awarded every mark for that item.
- **OFFICIAL — not full marks:** a real scored response and examiner comments; the score is stated. It is comparison material, not a perfect answer.
- **MARK-SCHEME answer:** wording or calculation from the relevant Pearson mark scheme, not a candidate script.
- **CONSTRUCTED full-credit target:** original wording written from the exact QP/MS and examiner guidance. It is not an official answer and is not guaranteed a precise score.

The 2019 Pearson exemplar booklets show real scripts and exact scores, but their 6-, 9- and 12-mark examples are not full marks. The 2023 examiner reports provide verified 6/6 and 9/9 scripts. In the checked 2019 exemplars and available 2023 reports, **no 12/12 candidate response was found**; the 12-mark models below are therefore constructed and labelled accordingly.

## 1. Archive-wide question-pattern map

The text index contains **671 standalone question-mark lines** across 42 usable QPs: **262 one-mark, 173 two-mark, 98 three-mark, 60 six-mark, 42 nine-mark and 36 twelve-mark items**. No other standalone mark values were found. The May/June 2022 P2R original in the archive is unreadable, so the inventory uses its verified 20-page recovered alternative paired with the original mark scheme. The filterable workbook `4EC1_Question_Type_Inventory.xlsx` contains a family map, a detailed prompt-pattern map, the item-level index with QP page and matching MS PDF path, official exemplar scores and source register.

| Marks | Common command / family | What full-credit work generally does |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Select one / state / identify | Gives one correct option or fact. Data/diagram reading may be involved. |
| 2 | Define; calculate; describe one impact/advantage | A definition usually needs two precise parts. A calculation needs the requested number, working where required, units/sign and rounding. A describe response gives one point and develops its effect. |
| 3 | Explain one reason/impact/advantage/way; draw and label a diagram | An explanation develops one economic idea. A diagram question awards marks for the specified curve/line change and requested labels. |
| 6 | Analyse | A levels-based, one-sided analysis. Develop causal chains and use relevant theory/context. **Evaluation is not required.** |
| 9 | Assess | A levels-based, balanced two-sided argument applied to the data/context. **A separate conclusion is not required and has no standalone mark.** |
| 12 | Evaluate | A levels-based evaluation requiring developed arguments and a supported judgement/conclusion. Evaluate the named issue before listing possible alternatives. |

### Assessment-objective patterns

Typical 4EC1 allocations in the selected mark schemes are:

- **6 marks:** AO2 (application/understanding) + AO3 (analysis), commonly 3 marks each.
- **9 marks:** AO2 + AO3 + AO4 (evaluation), commonly 3 marks each.
- **12 marks:** AO2 + AO3 + AO4, commonly 4 marks each.

Use the **exact question’s mark scheme** if its allocation differs. These are level-based questions: Pearson judges the whole response against the level descriptors. A long list of points is not automatically better than a smaller number of developed chains.

## 2. Direct first-sentence starters

You do not need a long introduction. Start by doing what the command word asks.

- **Define:** “Demand is the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at a given price.”
- **Calculate:** write the relevant figures into the calculation, then show the result with units.
- **Explain:** “One reason **[X]** may happen is … This means …”
- **Analyse:** “One impact of **[X]** on **[economy/group]** is …”
- **Assess:** “One benefit of **[policy/change]** is …” Then develop the other side in a new paragraph.
- **Evaluate:** “**[Policy/action]** may be effective because …” Then develop the counterargument and decide what it depends on.

## 3. Short-mark examples

### 2 marks — define demand

**MARK-SCHEME answer (June 2019 P1 Q1(c)):**

> Demand is the quantity of goods or services consumers are willing and able to buy at a given price.

The mark scheme awards one part for “willing and able to buy” and one for “at a given price.” The official Pearson booklet’s candidate A received **2/2**; candidate B received **1/2** because it omitted “able.” The examiner says examples do not earn marks for this definition question.

### 2 marks — calculate price elasticity of demand

**MARK-SCHEME worked example (June 2019 P1 Q2(c)):** price rises by 15%; quantity demanded falls by 12%.

> PED = percentage change in quantity demanded ÷ percentage change in price = −12 ÷ 15 = **−0.8**.

The exact mark scheme awards a calculation mark and an answer mark; for this item it allows a positive 0.8 in the specified working case. A formula by itself earns no mark. The real candidate answers were **2/2** and **0/2**. Always follow the paper’s exact instructions on sign, units and rounding.

### 3 marks — explain why concert-ticket demand may be price inelastic

**CONSTRUCTED full-credit target, based on June 2019 P1 Q1(h):**

> Fans may have few close substitutes for seeing their favourite artist perform live. If the ticket price rises, they may still want to attend because watching that specific artist is difficult to replace. Therefore, the quantity of tickets demanded may fall by a smaller proportion than the price rises.

Pearson’s real candidate A received **3/3** for a developed reason and concert context; candidate B received **2/3** because the reason was not developed. This constructed version is not a transcript of the candidate’s words.

### 3 marks — draw a supply-and-demand diagram

**OFFICIAL — full marks (June 2019 P1 Q1(g)):** a real candidate received **3/3** for the hurricane/crop-market diagram. The answer labelled a leftward supply shift, a higher equilibrium price and a lower equilibrium quantity. The second candidate received **2/3**.

For a similar diagram, identify the **one curve changed by the stated cause**, shift it in the correct direction, and label each item requested. Do not shift both curves when only one is affected. In the June 2023 P2 examiner report, the report likewise warns that shifting both curves on a one-curve question can earn zero.

## 4. Six-mark “Analyse”

**Key rule:** analyse the requested effect or mechanism. Use economic theory and the evidence where it helps. Pearson’s reports explicitly say 6-mark “Analyse” responses need a **one-sided** argument; evaluation is not required. Develop a causal chain rather than listing several undeveloped effects.

**CONSTRUCTED full-credit target — June 2019 P1 Q1(i), why Coca-Cola bought shares in Inca Kola:**

> Inca Kola was a major rival in Peru, with a slightly larger market share than Coca-Cola in 1995: 32.9% compared with 32.0%. Buying a large stake could give Coca-Cola more influence over Inca Kola’s decisions. This could help Coca-Cola protect its position in the Peruvian soft-drinks market rather than continually competing against a strong established brand. Coca-Cola could also receive part of Inca Kola’s profits, so it might benefit financially from the rival’s success as well as from its own sales.

This targets the exact QP/MS, which also notes that 48.5% of Inca Kola’s shares were owned by Coca-Cola by 2014. It avoids claiming that a 48.5% stake automatically guarantees control. The official candidate answers were **5/6 and 4/6**, so neither is full marks; this model is constructed.

**Retrieved full-mark analysis examples:**

- **OFFICIAL — 6/6:** June 2023 P2 Q1(h), analysing benefits of quotas to domestic farmers; examiner report p. 14. The commentary praises developed analysis linking protection of farmers with economic growth and prevention of dumping.
- **OFFICIAL — 6/6:** June 2023 P2R Q4(b), analysing the effect of inflation on export demand; report p. 38. The commentary praises the links to comparative inflation, exports and the current account.

Both reports are in the existing archive. The `Official exemplars` workbook tab gives their paths and official Pearson source URLs.

## 5. Nine-mark “Assess”

**Key rule:** offer a developed case on both sides and use the data/context. For 9-mark “Assess” questions, Pearson’s 2019 booklet and 2023 reports explicitly say a separate conclusion is **not** required; there is no separate conclusion mark. You can finish with a weighing sentence, but spend most of the time on balanced, developed reasoning.

**CONSTRUCTED full-credit target — June 2019 P2 Q2(g), benefits for Thailand of joining a trade bloc:**

> Membership of a trade bloc can give Thai firms access to a larger regional market. If trade barriers fall, firms may sell more goods to customers in other member countries. Higher output could let firms gain economies of scale, lowering average costs and helping them compete. The 2019 question’s data also shows a large rise in GDP per capita over the period reported, which is consistent with improved economic opportunities, although it does not prove membership alone caused that rise.
>
> However, fewer trade barriers can also expose Thai firms to stronger competition from imports. Domestic producers that cannot compete may lose sales, cut output or reduce employment. Thailand could also become too dependent on trade within the bloc, so the benefits would depend on how competitive its firms are and whether it maintains trade with non-members.
>
> The gains are likely to be greater when Thai firms can use the wider market to expand and lower costs; if they cannot withstand import competition, some domestic industries may lose. The data supports the possibility of benefits, but it should not be treated as proof that the trade bloc caused every improvement.

The exact-paper Pearson examples were **8/9 and 5/9**, not full marks. The official booklet notes that the 8/9 candidate used data, analysis and a counterargument; it also says a conclusion is not required. The wording above is a constructed target, not a candidate transcript.

**Verified full-mark Assess examples:**

- **OFFICIAL — 9/9:** June 2023 P2 Q2(g), assessing infrastructure spending and productive potential; report p. 21. The examiner praises contextual links, costs and time lags, and comparison with education/healthcare. A separate conclusion was not required.
- **OFFICIAL — 9/9:** June 2023 P2R Q2(g), assessing benefits of a fiscal surplus; report p. 23. The candidate balances benefits such as policing and lower inequality against the higher taxes that produced the surplus.

## 6. Twelve-mark “Evaluate”

**Key rule:** evaluate both sides and use the extract. The 12-mark “Evaluate” question requires a **supported judgement/conclusion**. Avoid writing a list of alternative policies without first evaluating the one in the question. The 2023 P2 report says alternatives are not required for full marks; a conclusion should judge effectiveness or explain what it depends on.

**CONSTRUCTED full-credit target — June 2019 P2 Q4(c), whether progressive taxation is the most effective way to reduce poverty and inequality in Vietnam:**

> Higher progressive tax rates on high earners could reduce the gap between disposable incomes. The extract says Vietnam had 210 super-rich people whose combined annual earnings were enough to take 3.2 million people out of poverty. Taxing some of that income could raise government revenue for healthcare, education and job creation. Better access to these services may improve skills and employment prospects for lower-income households, reducing poverty over time.
>
> However, higher tax rates may reduce the incentive to work, invest or expand a business. If firms invest less, Vietnam could create fewer jobs and grow more slowly, which could hurt lower-income workers. The policy’s success also depends on setting a workable rate and collecting the tax effectively; revenue will not reduce poverty if it is not used well.
>
> Other measures, such as targeted benefits, a living wage, education or healthcare investment, may reach poorer households more directly. But these also have costs and may not work without enough funding.
>
> Overall, progressive taxes could be an important way to reduce inequality in Vietnam if the higher revenue is collected and spent effectively. They are unlikely to be the most effective measure on their own: the outcome depends on tax enforcement, how high the rates are, and whether revenue is combined with policies that improve jobs and access to essential services.

The exact 2019 P2 candidate scripts received **9/12 and 7/12**; neither is perfect. In the June 2023 reports, exact-paper 12-mark responses received **10/12** for subsidies (P2 Q4(c)), **11/12** for pollution permits (P2R Q4(c)), and **11/12** for derived demand/labour demand (P1R Q4(c)). All are near-full, not full marks. No verified 12/12 candidate answer was found in the checked materials, so the model above is clearly **constructed**.

## 7. High-yield examiner warnings

- **“What is meant by…”:** usually needs two precise parts; examples do not earn definition marks.
- **Calculations:** show working, check the sign and decimal places, and include units or a percentage sign where required. A correct formula alone may earn no marks.
- **Diagrams:** shift only the curve affected by the cause and label the new curve and requested equilibrium values. A correctly drawn but unlabelled curve can lose a mark.
- **Explain (3):** give a reason/impact/effect and develop it. Repeating the extract is not explanation.
- **Analyse (6):** one-sided developed analysis; do not spend time evaluating.
- **Assess (9):** balanced two-sided reasoning applied to the data; a standalone conclusion is not required.
- **Evaluate (12):** two-sided evaluation plus a supported conclusion. Do not leave this high-mark question to the last minute.
- **Use evidence:** select data and explain how it supports the argument; copying numbers or paraphrasing the extract is not application.
