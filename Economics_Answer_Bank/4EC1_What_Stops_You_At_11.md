# 4EC1 — what stops an answer at 11 out of 12

**First, the fact that matters most: in the material checked for this bank — the three June 2023 examiner reports and the June 2019 exemplar booklet — there is no 12/12 Economics script.** In the
June 2023 examiner reports there are three commented 12-mark scripts and they score **11, 11 and 10**.
The June 2019 exemplar scripts score **9 and 7** — that figure is recorded, with page references, in
`Source_Notes/Official_Pearson_Exemplar_Index.md`; **the booklet itself is not in this repository**, so
it is second-hand and is marked as such here. So everything below is
calibration from *near*-full marks — which is exactly what a candidate targeting a 9 needs, because the
question is not "can I answer it", it is "what is the last mark for".

**All quotations below are from the three June 2023 examiner reports in this repository and are
verified character-for-character against the PDFs by `verify_economics_fingerprint.py`.**

---

## The three scripts, and the exact reason each lost a mark

| Paper | Item | Score | The examiner's stated reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4EC1/01R** | Q4(c) | **11/12** | *"It meets all the requirements for Level 3 with a balanced, two-sided argument, leading to a supported conclusion, but does not present an excellent organisation of information and application is only reasonable."* |
| **4EC1/02** | Q4(c) | **10/12** | *"The response does provide a detailed two-sided argument and uses the context well… It considers the cost of the subsidies and alternatives such as pollution permits and fines which might give additional tax revenue. **There is an attempt at a conclusion.**"* |
| **4EC1/02R** | Q4(c) | **11/12** | *"There is a balanced evaluation of the use of pollution permits with consideration of more effective methods which can be used in conjunction with pollution permits. **A conclusion is provided which does make a judgement.**"* |

**Read those three together and the pattern is not about knowledge.** All three had both sides and
context. The two that reached 11 had a conclusion that *judged*; the one that reached 10 had "an
attempt at a conclusion". The 11 that still missed 12 was told, in order, **organisation** and
**application** — the two words in the Level 3 descriptor that the board treats as the top-end
differentiators.

> *"Some candidates simply repeated the data rather than using it, thus reducing their ability to
> attain a top-level mark."* — 4EC1/01R

> *"Better responses used the information provided to help contextualise the answer rather than just
> paraphrasing it."* — 4EC1/02

## The three rules the reports state outright for the 12-marker

1. **Evaluate the thing in the question before anything else.**
   > *"It is important to note that to access the higher marks, candidates must evaluate the economic
   > concept in the question set before suggesting alternative methods otherwise this just becomes a
   > list of methods, rather than an evaluation of the area being examined."* — 4EC1/02

2. **Alternatives are optional; the judgement is not.**
   > *"Alternative methods are not required to access Level 4 and full marks can be achieved by only
   > evaluating the economic concept in the question."* — 4EC1/02
   >
   > *"A conclusion was required for this question and this was often lacking or was just a repeat of
   > the points made previously."* — 4EC1/02

3. **Do not leave it until last.**
   > *"Make sure you have plenty of time to answer the 12-mark question. Often, candidates leave this
   > to last minute and end up rushing this question when it represents a large percentage of the total
   > marks available."* — 4EC1/02

## One honest oddity in the source

Both P2 reports say *"access **Level 4**"*, but the published 12-mark band table has **three** levels
(1–4 / 5–8 / 9–12). This is in Pearson's own text, and it is not being tidied away here. **It changes
nothing that can be marked:** the band table is the binding document, and the sentence means "the top
band" — the only band it can mean, since there is no Level 4 to access.

## What a 12/12 has to contain — labelled as construction

**This section is inference from the Level 3 descriptor and the three commented scripts above. It is
not a Pearson statement and it is not a script.** A full-mark answer would have to satisfy every clause
of the top band at once:

| Descriptor clause (12-mark Level 3, 9–12) | What it demands in practice |
|---|---|
| *"specific and accurate knowledge… by developing relevant points"* | **AO2:** the economic terms are used correctly and applied to *this* case, not defined |
| *"excellent selectivity and organisation"* | the answer is **shaped** — the strongest two or three arguments, ordered, not a list |
| *"coherent and logical chain of reasoning"* | each paragraph: point → because → therefore → so what for the people in the extract |
| *"thorough analysis of issues"* | both sides carried to a consequence, not merely named |
| *"well balanced and coherent, leading to an evaluation that demonstrates full understanding"* | a judgement that answers the question's *whether* or *how effective*, and names what it depends on |
| *"conclusion is present"* | a final paragraph that decides something the body did not already say |

**The practical test:** if you covered the final paragraph, could a reader still not tell which way you
came down? Then it is not yet a judgement.

## Timing, in marks

The 12-marker is **15% of the paper** and the three long answers together are **48 of 80 marks — 60%**.
At 1.125 minutes per mark (90 minutes ÷ 80), the budget is:

| | Minutes |
|---|---|
| 12-mark Evaluate | **13.5** |
| each 9-mark Assess | **10.1** |
| each 6-mark Analyse | **6.75** |
| all 32 marks of 1/2/3-mark items | **36** |
| **total** | **90** |

Leaving the 12-marker "to last minute" is the specific mistake Pearson names in the report. **Write it
third of the four questions, not fourth.**
