# 4EC1 — the three long-answer command words, in Pearson's own words

**Why this file is short:** because the exam only uses three command words for every answer worth 6
marks or more, and Pearson has stated, in print, exactly what each one wants and what it does not.
Everything below is a quotation from the June 2023 examiner reports in this repository. **Each
quotation is checked character-for-character against the PDF by `verify_economics_fingerprint.py`** —
if a quote drifts or a report is replaced, the check fails.

**Sources:** `Paperlords_Archive/IGCSE/4EC1_Economics/May_2023/` —
`4EC1_Economics_May_2023_P1R_ER.pdf` (publication code `4EC1_01R_2306_ER`),
`..._P2_ER.pdf` (`4EC1_02_2306_ER`), `..._P2R_ER.pdf` (`4EC1_02R_2306_ER`).

**And the command word is fixed to the tariff, in all 42 papers:** 6 marks is always **Analyse**, 9
marks is always **Assess**, 12 marks is always **Evaluate**. Not "usually" — the count in every single
paper is one `analyse` per 6-marker, one `assess` per 9-marker, one `evaluate` per 12-marker.

---

## 6 marks — *Analyse*: one side, no judgement, no definition

> *"'Analyse' questions require a one-sided argument. There are no marks for evaluation."*
> — 4EC1/02

> *"The evaluation is ignored as there are no marks for AO4 on 'analyse' questions."*
> — 4EC1/01R

> *"Candidates should not evaluate 'analyse' questions. There are no marks available for doing so and
> it will result in less time being available for other questions."*
> — 4EC1/01R

> *"Many candidates often start the 'Analyse' questions with a definition of the key economic term in
> the question and this is not necessary as there are no knowledge marks available."*
> — 4EC1/02

> *"Focus on developing each applied chain of reasoning rather than listing several separate points."*
> — 4EC1/01R

**So a 6-mark answer is:** two or three chains, each *point → because → therefore → applied to this
case*. No "However". No conclusion. No definition opening. This is not a style preference — AO4 is
literally zero marks here, which is why the words earn nothing.

---

## 9 marks — *Assess*: two sides, still no conclusion

> *"'Assess' questions require a balanced, two-sided argument, which is applied. There is no
> requirement for a judgement or a conclusion, but the argument(s) and counter argument(s) should be
> developed and thorough, as well as in context."*
> — 4EC1/01R

> *"A conclusion is not required for the 9-mark 'Assess' questions."*
> — 4EC1/02

**So a 9-mark answer is:** the same chain work as a 6, then a second section that takes the other
side — both applied. The three AO4 marks here are for *balance and development*, not for a verdict.
Writing a conclusion is not penalised, but it is time taken from development, and development is
where the marks are.

---

## 12 marks — *Evaluate*: two sides **and** a judgement that decides something

> *"This is the only question on the paper requiring a supported judgement/conclusion."*
> — 4EC1/01R

> *"For your conclusion, do not just repeat earlier points and instead come to a judgement as to
> whether an economic concept is effective or what else does it depend on."*
> — 4EC1/02

> *"Make sure you have plenty of time to answer the 12-mark question. Often, candidates leave this to
> last minute and end up rushing this question when it represents a large percentage of the total
> marks available."*
> — 4EC1/02

> *"It is important to note that to access the higher marks, candidates must evaluate the economic
> concept in the question set before suggesting alternative methods otherwise this just becomes a list
> of methods, rather than an evaluation of the area being examined. Alternative methods are not
> required to access Level 4 and full marks can be achieved by only evaluating the economic concept in
> the question…"*
> — 4EC1/02

**So a 12-mark answer is:** both sides applied, then **evaluate the thing named in the question first**
— do not wander into alternative policies before you have judged the one asked about — and finish with
a judgement that says *effective / not effective, because…*, or *it depends on…* followed by the
condition. The words "it depends on" are only a judgement if you then **name what it depends on.**

## The one rule that separates Level 2 from Level 3 on all three

> *"Application marks will not be awarded simply for repeating evidence from the extracts. It needs to
> be used in the response."*
> — 4EC1/01R

> *"Simply copying the extract, or re-writing parts of it, is not going to lead to high marks!"*
> — 4EC1/01R

Quoting a figure is not application. Using the figure to move an argument is. The test to apply to
your own sentence: **if I delete the number, does the argument collapse?** If not, the number was
decoration.

## The short tariffs, in one table

| Tariff | Command word count in 42 papers | What the scheme awards |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | `which` 156 · `state` 51 · `define` 32 · `what` 24 | one mark, no development |
| **2** | `calculate` 94 · `what is meant by` 76 · `describe` 35 | two parts, or working + answer |
| **3** | `explain` 104 · `using the diagram` 65 | **identify + develop + context** |
| 6 | `analyse` 123 | AO2 3 + AO3 3 |
| 9 | `assess` 82 | AO2 3 + AO3 3 + AO4 3 |
| 12 | `evaluate` 41 | AO2 4 + AO3 4 + AO4 4 |

Three statements from the reports that turn those numbers into rules:

> *"'Explain' questions require one reason/way/advantage, which is in context and developed, to gain
> all 3 marks."* — 4EC1/01R

> *"'What is meant by' questions have two marks and require two parts in the explanation of the term.
> No marks are awarded for examples."* — 4EC1/01R

> *"There are no marks for stating a formula in 'calculate' questions."* — 4EC1/01R
