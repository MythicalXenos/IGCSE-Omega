## Q1(c) — 2 marks — "What is meant by the term demand?"

**Mark scheme:** AO1 2. 1 mark for amount willing to be purchased, 1 mark for
reference to price. *No marks for examples.*

- **Response A (2/2):** "Demand refers to the willingness and willing of
  consumers to purchase certain goods depending on the price."
  → 1 for willingness/ability, 1 for "depending on price".
- **Response B (1/2):** "…willing to buy at a given price over a period of time."
  → got the price mark, lost the other because it says **willing** but not **able**.

**Rule:** two-part definition; *able* and *willing* both required.

## Q1(f) — 2 marks — average cost calculation

**Mark scheme:** 1 for the calculation `$12 100 ÷ 2 000`, 1 for `$6.05`.

## Q1(g) — 3 marks — diagram: hurricane destroys farmland

**Mark scheme:** 1 leftward shift of supply labelled, 1 higher equilibrium price
labelled, 1 lower equilibrium quantity labelled.

- **Response A (3/3)** — all three labelled.
- **Response B (2/3)** — correct shift drawn but **not labelled**.

**Rule:** a correct curve with no label is a lost mark. Label the curve *and*
both new equilibrium points.

## Q1(h) — 3 marks — why concert ticket demand is price inelastic

**Mark scheme:** 1 identify, 1 develop, 1 context of music concerts.

- **Response A (3/3)** — few substitutes → no alternative → fans. All three.
- **Response B (2/3)** — reason and context present, **no development**.

**Rule:** "Explain" = reason + development + context. Context alone is not
development.

## Q1(i) — 6 marks — why Coca-Cola bought shares in Inca Kola

- **Response A (5/6).** Examiner: "clear knowledge and understanding and
  appropriate application. The response is reasonably organised but **not
  excellent**… thorough analysis."
  → Level 3 reached, but 6 refused because organisation was not excellent.
- **Response B (4/6).** "some decent application… but also some

**Rule:** at Level 3 the discriminator between 5 and 6 is **organisation**, not

## Q2(g) — 3 marks — why fares are higher outside the city centre

- **Response A (3/3)** — reason (less competition) + development (no incentive
  to keep prices low) + context (few people live outside the centre).
- **Response B (2/3)** — reason + development, **no context**.

**Rule:** the third mark is always *context*. Name the country, the firm, the
figure.

## Q3(e) — 9 marks — "assess the extent to which education and training is the best way to increase productivity"

**Level descriptors quoted in the paper:**
- L1 (1–3): one viewpoint only, unbalanced, incomplete evaluation (AO4).
- L2 (4–6): more than one viewpoint but *may lack balance*, evaluation *may not
  demonstrate full understanding*.
- L3 (7–9): excellent selectivity and organisation; interpretation excellent,
  thorough analysis; **more than one viewpoint, well balanced and coherent,
  leading to an evaluation that demonstrates full understanding and awareness**.

- **Response A (6/6).** "some applied knowledge and more than one viewpoint,
  reasonably balanced and coherent. However, **neither the analysis nor the
  evaluation is thorough**… fully meets level 2 → top of level."
- **Response B (5/6).** "clear match to level 2 and **the starting point is
  always the middle of the level**. The mark would only go up to the top of the
  level if the response completely met the requirements… does not have a good
  interpretation of the economic information."

**Rule (explicit, from the examiner):** markers start at the **middle** of a
level and move up only if it *fully* meets the descriptor; move down if not.

## Q4(c) — 12 marks — "evaluate how firms might be influenced by competing in an oligopoly"

Indicative content splits cleanly into **AO2/AO3** (first block) and **AO4**
(second block), which is how the 4/4/4 split is actually built:

**AO2/AO3:** few dominant firms (83.5% between three) · brand names and
advertising create high set-up costs → barriers to entry · dominant firms enjoy
high profit because of those barriers · economies of scale lower average cost,
further raising profit and dominance · small firms survive by specialising or
serving niches.

**AO4:** they must compete on non-price grounds to avoid expensive price wars ·
interdependence — one firm's advertising hits the others' market share · market
keeps changing so dominance only persists if firms keep innovating · successful
small firms risk takeover or being crowded out · **"it may depend upon how
market trends change"** · if they cannot keep up they lose dominance.

**Rule:** the AO4 block is where the 12-mark answer lives. Notice that AO4 here
is mostly *conditions and consequences over time*, not "however".
