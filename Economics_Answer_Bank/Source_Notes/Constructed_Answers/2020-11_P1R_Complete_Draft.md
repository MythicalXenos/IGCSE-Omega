### (i) [6]
Adverse weather such as the typhoon on Lamma Island can damage restaurants’ property and equipment. This makes it harder for the affected restaurants to provide meals and raises their repair costs; one owner estimated repairs at **HK$300,000–HK$400,000**. Higher costs can cause firms to supply less at each price, shifting the market supply curve to the left. If demand is unchanged, the lower supply creates a shortage at the previous price, putting upward pressure on prices and reducing the quantity of restaurant meals sold. The source says some restaurants expected to take two or three months to reopen, so the disruption could persist while repairs were completed.

### (g) [9]
Lanterns was set up by Jo and Fiona to help young people in a town with few job opportunities, rather than to maximise profit. Continuing with this social aim preserves the original purpose of the café and maintains work opportunities for local young people. The café has already employed **25 previously inexperienced people**, giving them experience that could help them find future work.

However, customers have complained about long waits, and the sisters say they need more experienced staff. If the café does not respond, dissatisfied customers may stop visiting and revenue may fall. Changing its focus toward sales or profit could allow Lanterns to hire experienced workers, improve service and attract more customers. Higher revenue and profit could also help the café remain open, protecting the jobs and training opportunities it provides.

Changing its aims may not solve the problem automatically. More experienced workers could raise costs, and moving away from the social objective could reduce the café’s value to the local community. The sisters might instead retain their aim while changing staffing, training or the ordering system to reduce delays. Whether a change is worthwhile depends on whether customer dissatisfaction is seriously threatening the café’s survival and whether more experienced staff would improve service enough to cover their cost. A mixed approach—keeping the local-employment purpose but giving greater attention to service quality and financial sustainability—could address both concerns.

### (a) [1]
**B — Flexibility.** A small firm can often respond quickly to customers or changing market conditions.

### (b) [1]

### (c) [3]
An increase in the retirement age means more older people remain in or enter the labour force. Draw a **rightward shift of labour supply** from SL to SL1, leaving labour demand unchanged. Label the new equilibrium at a **lower wage rate** and a **higher quantity of workers employed**.

### (d) [6]
Collusion occurs when firms cooperate instead of competing, for example by agreeing prices. In the pharmaceutical extract, firms allegedly agreed to raise prices, with one tablet costing **$4.70 instead of $0.13**. This can make medicines much more expensive for consumers, particularly people who need them regularly. Higher prices reduce consumers’ real income and may make some people unable to afford treatment. Collusion can also reduce consumer choice and make it harder for new firms to enter, allowing the firms involved to maintain high prices and profits.

### (e) [9]
Public investment to improve Cambodia’s roads could benefit the economy because more than half of its **6,000 km of roads** were classified as poor quality. Better roads may reduce vehicle damage and accidents, making it safer and more reliable for workers and businesses to travel. Firms could move goods more quickly and cheaply, raising productivity and supporting trade. Improved roads could also connect businesses to a wider market and encourage further economic activity. If business output and incomes rise, the government may receive more tax revenue and employment may increase.

There are costs and risks. Public funds used for roads cannot be spent on healthcare, education or other infrastructure. Building and maintaining roads may require borrowing or higher taxes. Benefits also depend on good project selection and maintenance: investment may be wasted if roads are poorly built, or if new capacity does not meet the needs of firms and households. The source notes overloaded trucks, which could damage improved roads again unless vehicle use is managed.

The likely benefit depends on the quality and location of projects, the resources used and whether maintenance prevents renewed deterioration. Investment is likely to be valuable where it repairs heavily used poor roads and improves transport links, but its net benefit will be smaller if costs crowd out higher-priority services or roads quickly return to poor condition.

## Question 4

### (a) [2]
At **350 units of capital goods**, the PPF shows **160 units of consumer goods**. Producing an additional 100 units of capital goods takes output to 450 capital goods, where consumer goods fall to **130 units**.

Opportunity cost = 160 − 130 = **30 units of consumer goods**.

### (b) [6]
Diseconomies of scale occur when a firm becomes so large that its average costs rise. Ben’s employer has grown considerably over the last two years and now has many more employees. More layers of management and checklists can make decisions take longer and increase bureaucracy. Delays make the firm less efficient and raise the cost of completing each task. Communication can also become harder: Ben feels his ideas are not listened to, which may reduce his motivation and make him consider leaving. Replacing experienced employees would add recruitment and training costs, increasing the firm’s average costs further.

### (c) [12]
Income elasticity of demand measures how responsive quantity demanded is to a change in income. If Amazon Prime Video is a luxury service in Japan, demand may rise more than proportionately as incomes increase. Amazon’s **11.5% market share** could then apply to a growing market, allowing it to gain additional subscribers and revenue. Knowing YED could help Amazon forecast demand and decide how much to invest in content, advertising or subscription pricing. The service costs **3,900 yen (about $36.75) for an annual membership**, so the effect of higher income may influence whether more households consider subscribing.

However, YED is only one influence on demand. Consumers can choose among Netflix, dTV, U-NEXT and Hulu as well as Amazon. The extract says Amazon’s share is higher than Netflix’s but lower than dTV’s, U-NEXT’s and Hulu’s, so competitors’ content, quality and prices may matter as much as income. A rise in income does not guarantee that households will choose Amazon, particularly if the service does not meet their preferences. Subscription prices and the availability of other entertainment may also affect demand.

Overall, YED is useful to Amazon for estimating how rising Japanese incomes could change demand and for planning capacity, marketing and pricing. It is not enough on its own to determine success: the firm also needs to respond to competitors and provide content that subscribers value. YED is therefore an important forecasting tool, but its usefulness depends on combining it with evidence about market share, rival services and consumer preferences.
