## Item-specific observations

### Question 1
- **1(c), luxury good:** Some candidates confused a luxury good with a normal good. The report reminds candidates that a two-mark “what is meant by” definition needs two parts; examples alone do not earn marks.
- **1(d), PES factor:** Generally well answered. A one-mark “state” response needs one concise factor, not a long discussion.
- **1(e), substitute good:** Many showed good understanding. The report cautions against using an example in place of a definition.
- **1(f), PES calculation:** A small number supplied a correct 0.75 but incorrectly attached a percent or currency unit. Show working and give the coefficient without an inappropriate unit.
- **1(g), demand diagram:** Generally strong. Label both the shifted curve and the new equilibrium points to secure the diagram marks.
- **1(h), sole local firm:** Candidates could identify customer disadvantages. For all three marks, explain one disadvantage in context and develop it.
- **1(i), why Robert stays small:** Responses often focused appropriately on the small firm. Higher-level answers needed clear development and thorough analysis. This is an “analyse” item: do not spend time on a counterargument.

### Question 2
- **2(c), sector pie chart:** Many correctly showed tertiary employment as largest and secondary as second. Draw sector boundaries clearly; an ambiguous chart can lose credit.
- **2(d), free rider:** The report notes variable understanding and reminds candidates that a one-mark definition is not an invitation to give examples.
- **2(e), congestion as external cost:** Some answers described costs to the motorist, rather than a third party. Link congestion to an external effect such as pollution harming pedestrians, then develop it.
- **2(f), pharmacies providing vaccination:** Some responses drifted into the following assessment question. Keep the answer on why the government uses private pharmacies and apply/develop the reason.
- **2(g), public flu-vaccination cost:** Responses should present balanced, developed arguments on whether the cost is justified and apply evidence. The report says an “assess” answer does not require a conclusion, but needs a thorough two-sided argument; merely copying the extract is insufficient.

### Question 3
- **3(c), total revenue:** The correct result was commonly calculated as **$80,750**. A missing dollar sign sometimes lost a mark; retain the correct units.
- **3(d), PED:** Given the higher revenue after the price rise, the expected one-word answer was **inelastic**. No further calculation was required; own-figure rule applied where relevant.
- **3(e), outward PPF shift:** Understanding was often sound, but development and thorough analysis were common weaknesses. “Analyse” is one-sided; develop applied reasons for the increased productive capacity.
- **3(f), trade unions:** Some confused trade unions with trading blocs. The report notes that candidates could make a balanced case about labour-market impacts and that an explained labour-market diagram could help. An “assess” answer need not conclude, but should balance its arguments.

### Question 4
- **4(a), excess demand from graph:** Many found the correct difference of **30**; some struggled to interpret the graph. Practise extracting both quantities before calculating.
- **4(b), NBC Universal / IOC deal:** Stronger answers recognised NBC’s unique product. To move beyond a list, explain how exclusivity creates market power and benefits the broadcaster.
- **4(c), Google advertising:** Generic comments about search engines were not enough; answers needed to analyse and evaluate the supplied market-share information. “Evaluate” requires a supported judgement.

## General examiner guidance

Use evidence from the extract to support economic reasoning rather than simply repeat it. Apply the command word carefully: “analyse” calls for developed one-sided reasoning; “assess” requires a developed, balanced argument but no conclusion is compulsory; “evaluate” requires a supported judgement. Include relevant data, diagrams and units, and make each causal link explicit. These points summarise the local June 2019 01R report only; they are not claims about the standard 01 paper or later sessions.
