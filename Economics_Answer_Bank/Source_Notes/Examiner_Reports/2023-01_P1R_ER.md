## Item-specific observations

### Question 1
- **1(c), supply:** Accurate definitions referred to producers being willing and able to sell at a given time/price. A two-mark “what is meant by” answer needs two parts; partial definitions can earn one mark, examples do not (ER p.3).
- **1(d), external economies of scale:** Some candidates named an internal economy instead. Use the correct economic term; this one-mark response needs no extra development (p.3).
- **1(e), unique product:** Define the term; an example does not replace a definition (p.3).
- **1(f), PES:** **1.3** earns both marks; a correct calculation without the correct final answer earns one. Do not include units with elasticity (p.3).
- **1(g), luxury-holiday demand diagram:** Label the new curve and new equilibrium points. Shift only the relevant curve; shifting both curves does not represent the income change (p.3).
- **1(h), privatisation and telecom workers:** Explain one disadvantage, develop it and apply it to context. A list of disadvantages is not development; definitions earn no credit on this explain question (p.4).
- **1(i), PPC:** Focus on the impact of the movement and the increased capital-goods production, using the figures. Limited development/selectivity restricts analysis. Do not add a counterargument to an “analyse” response (p.4).

### Question 2
- **2(c), trade union:** Give an accurate two-part definition (p.4).
- **2(d), average cost:** Many calculated average cost accurately; some lost a mark by omitting the dollar sign. Units matter in quantitative answers (p.4).
- **2(e), start-up costs as a barrier:** Describe a causal mechanism (cause or consequence) rather than defining a barrier or giving another example. Develop one reason for both marks (p.4).
- **2(f), oligopoly:** Develop and contextualise one disadvantage to consumers; listing several undeveloped disadvantages limits attainment (p.5).
- **2(g), division of labour at Foxconn:** Apply reasoning to Foxconn and develop it. Higher responses used a balanced, assessed argument; a conclusion/judgement is not required, but developed counterarguments are (p.5).

### Question 3
- **3(c), employment-sector pie chart:** Clear labels and accurate sector sizes are needed; ambiguity in the drawn divisions can lose marks (p.5).
- **3(d), external costs of the Sydney tower:** Develop the cause and/or consequence of construction costs imposed on third parties, such as local people. “Analyse” requires focused, applied one-sided development, not evaluation (p.5).
- **3(e), migrant labour in Dubai:** Candidates often developed benefits better than possible problems. For “assess”, balance the applied arguments; no conclusion is required (p.6).

### Question 4
- **4(a), total revenue for the inelastic-demand product:** Identify the inelastic product and use its data only; show working (p.6).
- **4(b), consumer benefit and sugary drinks:** Use the extract evidence to support developed analysis, not merely state reasons; link points to the data (p.6).
- **4(c), NT Massage expansion:** Address whether the family should expand versus remain small. Stronger responses developed relevant arguments on both sides in context instead of copying the extract. A supported judgement/conclusion is required for “evaluate” (p.6).

## General examiner guidance

Read every requirement carefully, including rounding instructions. Quantitative skills include diagrams, calculations and application of extract data. Simply repeating extract evidence does not earn application: use it in economic reasoning. “Analyse” does not require evaluation; “assess” and “evaluate” require two-sided argument, with a supported judgement specifically required for “evaluate”. Relevant evidence may come from extracts or candidates’ own examples, but should be applied rather than copied (ER p.7).
