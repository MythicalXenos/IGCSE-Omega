## Paper-wide guidance (report pp. 3, 7–8)

- Higher-level responses used relevant evidence to develop the economics; generic or extract-copying responses were weaker.
- Application is earned by using evidence to explain the issue, not simply repeating a passage.
- “Analyse” needs developed one-sided reasoning, not a counterargument. “Assess” requires developed two-sided balance but no conclusion. “Evaluate” needs a balanced argument and a supported judgement/conclusion.
- Read the whole command-worded question and use the extracts.

## Item-specific guidance

- **Q1(c), p. 3:** external costs require both a negative effect and a third party. A two-mark “what is meant by” question requires two parts; examples do not substitute for the definition.
- **Q1(d), p. 3:** state an example of an external benefit, not a definition. Keep one-mark “state” answers concise.
- **Q1(e), p. 3:** define wants (the report accepted desires/non-essential products); do not give an example instead of the definition.
- **Q1(f), p. 3:** the excess-demand answer is 900 in total; alternatively the monthly excesses 600, 200 and 100 earned full credit. Show working.
- **Q1(g), p. 4:** label the new demand shift and new equilibrium points; do not shift both curves when population falls.
- **Q1(h), p. 4:** a barrier-to-entry explanation must be developed and applied to the music industry. Definitions alone do not score the explain marks.
- **Q1(i), p. 4:** cover all four factors of production, apply each to coffee, and develop analysis. Do not add a counterargument to “analyse”.
- **Q2(c), p. 4:** state the full PES formula, including percentage change in quantity supplied divided by percentage change in price.
- **Q2(d), p. 4:** identify the variable costs and total them; use appropriate currency units (QR here).
- **Q2(e), p. 5:** define a takeover precisely as one firm buying control of another; avoid merely repeating the term.
- **Q2(f), p. 5:** respond to “over time”: explain why consumers gain time to find substitutes, rather than only defining elasticity.
- **Q2(g), p. 5:** explain how the fine changes incentives/costs and apply to speeding; repeating that fines reduce externalities is not a reason.
- **Q2(h), p. 5:** define the economic problem and compare public/private provision with the Japan/Cuba evidence. Strong answers were balanced and developed; no conclusion is required for “assess”.
- **Q3(c), p. 6:** draw a clearly labelled PPF and show calendars increasing while diaries decrease along the frontier; do not draw a supply-and-demand graph.
- **Q3(d), p. 6:** analyse one-sided reasons for raising Turkey’s minimum wage, such as income, motivation/productivity, inequality or benefit spending; develop the applied chain.
- **Q3(e), p. 6:** apply internal economies of scale accurately to the airport business (not general national benefits). Balance potential economies with limitations; no conclusion is required for “assess”.
- **Q4(a), p. 6:** identify the elastic-PED product, calculate total revenue and include appropriate units.
- **Q4(b), pp. 6–7:** focus analysis on the effect of occupational mobility on labour supply, not simply repeat Tony’s situation; develop each point.
- **Q4(c), p. 7:** keep the focus on restaurant productivity. The report noted practical gains (speed, cost, consistency, attractiveness) and possible losses (customer difficulty, equipment/repair costs, reduced personal service); compare technology and labour costs and give a supported judgement.
