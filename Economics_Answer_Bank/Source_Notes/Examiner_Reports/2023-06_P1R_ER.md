## Item-specific observations

### Question 1
- **1(c), division of labour:** A two-mark “what is meant by” answer needs both parts of the definition; examples do not replace the definition.
- **1(d), leftward supply shift:** Give one reason that causes a shift, not a price change that causes movement along supply. The direction must be explicit (for example, a *fall* in subsidies).
- **1(e), competition:** Define the term; an example or simply repeating it is insufficient.
- **1(f), total revenue:** Many calculated the correct result. Show working and use the correct currency symbol: **€18,750**.
- **1(g), mango demand diagram:** Label the rightward demand shift and both new equilibrium values. Missing curve/equilibrium labels lost marks.
- **1(h), Christine’s café:** One reason needs development and context. The report’s examples connect customer satisfaction/large meals to loyalty and future sales.
- **1(i), sectoral employment:** Stronger responses applied and analysed the data. The report’s full-mark example was Level 3; it cautions candidates not to evaluate an “analyse” item.

### Question 2
- **2(c), external benefit:** Give an example, not a definition. One concise example is enough.
- **2(d), PED:** Keep the negative sign: **1.9% ÷ −1.2% = −1.58**. The report notes that a formula alone earns no marks.
- **2(e), entrepreneur:** A vague reference to owning a business is insufficient; reference to organising factors of production, risk-taking or setting up the business. An example is not needed for a one-mark definition.
- **2(f), water as a factor of production:** Develop one reason. The report’s example identifies water as a natural resource and then classifies it as land.
- **2(g), rice supply:** The effect is movement along the supply curve, not a shift. Use the figure’s price/quantity evidence and develop one effect; the report notes that an effect is needed for credit.
- **2(h), public parks:** Stronger responses used evidence to assess benefits and limitations. Repeating the extract is insufficient; the report illustrates a Level 3 response and notes that it was unbalanced.

### Question 3
- **3(c), car-park demand diagram:** Label a downward-sloping demand curve and plot the data accurately, including **$5 at 20 spaces** and **$1 at 60 spaces**.
- **3(d), inward PPF shift:** Level 3 answers used the evidence and developed their points. “Analyse” needs one-sided developed reasoning, not evaluation; the report’s example received 5/6.
- **3(e), WarnerMedia/Discovery merger:** “Assess” requires an applied, developed two-sided argument; no conclusion is required. The report’s illustrated response received 7/9.

### Question 4
- **4(a), excess supply:** The correct answer is **60 meals**. Show the calculation.
- **4(b), airline oligopoly:** Knowledge was generally good, but development varied. Develop each applied reasoning chain; evaluation is not credited for “analyse.”
- **4(c), derived demand and labour:** Use the event, supermarket and technology evidence in a balanced argument. The report notes that some candidates misunderstood derived demand. This is the paper’s only item requiring a supported judgement/conclusion; its illustrated response received 11/12.

## General examiner guidance

Use evidence in causal reasoning rather than copying it. Read the full command word and calculation requirements, including units, signs and decimal-place instructions. “Analyse” does not require evaluation; “assess” requires a balanced, applied argument but no conclusion; “evaluate” requires two sides and a supported judgement.
