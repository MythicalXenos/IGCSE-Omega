## Introduction

> "Overall, a good awareness of economics was shown by candidates taking this
> paper… Where applicable on the levels-based questions, the ability of the most
> able students was shown through relating their knowledge and understanding to
> the evidence presented, whereas those struggling with such concepts typically
> answered questions with a more generic approach."

**The single discriminator across the whole paper: used the evidence vs wrote
generically.**

## Question 1

**1(c)** economies of scale. Good answers had both "reduced average costs" *and*
"growth of the firm". Partial definitions = 1 mark.
> Tip: two marks, two parts. No marks for examples.

**1(d)** 1 mark.
> Tip: "it is either right or wrong, there is no need to go into further detail."

**1(e)** excess supply.
> Tip: "Do not use examples for 'define' questions."

**1(f)** PES. "1.16" got both marks; correct calculation with wrong final answer = 1.
> Tip: **"Remember not to put any units in the final answer when calculating
> elasticity."**

**1(g)** diagram.
> Tip: "You must label the new shift and the new equilibrium points… **Do not
> shift both curves** as this will not show understanding of the scenario."

**1(h)** 3-mark explain — advantages. "There was only ONE mark available for ONE
advantage."
> Tip: "There are NO marks for definitions on 'explain' questions. They require a
> reason/benefit etc, development and context."

**1(i)** 6-mark analyse.
> Tip: "Do not present a counter argument for analyse questions — there are no
> marks for doing this and it will mean you have less time to spend on other
> questions."

## Question 2

**2(c)** opportunity cost — some gave examples instead of the term.
**2(d)** total fixed costs — some failed to show units, lost a mark.
> Tip: "Units are an important part of the answer… e.g. % or $/Tk for currency
> (**or none if appropriate, such as when calculating elasticity**)."

**2(e)** describe — "some candidates gave a definition rather than answering the
question. This did not result in any marks."
> Tip: "There is only 1 mark for an advantage/reason/benefit etc. on 'describe'
> questions so do not give more than 1… The second mark is for development."

**2(f)** explain — several gave two or three reasons instead of developing one.
> Tip: "'Explain' questions only ask for ONE reason/advantage/factor etc."

**2(g)** 9-mark assess, tourist tax.
> Tip: "Assess questions require a balanced two-sided argument which is applied.
> There is no requirement for a conclusion or judgement."

---

## Question 3

**3(c)** LRAC diagram — lost marks for "getting economies and diseconomies of
scale the wrong way round or not indicating the point at which the firm is most
efficient."
> Tip: "Be very clear when drawing your lines. Ambiguity is likely to result in
> no marks."

**3(d)** 6-mark analyse, external benefits of education in Japan.
> Tip: "Only one-sided arguments are needed for analyse questions. Focus on
> developing applied points."

**3(e)** 9-mark assess, why producers may not maximise profit.
> Tip: "Although no conclusion is needed, it is important to provide balance
> between the arguments and to develop the points in order to score high marks on
> 'assess' questions."

---

## Question 4

**4(a)** percentage change — "failed to round correctly to two decimal places, as
required by the question, or show the correct units."
> Tip: "It is advisable to show workings in 'calculate' questions."

**4(b)** 6-mark analyse, PED at a café.
> "too many candidates failed to use the data and actually provide an analysis.
> Some responses were descriptive, just repeating the data."
> Tip: "There is no set number of points required on any levels-based question but
> **each point made needs to be developed** to move up through the levels."

**4(c)** 12-mark evaluate, division of labour in a pastry factory.
> "some instances where data was just copied rather than points being developed."
> Tip: "A supported conclusion/judgement is needed for evaluate questions."

## Summary — Pearson's own closing advice (verbatim)

- "Be careful to read the whole of the question. Certain requirements are given
  which were not acted upon by some candidates in this series, e.g. when answers
  are required 'to two decimal places'."
- "Candidates need to understand the requirements of the command words in the
  questions. This will allow them to access marks requiring each of the four
  assessment objectives."
- "Quantitative Skills will be tested throughout the paper. These may be in the
  form of diagrams/graphs, calculations or using the data in the Extracts to
  provide the application in the questions."
- "Application marks will not be awarded for simply repeating evidence in the
  extracts. The evidence needs to be used in the response."
- "Analyse questions do not require evaluation but the command words 'assess' and
  'evaluate' do require a two-sided argument in order to achieve full marks. The
  evaluate question also requires a judgement/conclusion."
- "There may be more answer space provided than you need to write your responses."
- "The use of relevant evidence is required throughout… However, simply copying
  this material will not gain marks."
- **"As the descriptor tables for analyse, assess and evaluate questions do not
  change between questions with the same command word or exam series, it is
  advisable to practice writing responses using them."**

That last line is the most useful sentence in any ER: the level descriptors are
a **fixed rubric** for the whole qualification. You can practise against the exact
words that will be used to mark you.
