This digest paraphrases the report’s question-specific comments; it is not a candidate script or official answer.

## Question 1
- **1(c), mixed economy:** two-part term questions require both public and private sectors and what they do; examples alone do not earn the definition marks.
- **1(d), state an occupation:** one mark calls for a concise accurate example; no extended explanation.
- **1(e), producer:** give a definition, not merely an example.
- **1(f), YED:** many earned both marks; show full working so a correct method can earn credit if the final result is wrong.
- **1(g), diagram:** label the rightward supply shift and the new equilibrium outcomes; equilibrium price falls and quantity rises after removal of the indirect tax.
- **1(h), opportunity cost:** the next-best alternative must be stated; then develop it in context and explain a cause/consequence. A definition of opportunity cost alone is insufficient.
- **1(i), analyse expansion factors:** analyse is one-sided; no evaluation marks are available. Apply developed reasoning to the club’s situation.

## Question 2
- **2(c), PED calculation:** correct answer 19%; use the correct units/format for calculated changes.
- **2(d), social benefits:** identify private benefits and external benefits; examples do not substitute for explaining the term.
- **2(e), oligopoly feature:** “describe” needs one feature plus development; a definition alone is not enough.
- **2(f), explain discount:** one reason/effect is credited, developed and placed in the shop/discount context; avoid listing unrelated reasons.
- **2(g), regulation and externalities:** stronger assessments used a balanced, developed argument and used the Kenya evidence to answer the effectiveness question rather than repeating the extract.

## Question 3
- **3(c), elastic-demand diagram:** label the curve; show a smaller price-axis change and a larger quantity-axis change. Labels are required for full credit.
- **3(d), division of labour:** stronger Level 3 responses applied evidence and developed their reasoning. Explain how specialisation creates the claimed advantages rather than listing them.
- **3(e), compare supply elasticity:** top responses gave a balanced assessment and applied evidence about textile spare capacity and agricultural production.

## Question 4
- **4(a), total cost:** `$126.25` earns both marks; workings are recommended. Correct dollar units matter.
- **4(b), internal economies of scale:** candidates often showed knowledge but listed points. Develop the causal link from each internal economy to a benefit for MAS.
- **4(c), privatisation:** high-level answers used the evidence in a balanced argument; paraphrase alone is not application. Develop consumer benefits and costs and evaluate them in context.

## General use
Match the command word and tariff, show calculations, label diagrams and make evidence part of the causal explanation. This report digest guides practice; it does not certify a constructed response’s score.
