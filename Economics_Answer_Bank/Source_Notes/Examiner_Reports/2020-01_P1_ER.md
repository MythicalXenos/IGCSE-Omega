# January 2020 4EC1/01 examiner-report evidence digest

**Source identity:** Pearson, *International GCSE Economics 4EC1 Paper 01: Microeconomics and Business Economics, Examiners’ Report / Principal Examiner Feedback, January 2020*, publication code `4EC1_01_ER_2001`, local file `uploads/Incoming_Economics_2026-11/4EC1_01_pef_20200305.pdf` (7 pages). Cover, code and paper identity were checked. It is the standard P1 report and matches the January 2020 local P1 QP/MS. No report download or web search was needed for this audit. The report contains item feedback, not candidate scripts.

## Paper-wide guidance (report pp. 3, 6–7)

- Stronger levels-based answers applied economics to relevant evidence; generic material limited access to higher levels.
- Do not earn application by copying an extract: explain how its evidence answers the actual task.
- “Analyse” calls for developed applied reasoning and does not need a counterargument. “Assess” requires a balanced, applied two-sided argument; no conclusion is required. “Evaluate” requires balanced analysis and a supported judgement/conclusion.
- Read the command word and question carefully; use relevant evidence from the extracts.

## Item-specific guidance

- **Q1(c), p. 3:** the definition of supply needs both producer willingness and ability to sell and a reference to price or time; examples do not replace the two-part definition.
- **Q1(d), Q1(e), p. 3:** give a short secondary-sector occupation for “state”. For labour, the report accepted definitions referring to the workforce or human capital; define the term rather than giving an example.
- **Q1(f), p. 3:** show working for YED and avoid inappropriate units such as % or $ in the final elasticity value.
- **Q1(g), pp. 3–4:** label the demand shift and new equilibrium points; do not shift both curves when income changes demand.
- **Q1(h), p. 4:** develop and contextualise the foregone alternative to the hospital; a definition of opportunity cost alone does not answer the explain task.
- **Q1(i), p. 4:** responses needed clear economic application and developed analysis. No counterargument is required for “analyse”.
- **Q2(c), p. 4:** include “total” in both parts of the profit formula: total revenue minus total costs.
- **Q2(d), p. 4:** use the appropriate unit (none for PES); a percent sign is not an appropriate unit for the elasticity value.
- **Q2(e), p. 4:** avoid a vague definition such as only “money”; explain finance as funding/money used by a business.
- **Q2(f), p. 5:** develop the monopoly identification, for example by explaining that the firm has no competitors, is unique, or has no substitutes. A “describe” answer need not list several market structures.
- **Q2(g), p. 5:** explain non-excludability or non-rivalry and link it to street lighting and people benefiting without paying.
- **Q2(h), p. 5:** compare both choices, apply the candle-business evidence, and develop the analysis. “Assess” requires balance but no conclusion; merely copying the extract is insufficient.
- **Q3(c), p. 5:** show the PPC expansion clearly and label it; ambiguous curves may not receive credit.
- **Q3(d), p. 5:** use a one-sided, developed analysis of labour demand; do not add an evaluation/counterargument.
- **Q3(e), p. 6:** balance positive and negative externalities of fireworks; no conclusion is required for “assess”. Do not assess fireworks in general instead of third-party spillovers.
- **Q4(a), p. 6:** show calculation and appropriate units when calculating the quantity of cakes.
- **Q4(b), p. 6:** develop causal chains about why governments control takeovers; listing possible reasons without analysis limits attainment.
- **Q4(c), pp. 6–7:** answer the usefulness of PED in informing a VAT decision, not simply whether VAT should rise. Apply the South African data, evaluate both sides and finish with a supported judgement.
