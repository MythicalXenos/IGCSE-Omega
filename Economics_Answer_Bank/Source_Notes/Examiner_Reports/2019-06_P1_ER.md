# June 2019 4EC1/01 examiner-report evidence digest

**Source identity:** Pearson, *International GCSE Economics 4EC1/01 Examiners’ Report, June 2019*, publication code `4EC1_01_1906_ER`, local PDF `uploads/Incoming_Economics_2026-11/4EC1_01_pef_20190822.pdf` (42 pages). The report cover and publication code were checked; the report is paired with the June 2019 standard P1, not P1R. This is a summary of examiner guidance, not candidate exemplar text. Selected example-response observations are separately transcribed in `Official_Exemplars_June2019_P1.md`.

## Paper-wide guidance (report pp. 3, 40)

- For levels-based questions, stronger answers applied knowledge to the evidence; generic explanations constrained higher attainment.
- Application is not earned by copying extract facts: use them to explain the issue asked about.
- “Analyse” calls for developed applied reasoning, not counterargument/evaluation. “Assess” requires a developed two-sided argument; “evaluate” also needs a supported conclusion.
- Read the entire task and respect requested forms/units. Use extracts because they provide application evidence.

## Item-specific evidence

- **Q1(c), pp. 4:** demand needs both willingness and ability to buy and reference to a given price. Examples do not replace the definition; a two-mark response normally needs two components.
- **Q1(d), p. 5; Q1(e), p. 6:** give the correct social-cost formula; a definition of tertiary sector must identify services, not merely an example. One-mark answers should be brief.
- **Q1(f), p. 7:** include the correct currency unit in the final average cost. Showing working can earn a method mark even if the final answer is wrong.
- **Q1(g), pp. 8–9:** label the leftward supply shift and the new equilibrium points/quantities clearly.
- **Q1(h), p. 10:** explain a reason in context and develop it; merely defining price inelasticity misses the task. The report gives uniqueness/no substitutes for fans as a context-linked reason.
- **Q1(i), pp. 11–12:** some candidates confused Coca-Cola’s purchase of shares in Inca Kola with market share. Developed, relevant analysis is needed; do not add a counterargument to an “analyse” response. The example-response transcript records that a 5/6 response had applied analysis but insufficient development, and the commentary stresses organisation at the top of Level 3.
- **Q2(c), p. 13:** retain the negative sign in PED; −12/15 earns a method mark.
- **Q2(d), p. 14; Q2(e), p. 15:** innovation needs a sufficiently specific reference to a new product/idea, not simply “an idea”; for a leftward demand shift, explicitly state the direction (for example, a decrease in income). State one factor only when asked for one.
- **Q2(f), pp. 16–17:** give one passenger advantage and develop it; do not answer in terms of price, which the question excludes, or list alternatives without development.
- **Q2(g), pp. 18–19:** connect a reason, development and Dhaka context. A possible chain is lower demand outside the centre, associated with lower population density, so fewer auto-rickshaws compete for passengers. Avoid simply repeating the question.
- **Q2(h), pp. 20–23:** stronger answers were balanced and used the APS evidence to analyse the parking-space shortage rather than restating the extract. A diagram is optional. The illustrated full-mark response is applied and two-sided.
- **Q3(c), pp. 24–25:** clearly label the minimum wage and resulting labour quantities/equilibrium on the diagram.
- **Q3(d), pp. 26–27:** use the Canadian evidence and develop applied explanations. A counterargument is unnecessary for “analyse”; the report notes that a response that became descriptive after a promising start did not receive full marks.
- **Q3(e), pp. 28–30:** assess with a balanced comparison of education/training and alternatives or limitations. The report says no conclusion is required for “assess”, but arguments on both sides should be developed and thorough. Its illustrated response earned 8/9 despite strong two-sided application because evaluation was not fully balanced.
- **Q4(a), pp. 31–32:** excess supply is 50 − 20 = 30 goods; show working. Do not attach an incorrect currency or percentage unit.
- **Q4(b), pp. 33–35:** develop each applied chain instead of listing several points. The illustrated full-mark response matches clear knowledge, application and thorough analysis.
- **Q4(c), pp. 36–39:** use the Dutch supermarket data to support, not merely repeat, the argument; develop both sides. “Evaluate” requires a supported judgement: the example response received 11/12 because it lacked one.

These points inform the constructed answers but do not constitute examiner scoring of those answers or certify a mark outcome.
