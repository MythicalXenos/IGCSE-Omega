# November 2024 — 4EC1/01 — Examiners’ Report

**Official Pearson source, retrieved and read 2026-11-10.**
URL: `https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/International-GCSE/Economics/2017/Exam-materials/4ec1-01-pef-20250123.pdf`
Publication code: `4EC1_01_2411_ER`.

This is a concise summary of the report’s question-specific commentary, not a
transcription of candidate scripts.

## Overall finding

Stronger responses connected knowledge and understanding to the evidence; more
generic answers limited attainment on levels-based questions. Extended responses
needed development and application to relevant evidence.

## Question 1

- **1(c), positive externality:** two marks required the idea of “positive” and
  an effect on “third parties”; examples did not earn marks.
- **1(d), one-mark item:** answer directly; no detail was needed. Some candidates
  misread it and gave a definition.
- **1(e), takeover:** a correct definition earned the mark; do not repeat the
  term or give an example instead of defining it.
- **1(f), PES:** 0.39 earned both marks; correct working without the correct final
  answer earned one. Elasticity answers take no units.
- **1(g), diagram:** label the shift and new equilibrium points; do not shift both
  curves when the scenario changes only one.
- **1(h), explain:** the response needed one reason, context and a developed
  cause/consequence. Simply saying goods were price elastic did not explain why
  over time or address the opportunity cost.
- **1(i), analyse factors of production:** analyse is one-sided. No evaluation
  marks are available, so a counterargument wastes time.

## Question 2

- **2(c), total variable cost:** use the correct calculation and units; adding
  all supplied figures together showed misunderstanding.
- **2(d), private benefits:** two parts were needed—private benefits and external
  benefits. Examples do not earn marks for “what is meant by”.
- **2(e), describe monopoly:** one feature/reason earns the first mark and its
  development earns the second; definitions do not earn marks here.
- **2(f), explain:** one effect/reason/way can be credited, with context and
  development. One credited chain was reducing monopoly power, increasing
  competition and therefore reducing taxi fares. Avoid repeating the question.
- **2(g), assess trade unions:** high-level answers used a balanced, developed
  argument and applied evidence. Listing points or repeating the extract was
  insufficient; stronger responses developed both the achieved wage increase
  and the fact that the full 10.5% target was not achieved.

## Question 3

- **3(c), PPC:** label the axes “toothpaste” and “shampoo”; shifting the PPC
  outwards was incorrect for the scenario. Diagram labels matter.
- **3(d), analyse minimum wage:** Level 3 responses used the data and diagram,
  developed their points and recognised the significance of a 36% minimum-wage
  increase. Analyse requires one-sided, developed application rather than a list.
- **3(e), assess quality of labour and productivity:** high-level answers balanced
  reasons productivity may rise against factors that could reduce or prevent the
  effect, using the data. Assess requires a developed, applied two-sided argument,
  but no conclusion or judgement.

## Question 4

- **4(a), calculation:** 6,500 earned both marks; show full workings.
- **4(b), inefficient resource allocation:** develop and contextualise each
  analytical chain. Lists and repeated data did not demonstrate analysis.
- **4(c), evaluate economies of scale as X expanded:** top-level responses used
  evidence in a balanced argument and explained **why** economies of scale could
  reduce average costs as X expanded. Merely repeating evidence or failing to
  connect the argument to the average-cost mechanism limited marks. A supported
  judgement/conclusion is required for top Level 3.

## Practical takeaways

1. Use extract evidence in a causal explanation; quotation or paraphrase alone
   is not application.
2. Distinguish the command words: **analyse** is one-sided; **assess** is
   balanced without a required judgement; **evaluate** is balanced and requires
   a supported final judgement.
3. Match the tariff: short questions need the requested answer; “explain” and
   “describe” require development, while definitions belong only where asked.
4. Label diagrams and use correct units/precision. Show calculation workings.
