## Item-specific observations

### Question 1
- **1(c), mixed economy:** Strong answers referred to both public and private sectors and their role in providing goods and services. A two-mark “what is meant by” answer needs both parts; examples do not substitute for a definition.
- **1(d), tertiary-sector occupation:** Generally well answered. A one-mark “state” item needs one concise example.
- **1(e), government:** Generally good understanding. Define the term rather than supplying examples.
- **1(f), YED calculation:** Some candidates attached a percent or currency unit to the correct coefficient of 2.4. Show working and report the coefficient without an inappropriate unit.
- **1(g), supply diagram:** Generally strong. Label the shifted curve and new equilibrium points; do not shift both curves when only supply changes.
- **1(h), computer-system opportunity cost:** Answers often named and developed an opportunity cost, but fewer made clear what the firm gave up specifically in order to purchase the system. Explain the cost, develop it and apply it to the decision.
- **1(i), Dubai shopping-mall oligopoly:** Candidates identified oligopoly features, but limited application and development often restricted higher-level analysis. The report explicitly advises against counterargument on an “analyse” item.

### Question 2
- **2(c), PED calculation:** Some correct calculations omitted the negative sign. Include the sign in both the calculation and answer.
- **2(d), social-benefit formula:** Some answers confused benefits with costs. Give the full formula: private benefits plus external benefits.
- **2(e), consumer:** Avoid vague definitions such as simply “buyer”; make clear that the person buys for personal use rather than being a firm.
- **2(f), firm growth:** Developed factors were generally well understood. For “describe,” develop one factor rather than giving a list.
- **2(g), persistent sugar consumption:** Habits and influence of others were understood, but candidates needed to explain the reason and apply it to eating sugar.
- **2(h), government fines and sea-cucumber over-harvesting:** Arguments for and against were available, but responses were not always balanced or applied to the data. The report says an “assess” response needs a developed, balanced two-sided argument; a conclusion is not required. Repeating the extract is not sufficient.

### Question 3
- **3(c), elastic demand diagram:** Make the curve more horizontal than vertical and show quantity changing by more than price. Draw and label clearly to avoid ambiguity.
- **3(d), privatisation for Slovenia:** Understanding was generally sound; developed, applied analysis was the common weakness. “Analyse” is one-sided and requires developed applied points.
- **3(e), cars versus organic strawberries PES:** Many made a reasonable two-sided comparison. A balanced assessment is important; no conclusion is required.

### Question 4
- **4(a), Andorran monthly income:** The report gives the correct income as **$1,027.25**, while the QP/MS data are denominated in euros (€5.87 per hour). Preserve the QP/MS currency in the constructed answer (**€1,027.25**) rather than repeating the report’s apparent dollar-sign slip. Show working and units.
- **4(b), airline economies of scale:** Stronger responses developed the benefits for the smaller airline; listing types of economies without explaining the mechanism was weaker.
- **4(c), noodle-factory productivity:** Responses should balance division-of-labour benefits and drawbacks, analyse the supplied factory data rather than give a generic list, and conclude with a supported judgement because this is an “evaluate” item.

## General examiner guidance

Apply economic understanding to the evidence rather than giving generic statements or copying the extract. “Analyse” requires developed reasoning without evaluation; “assess” requires a developed two-sided argument but not a conclusion; “evaluate” requires a balanced argument and supported judgement. Show calculations, signs and units carefully, and label diagrams precisely. These observations are specific to the local January 2020 P1R report.
