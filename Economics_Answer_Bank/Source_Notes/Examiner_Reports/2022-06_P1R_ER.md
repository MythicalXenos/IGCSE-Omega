## Item-specific observations

### Question 1
- **1(c), inferior good:** “What is meant by” questions need two parts of a definition. Examples alone receive no credit; a partial definition may receive one mark.
- **1(d), demand shift:** State a reason that shifts demand left. A price change causes movement along the curve, not a shift. Identify the direction of income change (a *decrease* in income, not just “income”). Give one reason only.
- **1(e), production:** Define the term; an example alone is insufficient.
- **1(f), PED:** Many calculated the answer correctly. Preserve the negative sign: **−2.1% ÷ 1.6% = −1.31**. No marks for a formula alone.
- **1(g), supply diagram:** Label the leftward supply shift and both new equilibrium points (higher price, lower quantity); missing labels cost marks.
- **1(h), China’s sectoral employment:** A definition of the tertiary sector alone is insufficient. Develop one contextual reason; a relevant chain links China’s development and rising service-sector output with a change in employment.
- **1(i), Unilever profits:** Both positive and negative profit effects were creditable. Application and analysis of the extract differentiated responses. The report warns candidates not to evaluate an “analyse” question.

### Question 2
- **2(c), PES formula:** One mark; a concise correct formula is sufficient.
- **2(d), total variable costs:** Correct total **$127,000**. Some candidates calculated only $115,000; the report notes that correct calculation work without the correct final total/units could lose a mark. Use dollars and show working.
- **2(e), minimum wage:** The definition needs the legal amount paid to workers, set by government; a vague reference to money paid is insufficient. Examples are unnecessary.
- **2(f), scarcity:** Develop the reason: resources are finite, so not everyone’s wants can be fulfilled. A list of reasons does not replace development.
- **2(g), cocoa demand:** This is a movement along the demand curve, not a shift. Candidates could use the figure’s price/quantity data, for example price rising from $2.32 to $2.42 and quantity demanded falling from 400 kg to 300 kg. The report’s illustrated response describes a movement and a decrease in quantity demanded; use the printed question/figure carefully.
- **2(h), Tanzanian telecom oligopoly:** Stronger answers used evidence to weigh likely consumer benefits and disadvantages. Repeating the extract without explaining its implications was insufficient. Develop both sides and apply evidence such as market shares, firm exit and the merger.

### Question 3
- **3(c), PPF:** The diagram needs correctly labelled chairs/tables axes, a PPF, and a point showing fewer chairs and more tables. Label the diagram.
- **3(d), vaccination external benefit:** The report describes developed, applied responses as strongest. It gives a level-3, six-mark response as an example and notes that “analyse” requires one-sided developed reasoning, not evaluation.
- **3(e), substitutes and labour demand:** Strong responses offered a balanced argument using evidence. The report illustrates that machines may replace some shop workers, while online sales can increase demand for warehouse, delivery or technology workers. “Assess” requires a balanced, contextual argument; a conclusion is not required.

### Question 4
- **4(a), excess demand:** Correct answer **3,850 tickets**; the report recommends showing full calculations.
- **4(b), public sector in Cuba:** Knowledge of public-sector provision was generally sound, but analytical development varied. Develop each applied chain rather than listing separate points; the report gives a full-mark level-3 response as an example.
- **4(c), Facebook messaging services and economies of scale:** Stronger responses used evidence and balanced arguments. Repeating the extract limited achievement. “Evaluate” requires a supported judgement; the report’s example earned level 3, 12 marks.

## General examiner guidance

Read all question requirements, including specified decimal places; quantitative skills can involve calculations, diagrams, graphs and using extract data. Evidence must be used in an explanation rather than copied. “Analyse” calls for developed one-sided reasoning, “assess” for a balanced two-sided argument without a required conclusion, and “evaluate” for a balanced argument with a supported judgement.
