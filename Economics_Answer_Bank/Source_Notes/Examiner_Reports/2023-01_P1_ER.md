## Overall

Candidates generally showed good economics awareness and preparation. On
levels-based responses, stronger candidates applied knowledge to the evidence;
weaker responses tended to be generic.

## Question 1

- **1(c), division of labour:** Full definitions conveyed both division of the
  production process and workers performing one task; partial definitions
  earned one mark. A two-mark “what is meant by” question needs two elements;
  examples do not substitute for definition.
- **1(d), factor of production:** Some candidates named an incorrect factor.
  For a one-mark state question, use precise terminology and do not over-elaborate.
- **1(e), public sector:** Generally understood. Define the term rather than
  giving examples.
- **1(f), PED:** Strong calculation performance. `-1.28` earned both marks;
  correct method with an incorrect final answer could earn one. Do not include
  units in an elasticity answer.
- **1(g), demand diagram:** Label the shifted demand curve and the new
  equilibrium price and quantity; do not shift both curves.
- **1(h), explain:** Many gave relevant reasons, but some listed reasons rather
  than developing one. Pearson’s guidance: give one reason, develop it, and
  apply it to the context; definitions do not earn marks on explain questions.
- **1(i), YED and Megabus:** Some responses did not explain usefulness to the
  firm, copied the extract, or lacked development/selectivity. For analyse,
  develop relevant applied points; a counterargument is unnecessary.

## Question 2

- **2(c), barriers to entry:** Generally understood, though some gave examples
  instead of defining the term.
- **2(d), total costs:** Strong calculation performance; some lost a mark by
  omitting the dollar sign. Include appropriate units.
- **2(e), subsidy disadvantage:** Some defined subsidies or gave a disadvantage
  to a firm instead of answering from the government’s perspective. One
  relevant disadvantage plus development was required.
- **2(f), explain why a business may stay small:** Many gave relevant developed
  reasons, while some listed multiple reasons instead of developing and
  contextualising one.
- **2(g), assess economies of scale:** Responses often lacked developed
  analysis and balanced alternative viewpoints. Assess requires applied,
  developed arguments on both sides; no conclusion is required.

## Question 3

- **3(c), PES diagram:** Some diagrams lacked clear labels or failed to show a
  greater quantity change than price change. Draw curves and changes clearly.
- **3(d), government flood defences:** Candidates showed understanding, but
  insufficient development limited marks. Credit included public-good reasoning
  and benefits from reducing flood damage. Analyse needs a developed,
  applied one-sided argument.
- **3(e), trade unions and Morrisons:** Responses often made stronger points
  about worker benefits or higher costs than about countervailing views, such
  as productivity gains offsetting costs or costs passed to consumers. Apply
  points to the context and balance the argument for a high-level assess answer;
  a conclusion is not required.

## Question 4

- **4(a), opportunity cost on the PPC:** Many correctly identified the capital
  goods sacrificed when consumer-goods output rose; some did not connect the
  opportunity cost to capital goods. Show workings in calculations.
- **4(b), equilibrium price:** The concept was generally well studied and some
  used the evidence to explain price determination. Errors included claiming
  that increased supply automatically increases demand, or offering no analysis.
  Develop each point in a levels-based answer; there is no fixed number of points.
- **4(c), competition and restaurant customers:** Many understood competition,
  but some copied the extract instead of developing points. Stronger answers
  evaluated advantages and disadvantages for customers in the restaurant
  context. Evaluate requires a supported judgement/conclusion.

## Pearson’s closing guidance — paraphrase

Read all question requirements, including rounding; understand command words;
expect quantitative skills throughout; use rather than merely repeat extract
evidence; keep analyse one-sided, and make assess/evaluate answers balanced
(two-sided), with a judgement for evaluate. Extra answer space may be provided.
Practise against the level descriptors, which Pearson notes do not change for a
given command word across questions and series.
