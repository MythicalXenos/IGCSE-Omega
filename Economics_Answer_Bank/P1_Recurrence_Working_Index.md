## Family A — PED: few close substitutes / differentiated experience

**Cue:** “Explain/analyse why PED may be relatively inelastic” in a context where buyers value a particular live experience, location or product feature.

**Reusable chain:** distinctive experience or valued feature → alternative products may not be close substitutes → after a price rise, fewer buyers switch away → quantity demanded falls relatively little compared with the price rise.

**Why each arrow can follow:** PED measures how responsive quantity demanded is to price. If an alternative does not provide the feature the buyer wants, it is not a close substitute; switching becomes less attractive, so the quantity response may be smaller. The mark scheme may accept other routes too: for Marina Café, it also cites the price as a small share of income.

**Limits/context changes:** “No substitute” is not automatic. A fan who specifically wants to see that artist live may have no close substitute for that experience; a person who only wants coffee may regard another café selling the same brand as a close substitute. The data show a cheaper café and a distinctive view, but do not prove every customer values the view. Higher prices can still reduce quantity demanded; inelastic does not mean unchanged demand. Keep any inference conditional.

## Family B — PED becomes more elastic over time

**Cue:** a prompt says PED changes after months or asks why demand becomes more elastic over time.

**Reusable chain:** habits, contracts or limited alternatives can restrict immediate adjustment → with time consumers may change habits or producers may enter → more switching options / a larger response to a price change → PED may become more elastic.

**Limits/context changes:** this is conditional, not automatic. Habits may persist; market entry may be blocked; substitutes may remain poor. The change from −1.5 to −2.0 is a change in elasticity magnitude, not proof that every buyer reduced consumption. The mark scheme treats habit change and more firms/substitutes as alternative explanations, not required consecutive links.
